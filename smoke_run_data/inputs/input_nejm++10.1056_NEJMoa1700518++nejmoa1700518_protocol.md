# Evaluation Input

## 1.1 Background and Rationale

After initial anticoagulation for DVT or PE, recurrent VTE remains clinically important. Long-term VKA therapy is effective but carries bleeding, monitoring, and dose-adjustment burden; ASA has modest recurrence-prevention efficacy. This study evaluates whether reduced-dose rivaroxaban can maintain efficacy while improving safety, using ASA as comparator.

## 1.2 Objectives

### Primary Objectives

- **P1:** Evaluate superiority of rivaroxaban 10 mg and 20 mg versus ASA 100 mg for fatal or nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE.
  - **P1 outcome:** Fatal or nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE, comparing rivaroxaban 10 mg and 20 mg versus ASA 100 mg.

### Secondary/Exploratory Objectives

**Secondary objective**

- **S1:** Evaluate superiority of each rivaroxaban dose versus ASA for fatal/nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE, MI, ischemic stroke, or systemic non-CNS embolism.
  - **S1 outcome:** Fatal/nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE, MI, ischemic stroke, or systemic non-CNS embolism, comparing each rivaroxaban dose versus ASA.

**Safety objectives**

- **S2 — Principal safety objective:** Document incidence of major bleeding.
  - **S2 outcome:** Incidence of major bleeding.
- **S3 — Secondary safety objective:** Document incidence of CRNM bleeding.
  - **S3 outcome:** Incidence of CRNM bleeding.

**Additional study objectives—not labeled exploratory objectives**

- **S4 (a):** Evaluate the composite of nonfatal symptomatic VTE and all-cause mortality.
  - **S4 outcome:** Composite of nonfatal symptomatic VTE and all-cause mortality.
- **S5 (b):** Evaluate the composite of major bleeding and recurrent VTE.
  - **S5 outcome:** Composite of major bleeding and recurrent VTE.
- **S6 (c):** Evaluate the composite of major bleeding, recurrent VTE, MI, ischemic stroke, and systemic non-CNS embolism.
  - **S6 outcome:** Composite of major bleeding, recurrent VTE, MI, ischemic stroke, and systemic non-CNS embolism.

Exploratory analyses describe composites of the primary or secondary efficacy outcome with symptomatic venous thrombosis in other locations and net clinical benefit.

## 2.1 Trial Design

### Design Type

Phase III, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, active-comparator, event-driven trial. All efficacy and safety events are adjudicated centrally, and final analyses use adjudication results.

### Allocation Ratio

1:1:1.

### Intervention Summary

Oral once-daily treatment with food:

- Rivaroxaban 10 mg.
- Rivaroxaban 20 mg.
- ASA 100 mg.

Nominal treatment duration is 12 months, although final randomized patients may receive only 6 months. Double-dummy supplies comprise one rivaroxaban-or-placebo tablet and one ASA-or-placebo tablet daily.

The first dose is ≥24 hours after previous VTE treatment. With VKA, INR >3.0 delays randomization; INR 2.5–3.0 permits randomization but delays first intake until the next day; INR <2.5 permits dosing. A missed dose may be made up only on the same day; double dosing is not allowed.

Six months’ medication is initially supplied. IxRS determines at visit 4 whether a further six months is supplied. Treatment may restart after temporary interruption if total treatment does not exceed 12 months. Last randomized patients receive no second supply.

## 2.2 Randomization

**Method&nbsp;**

Central interactive voice/web response system (IxRS), with a unique randomization number identifying treatment allocation. The investigator provides study-center identification and DVT/PE presentation.

- **Stratification:** Country and investigator-reported index event: DVT only versus PE with or without DVT.
- **Minimization or blocking:** Not specified.

## 2.3 Sample Size

### Planned Sample Size

Approximately 950 patients per arm; 2,850 total, subject to adjustment using observed overall primary-event incidence.

- **Calculation outcome:** Confirmed primary efficacy outcome of fatal or nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE; time-to-first-event survival outcome.
- **Control assumption:** ASA event rate of 5% per year, modeled exponentially.
- **Treatment effects:** HR 0.30 for rivaroxaban 20 mg versus ASA and HR 0.40 for rivaroxaban 10 mg versus ASA.
- **Variability:** No separate variability parameter specified; simulations used exponential event, discontinuation, and withdrawal processes.
- **Alpha:** Two-sided 0.05 for sequential superiority tests.
- **Power:** 90%; 10,000-trial simulation estimated joint Cox-regression power of 0.901.
- **Assumed test:** Stratified Cox proportional-hazards superiority analysis with upper 95% CI bound below 1; stratified log-rank testing is supportive.
- **Required events:** At least 80 confirmed primary efficacy outcomes.
- **Non-inferiority/equivalence margin:** Not applicable; comparisons are superiority tests.
- **Discontinuation and loss assumptions:** Exponential annual 10% treatment discontinuation and 1% loss to follow-up/withdrawn consent. Last subjects receive 6 months’ treatment. After simulated discontinuation, simulated event time uses inferior-control treatment; losses before the simulated event are censored.
- **Design effect/clustering:** Not applicable; individually randomized, non-clustered trial.
- **Basis for assumptions:** The 20-mg assumption derives from an adjusted indirect comparison of EINSTEIN Extension and pooled ASA evidence. The indirect estimate was HR 0.264 (95% CI 0.12–0.59); HR 0.30 was conservative. The 10-mg assumption reflects orthopedic rivaroxaban and apixaban extended-treatment evidence; HR 0.40, a 60% reduction, was set versus an expected HR 0.30 from the reduced-dose rationale.
- **Sample-size adjustment:** Blinded pooled projection based on observed primary-event incidence adjusts enrollment. Projection iterates total N until the probability of ≥80 total events is ≥0.50, also presenting alternatives such as probability about 0.80.
- **Calculation software:** SAS Macro by Shih and three-arm simulations.
- **Simulation rationale:** The event-driven design targets 80 events to demonstrate superiority of both doses. Simulations run 10,000 trials, apply administrative censoring at one year/end of trial, select trials/observation time reaching the target, and fit Cox and log-rank tests. Joint power is defined as both 20-mg/ASA and 10-mg/ASA HR 95% CI upper bounds ≤1, yielding 0.901.

## 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

A fixed sequence controls familywise error:

1. Test H0: HR(20 mg/ASA)=1 versus two-sided H1: HR≠1.
2. Only if significant, test H0: HR(10 mg/ASA)=1 versus two-sided H1: HR≠1.

Each test is two-sided at 0.05. Rivaroxaban 20 mg is superior if its HR 95% CI upper bound is <1. Rivaroxaban 10 mg is superior only if 20 mg is superior and its own upper bound is <1.

## 2.5 Timing of Analyses

SAP revisions are issued before database lock. Final analyses use adjudicated outcomes through each patient’s individual intended treatment limit. Standard limits are day 353, visit day if day 353–367, or day 367; defined administrative shortening rules also apply.

Assessments occur at baseline, Day 1, Day 30±7, Day 90±7, Day 180±7, Day 270±7, Day 360±7, and a 30±7-day post-treatment observation visit. Potential outcomes/AEs are elicited at all contacts.

Visit 7 may be by telephone or clinic and occurs early, 30±7 days after stopping, after premature discontinuation. Other visits continue after premature treatment stop unless participation is refused. Investigators may arrange additional visits.

## 3.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

### Inclusion Criteria

- Confirmed symptomatic DVT and/or PE.
- Received 6–12 months of anticoagulation without interruption >1 week.
- Written consent.

### Exclusion Criteria

1. Country-specific legal lower age limit.
2. Indication for therapeutic-dose anticoagulants.
3. Hypersensitivity to investigational or comparator therapy.
4. Any contraindication in local investigational/comparator labeling.
5. Indication for antiplatelet therapy or conventional NSAID.
6. Hepatic disease with coagulopathy and clinically relevant bleeding risk.
7. Calculated CrCl <30 mL/min.
8. Active bleeding or anticoagulant-contraindicating high bleeding risk.
9. Life expectancy <6 months.
10. Systemic strong dual CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors: HIV protease inhibitors, ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, or posaconazole.
11. Childbearing potential without proper contraception, pregnancy, or breastfeeding.
12. Investigational drug/device study participation within 30 days before randomization.
