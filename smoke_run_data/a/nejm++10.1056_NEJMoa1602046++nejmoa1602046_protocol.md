# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

IPM 027 is a pivotal Phase III evaluation of whether a 25-mg dapivirine silicone-elastomer vaginal matrix ring (Ring-004), replaced every 4 weeks, safely prevents HIV infection in healthy HIV-negative women compared with an indistinguishable placebo ring. The program addresses the need for woman-controlled HIV-prevention options and evaluates a monthly product intended for sustained use independent of sexual activity.

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- To determine the efficacy of dapivirine ring use every 4 weeks for approximately 24 months (104 weeks) in preventing HIV-1 infection among healthy, sexually active HIV-negative women.
- To assess the safety of dapivirine ring use on that schedule.

**Secondary Objectives**

- Compare HIV-2 incidence; curable STI incidence and vaginal-flora changes; and determine pregnancy incidence.
- Assess reported 4-week ring-use adherence and acceptability.
- Assess frequency and type of HIV-1 resistance in women acquiring HIV-1 while using investigational product (IP).

**Exploratory Objectives**

- Evaluate HSV-2/HIV-1 association; contraception method, pregnancy and HIV-seroconversion relationships; drug concentrations versus HIV-1 seroconversion/viral resistance; and drug concentrations versus self-reported adherence.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Multi-centre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pivotal Phase III safety-and-efficacy trial.
- **Allocation Ratio:** 2:1 dapivirine:ring placebo.
- **Intervention Summary:** Both arms use a ring continuously, with self-insertion/replacement at 4-week intervals for approximately 104 weeks and exit follow-up 6 weeks after ring discontinuation.
  - Dapivirine arm: Ring-004, a silicone-elastomer vaginal matrix ring containing 25 mg dapivirine.
  - Placebo arm: placebo vaginal ring.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** An automated IXRS (Interactive Voice/Web Response System) assigns a unique participant identification number and treatment; a master randomization list links participant identification number to assignment.
- **Stratification Factors:** Research centre.
- **Minimization or Blocking:** A pre-specified block size is used; minimization is not reported in protocol.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** Approximately 1,950 women: 1,300 dapivirine and 650 placebo; 1,959 were enrolled and accrual closed.
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** HIV-1 seroconversion incidence rate in the two treatment arms.
- **Outcome Type:** Time-to-event incidence rate under proportional hazards.
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** Average annual placebo-arm HIV incidence >4%, allowing for reduction from risk-reduction counselling.
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** Microbicide efficacy ≥50% in preventing HIV-1 infection.
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** 50% reduction in HIV-1 seroconversion rate; scenario table also evaluates 40% and 60% efficacy.
- **Variability Assumption:** Proportional hazards between groups.
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Two-sided 0.05 for the calculation.
- **Power:** 81% for 50% efficacy; 61% for 40% and 94% for 60%.
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Two-sided log-rank test.
- **Required Number of Events:** The protocol reports expected HIV-1 seroconversions, not a required-event target: 96 at 50% efficacy, 104 at 40%, and 88 at 60%.
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable; this is a superiority efficacy trial.
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** ≤10% early-discontinuation/loss-to-follow-up assumption; sample size increased from 1,650 to 1,950 to mitigate product nonadherence and preserve power.
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable; individual randomization, with centre stratification rather than cluster randomization.
- **Basis for Assumptions:** The assumed placebo incidence incorporates expected risk-counselling effects; the increase followed high nonadherence at one research centre.
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** Amendment 3 increased planned enrollment from 1,650 to approximately 1,950; it was an amendment, not a prospectively described re-estimation algorithm.
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol.
- **Sample Size Rationale:** With 1,950 participants, the trial also has 95% probability to detect an AE occurring at ≥0.3% among those assigned IP.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

The primary efficacy null hypothesis is no difference in HIV-1 seroconversion rate/population survival curves between dapivirine and placebo; the design tests superiority. Primary safety analysis is descriptive.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** Regular DSMB safety reviews and an efficacy analysis at approximately 50% of expected efficacy endpoints, conducted for the 18 November 2013 DSMB meeting by an independent statistician.
- **Stopping Guidelines:** DSMB could recommend pause/stop for overwhelming efficacy, futility, safety concerns, or unacceptable trial performance; it recommended continuation after the interim analysis.
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** Lan-DeMets implementation of an O’Brien-Fleming grouped sequential stopping boundary; interim/final alpha adjusted for repeated efficacy review.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** Final analyses occur only after database lock; blinded review, including deviation classification, occurs before lock and treatment codes release after lock.
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Screening lasts up to 28 days; IP use is approximately 104 weeks, with exit 6 weeks after last product use. On-IP visits use ±7-day windows. HIV and pregnancy testing, AE/concomitant review, diary/adherence data and ring exchange occur every 4 weeks; pelvic/STI/safety laboratory assessments every 12 weeks; acceptability every 24 weeks from week 24 (also week 4); cervical cytology at screening, week 52 and last product-use visit.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** The final primary efficacy test uses adjusted two-sided alpha 0.0466 (one-sided 0.0233) after the interim analysis; alpha was 0.05 in the original protocol and sample-size calculation.
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** Lan-DeMets/O’Brien-Fleming grouped sequential adjustment applies to the single primary efficacy comparison across interim and final looks. With 40 expected interim events, planned one-sided levels were .0015 interim and .0245 final; actual interim had 52 events and used .0054, yielding final .0233 one-sided.
- **Confidence Intervals:** 95% CIs are used for incidence rates, effect estimates, proportions, and differences where specified.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** Adherence uses self-reported ring use, visit attendance, returned rings, and active-arm plasma/vaginal drug concentrations/residual ring levels. A nonadherence event is a missed scheduled visit, self-report of ring out ≥12 hours, or non-return of a used ring; three events in 12 scheduled visits triggers censoring for product-adherence analysis.
- **Adherence Assessment:** Adherence questionnaires and diary cards are collected at 4-weekly visits; active-arm residual ring, plasma, and vaginal-fluid dapivirine measurements provide objective assessments.
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Missed or late visits are protocol deviations. A protocol violation is a major deviation that may affect data integrity or participant safety; examples include inappropriate enrolment, IP errors, and defined nonadherence.
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** All recorded deviations are listed and summarized overall, by centre and arm. Before lock, blinded clinical, quality, biometrics and statistician review finalizes major deviations and their PP exclusion/censoring consequences.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** All randomized participants, analyzed in assigned dapivirine or placebo arm regardless of adherence.
- **Per Protocol:** A subset of mITT: HIV-1 negative at enrolment and without major protocol deviations; qualifying major deviations can exclude the participant or censor data at the last negative HIV-1 result before the deviation.
- **Other Populations:** Safety population comprises randomized women receiving ≥1 dapivirine or placebo ring. mITT includes ITT participants never determined HIV-seropositive at enrolment and is the primary efficacy population. Population counts/exclusions are summarized overall, by centre and arm.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

Screening records consent, unique identifier, demographics, locator and menses data, medical history and recent concomitants, eligibility, pregnancy/urinalysis, HIV testing, general and pelvic examination, STI/cytology specimens, RPR and hematology/chemistry. Screening 2/enrolment occurs within 28 days; women failing screening may be rescreened once.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

Eligible women are 18–45 years, able to consent and attend procedures, sexually active (≥1 penetrative penile-vaginal act/month in the prior 3 months), HIV-negative at screening/enrolment, using stable contraception unless postmenopausal or surgically sterilized, asymptomatic for genital infection at enrolment, locatable, and not participating in conflicting investigational studies. Exclusions include current/recent intended pregnancy or breastfeeding, specified injection-drug/research/vaccine histories, untreated significant urogenital infection or grade ≥2 pelvic finding, specified genital/medical/allergy conditions, clinically important baseline laboratory abnormalities, disqualifying cytology, and investigator-judged risk or inability to adhere.

### 4.3 Recruitment

Research centres may use formal or informal pre-screening; formal IRB/IEC-approved methods may include clinical testing. Enrolment was anticipated over 6 months per centre, with Sponsor/Principal Investigator monitoring, potential target reallocation, and possible extension to meet accrual targets.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Permanent trial discontinuation can follow consent withdrawal, investigator-judged serious noncompliance, three consecutive missed visits/loss to follow-up, confirmed pregnancy, HIV positivity, safety concerns, or investigator/Sponsor/IRB/IEC/government decision; participants are not replaced.
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** Date and reason are recorded in source documents/CRFs. Early discontinuers are encouraged to complete last-product-use procedures and may have optional exit follow-up 6 weeks later.
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Reasons include refusal to retain ring without AE/discomfort, pregnancy, HIV, loss of contact after reasonable attempts, clinically relevant laboratory/genital safety findings, and other safety/authority decisions.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Baseline is the last assessment before first ring insertion, from Screening 1, Screening 2, or enrolment. Summaries include demographics (age, race, height, weight, BMI, education), relationship/sexual history, medical/surgical and obstetric/gynaecological history, physical examination, cytology, syphilis, hematology/biochemistry, pregnancy, HSV-2 and HIV tests.
- **Summary Methods:** Continuous variables use n, mean, SD, quartiles and range; categorical variables use frequency and relative frequency, overall, by research centre and treatment arm.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**Outcome: HIV-1 seroconversion incidence rate**

- **Definition:** Confirmed HIV-1 seroconversion rate per 100 person-years of product-use follow-up. A trial endpoint is only a post-enrolment HIV-1 seroconversion confirmed by the independent IPM 027 HIV Seroconversion Monitoring Committee (HSMC). Exit-visit positives are reverse-sequentially HIV-RNA-PCR dated; infection at/before last product-use visit is included, later infection is censored at the last HIV-1 test at/before last product-use visit.
- **Measurement Units:** Number of confirmed seroconversions and incidence density rate per 100 person-years, with 95% CI.
- **Timing of Measurement:** HIV testing occurs at screening, every 4-week on-IP visit, last product-use visit and exit 6 weeks later.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Time-to-seroconversion is first positive trial-visit date minus enrolment; censor at last qualifying negative test.

**Outcome: All adverse events (primary safety endpoint)**

- **Definition:** Full descriptive evaluation of all AEs, regardless of grade or relatedness; treatment-emergent AE starts or worsens after first IP application.
- **Measurement Units:** Participant frequencies/proportions by MedDRA SOC/PT, severity, seriousness, relatedness and action.
- **Timing of Measurement:** AEs are elicited/recorded through trial and exit follow-up; unresolved related events may be followed beyond last visit.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** TEAE, serious TEAE, grade 3/4 TEAE, product-related TEAE and IP-discontinuation indicators are derived for summaries.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**Outcome: HIV-2 seroconversion**

- **Definition:** HIV-2 seroconversion rate during IP use; exit positives are PCR-dated and included only if infection is at/before last product use.
- **Measurement Units:** Events and rate per 100 person-years with 95% CI.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Every 4 weeks through last product use and exit at 6 weeks.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Censor at last HIV-2 test at/before last product use when no qualifying event.

**Outcome: Curable STIs and vaginal-flora change**

- **Definition:** Curable STI is N. gonorrhoeae, C. trachomatis, or T. vaginalis; vaginal-flora change is post-enrolment Nugent-category increase from normal to altered/abnormal or altered to abnormal.
- **Measurement Units:** STI incidence density rate and 95% CI; total Nugent score/category.
- **Timing of Measurement:** STI and flora specimens at enrolment and 12-weekly visits through last product use.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Time to first flora change; abnormal-enrolment participants are censored at day 1.

**Outcome: Pregnancy**

- **Definition:** Confirmed positive urine or serum pregnancy test during trial.
- **Measurement Units:** Pregnancy incidence density rate with 95% CI.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Urine testing at every trial visit; serum confirmation at investigator discretion.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Follow-up is enrolment to pregnancy or completion, whichever first.

**Outcome: Self-reported ring adherence**

- **Definition:** Proportion reporting use of a 4-weekly ring; self-reported days worn divided by days expected.
- **Measurement Units:** Percentage adherence, categorized <80%, ≥80–<90%, and ≥90%; participant proportion with 95% CI.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Questionnaires and diary-card aid at each 4-weekly visit from week 4 through last product-use visit.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Days out are accumulated across scheduled/preceding unscheduled visits.

**Outcome: Ring acceptability**

- **Definition:** Proportion willing to keep a similar HIV-protective ring inserted every day; positive response is the questionnaire’s affirmative top-two response categories at the last available assessment.
- **Measurement Units:** Participant proportions and dapivirine-minus-placebo difference, each with 95% CI.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Week 4 and then every 24 weeks through last product-use visit.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Last available positive willingness response classifies acceptability.

**Outcome: HIV-1 drug-resistance mutations**

- **Definition:** Resistance-associated mutations among HIV-1 seroconverters; all seroconverters undergo viral genotyping.
- **Measurement Units:** Number/type of mutations and proportion with ≥1 mutation, overall/by arm, with 95% CI.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Genotyping after confirmed HIV seroconversion, with stored samples used as specified by the HIV algorithm.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Individual mutations are tabulated by PI, NRTI and NNRTI class; days on IP and adherence may be evaluated as predictors.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** Primary HIV-1 analysis uses mITT and PP populations, stratified two-sided log-rank testing by centre at adjusted alpha .0466, Kaplan-Meier curves, Cox model hazard ratio and incidence rate/95% CI. Complementary analyses exclude Research Centre 03, censor defined nonadherence, and model adherence time-varyingly; lost-to-follow-up sensitivity imputes placebo-arm hazard rate. HIV-2 analysis, conditional on sufficient events, uses centre-stratified two-sided .05 log-rank testing, hazard ratio/95% CI and Kaplan-Meier curves. Curable-STI rates are reported with 95% CIs; total Nugent score uses GEE for longitudinal within-participant correlation and tests a time-by-treatment interaction, with p<.05 indicating differing longitudinal flora change. Time to first flora change uses censoring at last flora assessment (day 1 if abnormal at enrolment), Kaplan-Meier, centre-stratified .05 log-rank and Cox analyses. Pregnancy rate uses enrolment-to-pregnancy/completion person-days, and a listing presents pregnancy, contraception and HIV seroconversion. Adherence, acceptability and resistance analyses are primarily descriptive as specified in their outcome blocks.
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Models generally include treatment and centre main effects with treatment-by-centre interaction. Relevant imbalances/covariates may be included; planned primary-efficacy subgroup covariates include age, marital status, partners, partner knowledge, genital symptoms/STI, contraception and vaginal-product use.
- **Assumption Checks:** Kaplan-Meier curves, Schoenfeld residuals, log(-log survival) versus log time plots and LOWESS assess proportional hazards.
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** Severe proportional-hazards violations prompt exploration of more appropriate models, such as time-by-treatment interaction in semiparametric Cox regression.
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Primary-efficacy complementary analyses and placebo-hazard lost-to-follow-up imputation are robustness analyses.
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Primary efficacy is repeated for: age <25 versus ≥25 years; age ≤21 versus >21 years; married versus not married; 0–1 versus ≥2 baseline male partners; baseline partner knowledge of ring use (yes/no); baseline genital symptoms/STI (yes/no); long-acting progestin during IP use (yes/no); DMPA versus norethisterone among long-acting progestin users; and self-reported use of vaginal products at ≥80% of scheduled visits (yes/no). Each analysis is centre-stratified and includes treatment, subgroup and treatment-by-subgroup interaction, with subgroup Kaplan-Meier curves. A multivariate stepwise sensitivity analysis considers subgroup variables 2–6 that are significant in individual analyses at 5%, retaining only variables significant at 5% in multivariable modeling. The ≤21 versus >21 subgroup is also used for primary safety, STI and pregnancy analyses if appropriate.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** Missed-visit missingness is expected; protocol anticipates it will be balanced across arms. For adherence, missing-data patterns may be informative. Censoring for survival analysis assumes censoring unrelated to HIV acquisition and equal survival probabilities for early/late recruits.
- **Handling Methods:** No prospective general imputation is planned. For time-varying product adherence only, missing scheduled ring residual and/or plasma dapivirine results use LOCF. Partial/missing AE or concomitant-medication start dates are imputed under predefined conventions; no other date imputation is performed. If early discontinuation exceeds 10% (excluding Centre 03), discontinuation covariates are explored.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

Exploratory analyses assess HSV-2, contraception/pregnancy/HIV relations, plasma/vaginal/ring dapivirine concentrations versus outcomes and self-report, and qualitative interview/focus-group data. If placebo participants are reassigned to active ring after an early analysis, post-reassignment data through completion/termination are listed and summarized descriptively.

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** An AE is any untoward medical occurrence, including unfavorable unintended sign, symptom, disease or clinically significant laboratory abnormality, temporally associated with participation regardless of relatedness. SAE criteria include death, life-threatening event, inpatient/prolonged hospitalization, significant disability, congenital anomaly, or important medical event.
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Safety analyses are descriptive in the safety population, by MedDRA SOC/PT and arm. They separately summarize Grade 2 investigator-related AEs, Grade 3/4 AEs, SAEs and AEs causing IP discontinuation, as well as TEAEs, product-related TEAEs and deaths. Frequent AEs (≥2% overall) have centre-adjusted robust-linear-regression incidence differences/95% CIs and an AE dot plot. Urogenital AEs and STIs receive separate Appendix-6 grouped-PT tabulations.
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Non-genital AEs use current DAIDS adult/pediatric grading and genital complaints use Female Genital Grading Table. Investigator classifies related/not related; a missing IP-relationship CRF field is assumed related for final-SAP analysis. Severity is mild, moderate, severe, potentially life-threatening, and Grade 5 death in analysis. AEs are monitored to resolution/cause or lack of anticipated change.
