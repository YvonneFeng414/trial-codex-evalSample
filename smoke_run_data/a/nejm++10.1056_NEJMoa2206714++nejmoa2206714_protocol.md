# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

J2T-DM-KGAB (DRM06-AD04; ADvocate1) is a phase 3 study of lebrikizumab monotherapy versus placebo in adolescents and adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD).

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- Evaluate safety and efficacy of lebrikizumab compared with placebo in moderate-to-severe AD. For FDA, the primary endpoint is Week-16 IGA 0/1 with >=2-point improvement; for EMA the Week-16 IGA endpoint and EASI-75 are co-primary.

**Secondary Objectives**

- Evaluate induction and maintenance efficacy, health outcomes/quality of life, rescue use, and serum lebrikizumab concentration.

**Exploratory Objectives**

- Provide supportive analyses of additional efficacy and patient-reported outcomes, and descriptive acceptability/tolerability results from modified SQAAQ in participating EU adolescents.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** International randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group phase 3 study: screening; 16-week double-blind induction; 36-week maintenance; escape treatment where applicable; and approximately 12-week safety follow-up after last injection for early terminators or those not entering the extension.
- **Allocation Ratio:** Induction 2:1 (LEB250Q2W:placebo); Week-16 eligible responders rerandomized 2:2:1 (LEB250Q2W:LEB250Q4W:placebo).
- **Intervention Summary:** All injections are subcutaneous.
  - Induction LEB250Q2W: 500 mg at baseline and Week 2 (two PFS-NSD loading doses), then 250 mg every 2 weeks through Week 14.
  - Induction placebo: 4 mL at baseline and Week 2, then 2 mL every 2 weeks through Week 14.
  - Maintenance responders: lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W, lebrikizumab 250 mg Q4W, or placebo Q2W; placebo injections maintain blinding.
  - Escape: Week-16 nonresponders, induction rescue users, or rerandomized patients losing EASI-50 at Weeks 24/32/40/48 receive open-label lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W to Week 52; those without EASI-50 after 8 escape weeks discontinue.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** Electronic data-capture (EDC) allocation at baseline after eligibility; EDC rerandomizes maintenance patients according to Week-16 IGA/EASI response and induction rescue use. Protocol Amendment 1 replaced the earlier IWRS wording with EDC.
- **Stratification Factors:** Geographic region (US, EU, rest of world), age (12 to <18 versus >=18 years), and baseline disease severity (IGA 3 versus 4).
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Not reported in protocol.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** Approximately 400 total, randomized 2:1 lebrikizumab:placebo.
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Week-16 IGA 0/1; EMA additionally EASI-75.
- **Outcome Type:** Binary response proportions.
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** IGA 0/1 7.7%; EASI-75 11.5%.
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** IGA 0/1 34.7%; EASI-75 48.0%.
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** Difference calculated from the stated Phase 2b assumptions: 27.0 percentage points for IGA 0/1 and 36.5 points for EASI-75.
- **Variability Assumption:** Binomial response proportions as stated above; no separate variance parameter reported.
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Two-sided 0.05.
- **Power:** >95% for each endpoint with 96 lebrikizumab and 48 placebo patients; EMA overall power at least 90% for both co-primary endpoints.
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Two-group continuity-corrected chi-square test.
- **Required Number of Events:** Not applicable: calculation is based on fixed-time binary response proportions, not event accrual.
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable: superiority comparison.
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** Not reported in protocol; total enrollment was enlarged for safety information and sufficient maintenance responders.
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable: individually randomized, non-clustered design.
- **Basis for Assumptions:** DRM06-AD01/J2T-DM-KGAF Phase 2b rescue-medication nonresponse sensitivity results.
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** Not reported in protocol.
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol.
- **Sample Size Rationale:** The 96:48 calculation is increased to approximately 400 to collect safety information and ensure sufficient Week-16 responders for maintenance.

For FDA, the calculation uses IGA 0/1 (34.7% versus 7.7%); 96 LEB250Q2W and 48 placebo patients give >95% power using a two-group continuity-corrected chi-square test at two-sided .05. For EMA, the same allocation gives >95% power at two-sided .05 for each co-primary endpoint (IGA 0/1 and EASI-75, whose assumed rates are 48.0% versus 11.5%), yielding overall power >=90% for both co-primary endpoints.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

The induction superiority null is equality of LEB250Q2W and placebo for Week-16 IGA 0/1 with >=2-point improvement; EMA additionally tests equality for Week-16 EASI-75. FDA’s graphical family contains IGA01 W16 followed by EASI-75 W16, EASI-90 W16, Pruritus NRS-4 at Weeks 16/4/2, IGA01 W4, adult IGA01 W16, and sleep-loss response W16. EMA’s induction graphical family starts with co-primary IGA01 W16 and EASI-75 W16 and includes EASI-90 W16/W4, EASI percent change W16, pruritus percent change and response Weeks 16/4/2, DLQI response/change W16, and sleep-loss response/change W16.

For EMA maintenance, Q2W and Q4W each follow the prespecified ordered sequence: persistence of EASI-75, persistence of IGA response, persistence of >=4-point pruritus response, then percent EASI change at Week 52. Maintenance inferential comparisons are LEB-responder Q2W versus placebo and LEB-responder Q4W versus placebo.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** Unblinded Week-16 DBL when the last patient completes Week 16/ETV, with induction assignment unblinded and maintenance remaining blinded; Week-52 unblinded DBL is the final efficacy analysis to Week 52. An independent DSMB reviews cumulative safety regularly and ad hoc.
- **Stopping Guidelines:** The trial will not stop early for efficacy after interim analyses; DSMB may recommend continuation unchanged, continuation with modification, or termination for safety.
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** No alpha adjustment for DSMB interim safety assessments.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** Final DBL follows safety follow-up, early discontinuation, or extension enrollment; it may be combined with Week-52 DBL depending on regulatory timeline.
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Induction visits baseline and Weeks 2/4/6/8/10/12/14/16 use +/-3-day windows; maintenance Weeks 20/24/28/32/36/40/44/48/52 use +/-5-day windows. IGA/EASI/BSA are scheduled through Week 16; IGA/EASI/BSA/SCORAD and intermittent PRO/safety assessments continue in maintenance, with Week-52 end-of-study/ETV assessments and a 12-week safety follow-up when required. Pruritus NRS and sleep-loss are daily diaries mapped to baseline and Weeks 1, 2, 4, then scheduled visits through 52; baseline needs >=4 of 7 daily reports, while postbaseline weekly values are prorated if >=1 report. Out-of-window assessments are retained unless stated otherwise.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** Except where noted, tests are two-sided at 0.05.
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** FDA uses a prespecified graphical closed-testing approach controlling two-sided family-wise type-I error .05 across primary and major secondary induction endpoints. EMA separately applies graphical .05 family-wise control to induction and maintenance families; Week-52 maintenance major secondary endpoints follow the prespecified Q2W/Q4W hierarchy.
- **Confidence Intervals:** Crude binary proportions use two-sided asymptotic, non-continuity-corrected 95% CIs. Stratified risk differences use the Mantel-Haenszel-Sato method; CMH adjusted odds ratios and 95% CIs are reported. Continuous ANCOVA/MMRM analyses report LS-mean difference, standard error, p-value, and 95% CI.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** Compliant means receiving >=75% of expected injections during the relevant treatment period.
- **Adherence Assessment:** Expected injections derive from dispensing data; administered injections derive from the administration eCRF and dosing diary; compliance percentage is 100 times administered/expected.
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Important deviations are those that can materially affect data completeness, accuracy, reliability, or patient rights, safety, or well-being; examples include eligibility violations, interfering concomitant medication, and <75% compliance.
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Summarize patients with important deviations by category/subcategory and treatment for ITT induction and all maintenance populations; provide ITT patient listings. COVID missed visits/windows are deviations.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** All randomized patients, regardless of receipt, correct treatment, or protocol adherence; analyzed as randomized and used for induction efficacy/health outcomes unless stated otherwise.
- **Per Protocol:** Per-protocol set analyses are not planned in final SAP Version 4. The final protocol nevertheless defines PP as ITT patients completing Week 16 without important violations, excluding specified eligibility/prohibited-treatment/visit/compliance failures while retaining stated AE- or lack-of-effect discontinuations.
- **Other Populations:** Induction Safety includes randomized patients receiving >=1 induction dose. MPP is baseline LEB250Q2W patients rerandomized at Week 16 and dosed in maintenance, analyzed as rerandomized and censored before escape. MSP comprises analogous baseline-placebo rerandomized patients. W16 and W24-48 Escape populations support descriptive efficacy; All Lebrikizumab Safety includes randomized patients receiving >=1 lebrikizumab dose in combined periods.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

Screening lasts no more than 30 days and begins electronic diary collection. It includes consent/assent, eligibility review, medical history/review of systems, demographics, vital signs, physical examination including height/weight, concomitant therapy, IGA/BSA/EASI, blood laboratory/pregnancy testing, urinalysis, and diary training for daily pruritus/sleep loss and weekly POEM. The final protocol also specifies adolescent immunization-record review and screening clinical laboratory/infection procedures, including TB assessment where applicable.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

No prospective enrollment waiver is permitted. Inclusion criteria are: adult or adolescent 12 to <18 years weighing >=40 kg; AD by American Academy of Dermatology criteria for >=1 year; baseline EASI >=16, IGA >=3/4, and AD BSA >=10%; inadequate topical response or medical inadvisability; stable nonmedicated moisturizer twice daily for >=7 days; >=4 of 7 baseline pruritus/sleep diary entries; ability to comply; specified WOCBP highly effective contraception/abstinence for treatment plus 18 weeks, male barrier contraception for 18 weeks, and consent/assent.

Exclusions preserve the protocol numbering: prior lebrikizumab; anaphylaxis; recent topical AD treatment, dupilumab/tralokinumab, systemic immunomodulator/phototherapy, investigational product/biologic, prescription moisturizer, tanning, or live vaccine; uncontrolled chronic disease/severe asthma; recent/active infection, hepatitis/cirrhosis, parasitic infection risk, immunosuppression or HIV; significant screening laboratory findings; assessment-interfering skin disease; malignancy within 5 years except fully treated in-situ cervical or resolved nonmetastatic basal/squamous skin cancer; severe illness/psychological condition; and pregnancy, breastfeeding, or pregnancy planning.

### 4.3 Recruitment

Approximately 400 patients, including approximately 50 adolescents, are planned at approximately 100 sites in the US, EU, and Asia/Pacific region.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Study, induction, and maintenance completion/discontinuation and primary discontinuation reason are summarized; maintenance escape entry is also summarized.
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** All ITT patients discontinuing study treatment are listed with reason and discontinuation timing; early terminators and non-extension patients have a follow-up visit approximately 12 weeks after last injection.
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Early termination may be consent withdrawal; AE/laboratory abnormality/intercurrent illness; serious violation, persistent noncompliance, or prohibited therapy; or lost to follow-up. For lost-to-follow-up patients, two contact attempts then certified letter are documented. Treatment is discontinued for material intercurrent illness, persistent clinically significant treatment-related AE, unacceptable toxicity, pregnancy, or systemic rescue; pregnancy is followed for >=4 weeks after delivery, and SAEs are followed to resolution/clinically acceptable level. Escape patients without EASI-50 after 8 weeks discontinue.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Demographics include age/age groups, sex, race, US ethnicity, region/country, weight/height/BMI and categories. Disease history includes onset/duration, affected areas, prior AD treatments/systemic treatment, IGA, EASI, SCORAD, BSA, pruritus/sleep loss, POEM, DLQI/CDLQI, EQ-5D, PROMIS, and ACQ-5 for comorbid asthma.
- **Summary Methods:** Continuous variables use descriptive statistics; categorical variables use counts and percentages, without formal treatment comparisons unless specified. Baseline is last available pre-injection efficacy/health outcome value; diary baseline is the 7-day pre-injection mean with >=4 reports.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**Outcome: Week-16 IGA 0/1 with >=2-point improvement**
- **Definition:** Proportion with IGA 0 or 1 and >=2-point reduction from baseline at Week 16.
- **Measurement Units:** Binary responder proportion; IGA is the trained/certified assessor’s five-point 0-4 static global AD assessment, performed before EASI and BSA: 0 clear, 1 almost clear, 2 mild, 3 moderate, and 4 severe.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Week 16; IGA response is also evaluated at scheduled induction visits and, for maintenance responders, through Week 52.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** The endpoint is missing if baseline or observed IGA is missing. Primary hybrid estimation uses within-arm MCMC-MI; post-rescue or lack-of-efficacy discontinuation values are set to baseline through Week 16, and only imputed IGA values use standard rounding to derive response.

**Outcome: Week-16 EASI-75 (EMA co-primary)**
- **Definition:** >=75% reduction from baseline EASI at Week 16.
- **Measurement Units:** Binary response derived from EASI, a 0-72 score. Four signs (erythema, edema/papulation, excoriation, lichenification) are each 0 none to 3 severe, with half scores allowed between 1/2/3; percent involvement is 0=0%, 1=1-9%, 2=10-29%, 3=30-49%, 4=50-69%, 5=70-89%, 6=90-100%. Region scores for head/neck, trunk, upper limbs and lower limbs are weighted 0.1/0.3/0.2/0.4. If regional involvement is zero, its signs can be missing; otherwise a missing component makes EASI missing.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Week 16.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** MCMC-MI; imputed Week-16 EASI is compared directly with observed baseline without rounding.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**Outcome: Induction and maintenance efficacy responses**
- **Definition:** Induction major/other outcomes retain their protocol labels: EASI-50/75/90, EASI percent change, IGA [0], IGA response, pruritus NRS change and >=4-point response, sleep-loss change and >=2-point response, BSA change, SCORAD change and SCORAD75/90, and time to first rescue/flare. Maintenance includes persistence of Week-16 EASI-75, IGA response, and pruritus response; percent EASI/SCORAD change; and time to loss of EASI-50, EASI-75, or IGA response. Loss analyses use Kaplan-Meier/log-rank and the table-specified eligible Week-16 responder subsets.
- **Measurement Units:** Binary proportions, continuous scores/percent change, and time-to-event.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Induction scheduled visits through Week 16; maintenance scheduled visits through Week 52; time-to-loss analyses during maintenance.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Change is observed minus baseline; percent change uses baseline. Daily pruritus/sleep-loss weekly means are mapped to visits; maintenance response is relative to original baseline and requires Week-16 response where stated. Maintenance secondary and escape populations have descriptive analyses only, with no between-group/overall comparisons.

**Outcome: Health outcomes and other secondary assessments**
- **Definition:** POEM (seven-item, 0-28; one unanswered item scores 0 and >1 means unscored); DLQI/CDLQI (10 items, 0-30, with >=4-point improvement and specified domain rules); EQ-5D-5L (five dimensions, VAS and UK/US index); PROMIS anxiety/depression T-scores; ACQ-5 mean among self-reported asthma patients; modified SQAAQ ten-item self/caregiver acceptance/tolerability after injection; serum lebrikizumab concentration; and rescue-medication outcomes.
- **Measurement Units:** DLQI/CDLQI 0-30; EQ-5D VAS 0-100 and US/UK index scores; PROMIS T-scores; ACQ-5 mean 0-6; POEM 0-28; SQAAQ 7-point Likert responses. Lower ACQ-5 is better; higher DLQI/CDLQI and PROMIS represent greater impairment/anxiety/depression.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Scheduled induction and maintenance visits; POEM weekly eDiary uses the last assessment in the defined 7-day window, and SQAAQ follows each self/caregiver injection.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Questionnaire totals/domain scores and change from baseline follow Table 6.11; DLQI/CDLQI >=4-point improvement and ACQ-5 <=-0.5 are derived responses. DLQI/CDLQI score one unanswered question as zero but treat >=2 unanswered as missing; EQ-5D partial assessments cannot be saved; PROMIS can derive totals from partial item response theory data; SQAAQ missing values remain missing and results are by injection sequence.

**Outcome: Detailed maintenance, rescue, and patient-reported analyses**
- **Definition:** Time to IGA loss is first IGA >=2 with >=2-point deterioration versus Week-16 response; time to EASI-50 loss is first percent change >-50 and time to EASI-75 loss is first loss of >=75% response. Patients without a loss are censored at last maintenance-blinded visit or systemic-rescue date, as applicable. Flare is initiation or intensification of rescue therapy; time to first rescue may have Kaplan-Meier curves, and percentage rescued by visit is summarized in ITT, MPP, and W16 escape populations for their respective periods. SCORAD equals A/5+7B/2+C (extent 0-100, six-sign intensity 0-18, symptoms 0-20); SCORAD75/90 require >=75%/>=90% improvement. BSA is percent affected surface by palm=1% rule.
- **Measurement Units:** POEM has seven weekly symptom items scored 0-4 (0-28); DLQI/CDLQI each have six domains: symptoms/feelings, daily activities, leisure, work/school, personal relationships, treatment; EQ-5D-5L analyzes mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, anxiety/depression as 1-5 categories and at Week 52 in MPP, plus VAS and country index scores. PROMIS anxiety/depression are eight-item raw-to-T-score measures; ACQ-5 is the five-item mean, assessed only in self-reported comorbid asthma; SQAAQ reports strongly agree/agree for each of 10 items by each self/caregiver injection sequence.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Induction primary/key-secondary analyses are ITT at their stated Weeks 1/2/4/16; maintenance key secondary analyses are MPP at Week 52 in the responders specified for each endpoint. MPP compares each LEB Q2W and Q4W regimen with placebo; MSP and W16/W24-48 escape analyses are descriptive at scheduled visits/every four weeks after retreatment and have no between-group comparisons.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** POEM missing/response rules, DLQI/CDLQI domain and unanswered-item rules, PROMIS partial-item derivation, ACQ-5 missing rule, and SQAAQ missing handling are as stated in Table 6.11. For every endpoint, Tables 6.11-6.12 assign a primary hybrid, supportive hybrid/composite/hypothetical, or descriptive role, population, comparison, time point, and CMH/ANCOVA/MMRM/KM method.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** Primary hybrid estimands use ITT induction and MPP maintenance analyses: IGA/EASI/pruritus categorical responses use CMH with MCMC-MI, and EASI/pruritus percent change uses ANCOVA with MCMC-MI. FDA primary is IGA W16; EMA co-primary are IGA and EASI-75 W16; protocol/agency-designated key secondary analyses use the endpoint-timepoint roles in Tables 6.11-6.12. Maintenance MPP compares LEB responder Q2W versus placebo and Q4W versus placebo, with response persistence at W52 and percent EASI change W52 as specified. Supportive hybrid uses MCMC-MI; categorical composite uses CMH/NRI; continuous hypothetical uses observed-data MMRM in induction and ANCOVA/LOCF in maintenance. Time-to-loss uses KM/log-rank; MSP and W16/W24-48 escape analyses are descriptive with no between-group/overall comparisons.
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Induction models adjust for region, age, and baseline IGA severity; ANCOVA includes treatment, baseline, and stratifiers. MMRM adds visit, baseline-by-visit and treatment-by-visit.
- **Assumption Checks:** MMRM uses unstructured within-patient covariance; convergence is assessed and invokes the stated covariance fallback. Treatment-by-region interaction at 10% is a prespecified heterogeneity/subgroup assessment, not a model-assumption check; safety p-values are flagging rather than confirmatory.
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** If unstructured MMRM covariance fails convergence, use heterogeneous Toeplitz then heterogeneous autoregressive covariance; REML and Kenward-Roger are used.
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Tipping-point analyses, conditional on statistically significant primary/key secondary results, examine loss of significance under varying missing response probabilities; supportive composite analyses use NRI and continuous hypothetical analyses use MMRM/LOCF as defined.
- **Subgroup Analyses:** For Week-16 IGA 0/1, EASI-75/90, and pruritus response, MCMC-MI logistic models include treatment, subgroup, and treatment-by-subgroup interaction with Firth correction at 10%; subgroups include age, sex, race/ethnicity, region, weight/BMI, AD duration, baseline IGA/pruritus, and prior systemic therapy. <10% subgroup categories are descriptive only.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** Primary hybrid MCMC-MI analyses use missing-at-random after specified baseline-setting for rescue/lack-of-efficacy discontinuation. MMRM assumes missingness relates to observed data and borrows within-arm information through repeated-measure correlation. Composite estimands define post-intercurrent-event and intermittent categorical values as nonresponse; hypothetical estimands use the corresponding MMRM/LOCF rules.
- **Handling Methods:** Induction primary hybrid analyses use within-arm MCMC-MI (25 datasets, relevant baseline/postbaseline model, SAS PROC MI MCMC and PROC MIANALYZE; prespecified seeds in Table 6.7). Rescue, systemic rescue, or lack-of-efficacy discontinuation values are set to baseline through Week 16 before remaining-value imputation. Categorical composite NRI assigns rescue/discontinuation/intermittent missingness and randomized patients with no postbaseline observation to nonresponse. LOCF makes post-rescue/discontinuation values missing and uses baseline if no postbaseline value; repeated continuous endpoints use no-explicit-imputation MMRM. Maintenance hybrid MCMC-MI uses Table 6.10 seeds; maintenance NRI follows rescue, discontinuation, or escape; maintenance hypothetical continuous analyses use LOCF. COVID missingness uses these same rules.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

PK/PD inferential methods are deferred to a separate PK/PD analysis plan. Protocol-specified PK summaries by visit include arithmetic/geometric mean, minimum, maximum, SD and %CV serum concentration; all patients provide PK/ADA samples, positive ADA may undergo neutralizing-antibody testing, and ADA concentration profiles/possible efficacy-safety effects are evaluated. Additional analyses include disposition, injection exposure/missed injections and >=75% compliance, prior/concomitant therapy coded using WHO Drug Dictionary/ATC, rescue and flare analyses, immunogenicity listings/summaries, clinical-trial-registry outputs, and annual DSUR reporting.

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** An AE is any untoward medical occurrence; a TEAE first occurs or worsens after baseline. Baseline LLT maximum severity is used, missing baseline severity is mild, missing postbaseline severity is severe, and events on first-dose day are posttreatment. SAEs are events causing death, life-threatening risk, inpatient/prolonged hospitalization, persistent/significant disability, congenital anomaly, or another medically important event. SAEs are recorded from consent to study end and reported regardless of causality within 24 hours; SAEs are followed until resolution or clinically acceptable stabilization.
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Safety Population (induction), MPP (maintenance), and All Lebrikizumab Safety (combined periods/follow-up) summarize exposure, TEAEs/SAEs/discontinuations, laboratory shifts and ANCOVA, vital-sign shifts, adolescent growth, immunogenicity, and AESIs. Fisher exact tests and odds ratios are used for induction and MPP comparisons. AEs use MedDRA and medications WHO Drug Dictionary/ATC coding; investigator and sponsor causality assessments are collected.
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** AEs use MedDRA, severity and relatedness; potentially related TEAEs are summarized. AESIs include hepatic safety, eosinophilia/eosinophil-related disorders, infections including opportunistic/herpes/parasitic/skin infections, conjunctivitis/keratitis, immediate/nonimmediate hypersensitivity, injection-site reactions, malignancy, AD exacerbation, and suicide/self-injury SMQ; protocol AESI reporting is within 48 hours. Treatment-emergent ADA is defined by >=2-fold increase from minimum required dilution if baseline negative or >=4-fold increase if baseline positive.
