# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale
Oral factor Xa (FXa) inhibitors are widely used, but acute intracranial hemorrhage (ICrH) while anticoagulated has high mortality and disability, and preventing hematoma expansion is a key treatment goal. Andexanet alfa is a recombinant, catalytically inactive FXa analogue intended to reverse FXa inhibition; ANNEXA-I compares andexanet with usual care in this setting.

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- Compare andexanet with usual care for effective hemostasis at 12 hours post-randomization.

**Secondary Objectives**

- Compare percent change in anti-fXa activity from baseline to nadir during the first 2 hours post-randomization.

**Exploratory Objectives**

- Additional efficacy objectives are thrombin generation, neurologic deterioration, mRS, NIHSS/GCS, imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa/hemostasis correlation, EQ-5D-5L, and utility-weighted mRS. Safety objectives cover thrombotic events (TEs), mortality, invasive procedures, hospital use, adverse events (AEs), vital signs, and immunogenicity.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Phase 4, prospective, multicenter, randomized, open-label clinical trial; the EAC and imaging core laboratory are blinded.
- **Allocation Ratio:** 1:1 andexanet:usual care.
- **Intervention Summary:** Usual care is investigator-selected treatment, including no treatment, other than andexanet initiated within 3 hours. Andexanet has no crossover and comprises:
  - Low dose: 400-mg IV bolus over about 15 minutes, then 480 mg at 4 mg/min for 120 minutes.
  - High dose: 800-mg IV bolus over up to about 30 minutes, then 960 mg at 8 mg/min for 120 minutes.

  Regimen selection uses the preceding FXa inhibitor, its last dose and its timing; patients qualifying through local anti-fXa >100 ng/mL with last dose >15 hours/unknown receive high dose. Andexanet should start no later than 30 minutes after randomization and preferably within 2 hours of baseline brain imaging.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** Sponsor-generated randomization scheme after consent, eligibility/baseline assessments, and documentation of intended usual care.
- **Stratification Factors:** Intended usual-care agent (plasma-derived coagulation-factor concentrate versus other) and symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time (<180 versus ≥180 minutes).
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Not reported in protocol.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** Approximately 900 participants, 450 per group; preplanned interim SSR could increase total enrollment to 1200.
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Rate of effective hemostasis.
- **Outcome Type:** Binary proportion.
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** 70% effective hemostasis.
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** 80% effective hemostasis.
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** 10 percentage points, representing a 33% reduction in risk of not achieving effective hemostasis.
- **Variability Assumption:** Not applicable; the calculation is for a binary proportion and reports arm rates.
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Overall two-sided 0.05, accounting for the interim analysis.
- **Power:** Approximately 90%.
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** CMH comparison of effective-hemostasis proportions, stratified by symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time.
- **Required Number of Events:** Not applicable; the endpoint is a binary proportion rather than time-to-event.
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable; the stated hypothesis is a superiority comparison.
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** The calculation accounts for early discontinuation; no separate numerical inflation is reported.
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable; this is individually randomized, not cluster-randomized.
- **Basis for Assumptions:** Andexanet rate derives from ANNEXA-4 efficacy results; 10% was considered clinically meaningful.
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** At interim, conditional power 30%–90% permits DSMB-recommended increase up to 1200; no increase if CP <30% or >90%.
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol.
- **Sample Size Rationale:** The design targets a clinically meaningful difference while accounting for early discontinuation and interim type-I error.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework
The primary hypothesis is H0: πUC−πandexanet=0 versus HA: πUC−πandexanet≠0. The primary objective succeeds when the andexanet effective-hemostasis proportion is higher under the prespecified alpha-spending thresholds; the secondary endpoint is tested only after a significant primary endpoint.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** One formal primary-efficacy interim after approximately 50% (450) of anticipated participants have adjudicated hemostasis; an independent DSMB statistician performs the analysis and DSMB assesses efficacy and safety. Enrollment is not paused.
- **Stopping Guidelines:** DSMB may recommend stopping/altering for efficacy or safety; interim primary p<0.0310 supports an efficacy stop.
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** Lan–DeMets alpha spending based on Pocock boundaries controls overall type-I error at 5%; final primary threshold is p<0.0277.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** Final analyses occur after study completion and final database lock. If stopped after interim, DCO1 supports confirmatory efficacy and DCO2 captures all participants for safety and efficacy sensitivity analyses.
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Anti-fXa is baseline, 1 and 2 hours; thrombin generation is baseline, 1 and 12 hours; TFPI is baseline, 1, 2 and 12 hours and Day30. CT/MRI is baseline and about 12 hours. NIHSS is baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 72 hours, with 12/24-hour assessments blinded; GCS is baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 72 hours. Premorbid mRS is baseline and mRS/EQ-5D-5L are Day30 or early termination. Day 7 and 14 calls and Day30 (+7) follow-up occur, with an additional approximately Day120 visit for positive anti-andexanet antibodies.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** Overall family-wise type-I error is 5%; interim and final levels are .0310 and .0277, respectively.
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** Hierarchical testing controls the family-wise error: test primary, then test secondary only if primary is statistically significant, using the same interim/final alpha level; Lan–DeMets/Pocock spending also controls across looks.
- **Confidence Intervals:** Report 95% CIs for primary proportion difference and, where specified, model estimates including ANCOVA/MMRM/logistic analyses.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** Participant compliance is not evaluated because procedures occur in hospital under trained professionals; andexanet exposure/modifications and usual-care therapies are summarized.
- **Adherence Assessment:** Bolus/infusion duration, dose modifications, and usual-care initial therapy are tabulated.
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Deviations due to noncompliance that render a participant nonevaluable for key endpoints are important; PPS excludes important deviations affecting primary hemostatic efficacy. These include specified eligibility failures; wrong randomized treatment or treatment before randomization; procoagulant use in assigned-andexanet participants without andexanet within 3 hours; andexanet in usual-care participants before 12 hours; bolus/infusion underdose (<360/432 mg low, <720/864 mg high), late bolus/infusion, high-dose assignment receiving low dose; and evaluability failures. NIHSS must be baseline and 12-hour (11–13 h) and imaging baseline and 12-hour (11–15 h), without confounding sedation/intubation or inconsistent modality unless EAC can determine efficacy.
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Deviations are tabulated and listed by treatment group and total, including participant-level data; the disposition tabulation identifies inclusion/exclusion failures and the relevant protocol version. The protocol-deviation review plan supplies further details.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** All randomized participants, analyzed by randomized intervention; randomized participants who never signed consent are excluded. Primary, secondary and additional efficacy analyses use ITT.
- **Per Protocol:** ITT participants without important protocol deviations that affect primary efficacy; detailed inclusion/exclusion rules are specified for PPS.
- **Other Populations:** Screened=all signed consent; enrolled=all signed consent excluding screen failures; safety=all randomized with ≥1 intervention, including randomized usual-care no-treatment participants, analyzed as actually received.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data
Screened participants, screen failures and their reasons are tabulated, with a by-participant screen-failure listing. Signed informed consent from the participant/LAR is required before screening procedures.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary
Eligible participants are adults with acute intracerebral bleeding of specified volume, CT/MRI within 2 hours before randomization, and oral apixaban, rivaroxaban or edoxaban at protocol-specified doses within 15 hours; symptom onset-to-baseline scan must be <6 hours. Randomization outside 15 hours or after unknown last-dose time requires standard-of-care local anti-fXa >100 ng/mL within 2 hours before consent and mandates high-dose andexanet. Consent may be participant, LAR/proxy, or emergency consent where lawful. Key exclusions include planned hematoma-affecting surgery within 12 hours, GCS<7 (with specified non-neurologic sedation exception), NIHSS>35, hematoma volume ≤0.5 or ≥60 mL, inability to use the same CT/MRI modality, expected survival <1 month, recent thrombotic/cardiovascular events, cardiogenic shock, severe sepsis/septic shock, pregnancy/lactation (and required pregnancy testing/contraception precautions), restricted anticoagulants/blood products, prior/planned andexanet, investigational drug, tumor-related bleeding and hypersensitivity.

### 4.3 Recruitment
Approximately 900 participants are planned at approximately 250 sites in North America, Europe and Asia. Participants are usually enrolled in the emergency department but may be enrolled from other hospital departments.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Participants can discontinue study participation or andexanet; study completion is Day30 follow-up, with an additional antibody-related follow-up if applicable.
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** AEs, survival and antibody data are followed through Day30. Participants discontinuing procedures may still have Day30 mortality collected; staff make efforts to obtain Day30 and, where applicable, Day120 follow-up. Early termination includes bleeding assessment, mRS, EQ-5D, survival and antibody sampling when not already completed.
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Disposition tabulations include early discontinuation and reasons by treatment/total.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Demographics; FXa inhibitor/indication; intended usual care; timing; anti-fXa; hematoma volume/location/ICrH score; clinical presentation, injury mechanism, medical/surgical history and medications are summarized.
- **Summary Methods:** Continuous variables: N, mean, median, SD, quartiles, IQR, minimum/maximum. Categorical variables: n/% among nonmissing values, by arm and total; no baseline inferential analyses.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes
**Outcome: Effective hemostasis at 12 hours post-randomization**

- **Definition:** Excellent/good requires all: NIHSS increase <7 from baseline at 12 hours, hematoma-volume increase ≤35% baseline-to-12-hour CT/MRI, and no rescue treatment between >3 and <12 hours. EAC/adjudication algorithm determines status; poor/none is failure.
- **Measurement Units:** EAC categories are excellent/good, poor/none and nonevaluable (administrative or clinical); the primary binary analysis codes excellent/good=1 and poor/none/nonevaluable=0. Volume change is percent and NIHSS change is points. Out-of-window, missing, uninterpretable, sedated/intubated or confounded assessment, and core-lab-requested cases are referred to EAC, whose determination is final.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Baseline and 12 hours post-randomization; 12-hour imaging is targeted 11–15 hours and NIHSS 11–13 hours for PPS assessment.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Relative hematoma-volume change and baseline-to-12-hour NIHSS change; rescue medication/procedure identification is derived from CRFs and timing.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes
**Outcome: Percent change in anti-fXa activity from baseline to nadir in first 2 hours**

- **Definition:** Nadir is the minimum post-randomization anti-fXa at 1 or 2 hours; percent reduction is maximum reduction from baseline divided by baseline ×100.
- **Measurement Units:** Anti-fXa activity in ng/mL and derived percent change.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Baseline, 1 and 2 hours post-randomization.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Values >950 and <4 ng/mL are set to 950 and 4; ranked percent change to nadir is analyzed.

**Outcome: Additional efficacy endpoints**

- **Definition:** ETP and four other thrombin-generation parameters; neurologic deterioration; mRS; NIHSS/GCS; imaging-only hemostasis; rescue; anti-fXa correlation; EQ-5D-5L; utility-weighted mRS.
- **Measurement Units:** ETP laboratory measures; NIHSS/GCS/mRS scores; proportions; EQ-5D-5L index/visual analog scale; utility values.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Thrombin baseline/1/12h; neurologic and imaging endpoints through 72h; mRS/EQ-5D/utility at 30 days; rescue >3 to <12h.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** mRS Day30 dichotomized 0–3 versus 4–6; imaging-only success ≤35%; 24-h neurologic deterioration NIHSS increase ≥4 or GCS decrease ≥2.

**Outcome: Thrombin generation and ETP**

- **Definition:** Central-laboratory tissue-factor-dependent thrombin generation measures ETP (primary measure), peak height, time to peak, lag time and velocity index. ETP change is analyzed by MMRM with baseline ETP, time stratum, treatment, visit and treatment×visit; participant is random and covariance is unstructured with specified fallback.
- **Measurement Units:** Central-laboratory plasma thrombin-generation parameter values and change/percent change from baseline.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Baseline, 1 and 12 hours post-randomization.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Values below measurement capacity are replaced by zero.

**Outcome: Neurologic deterioration, NIHSS and GCS**

- **Definition:** 12-hour deterioration is NIHSS increase ≥7; 24-hour deterioration is NIHSS increase ≥4 or GCS decrease ≥2 from baseline. NIHSS/GCS change is also assessed longitudinally.
- **Measurement Units:** NIHSS and GCS scores, score changes/percent changes, and binary deterioration proportions.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Deterioration at 12/24 hours; NIHSS/GCS baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 72 hours.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** CMH compares deterioration proportions; separate MMRMs compare score changes and report LS differences/95% CIs.

**Outcome: Modified Rankin Scale**

- **Definition:** Premorbid baseline and Day30/early-termination mRS; Day30 functional independence is score 0–3 and dependence/death is 4–6.
- **Measurement Units:** Ordinal 0–6 mRS and dichotomized functional-independence status.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Baseline and Day30 post-randomization or early termination.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Logistic regression adjusts for baseline mRS, time stratum and treatment, reporting treatment OR/95% CI; shifts are summarized.

**Outcome: Imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa correlation and EQ-5D-5L/utility mRS**

- **Definition:** Imaging-only success is hematoma-volume increase ≤35%; rescue is medication/procedure between >3 and <12 hours; correlation relates effective hemostasis to anti-fXa change. EQ-5D-5L includes five dimensions plus index and visual analog scale; utility-weighted mRS averages corresponding EQ-5D utilities by mRS category.
- **Measurement Units:** Binary proportions, anti-fXa change, EQ-5D dimension levels/index/VAS and mRS utility values.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Imaging/rescue through 12 hours; anti-fXa baseline-to-2-hour nadir; EQ-5D-5L and utility mRS Day30.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** CMH analyzes imaging/rescue; logistic model/AUC evaluates correlation; EQ-5D dimensions use CMH and index/VAS ANCOVA; correlation analysis imputes anti-fXa as for the secondary endpoint and treats nonevaluable hemostasis as poor/none.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** ITT primary outcome by CMH stratified on onset-to-baseline imaging (<180/≥180 min), reporting difference, 95% CI and p-value. Secondary ITT ranked-ANCOVA percent anti-fXa change to 2-h nadir adjusts for stratum and baseline anti-fXa. Tertiary methods are MMRM for ETP/NIHSS/GCS, CMH for binary neurologic/imaging/rescue endpoints, logistic regression for mRS and correlation, and CMH/ANCOVA for EQ-5D-5L.
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Secondary ANCOVA adjusts for time stratum and baseline anti-fXa; mRS logistic model adjusts baseline mRS/time stratum/treatment; MMRMs include time stratum, treatment, visit and treatment×visit.
- **Assumption Checks:** MMRM uses unstructured covariance; convergence failure is addressed by AR(1), Toeplitz, then compound symmetry.
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** The specified MMRM covariance fallback sequence is AR(1), Toeplitz, then compound symmetry.
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Primary robustness analyses add intended-PCC stratification; analyze PPS; exclude administrative nonevaluable cases; use worst-case, jump-to-control and tipping-point missing-data analyses; and use five strata separating pre-Amendment-1 patients and post-Amendment-1 intended-PCC/no-PCC × time-stratum patients. If stopped at interim, final-DCO efficacy is a sensitivity analysis. If SSR increases N, final testing uses the Cui–Hung–Wang weighted stage-1/stage-2 statistic (weight .5).
- **Subgroup Analyses:** ITT CMH analyses include age, sex, race, region, prior FXa inhibitor/indication, anti-fXa cutpoints, ICrH score, hematoma volume/location, last-dose timing, andexanet dose and usual care; <5-person subgroups are descriptive only.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** The protocol does not name a general missingness mechanism. Primary nonevaluable status, whether administrative or clinical, is poor/none; EAC decides uncertain/out-of-window/uninterpretable primary information.
- **Handling Methods:** For primary imaging/NIHSS out-of-window, missing or uninterpretable data, EAC determines whether an alternate assessment is adequate and whether the issue is administrative/clinical; nonevaluable is poor/none. Baseline is the most recent nonmissing assessment in the protocol visit window before randomization, or, if absent, the closest pre-treatment assessment; outside-window data remain analyzable but are protocol deviations. Secondary anti-fXa excludes missing baseline values; an out-of-window baseline can replace missing baseline, values >950/<4 ng/mL are set to 950/4, and remaining 1/2-hour data are multiply imputed 100 times using treatment, time, time×treatment and baseline anti-fXa, with median p-value. Tertiary analyses use no imputation except the anti-fXa/effective-hemostasis correlation, which uses that MI method; ETP below capacity is set to zero. For partial concomitant-medication dates, missing start day/month is set to 01/01-Jan and missing end day/month to month-end/last study-contact, survival or death day (year remains unimputed); uncertainty classifies medication as both prior and concomitant. Primary administrative-nonevaluable sensitivity uses worst-case, jump-to-control and tipping point.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses
TFPI values and changes through Day30 are descriptively summarized. An additional anti-fXa analysis excluding out-of-stability samples may assess stability impact. Local laboratory summaries include hematology (hemoglobin, hematocrit, WBC/differential and platelets), INR, routine chemistry (electrolytes, glucose, BUN/creatinine, liver enzymes, albumin, alkaline phosphatase, calcium/phosphorus and bilirubins), pregnancy testing and locally qualifying anti-fXa; central assays include anti-fXa, antibodies/neutralizing antibodies, thrombin generation and TFPI. Values/change, normal-range shifts and protocol-defined clinically significant abnormalities are summarized. Temperature, systolic/diastolic blood pressure, heart rate and respiratory rate are summarized as actual/change values and clinically significant outliers at each visit. Initial-hospital LOS is discharge date/time minus admission date/time +1; same-day admissions/discharges without times equal one day, and missing discharge is imputed using Day30/death as applicable. Initial-hospital/ICU duration and 30-day rehospitalization are summarized, with <6-hour contiguous inpatient/ICU transfers treated as one hospitalization unless the first discharge is to home care, hospice, long-term/intermediate care or another hospital. Invasive intracranial procedures are summarized, and Day30 immunogenicity uses postbaseline-positive/increased-titer response, baseline within 120 minutes before randomization and high/low-dose summaries.

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** An AE is any untoward medical occurrence regardless of attribution; a TEAE occurs after treatment initiation. The AESI is an EAC-adjudicated TE.
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Safety Set analyses summarize TEAEs through Day30, SAEs, relationship, severity, deaths, TEs, laboratory values/shifts, vital signs, hospitalization, procedures and immunogenicity by treatment/total. MedDRA SOC/PT tables count each participant once per SOC/PT and sort SOC alphabetically/PT by total frequency; event-level occurrences are also counted. Summaries cover SAEs/TESAEs, interruption/withdrawal, related events, worst severity, frequent TEAEs (≥5%) and adjudicated fatal TEs. Death summaries include all-cause, cardiovascular and noncardiovascular, 30-day/index-hospital and bleeding mortality (death within 72 hours without identified TE); Cox and Kaplan–Meier use last-assessment censoring. TE summaries also classify anticoagulant use before/after first TE or no use.
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Investigator relationship is related/not related; severity is mild, moderate or severe, with severe including life-threatening/fatal events.

### 5.7 Software and References

- **Statistical Software:** SAS version 9.4 or higher performs statistical analysis; additional software may produce graphics or methods unavailable in SAS.
- **Other Referenced Documents:** Governing documents are Protocol Amendment 3 (01 Dec 2022) and SAP v1.1 (17 Apr 2023). The EAC Adjudication Charter, DSMB Charter, Blinding Plan, Laboratory Manual and protocol-deviation review plan provide operational details. Medical history/AEs use MedDRA and medications use WHODrug; the SAP references Lan–DeMets/Pocock, Rubin MI, Eekhout median-p and Cui–Hung–Wang adaptive methods.
