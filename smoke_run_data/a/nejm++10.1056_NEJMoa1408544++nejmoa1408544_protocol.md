# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale
CAPiTA is a phase 4 trial of 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (13vPnC) to prevent vaccine-serotype pneumococcal community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) in older adults in the Netherlands. The final controlling protocol is Amendment 9 (01 October 2013); the supplied supplement also contains final SAP material and addenda.

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**
- Demonstrate 13vPnC efficacy in prevention of a first episode of confirmed vaccine-type (VT) pneumococcal CAP.

**Secondary Objectives**
- Demonstrate efficacy against first confirmed nonbacteremic/noninvasive (NB/NI)-VT pneumococcal CAP and first VT-IPD.

**Exploratory Objectives**
- Evaluate prevention of: all confirmed VT-CAP; first confirmed pneumococcal, NB/NI pneumococcal, CAP, and IPD; first/all confirmed VT-CAP by individual 13vPnC serotype; first/all NVT and pneumococcal CAP; first/all NB/NI-VT-CAP and NB/NI-VT-CAP by individual serotype; first/all culture-confirmed VT, pneumococcal, and NVT CAP; and first/all probable VT, pneumococcal, NVT and possible pneumococcal CAP.
- Evaluate all CAP; all VT-IPD, first/all NVT-IPD, all IPD; deaths from confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP/VT-IPD, pneumococcal CAP/IPD and all causes; hospitalization with CAP plus influenza A/B and specified respiratory viruses; age efficacy trends and economic effect.
- Evaluate VT, all, NVT and individual-serotype S. pneumoniae carriage and H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis, and S. aureus carriage at 6, 12, 24 months; safety objectives are SAE incidence, subset reactogenicity/AEs and deaths; immunogenicity objective is serotype IgG/OPA by age and sex at baseline and approximately months 1, 12, 24.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design
- **Design Type:** Phase 4, parallel-group, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial conducted at multiple sites/regions in the Netherlands by the Julius Center.
- **Allocation Ratio:** 1:1, 13vPnC versus placebo.
- **Intervention Summary:** One 0.5-mL intramuscular deltoid dose at Visit 1.
  - 13vPnC: serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, and 23F conjugated to CRM197; 2 micrograms each except 6B 4 micrograms, pH 5.8, 5 mM succinate, 0.85% sodium chloride, 0.02% polysorbate 80, and 0.125 mg aluminum phosphate.
  - Placebo: pH 5.8, 5 mM succinate, 0.15 M sodium chloride, 0.02% polysorbate 80, and 0.125 mg aluminum per 0.5 mL.

### 2.2 Randomization
- **Method:** Sponsor/designee randomization schedule; eligible consented subjects receive the next available syringe, whose PAC identifies allocation.
- **Stratification Factors:** Age stratification applies to the immunogenicity subset (65-69, 70-79, and >=80 years; at least 300 in each); no trial-wide randomization stratification is reported.
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Randomized block design with vaccine groups randomly ordered within blocks; several blocks assigned at a time.

### 2.3 Sample Size
- **Planned Sample Size:** Approximately 85,000 (42,500/group); immunogenicity subset 2,000 (1,000/group).
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** First confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP; supplementary power statements address NB/NI-VT-CAP and VT-IPD.
- **Outcome Type:** Time-to-first confirmed clinical event, analyzed as incidence rate/relative risk.
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** VT-CAP incidence 1.2/1000/year; assumed placebo CAP, confirmed pneumococcal CAP, and confirmed VT-CAP rates were 10, 2.5, and 1.2/1000/year.
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** True VT-CAP efficacy at least 52%; specificity-adjusted efficacy 48.4% in the revised model.
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** Vaccine efficacy >0 (lower confidence bound); interim clinical-significance threshold lower bound >0.20 for VT-CAP.
- **Variability Assumption:** Test specificity at least 97% initially; SSUAD subsequently validated at 98%.
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Two-sided 0.05 experiment-wise.
- **Power:** 90% for primary VT-CAP; >=80% for NB/NI-VT-CAP under stated assumptions; >=90% for VT-IPD if true efficacy >=90%; subset IgG precision 0.10-0.16 for 95% CI differences.
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Breslow-Day conditional binomial derivation for revised primary event target; exact conditional confidence interval analysis with O'Brien-Fleming adjustment.
- **Required Number of Events:** Originally 156 primary events; revised to 65 interim and 130 final first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events; 20 VT-IPD events for stated power.
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable; this is an efficacy/superiority framework.
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** 5% loss to follow-up assumed.
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable; individually randomized, non-clustered trial.
- **Basis for Assumptions:** Clinical feasibility, one enrollment season, >=2 years follow-up, incidence assumptions, assay specificity, DMC review, and an external consultant-supported revised model.
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** DMC administrative review led to revised 65/130 event targets after observed incidence was lower than predicted.
- **Software Used for Calculation:** No software is named specifically for the sample-size calculation; SAS Release 9.1.3 or higher and Proc-StatXact v8.0 are named for analysis/reporting, not calculation.
- **Sample Size Rationale:** 85,000 was selected to accrue required primary events; the 2,000-person subset was based on six-month safety regulatory requirements and feasibility.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework
The primary inference is vaccine efficacy = 1 - relative risk for first confirmed VT-CAP, with success when the adjusted two-sided 95% CI lower bound exceeds 0. NB/NI-VT-CAP is hierarchically tested only following clinically significant primary efficacy; VT-IPD is evaluated once at study conclusion and is efficacious if its two-sided 95% CI exceeds 0.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses
- **Interim Analyses Planned:** One per-protocol interim analysis at about one year after last vaccination or >=65 first confirmed VT-CAP events; a separate administrative DMC look assesses design assumptions only.
- **Stopping Guidelines:** Continue to >=130 events unless harm (adjusted upper efficacy bound <0) or both clinically significant VT-CAP and statistically significant NB/NI-VT-CAP efficacy; DMC can recommend discontinuation for safety.
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** One-look O'Brien-Fleming: alpha approximately 0.0052 interim and 0.0480 final, experiment-wise <=5%; administrative look has no alpha adjustment.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses
- **Final Analysis Timing:** If no interim efficacy declaration, final efficacy inference occurs after investigator sign-off of >=130 per-protocol first confirmed VT-CAP events, cleaning, unblinding, and database freeze; interim-declared efficacy uses interim dataset for inference, with overrunning data supplementary.
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** CAP/IPD surveillance runs through the case-acquisition stopping rule; immunogenicity blood at baseline, month 1, 12, 24; carriage at baseline, 6, 12, 24 months; safety windows are 7-day reactogenicity, 28-day unsolicited AE, and SAE windows specified below.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals
- **Alpha Level:** Two-sided 5% experiment-wise alpha, partitioned by O'Brien-Fleming for primary sequential testing.
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** The hierarchy is primary VT-CAP then NB/NI-VT-CAP under Glimm/O'Brien-Fleming. Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at 5% applies to selected exploratory endpoints (all confirmed VT-CAP, first confirmed pneumococcal/NB-NI pneumococcal CAP, first CAP/IPD, and specified mortality endpoints); remaining exploratory endpoints are descriptive with 95% CIs.
- **Confidence Intervals:** Exact conditional adjusted two-sided 95% CIs for primary efficacy; exact two-sided 95% CIs for carriage relative risks; log-scale Student-t/back-transformed 95% CIs for immunogenicity.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations
- **Adherence Definition:** One administered dose by designated staff; exposure/compliance issues are not relevant beyond receipt because only one dose is given.
- **Adherence Assessment:** Administration date, PAC, volume, route, and site are recorded in CRF; e-diary completeness is assessed for subset reactogenicity data.
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Major violations are determined by clinical scientist or medical monitor. Per-protocol exclusions include hospitalization/long-term-care residence >48 hours immediately before onset, subsequent pneumococcal vaccine, specified immune-suppression/malignancy conditions, and immune-status committee suppression; post-deficiency cases remain mITT but are excluded from per-protocol.
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Major deviations from SAP/protocol methods must be discussed in the clinical study report.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations
- **Intention-to-Treat:** Modified ITT is case-based: CAP/IPD identified by criteria with onset >=14 days after vaccination; all efficacy objectives are also assessed in mITT, which has immune-deficient/suppressed and immune-competent subgroups.
- **Per Protocol:** Primary/secondary set: eligible, vaccinated, qualifying signed-off case onset >=14 days, no major violation; excludes cases after >48-hour hospital/LTC residence, subsequent pneumococcal vaccine, listed immune-deficiency/suppression or committee suppression. Post-deficiency cases are mITT only; PP is primary except exploratory all-CAP.
- **Other Populations:** Evaluable/all-available carriage and immunogenicity sets require valid determinate results, with evaluable sets also requiring eligibility, subset membership, windows, and no major violations; safety includes vaccinated subjects with safety data and is used for mortality.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data
Potential participants are identified from GP information-system records of patients eligible for influenza vaccination; consent precedes all study procedures. Demographics and abbreviated medical/social history are collected at Visit 1.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary
Adults >=65 years at vaccination, registered with a referring GP and able to meet requirements are eligible. Key exclusions are prior pneumococcal vaccination, institutional/semiskilled care, vaccine contraindication, recent investigational product, severe vaccine reaction, and defined immune deficiency/suppression; subset subjects must complete e-diary procedures and must not have influenza vaccination in the preceding 7 days. Acute febrile illness >=38.0 C or recent acute illness delays enrollment.

### 4.3 Recruitment
Up to approximately 85,000 subjects are enrolled through GPs and the Julius Center at multiple sites/regions in the Netherlands; 2,000 from one region comprise the immunogenicity subset.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up
- **Withdrawal Levels:** Subjects withdrawn after vaccination are not replaced; those withdrawn after consent but before vaccination are replaced.
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** GPs report SAEs/deaths at least every 2 weeks during SAE collection; death/loss-to-follow-up contacts changed from monthly to at least every 2 months after Amendment 6.
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Subject disposition, including number/percentage withdrawn and reasons (including AE, subject request, protocol violation/noncompliance, and sponsor termination), will be tabulated by vaccine group.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics
- **Baseline Variables:** Sex, race, age at vaccination, and medical/social history including heart disease, lung disease/asthma, diabetes/insulin, liver disease, splenectomy, and smoking; comorbidities and prior seasonal/H1N1 influenza vaccination are also summarized for efficacy sets.
- **Summary Methods:** Descriptive statistics by vaccine group and age are produced for immunogenicity, mITT, per-protocol, carriage, and all-available/evaluable populations, with subject disposition/patient-flow diagram. Age derives from vaccination date minus birth date plus one divided by 365.25, truncated to one decimal.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes
**Outcome: First episode of confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP**
- **Definition:** First CAP episode meeting clinical, radiological, and microbiological criteria and confirmed VT. Clinical CAP requires >=2 of cough, purulent/changed sputum, temperature >38.0/<36.1 C, compatible auscultation, leukocytosis >10x10^9/L or >15% bands, CRP >3x upper limit, or room-air PO2 <60 mmHg. Radiographs within 48 hours are adjudicated. Confirmed pneumococcal CAP requires S. pneumoniae culture from blood/pleural/other sterile site, positive Binax, or positive VT-SSUAD; culture is definitive over discrepant SSUAD, another confirmed sterile-site pathogen excludes the nonculture pathway, multiple SSUAD serotypes count once as VT-CAP but separately by serotype, and SSUAD serotype assigns an untyped culture isolate. Culture-confirmed, probable (evaluable sputum culture), and possible (evaluable sputum microscopy) categories use the stated serotype rules; sterile sites include blood, CSF, pleural/peritoneal/pericardial fluid, surgical aspirate, bone and joint fluid. Episodes are distinct if >=42 days after resolution or different serotype/sterile-site organism.
- **Measurement Units:** Case incidence rate and relative risk; vaccine efficacy = 1 - relative risk.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Symptom onset >=14 days after vaccination; interim at >=65 cases/about one year after last vaccination and final at >=130 signed-off cases if needed.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Exact conditional CI conditional on total cases; VT derivation uses the clinical/radiologic/microbiological algorithm, Binax/SSUAD and culture; NB/NI excludes bacteremic/invasive disease.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes
**Outcome: First confirmed NB/NI-VT pneumococcal CAP**
- **Definition:** First confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP that is nonbacteremic/noninvasive under microbiological categorization.
- **Measurement Units:** Case incidence/relative risk and vaccine efficacy.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Onset >=14 days post-vaccination; conditionally tested after clinically significant primary efficacy at interim and, if study continues, in final hierarchical inference at >=130 primary events.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Same exact O'Brien-Fleming-adjusted efficacy framework as primary endpoint.

**Outcome: First VT-IPD**
- **Definition:** First invasive pneumococcal disease episode categorized as vaccine type.
- **Measurement Units:** Case incidence/relative risk and vaccine efficacy.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Onset >=14 days after vaccination; assessed once at study conclusion.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Efficacy criterion is two-sided 95% CI exceeding 0.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods
- **Primary Analysis Methods:** Per-protocol incidence-rate relative-risk efficacy analysis with exact conditional two-sided O'Brien-Fleming-adjusted 95% CI; mITT analyses support all efficacy objectives, and all-CAP exploratory objectives use mITT only.
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Immunogenicity postvaccination summaries use ANCOVA with prevaccination result as covariate; no covariate-adjusted primary efficacy model is reported.
- **Assumption Checks:** DMC administrative review evaluates design assumptions; MI model fit is assessed using per-protocol first-episode cases with SSUAD.
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** Not reported in protocol; sensitivity analyses specify alternatives for endpoint SSUAD missingness.
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Missing-SSUAD primary/secondary case sensitivity includes MAR multiple imputation, best/worst and intermediate MNAR allocation scenarios, and if appropriate a Binax/culture-positive supporting analysis; imputed analyses retain O'Brien-Fleming/Clopper-Pearson framework.
- **Subgroup Analyses:** For CAP/IPD endpoints showing a treatment difference, mITT subgroup analyses use immune status, age <75/>=75, sex, and smoking; immune responses and safety are also summarized by age/sex.

### 5.4 Missing Data
- **Missing Data Assumptions:** For missing SSUAD due to clinical error/inability to obtain sample, MAR is assumed for primary sensitivity assessment; intermediate worst-case scenarios examine MNAR. Missingness after commercial 13vPnC is profiled but not formally assessed.
- **Handling Methods:** Missing assay results are not replaced/imputed. MI applies only when SSUAD is the sole missing endpoint information; generate 2,000 SAS PROC MI datasets (seed 9152009) using treatment, age, sex, race and eligible PP cases. Unadjudicated imputed positives assume negative immune-status adjudication; each dataset uses Clopper-Pearson/O'Brien-Fleming inference and reports the proportion with lower bound >0. Best/worst and allocation-ratio analyses are also run.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses
Deaths are MedDRA-categorized and summarized by group; time-to-death is displayed with Kaplan-Meier methodology and statistically analyzed. Health outcomes use frequencies/percentages for categorical data and mean/SD for continuous data. Carriage uses positivity and exact 95% CI relative risks. IgG/OPA are log transformed, summarized as geometric means/ratios and CIs, graphically assessed, and may support estimation of protective cutoffs.

### 5.6 Harms
- **Adverse Event Definitions:** AE is any untoward, undesired, or unplanned sign, symptom, disease, or laboratory/physiologic observation after test article, irrespective of causality; solicited subset reactogenicity includes local pain/redness/swelling/arm limitation and listed systemic events, with fever oral temperature >=38.0 C.
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Analyze according to actual vaccine received. By group, report subjects with >=1 event and event counts; MedDRA-code unsolicited AEs, SAEs, deaths, and newly diagnosed chronic conditions. Fisher exact comparisons assess reactogenicity, AEs and SAEs; report placebo subjects subsequently receiving open-label 13vPnC separately. Windows are 7-day reactogenicity, 28-day unsolicited AE, and SAEs through day 28 (all) and six months (subset).
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Pain is mild/moderate/severe by activity interference; redness/swelling use caliper conversion and size bands; arm movement and fever use stated ordinal scales; additional AE summaries may be by severity or vaccine relationship.

### 5.7 Software and References
- **Statistical Software:** SAS Release 9.1.3 or higher for tables/figures and analyses; Proc-StatXact v8.0 from SAS may be used for exact confidence intervals; SAS PROC MI performs missing-SSUAD multiple imputation.
- **Other Referenced Documents:** Final Amendment 9 protocol; final SAP version 5 material; SAP Addenda I (timing/per-protocol), II (IgG/OPA outcome modelling), III (missing-data sensitivity), and IV (case categories/case-level populations); DMC charter and CDPP are referenced separately.
