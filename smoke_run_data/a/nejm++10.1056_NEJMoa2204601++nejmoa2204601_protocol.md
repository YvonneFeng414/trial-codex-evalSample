# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale
Pediatric type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) has limited glucose-lowering options. This Phase 3 study evaluates whether once-weekly dulaglutide added to diet and exercise, with or without metformin and/or basal insulin, improves glycemic control versus placebo; HbA1c was selected as an accepted endpoint for pediatric T2DM glycemic trials.

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- Test superiority of pooled dulaglutide 0.75 mg and 1.5 mg SC once weekly versus placebo for change in central-laboratory HbA1c from baseline to Week 26 in children and adolescents with inadequately controlled T2DM.

**Secondary Objectives**

- Key efficacy objectives compare individual 0.75-mg and 1.5-mg doses (and, where specified, pooled dulaglutide) with placebo for individual-dose HbA1c change, FBG change, HbA1c <7.0%, and BMI change at Week 26; other secondary efficacy objectives assess HbA1c/FBG/BMI changes and HbA1c <7.0% at Week 52.
- Secondary safety objectives at Week 26 versus placebo and Week 52 within dulaglutide groups cover self-reported hypoglycemia/rescue; adjudicated pancreatitis/pancreatic enzymes; thyroid TEAEs/calcitonin; and ADA, allergic/hypersensitivity, and injection-site reactions. PK/PD characterizes PK and exposure relationships with safety/efficacy.

**Exploratory Objectives**

- At Weeks 26 and 52 unless stated otherwise, assess HbA1c ≤6.5%; HbA1c <7.0% and HbA1c ≤6.5% without severe, documented symptomatic (<70 mg/dL), or probable hypoglycemia; weight, BMI SDS, height/height SDS, waist, morning prolactin/IGF-1/estradiol/testosterone in males/LH/cortisol, lipids, Tanner stage, EQ-5D-Y VAS and domain levels, and basal-insulin-dose change. Assess HbA1c change at Week 13 and insulin resistance, beta-cell function, and adiponectin at Weeks 13 and 26.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-arm superiority trial with a 26-week open-label extension and 30-day safety follow-up.
- **Allocation Ratio:** 1:1:1 at baseline (Visit 2).
- **Intervention Summary:** SC study treatment is administered weekly.
  - Dulaglutide 0.75 mg/week during double blind and open label.
  - Dulaglutide 1.5 mg/week during double blind and open label.
  - Placebo/week during double blind; switches to dulaglutide 0.75 mg/week at Week 26.

  - **Week-26 reporting labels:** All Dula (pooled 1.5/0.75 mg), Dula 1.5, Dula 0.75, and Placebo; placebo is reference.
  - **Post-Week-26 sequence labels:** All Dula (0.75/0.75 and 1.5/1.5 pooled), Dula 1.5/1.5, Dula 0.75/0.75, and Placebo/Dula 0.75. No between-group inference is made after Week 26. Volumes/adjuvant content are not reported.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** Computer-generated random sequence implemented through IWRS after eligibility confirmation at Visit 2.
- **Stratification Factors:** Screening HbA1c <8.0% or >=8.0%, metformin use yes/no, and insulin use yes/no.
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Not reported in protocol.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** Approximately 150 enrolled (50/arm) to obtain 120 Week-26 completers; approximately 750 screened, based on an estimated 80% screen-failure rate.
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Mean change in HbA1c from baseline to Week 26, pooled dulaglutide versus placebo.
- **Outcome Type:** Continuous percentage-point HbA1c change.
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** Not reported in protocol; the calculation reports the between-arm difference rather than an absolute placebo value.
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** Not reported in protocol; the calculation reports the between-arm difference rather than an absolute dulaglutide value.
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** -0.65% mean HbA1c-change difference for pooled dulaglutide versus placebo.
- **Variability Assumption:** SD 1.0% initially; SSR scenario SD 1.3%. The 2019 SSR used SAC-estimated HbA1c SD 1.4%, assumed difference -0.8%, and 10% Week-26 HbA1c missingness.
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Two-sided 0.05; SSR does not require alpha adjustment.
- **Power:** At least 90% pooled and 80% each dose initially; up to 189 enrolled (63/arm) gives at least 80% pooled and 69% each-dose power.
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Superiority comparison of HbA1c change; SSR estimates blinded Week-26 SD with REML MMRM containing baseline HbA1c, insulin/metformin, treatment, visit, and treatment-by-visit.
- **Required Number of Events:** Not applicable: the powered endpoint is continuous HbA1c change, not time-to-event.
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable: this is a superiority trial.
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** 20% dropout assumption.
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable: individually randomized parallel-arm trial; no clustering described.
- **Basis for Assumptions:** Uncertainty reflects limited pediatric GLP-1 RA experience, broad baseline HbA1c values, and inclusion of metformin and/or basal-insulin users.
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** SAC could recommend variability-only SSR after about 100 enrolled, bounded 150-189; Lilly decides implementation. SSR was performed in 2019 and confirmed approximately 150.
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol.
- **Sample Size Rationale:** Preserve power while limiting exposure to placebo and accommodating uncertainty in pediatric endpoint variability.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework
Two primary estimands compare pooled dulaglutide with placebo for Week-26 HbA1c: efficacy (ITT, post-rescue censored) and treatment regimen (ITT regardless of rescue/adherence); each has full two-sided .05. After H1 pooled-HbA1c success, the graphical procedure transfers alpha among H2-H3 individual HbA1c, H4-H6 HbA1c <7%, H7-H9 FBG, and H10-H12 BMI hypotheses (pooled, 1.5 mg, 0.75 mg versus placebo). The scheme is separately run for treatment-regimen washout-MI, efficacy, and EMA-restricted efficacy populations and may be revised using blinded Week-26 correlations before lock.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** No formal efficacy interim analysis. An independent DMC reviews unblinded safety about every 6 months after 50 patients are enrolled until all reach Week 26; a limited external-to-study-team PK group may access unblinded data to initiate PopPK/PD development.
- **Stopping Guidelines:** Detailed DMC analysis and communication procedures are deferred to a separate DMC charter. No DMC efficacy analyses are planned; safety reviews begin after 50 enrolled and are approximately six-monthly, but may begin sooner and occur annually if enrollment is slow.
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** No alpha spending for DMC safety interims because DMC plans no efficacy analyses; no alpha adjustment for variability-only SSR.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** Primary database lock is after all randomized patients complete Week 26/Visit 9; final lock is after all complete the 4-week safety follow-up/Visit 801. Sponsor unblinds after primary lock to analyze/report primary data.
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Baseline is Visit 2. Week 13 is fasting with pre-dose and 1-to-12-hour post-dose PK; Week 26 is fasting, pre-dose PK, central laboratories/ECG/EQ-5D-Y and has ±6-day window; Week 39 permits PK ≤2 days postdose; Week 52 is fasting with PK/ECG/EQ-5D-Y and ±7-day window; Visit 801/Week 56 is ~30 days later with safety follow-up and PK. EQ-5D-Y is baseline, Weeks 26/52 or early termination.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** Unless otherwise noted, treatment-effect tests use two-sided alpha .05; subgroup interaction tests use two-sided alpha .10.
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** Following H1, graphical alpha transfers along prespecified arrows through H2-H12 to strongly control two-sided family-wise .05. H1-H3 test pooled, 1.5-mg, and 0.75-mg HbA1c; H4-H6 HbA1c <7%; H7-H9 FBG; H10-H12 BMI, all versus placebo at Week 26. The blinded Week-26 correlation matrix may revise the graph before lock; the same scheme runs separately for treatment-regimen washout-MI, efficacy, and EMA-restricted efficacy estimands.
- **Confidence Intervals:** 95% two-sided CIs accompany treatment differences.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** A participant is compliant for a period if at least 75% of expected doses are taken; compliance percentage is actual injections divided by prescribed injections times 100.
- **Adherence Assessment:** Direct questioning and remaining study drug are reviewed at each visit; dose-regimen deviations are recorded in the CRF.
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Important deviations are selected for potential primary-endpoint impact. PP exclusion includes absent consent or consent date, randomization age <10/≥18, known T1DM, ketoacidosis after antidiabetes treatment, GAD65/IA2 antibodies, HbS/HbC/HbE/HbSC/elevated HbF or another HbA1c-interfering disorder, prohibited antihyperglycemic medicines, chronic prescription weight-loss medicine, screen HbA1c eligibility failure, missing baseline/Week-26 HbA1c, <75% overall compliance, or systemic glucocorticoids >14 days.
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Important deviations are listed for all randomized participants and summarized by treatment; the complete list is captured in an Excel file converted to SDTM, while decisions not to classify deviations as important are documented in the eTMF.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** ITT includes all randomized participants who take at least one assigned-treatment dose. It is the efficacy and safety population; EMA primary HbA1c excludes lifestyle-only metformin-naïve participants. Safety includes post-rescue measurements except hypoglycemia, for which post-rescue data are censored.
- **Per Protocol:** PP is ITT with no important primary-objective-affecting deviation, >=75% compliance through Week 26, double-blind completion, and nonmissing baseline and Week-26 HbA1c.
- **Other Populations:** Six populations are defined: All Entered (signed consent), All Randomized (allocated), Nonrandomized (entered but not allocated), ITT, PP and HO (ITT participants consenting to the health-outcomes addendum with ≥1 test). Listings use All Randomized.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data
Individuals failing participation criteria may rescreen up to twice, at least 8 weeks apart; each rescreen requires new consent and a new identification number. Screening includes eligibility laboratories, T1DM antibodies, HbA1c, background treatment, anthropometry, and consent/assent.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary
Eligible participants are male/female age 10-<18 years with IDF-ISPAD T2DM; lifestyle measures, metformin and basal insulin stable for ≥8 weeks before screening (metformin ≥1000 mg/day or highest tolerated/local-approved dose); screening HbA1c >6.5% to ≤11.0% (≤9.0% if newly diagnosed/lifestyle only); BMI >85th percentile; weight ≥50 kg; and assent/parent consent. The double-blind treatment-period, not eligibility, rule limits background metformin/basal-insulin change to ±15% except protocol-specified circumstances. Exclusions include T1DM/antibodies, ketoacidosis/hyperosmolar syndrome, GI/pancreatic/thyroid/renal/cardiovascular risks, Hb variants, pregnancy/breastfeeding or ineffective contraception, unstable psychiatric treatment, prohibited medicines/other trials. Other antihyperglycemics require ≥3-month washout; bolus insulin requires ≥6-week washout except ≤14-day acute-condition rescue.

### 4.3 Recruitment
Approximately 750 participants are expected to be entered/screened to randomize approximately 150, based on 80% screen failure. Enrollment of lifestyle-only, metformin-naive participants is capped operationally at approximately 25% of planned enrollment through IWRS.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Study drug may be discontinued for confirmed exclusion/safety conditions, intolerance, severe allergic reaction, or laboratory criteria; study discontinuation occurs for withdrawal of consent, loss to follow-up, specified inadvertent enrollment, pregnancy, or breastfeeding.
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** Participants who stop study drug remain for scheduled efficacy/safety assessments where possible; early study discontinuation triggers end-of-study procedures and Visit 801 about 30 days later. Sites make diligent contact attempts and may collect vital status within legal/ethical bounds.
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Disposition summaries give reasons by treatment; discontinuations, study-drug discontinuations, AE/death discontinuations, and completion through Weeks 26/52/Visit 801 are summarized, with Fisher testing.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Demographic and baseline clinical measures, including Tanner stage by sex, are summarized; baseline for each parameter is the last nonmissing pre-first-dose value, and Visit 2 is the baseline visit.
- **Summary Methods:** Continuous characteristics use n, mean, SD, median, minimum, and maximum and ANOVA with treatment fixed effect; categorical variables use n/frequency/percent and Fisher’s exact test.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes
**Outcome: Central-laboratory HbA1c change from baseline to Week 26**
- **Definition:** Change from the baseline central-laboratory HbA1c to Week 26/Visit 9, comparing pooled dulaglutide with placebo.
- **Measurement Units:** HbA1c percentage points.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Baseline Visit 2 and Week 26/Visit 9; Week-26 clinic window is +/-6 days.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Baseline is last nonmissing pre-first-dose HbA1c; change from baseline is analyzed. Baseline HbA1c defines <8.0%/>=8.0% strata except where baseline value is modeled directly.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes
**Outcome: Key secondary Week-26 efficacy outcomes**
- **Definition:** Individual-dose HbA1c change; FBG change; proportion with HbA1c <7.0%; and BMI change, each baseline to Week 26. Other secondary outcomes are HbA1c/FBG/BMI change and HbA1c <7.0% at Week 52.
- **Measurement Units:** HbA1c percent; FBG mg/dL or mmol/L as recorded; BMI kg/m2; target is HbA1c <7.0%.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Week 26 and Week 52, with Week-13 assessments for selected exploratory HbA1c and PD measures.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Change from baseline is calculated; BMI derives from standardized height/weight measurement. Target attainment is binary.

**Outcome: Secondary safety, PK/PD, and health outcomes**
- **Definition:** Hypoglycemia/rescue, pancreatitis/pancreatic enzymes, thyroid TEAEs/calcitonin, ADA and allergic/hypersensitivity/injection-site outcomes, dulaglutide PK/exposure-response, and EQ-5D-Y.
- **Measurement Units:** Event incidence/rates; laboratory units as assay-reported; EQ-5D-Y VAS and five 3-level domains.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Safety evaluates Weeks 26/52; EQ-5D-Y at baseline/Weeks 26/52 or ET; six PK samples in the main protocol plus addendum samples.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Treatment-emergent ADA is negative-to-positive titer >=1:4 or positive-to-positive >=4-fold rise; total hypoglycemia definitions and PG thresholds are specified.

**Outcome: Additional secondary endpoint detail**
- **Definition:** Safety endpoints include pancreatic-enzyme change and adjudicated pancreatitis, thyroid TEAEs/calcitonin, ADA incidence/types, allergic/hypersensitivity and injection-site reactions; PK samples measure dulaglutide concentration and exposure-response; EQ-5D-Y includes a VAS and five 3-level domains.
- **Measurement Units:** Enzyme/calcitonin and drug concentration use central-laboratory/assay units; ADA is titer/type; events are incidence or rate; EQ-5D-Y uses VAS/domain levels.
- **Timing of Measurement:** Safety is assessed through Weeks 26/52; main protocol obtains six PK samples (Week 13 pre/post-dose, Week 26 pre-dose, Week 39 ≤2 days postdose, Week 52 and Visit 801); EQ-5D-Y is baseline/26/52/ET.
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** BMI is kg/m² from standardized weight/height; treatment-emergent ADA is negative-to-positive ≥1:4 or positive-to-positive ≥4-fold; total hypoglycemia uses PG <70 and <54 mg/dL categories.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** Final protocol describes efficacy MMRM and treatment-regimen ANCOVA with MI; final SAP Version 7 implements treatment-regimen MI as a washout modified placebo PMM. Efficacy MMRM/REML uses post-rescue censoring; treatment-regimen uses all rescue/adherence data. FDA primary is treatment-regimen, EMA primary is efficacy excluding lifestyle-only/metformin-naïve, and other audiences use efficacy.
- **Covariate Adjustment:** MMRM generally includes insulin/metformin use, HbA1c strata, treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit, and corresponding baseline; primary HbA1c replaces strata with baseline HbA1c. ANCOVA includes baseline, stratification factors, and treatment.
- **Assumption Checks:** For PD biomarkers, normality is assessed visually with histogram/QQ plot and Shapiro-Wilk test before formal analysis.
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** If unstructured MMRM fails, use heterogeneous Toeplitz, heterogeneous autoregressive, heterogeneous compound symmetry, Toeplitz, autoregressive, then compound symmetry; the first convergent covariance is used. Hypoglycemia negative-binomial failure uses stratified Wilcoxon rank-sum.
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Apply efficacy MMRM using actual received dose for 1.5-to-0.75-mg switches. For Week-26 HbA1c <7%, logistic regression omitting visit and treatment-by-visit is supportive; rescued or missing participants are nonachievers. PP and completer sensitivity approaches in prior protocol versions were removed in final SAP Version 7 and are not final analyses.
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Final SAP uses treatment-regimen washout-MI analyses; final protocol describes efficacy-estimand MMRM. Both add three-way visit-by-treatment-by-subgroup plus treatment-by-subgroup and visit-by-subgroup interactions. SAP Version 7 candidate cutoffs are age <14/>14 and baseline HbA1c ≤8/>8; final protocol lists age ≤14/>14 and HbA1c <8/≥8. Other candidates are sex, race, ethnicity, US/non-US region, diabetes duration, baseline BMI/weight, metformin, insulin, monotherapy, and metformin-plus-insulin; interaction alpha .10.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** Efficacy-estimand MMRM is under MAR with post-rescue observations censored. Treatment-regimen washout multiple imputation is a modified placebo-based pattern-mixture approach under MNAR.
- **Handling Methods:** Efficacy target analyses treat post-rescue data as missing. Treatment-regimen continuous endpoint ANCOVA is fitted to complete data after washout MI: dulaglutide missing values use placebo baseline/Week-26 data, placebo missing values use placebo baseline/intermediate/Week-26 data; SAS PROC MI/MIANALYZE implements this. Treatment-regimen HbA1c-target missing endpoint data are nonachievers. Safety laboratory endpoint analyses use LOCF; target sensitivity also treats rescued or missing Week-26 participants as nonachievers.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses
PK/PD analysis is separately planned by Lilly/designee. Exploratory ITT analyses censor post-rescue measurements and include Week-13 HbA1c, target composites, WHO age/sex BMI/height SDS, body weight/waist, basal insulin, insulin-resistance/beta-cell/adiponectin biomarkers, and PRO analyses. Safety additions include exposure, rescue hyperglycemia, ADA, malignant/renal events, nausea/vomiting, vital signs, ECG, laboratories, and pubertal progression. All PRO analyses are exploratory in HO participants: EQ-5D-Y VAS (and index only if an index formula becomes available) uses MMRM; its five domains are descriptive and outputs cover age <13/≥13, ≤14/>14 and all ages. PedsQL Generic (physical/emotional/social/school scales, physical and psychosocial summary and total) and Diabetes 3.2 (Diabetes, Treatment I/II, Worry, Communication and total) scores use stated transformations/subscales; Worry uses MMRM through Weeks 26/52 and other PedsQL scores use analogous MMRM. Items score 0-4 then reverse to 100/75/50/25/0; scales with >50% missing are not computed, otherwise completed-item mean imputes missing items. After Week 26, results are summarized by treatment sequence without between-group inference.

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** AE is an untoward medical occurrence; TEAE is a new post-first-dose event or worsening preexisting condition. SAE includes death, hospitalization, life-threatening event, disability, congenital anomaly, or other medically important event.
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** All Randomized is the listing population; ITT is analysis population and includes post-rescue measures except hypoglycemia, for which post-rescue data are censored. Exposure is first to last dose (specified date replacement if missing). AEs are MedDRA SOC/PT-coded and Fisher-compared. Hypoglycemia incidence uses Fisher and rate uses negative binomial with log-days offset (Wilcoxon fallback); safety analyses cover AEs/SAEs/AESIs, rescue, nausea/vomiting, renal/malignant events, vitals, ECG, labs, ADA, and pubertal progression.
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Investigator records severity, seriousness, and relationship. Related denotes direct cause/effect; possibly related is not demonstrated/probable but not impossible; unrelated is definitely not associated. Hypoglycemia includes documented symptomatic, asymptomatic/unspecified, probable, severe (another-person assistance), nocturnal, and total categories at PG <70 and <54 mg/dL. Pancreatitis is adjudicated; thyroid, allergic/hypersensitivity/injection-site, nausea/vomiting and renal events have protocol-defined listings/summaries.

### 5.7 Software and References

- **Statistical Software:** R or SAS Version 9.4 or higher for statistical analyses; SAS PROC MI and MIANALYZE implement washout multiple imputation.
- **Other Referenced Documents:** The protocol defers detailed PK/PD analysis to a separate analysis plan and DMC procedures to a DMC charter/unblinding plan. EQ-5D-Y and PedsQL manuals govern scoring; WHO 2008 growth standards govern age/sex SDS derivation. Method references include Bretz graphical procedures, NRC missing-data guidance, and Permutt washout methodology.
