# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** The study evaluates subcutaneous dupilumab in children aged 6 to <12 years with uncontrolled persistent asthma receiving background controller therapy.

### 1.2 Objectives

**[Provided]** The primary objective is to evaluate dupilumab efficacy.

**[Provided]** Secondary objectives are to assess safety and tolerability; patient-reported outcomes (PROs), including health-related quality of life; systemic exposure and anti-drug antibodies; and the association of treatment with pediatric immune responses to tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis and/or seasonal trivalent or quadrivalent influenza vaccination.

**[Provided]** Exploratory objectives are to investigate biomarkers as predictors of, or associations with, response; optional genetic profiles; inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) or second-controller step-up; and additional PROs.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a multinational, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study. Screening lasts 4 ± 1 weeks, treatment lasts up to 52 weeks, and a 12-week post-treatment period applies to participants not entering the one-year extension.

**[Provided]** Participants receive subcutaneous dupilumab or matching placebo every two weeks (Q2W), in a 2:1 ratio, on background medium-dose ICS plus a second controller or high-dose ICS alone or with a second controller.

**[Provided]** At baseline weight ≤30 kg, the assigned dose is dupilumab 100 mg Q2W or matching placebo, volume 0.67 mL. At weight >30 kg, the assigned dose is dupilumab 200 mg Q2W or matching placebo, volume 1.14 mL. Investigators and patients are unblinded to dose level because volumes differ.

**[Provided]** Background controllers continue. A temporary ICS increase up to fourfold for ≤10 days is permitted; permanent controller step-up may follow at least two severe exacerbations.

**[Provided]** Scheduled visits use a ±5-day window. Exceptions for early treatment discontinuation/end-of-treatment assessment and telephone follow-up are specified, but their details are not supplied.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the early-discontinuation, end-of-treatment, and telephone-follow-up exceptions. These determine assessment timing and follow-up completeness.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** After screening, central IVRS/IWRS permuted-block randomization assigns dupilumab and matching placebo in a 2:1 ratio.

**[Provided]** Stratification factors are screening ICS dose (medium/high), screening eosinophils (<0.3/≥0.3 Giga/L), and region:

- **[Provided]** Latin America: Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Chile, Mexico.
- **[Provided]** Eastern Europe: Poland, Hungary, Romania, Lithuania, Russia, Ukraine, Turkey.
- **[Provided]** Western Countries: Australia, Canada, Italy, South Africa, Spain, USA.

**[Provided]** Randomization strata are distinct from pooled-country regional analysis. Minimization is not specified.

**[Proposed]** Restrict access to the allocation schedule and document treatment unblinding; this supports allocation concealment and trial integrity, assuming the central system can maintain an audit trail.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** At least 402 participants are planned overall: 268 assigned dupilumab and 134 placebo. The three powered populations are retained separately:

- **[Provided]** Type 2 inflammatory phenotype, defined by eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L or FeNO ≥20 ppb: approximately 345 participants, 230/115 dupilumab/placebo; assumed rates 0.322 versus 0.7 per year, a 54% relative-risk reduction, with 94% power.
- **[Provided]** Eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L: 327 participants, 218/109; assumed rates 0.322 versus 0.7 per year, a 54% relative-risk reduction, with 93% power.
- **[Provided]** Eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L: 255 participants, 170/85; assumed rates 0.32 versus 0.8 per year, a 60% relative-risk reduction, with 96% power.

**[Provided]** The calculation uses the annualized severe-exacerbation rate during 52 weeks, a negative-binomial count/rate comparison, dispersion 1.5, 2:1 allocation, two-sided alpha 0.05, linear discontinuation of 20% at one year, and average exposure of 0.9 year.

**[Provided]** Projected prevalences are 86% for Type 2, 81% for eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, and 64% for eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L. Sizing assured target power across the three biomarker-defined populations under 2:1 allocation.

**[Provided]** No required event count or calculation software is specified. A non-inferiority/equivalence margin is not applicable to the superiority comparison; design effect/clustering is not applicable to this individually randomized trial.

**[Provided]** Amendment 2 increased the sample size from 294 to 471. Amendment 3 revised the primary populations and final calculation to 402.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** Superiority hypotheses compare dupilumab with placebo for severe-exacerbation rate and sequential efficacy endpoints. The final statistical analysis plan uses regional hierarchical sequences to control two-sided Type I error at 5%.

**[Provided]** The US/reference sequence contains the following orders:

1. **[Provided]** Orders 1–3: severe-exacerbation rate in eosinophils ≥0.3, eosinophils ≥0.15, then Type 2.
2. **[Provided]** Orders 4–6: Week-12 pre-bronchodilator (pre-BD) FEV1 in the same population order.
3. **[Provided]** Orders 7–9: Week-24 ACQ-7 in the same population order.
4. **[Provided]** Orders 10–12: severe-exacerbation rate, Week-12 pre-BD FEV1, then Week-24 ACQ-7 in FeNO ≥20 ppb.
5. **[Provided]** Orders 13–14: severe-exacerbation rate, then Week-12 pre-BD FEV1, in the full intention-to-treat (ITT) population.
6. **[Provided]** Orders 15–19: severe-exacerbation rate in high-ICS eosinophils ≥0.3, eosinophils ≥0.15, Type 2, FeNO ≥20 ppb, then full ITT.
7. **[Provided]** Orders 20–24: Week-12 FeNO in those same five populations and order.

**[Provided]** The EU/reference sequence uses the same ordered endpoint groups, but the first three population sequences are Type 2, eosinophils ≥0.15, then eosinophils ≥0.3; this order is retained for the high-ICS and FeNO groups.

**[Proposed]** Within each prespecified sequence, test at two-sided 0.05 and stop confirmatory testing after the first failure; this implements a fixed sequence, assuming each preceding hypothesis must be rejected before the next is tested.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify how the US/reference and EU/reference sequences map to datasets and intended claims, and whether any claims across sequences require joint multiplicity control. This determines the applicable testing family and error-control interpretation.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** The final database lock occurs after every randomized participant completes Week 52 or discontinues earlier. Analyses through lock are final clinical study report (CSR) analyses. Two originally planned locks were integrated into one final lock; the calendar date is not specified.

**[Provided]** The primary rate is assessed through Week 52; key pre-BD FEV1 is assessed at Week 12. Spirometry occurs at screening, randomization, and subsequent visits. Post-bronchodilator assessments include Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, 52, and 64.

**[Clarification Required]** Decide how post-lock data will be handled in the CSR or an addendum. This determines the completeness and reporting of later follow-up, including Week-64 data.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The sample-size calculation and regional hierarchical sequences use two-sided 5% significance.

**[Proposed]** Report effect estimates with two-sided 95% confidence intervals, identifying intervals as nominal unless multiplicity-adjusted; this supports interpretation while avoiding an unsupported claim of simultaneous coverage.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Classify important deviations before treatment unblinding and summarize them by assigned group; this limits classification bias, assuming sufficient blinded information is available.

**[Clarification Required]** Define important-deviation criteria and their consequences for each analysis population. These choices affect sensitivity analyses and interpretation.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Proposed]** Define full ITT as all randomized participants analyzed by assigned treatment; this preserves randomized comparisons, assuming randomization records are complete.

**[Proposed]** Define a safety population as participants receiving at least one dose, analyzed by treatment received; this supports exposure-based safety summaries, assuming actual treatment can be established.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the measurement time, missing-value rules, and data sources for eosinophil, FeNO, Type 2, and high-ICS population membership. These decisions determine who enters each powered population and hierarchical test.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify whether a per-protocol population is required and, if so, its exclusions. This determines whether a supplementary adherence-focused analysis is interpretable.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Summarize numbers screened, randomized, and not randomized, with reasons for screen failure; this describes selection, assuming screening records capture these reasons.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** Age 6 to <12 years; physician-diagnosed persistent asthma for ≥12 months; and uncontrolled persistent asthma.
- **[Provided]** Medium-dose ICS plus one controller, or high-dose ICS alone or with a second controller, for ≥3 months, with a stable dose for ≥1 month before screening; a third controller is not permitted.
- **[Provided]** Pre-BD FEV1 ≤95% predicted or FEV1/FVC <0.85 at screening/baseline; ≥10% reversibility, with stated alternatives.
- **[Provided]** A prior-year systemic-corticosteroid-treated exacerbation or hospital/emergency care; and screening evidence of uncontrolled asthma by ACQ-5 ≥1.5, reliever use, sleep awakening, or symptoms.
- **[Provided]** Ability to comply, caregiver comprehension, required assent/consent, and questionnaire completion.

**[Provided]** Exclusion criteria include:

- **[Provided]** Age <6 or ≥12 years, or weight <16 kg; chronic lung disease or life-threatening asthma; interfering disease, malignancy, or inability to follow procedures.
- **[Provided]** Timing-restricted omalizumab or other biologic or immunosuppressant use, allergen immunotherapy, investigational medicines, or prohibited concomitants; prior participation in a dupilumab study or a site-staff relationship.
- **[Provided]** <80% diary-recorded background adherence; specified corticosteroid exposure, including prohibited systemic or topical steroid use.
- **[Provided]** Clinically significant illness, including stated tuberculosis provisions, or major medical disease; pregnancy or contraception restrictions.
- **[Provided]** Parasite, HIV, infection, immunosuppression, autoimmune disease, biologic hypersensitivity, or viral-hepatitis risk.
- **[Provided]** Liver injury or specified liver/laboratory abnormalities, including CPK, platelet, eosinophil, and creatinine thresholds; consent withdrawal or study-level enrollment cessation.

**[Clarification Required]** Supply the reversibility alternatives; operational definitions of uncontrolled-asthma evidence and ICS dose categories; medication restrictions and timing; tuberculosis, pregnancy, and contraception provisions; and numerical laboratory thresholds. These are needed to apply eligibility consistently.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary efficacy rate is the annualized rate of severe exacerbations through Week 52. The powered populations are Type 2, eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, and eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L.

**[Clarification Required]** Define a severe exacerbation, event start and end, separation of recurrent events, and ascertainment after treatment discontinuation. These determine the event count and primary estimand.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** Sequential efficacy endpoints include Week-12 pre-BD FEV1, Week-24 ACQ-7, and Week-12 FeNO in the populations and order specified in Section 2.4.

**[Provided]** Secondary assessments cover safety and tolerability, PROs including health-related quality of life, systemic exposure, anti-drug antibodies, and vaccine immune responses. Exploratory assessments cover biomarkers, optional genetics, controller step-up, and additional PROs.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify endpoint scales, units, baseline and change definitions, vaccine-response measures and timing, exposure and antibody measures, and controller-step-up definitions. These are necessary to calculate and interpret the outcomes.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** Analyze severe-exacerbation counts using negative-binomial regression with a log link, assigned treatment and randomization stratification factors as covariates, and log observed follow-up in years as an offset; report the dupilumab/placebo rate ratio and 95% confidence interval. This matches the assumed count/rate outcome, provided follow-up and events are reliably ascertained and the model is adequate.

**[Proposed]** Prespecify a simplified model if sparse strata or nonconvergence prevent the planned fit; this supports a reproducible estimate, assuming simplification is decided without using unblinded treatment effects.

**[Proposed]** Analyze longitudinal continuous efficacy measures using a repeated-measures model with treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline value, and appropriate stratification factors; estimate treatment contrasts at the specified weeks. This uses available repeated observations, assuming suitable data and model assumptions.

**[Clarification Required]** Define the estimands, including handling of treatment discontinuation, controller step-up, rescue medication, death, and other intercurrent events; specify which observations contribute and the time at risk. These choices determine what each treatment comparison means.

**[Clarification Required]** Define pooled-country regions and their intended analyses separately from randomization regions. This prevents conflating two distinct regional classifications.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Summarize missing outcomes and follow-up by assigned treatment, visit, and reason; this identifies potential bias, assuming reasons are recorded.

**[Proposed]** Use the repeated-measures model as a primary continuous-outcome approach under a missing-at-random assumption, and perform prespecified sensitivity analyses under departures from that assumption; this assesses robustness to unverified missingness assumptions.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify imputation or other handling of missing biomarker eligibility, incomplete exacerbation follow-up, and missing outcomes after intercurrent events, including sensitivity-analysis assumptions. These decisions can materially change estimates and population membership.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Summarize treatment effects across prespecified biomarker and high-ICS populations and investigate biomarker–treatment interactions; this addresses response associations, assuming measurements are sufficiently complete. Treat analyses outside the hierarchy as exploratory.

**[Proposed]** Summarize optional genetic profiles, controller step-up, additional PROs, exposure, antibodies, and vaccine responses using methods appropriate to their measured scales; this addresses the stated objectives, assuming the necessary data and consent are available.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify genetic consent and analysis scope, subgroup definitions, and methods for exposure, immunogenicity, and vaccine-response associations. These determine feasible analyses and interpretation.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Proposed]** Summarize adverse events, serious adverse events, discontinuations due to adverse events, and relevant laboratory findings by treatment received, including follow-up where available; this supports safety assessment, assuming events and exposure dates are captured.

**[Clarification Required]** Define adverse-event coding, treatment-emergent risk windows, severity and relatedness rules, and post-lock safety reporting. These determine which harms enter each summary.
