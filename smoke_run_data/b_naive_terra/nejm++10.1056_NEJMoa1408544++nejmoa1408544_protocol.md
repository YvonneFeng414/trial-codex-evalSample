# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** This phase 4 trial evaluates 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (13vPnC) for prevention of vaccine-serotype pneumococcal community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) in older adults in the Netherlands. The final controlling protocol is Amendment 9, dated 01 October 2013.

### 1.2 Objectives

**[Provided]** **P1:** Demonstrate efficacy against a first episode of confirmed vaccine-type (VT) pneumococcal CAP.

**[Provided]** **S1:** Demonstrate efficacy against first confirmed nonbacteremic/noninvasive (NB/NI)-VT pneumococcal CAP and first VT-IPD.

**[Provided]** **S2:** Evaluate all confirmed VT-CAP; first confirmed pneumococcal, NB/NI pneumococcal, CAP, and IPD; first/all confirmed VT-CAP by individual 13vPnC serotype; first/all NVT and pneumococcal CAP; first/all NB/NI-VT-CAP and NB/NI-VT-CAP by individual serotype; first/all culture-confirmed VT, pneumococcal, and NVT CAP; and first/all probable VT, pneumococcal, NVT and possible pneumococcal CAP.

**[Provided]** **S3:** Evaluate all CAP; all VT-IPD, first/all NVT-IPD, all IPD; deaths from confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP/VT-IPD, pneumococcal CAP/IPD and all causes; hospitalization with CAP plus influenza A/B and specified respiratory viruses; age efficacy trends; and economic effect.

**[Provided]** **S4:** Evaluate VT, all, NVT and individual-serotype *S. pneumoniae* carriage and *H. influenzae*, *M. catarrhalis*, and *S. aureus* carriage at 6, 12 and 24 months. Safety objectives are SAE incidence, subset reactogenicity/AEs and deaths. Immunogenicity objectives are serotype IgG/OPA by age and sex at baseline and approximately months 1, 12 and 24.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** The trial is parallel-group, randomized, placebo-controlled and double-blind, conducted at multiple sites or regions in the Netherlands by the Julius Center. Allocation is 1:1 to 13vPnC or placebo.

**[Provided]** Participants receive one 0.5-mL intramuscular deltoid dose at Visit 1.

**[Provided]** 13vPnC contains serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F and 23F conjugated to CRM197: 2 micrograms each except 6B at 4 micrograms. Its formulation is pH 5.8, 5 mM succinate, 0.85% sodium chloride, 0.02% polysorbate 80 and 0.125 mg aluminum phosphate.

**[Provided]** Placebo contains pH 5.8, 5 mM succinate, 0.15 M sodium chloride, 0.02% polysorbate 80 and 0.125 mg aluminum per 0.5 mL.

**[Clarification Required]** Confirm diagnostic criteria, specimen and assay procedures, case adjudication, episode separation rules, and the case-acquisition stopping rule. These determine which events enter each endpoint and when surveillance ends.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** A sponsor/designee generates the randomization schedule. Eligible consented subjects receive the next available syringe, whose PAC identifies allocation. Vaccine groups are randomly ordered within blocks; several blocks are assigned at a time.

**[Provided]** Immunogenicity-subset age stratification uses 65–69, 70–79 and ≥80 years, with at least 300 participants in each. Trial-wide randomization stratification is not specified.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify block sizes, PAC concealment and emergency unblinding procedures, and whether any trial-wide stratification applies. These decisions affect allocation protection and analysis implementation.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** The planned total is approximately 85,000 participants, 42,500 per group. The immunogenicity subset is 2,000, 1,000 per group.

**[Provided]** The calculation outcome is first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP, a time-to-first confirmed clinical event analyzed as incidence rate/relative risk. Supplementary power statements address NB/NI-VT-CAP and VT-IPD.

**[Provided]** Assumptions include placebo CAP, confirmed pneumococcal CAP and confirmed VT-CAP rates of 10, 2.5 and 1.2 per 1,000/year, respectively; true VT-CAP efficacy of at least 52%; 5% loss to follow-up; and initially at least 97% test specificity. SSUAD was subsequently validated at 98% specificity, with specificity-adjusted efficacy of 48.4% in the revised model.

**[Provided]** The design uses two-sided 0.05 experiment-wise alpha, with 90% power for primary VT-CAP, at least 80% for NB/NI-VT-CAP under the stated assumptions, and at least 90% for VT-IPD if true efficacy is at least 90%. The VT-IPD power statement requires 20 events.

**[Provided]** The original primary event target was 156. Following DMC administrative review prompted by lower-than-predicted observed incidence, the targets were revised to 65 interim and 130 final first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events. The revised primary target uses a Breslow–Day conditional binomial derivation and exact conditional confidence interval analysis with O’Brien–Fleming adjustment.

**[Provided]** The target effect is vaccine efficacy above zero based on the lower confidence bound; the interim clinical-significance threshold is a VT-CAP lower bound above 0.20. The subset IgG precision is 0.10–0.16 for 95% CI differences.

**[Provided]** There is no non-inferiority or equivalence margin and no clustering design effect: this is an individually randomized efficacy/superiority trial. Calculation software is not specified.

**[Provided]** The assumptions reflect clinical feasibility, one enrollment season, at least two years’ follow-up, incidence and assay-specificity assumptions, DMC review and an external consultant-supported revised model. The total sample was selected to accrue the required primary events; the subset size was based on six-month safety regulatory requirements and feasibility.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the exact NB/NI power assumptions and the scale and interpretation of the IgG precision statement. They are needed to reproduce those calculations.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** Primary vaccine efficacy is \(1-\text{relative risk}\) for first confirmed VT-CAP. Success requires the adjusted two-sided 95% CI lower bound for efficacy to exceed zero.

**[Provided]** NB/NI-VT-CAP is hierarchically tested only following clinically significant primary efficacy. VT-IPD is evaluated once at study conclusion and is efficacious if its two-sided 95% CI lower bound exceeds zero.

**[Clarification Required]** Define clinically significant primary efficacy for purposes of the NB/NI gate, particularly at final analysis, and specify the NB/NI testing procedure and multiplicity allocation. The supplied 0.20 threshold is explicitly an interim threshold; the full hierarchy cannot otherwise be implemented unambiguously.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** The revised interim target is 65 first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events. The primary analysis uses an O’Brien–Fleming adjustment, and the interim clinical-significance threshold is a VT-CAP efficacy CI lower bound above 0.20.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the interim testing boundaries, information fraction, exact adjusted CI construction, decision authority and operational consequences of each possible result. These are necessary to implement the group-sequential procedure and distinguish statistical success from clinical significance.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** If efficacy is not declared at interim, final efficacy inference occurs after investigator sign-off of at least 130 per-protocol first confirmed VT-CAP events, cleaning, unblinding and database freeze. If efficacy is declared at interim, inference uses the interim dataset, with overrunning data supplementary.

**[Provided]** CAP/IPD surveillance runs through the case-acquisition stopping rule. Immunogenicity blood is collected at baseline and months 1, 12 and 24; carriage is assessed at baseline and months 6, 12 and 24. Safety includes 7-day reactogenicity and 28-day unsolicited AE windows; SAE assessment windows are not specified.

**[Clarification Required]** Define the case-acquisition stopping rule, database cutoffs, treatment of events crossing cutoffs, SAE assessment windows and exact day boundaries. These determine event inclusion and safety exposure periods.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** Experiment-wise alpha is two-sided 0.05. Primary inference uses an adjusted two-sided 95% CI; VT-IPD uses a two-sided 95% CI at study conclusion.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify how experiment-wise error control applies across the primary, NB/NI-VT-CAP and VT-IPD tests, including repeated looks and any dependence between tests. The stated individual rules do not fully specify the joint procedure.

**[Proposed]** Report effect estimates and confidence intervals alongside event counts and exposure; label exploratory results as descriptive rather than confirmatory. Rationale: this makes precision and multiplicity transparent; assumption: no additional confirmatory tests are designated.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Clarification Required]** Define important deviations and their consequences for each analysis population before unblinding. The supplied per-protocol event target does not define per-protocol exclusions.

**[Proposed]** Document deviations by type and treatment group, with blinded review of their population impact. Rationale: consistent classification limits bias; assumption: sufficient deviation records are available.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Provided]** The primary event targets refer to first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events.

**[Clarification Required]** Define the per-protocol population, its risk-period start and censoring rules, and the randomized, safety, carriage and immunogenicity analysis populations. These decisions affect denominators and interpretation.

**[Proposed]** Summarize efficacy additionally by randomized assignment and safety by treatment received. Rationale: these provide complementary effectiveness and exposure-based summaries; assumption: assignment and administration records are available.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Summarize numbers screened, eligible, randomized and vaccinated, with reasons for screening failure where recorded. Rationale: this describes participant flow; assumption: screening records support these counts.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Participants must be at least 65 years old at vaccination, registered with a referring GP and able to meet study requirements. Subset subjects must complete e-diary procedures.

**[Provided]** Exclusions are prior pneumococcal vaccination, institutional/semiskilled care, vaccine contraindication, recent investigational product, severe vaccine reaction, and defined immune deficiency/suppression. Subset subjects are excluded for influenza vaccination in the preceding 7 days.

**[Provided]** Acute febrile illness of at least 38.0°C or recent acute illness delays enrollment.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the operational definitions and time limits for the exclusions and illness-related delay. These are needed for consistent eligibility assessment.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** The trial is conducted across multiple sites or regions in the Netherlands; one enrollment season was part of the sample-size assumptions.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify recruitment procedures, enrollment dates and site-level targets if needed for operational implementation. They are not supplied.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Proposed]** Record withdrawal, loss to follow-up and reasons separately from discontinuation of study procedures, and summarize follow-up by group. Rationale: these distinctions support interpretation of missing outcomes; assumption: the relevant dates and reasons are recorded.

**[Clarification Required]** Define withdrawal procedures, permitted outcome follow-up after withdrawal, censoring dates and handling of death. These determine observable person-time and the estimand.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Proposed]** Summarize baseline characteristics, including age and sex, by randomized group and for relevant subsets. Rationale: this describes comparability and subset composition; assumption: these data are collected.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify other baseline variables and their definitions. The supplied input does not identify a complete baseline dataset.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is the first episode of confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the confirmed VT-CAP case definition, qualifying surveillance period and analysis risk period. These determine the primary event count and person-time.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** Secondary outcomes are first confirmed NB/NI-VT pneumococcal CAP and first VT-IPD. The S2–S4 exploratory outcomes are those listed in Section 1.2.

**[Clarification Required]** Define confirmed, probable, possible, culture-confirmed, VT and NVT classifications; the specified respiratory viruses; mortality attribution; and economic measures. These definitions are necessary to classify and analyze the exploratory outcomes consistently.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** For first-event efficacy, report events and observed person-time by group, estimate the incidence-rate ratio, and express efficacy as \(1-\text{rate ratio}\). Use the specified exact conditional CI framework and apply the prespecified interim adjustment to primary inference. Rationale: this matches the stated incidence-rate/relative-risk framework; assumption: the finalized risk periods and model support this analysis.

**[Proposed]** For an exact conditional binomial implementation, condition on the total event count and use a vaccine-arm event probability determined by the rate ratio and group person-times. Rationale: this operationalizes the stated conditional approach; assumption: an appropriate independent Poisson rate model and treatment of observed person-time are accepted.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the exact conditional estimator, treatment of event-dependent person-time, CI ordering and group-sequential calibration. The supplied description alone does not uniquely determine the analysis or guarantee its error properties.

**[Proposed]** Summarize recurrent outcomes with event counts and person-time and, where appropriate, a recurrent-event rate model; summarize carriage prevalence by visit and immunogenicity by serotype, age and sex. Rationale: these methods reflect the different outcome types; assumption: event histories, specimens and assay results are available.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify recurrent-event definitions, immunogenicity summaries and transformations, carriage comparisons, age-trend model, economic perspective and analytic methods. These choices materially affect the exploratory results.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Describe missingness and follow-up by group and outcome, and conduct sensitivity analyses for missing outcome ascertainment and incomplete subset measurements. Rationale: missingness can bias comparisons; assumption: available records permit assessment of missingness.

**[Clarification Required]** Define censoring, imputation or weighting rules and sensitivity-analysis assumptions, including how deaths and withdrawals affect each endpoint. Different choices target different effects and may change conclusions.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Present efficacy by prespecified age group and individual serotype where data permit, and report overrunning data separately when interim efficacy is declared. Rationale: these address exploratory objectives without replacing the controlling inference; assumption: subgroup and event classifications are reliable.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify subgroup boundaries beyond the immunogenicity age strata, interaction tests and any additional sensitivity analyses before unblinding. These choices affect interpretation and reproducibility.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Provided]** Safety objectives include SAE incidence, subset reactogenicity/AEs and deaths. Reactogenicity is assessed over 7 days and unsolicited AEs over 28 days.

**[Proposed]** Summarize participants with each safety outcome by treatment received, including severity and relatedness when recorded. Rationale: participant-level summaries describe the safety profile; assumption: safety records contain the relevant classifications.

**[Clarification Required]** Define SAE collection windows, severity and causality rules, and safety follow-up after withdrawal. These are necessary for comparable safety summaries.

### 5.7 Software and References

**[Provided]** Sample-size calculation software is not specified.

**[Proposed]** Record the software, versions and analysis programs used for the final analyses. Rationale: this supports reproducibility; assumption: these details will be retained.
