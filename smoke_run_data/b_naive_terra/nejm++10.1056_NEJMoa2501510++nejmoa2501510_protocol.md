# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** C3G and IC-MPGN are complement-driven glomerular diseases that can occur in native kidneys or recur after transplantation. Pegcetacoplan inhibits C3 and C3b to limit inappropriate complement activation and glomerular C3-deposit injury. Proteinuria reduction is the expected early renal signal.

### 1.2 Objectives

**[Provided]** The primary objective is to assess twice-weekly subcutaneous (SC) pegcetacoplan versus placebo in primary C3G/IC-MPGN based on proteinuria reduction.

**[Provided]** Secondary objectives are to assess eGFR and additional disease-related parameters and evaluate safety over 52 weeks of treatment.

**[Provided]** The exploratory objective is to characterize additional clinical, laboratory, and histologic findings in response to pegcetacoplan.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a phase 3, randomized, multicenter, placebo-controlled, double-blind study with a 10-week screening period, a 26-week randomized controlled period (RCP), a 26-week open-label period (OLP), and 8 weeks of follow-up for participants not entering the long-term extension.

**[Provided]** Participants are allocated 1:1 to SC infusion of pegcetacoplan or matching placebo twice weekly during the RCP. During the OLP, both groups receive open-label pegcetacoplan.

**[Provided]** Adults and adolescents weighing at least 50 kg receive 1080 mg/20 mL twice weekly. Adolescents weighing 35 to less than 50 kg receive 648 mg first, then 810 mg/15 mL twice weekly. Adolescents weighing 30 to less than 35 kg receive 540 mg for the first two doses, then 648 mg/12 mL twice weekly.

**[Provided]** Participants, sponsor, investigators, and site staff remain blinded to RCP allocation through completion of all week-26 assessments and RCP database lock. Central evaluations, including central pathology, are blinded. Only non-site drug-supply/logistics personnel are unblinded.

**[Clarification Required]** The emergency unblinding procedure and authorized access require specification because participant safety may require allocation disclosure while the stated RCP blinding must be protected.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** Randomization is centrally performed using a computer-generated schedule with stratified permuted blocks. Stratification is by posttransplant recurrence versus nontransplant, then by availability versus unavailability of a baseline renal biopsy from screening or historically within 28 weeks.

**[Clarification Required]** Block sizes and the handling of incorrect or ambiguous stratification data require specification because they affect allocation implementation and analysis.

**[Clarification Required]** Minimization is not specified; whether it has any role requires confirmation because the stated method is stratified permuted-block randomization.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** Approximately 80–100 randomized participants were planned, 40–50 per arm, including at least 78 native-kidney participants and at least 63 participants with native-kidney C3G. Final enrollment after closure was 124.

**[Provided]** The amendment 4 calculation used the week-26 log-transformed uPCR ratio to baseline. Assumed control and pegcetacoplan reductions were 20% and 60%, with mean log ratios of −0.22 and −0.92, respectively, and a log-scale SD of 0.88. Seventy participants before inflation were expected to provide at least 90% power at one-sided alpha 0.025. A 10% inflation allowed for missing assessments and COVID-19 impact.

**[Derived]** The assumed pegcetacoplan-minus-control difference in mean log ratios is −0.70.

**[Provided]** The amendment 4 calculation test and calculation software were not specified. Conditional-power re-estimation was not retained.

**[Provided]** The original protocol used a two-sample t-test and planned conditional-power re-estimation at approximately 50% completion, without type I error adjustment and capped at 120 participants. Software for that re-estimation was not specified.

**[Provided]** No event count, non-inferiority or equivalence margin, or clustering design effect applies: the design is individually randomized, non-clustered, and intended for superiority comparison with placebo.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** The primary null hypothesis is no between-group difference in the week-26 log-transformed uPCR ratio to baseline; the alternative is a difference.

**[Provided]** Separate comparative hypotheses test no difference versus a difference in composite renal response, ≥50% uPCR response, eGFR change, histologic activity-score change, C3c response, proteinuria below 1 g/day, albumin normalization, C3 normalization, FACIT-Fatigue change, and KDQOL change.

**[Provided]** Primary, key secondary, and additional secondary endpoints are tested in a fixed sequence to control trial-wise type I error.

**[Clarification Required]** The exact sequence, designation of each endpoint as key or additional, and rules for continuing testing require specification; without them, multiplicity-controlled secondary conclusions cannot be determined.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** The original conditional-power re-estimation plan was not retained in amendment 4.

**[Clarification Required]** Whether any other interim efficacy, futility, or safety analysis is planned requires confirmation because its timing and access to unblinded data affect error control and blinding.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** The formal statistical analysis plan (SAP) is finalized before database lock. RCP analysis follows completion or discontinuation of all participants; entry, review, cleaning, and finalization of corresponding data; week-26 database lock; and unblinding.

**[Provided]** Primary and secondary endpoints are assessed at week 26 and evaluated at week 52 as exploratory. Primary uPCR uses an equal-weighted average over weeks 24, 25, and 26. Twenty-four-hour urine collections occur at weeks 24 and 48.

**[Clarification Required]** The timing of the week-52 database lock and analysis requires specification to ensure that exploratory and safety data are sufficiently complete.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The amendment 4 sample-size calculation used one-sided alpha 0.025.

**[Proposed]** Use two-sided 5% tests and 95% confidence intervals for the stated two-sided hypotheses. Rationale: this matches the planning alpha across two tails; assumption: the final SAP adopts two-sided testing. Interpret a primary difference as favorable only when uPCR is lower with pegcetacoplan.

**[Clarification Required]** The fixed-sequence stopping rule, including how to proceed after a statistically significant difference favoring placebo, requires specification because the alternative is two-sided but the clinical objective concerns reduction.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Record deviations, classify important deviations before unblinding, and summarize them by randomized group. Rationale: this supports assessment of trial conduct; assumption: an agreed deviation classification is available.

**[Clarification Required]** Criteria for important deviations and their effect on any supportive population require specification to avoid outcome-dependent exclusions.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Proposed]** Define the full analysis set as all randomized participants, analyzed according to assigned treatment. Rationale: this preserves the randomized comparison; assumption: randomization records identify all assigned participants.

**[Proposed]** Define the safety population as participants receiving at least one dose of study drug, summarized by actual treatment received within each period. Rationale: safety should reflect exposure; assumption: dosing records are adequate.

**[Clarification Required]** Whether a per-protocol population is required, and its exclusion rules, require specification because such a population could change supportive efficacy estimates.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Summarize numbers screened, enrolled, randomized, and not randomized, with recorded reasons for screen failure. Rationale: this describes selection into the study; assumption: screening records are retained.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Inclusion requires primary C3G/IC-MPGN, active renal disease, proteinuria at least 1 g/day, first-morning urine (FMU) uPCR at least 1000 mg/g, eGFR at least 30 mL/min/1.73 m², a stable renal regimen, required vaccination status, reproductive safeguards, and capacity to consent or assent and administer drug, or caregiver support. Adults are eligible; where approved, adolescents aged 12–17 years weighing at least 30 kg are eligible.

**[Provided]** Exclusions include prior pegcetacoplan, improving disease, relevant secondary disease, infection or malignancy, prohibited complement therapies, pregnancy or breastfeeding, severe infection, and hereditary fructose intolerance.

**[Clarification Required]** Operational definitions and required documentation for active or improving disease, stability of renal regimen, vaccination, reproductive safeguards, relevant secondary conditions, prohibited therapies, and infection severity are needed for consistent eligibility decisions.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** Recruitment is multicenter, with planned native-kidney and native-kidney C3G minimums as stated in Section 2.3.

**[Clarification Required]** Participating locations, recruitment procedures, and how the minimum subgroup targets were monitored require specification for implementation.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Provided]** Participants not entering the long-term extension have an 8-week follow-up period.

**[Proposed]** Distinguish discontinuation of study drug from withdrawal from data collection and document reasons and dates. Seek outcome and safety follow-up after drug discontinuation when permitted. Rationale: this limits missing data; assumption: participant consent and applicable requirements permit collection.

**[Clarification Required]** Follow-up procedures and timing after early discontinuation, withdrawal, or death require specification because they determine available endpoint and safety data.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Proposed]** Summarize demographic, disease, renal-function, proteinuria, biopsy-availability, transplant-status, and relevant treatment characteristics by randomized group. Rationale: this describes the study population; assumption: the corresponding data are collected.

**[Clarification Required]** The baseline assessment window and rules for selecting among multiple measurements require specification because baseline values enter eligibility, change scores, and adjusted analyses.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is proteinuria reduction with pegcetacoplan versus placebo, assessed using the week-26 log-transformed uPCR ratio to baseline and an equal-weighted average over weeks 24, 25, and 26.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify whether averaging occurs before or after log transformation, the urine specimen and baseline value used, and handling of partially observed weeks. These choices can produce different primary values and estimates.

**[Proposed]** Subject to that clarification, define the treatment contrast as pegcetacoplan minus placebo in the mean of the three weekly log ratios. Exponentiate the contrast to report the ratio of geometric uPCR ratios, with its confidence interval. Rationale: this gives an interpretable relative comparison; assumption: the intended endpoint averages weekly log ratios.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** Secondary outcomes include eGFR, additional disease-related parameters, and safety over 52 weeks. The comparative secondary hypotheses listed in Section 2.4 include renal, histologic, laboratory, fatigue, and quality-of-life outcomes.

**[Provided]** Additional clinical, laboratory, and histologic findings are exploratory. Primary and secondary endpoints evaluated at week 52 are also exploratory.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify component definitions and thresholds for the composite and other responses, normalization, histologic activity, and patient-reported outcomes; also specify assessment windows and derivation rules. These are necessary to reproduce the secondary analyses.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** If the primary endpoint averages weekly log ratios, fit a repeated-measures model to weeks 24, 25, and 26 log ratios with treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline log uPCR, and the randomization stratification factors; estimate the equal-weighted average of the three treatment contrasts. Rationale: this uses available repeated measurements and respects equal weighting; assumption: the model and endpoint scale are appropriate.

**[Proposed]** Use an unstructured within-participant covariance where estimable, with a prespecified simpler covariance fallback if it fails. Rationale: this accommodates correlation while allowing model fitting; assumption: the SAP specifies the fallback before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** Analyze continuous secondary outcomes with adjusted treatment contrasts and binary responses with adjusted group comparisons, reporting estimates and confidence intervals. Rationale: methods should match outcome type; assumption: endpoint definitions and sufficient data are available.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the primary estimand’s handling of death, kidney replacement, transplantation, and other events that may make uPCR undefined. These decisions affect the clinical question and cannot be resolved by ordinary missing-data imputation alone.

**[Proposed]** For drug discontinuation or use of additional treatment, retain subsequently observed outcomes in the primary comparison where meaningful. Rationale: this supports a treatment-policy comparison; assumption: the final estimand adopts that strategy and outcomes remain defined.

**[Derived]** Because both randomized groups receive pegcetacoplan during the OLP, a week-52 comparison by original assignment is not a comparison of pegcetacoplan with concurrent placebo.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Use the repeated-measures primary model for missing postbaseline values under a missing-at-random assumption, and collect reasons for missingness. Rationale: it can use incomplete observed trajectories; assumption: missingness is adequately explained by included observed data.

**[Proposed]** Prespecify multiple imputation for missing baseline uPCR and sensitivity analyses for departures from missing at random, including unfavorable shifts for missing pegcetacoplan outcomes. Rationale: results may depend on untestable missing-data assumptions; assumption: sufficient observed data support the imputation models.

**[Clarification Required]** Imputation variables, shift values, and handling of outcomes undefined after intercurrent events require specification before analysis because they can materially change inference.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Summarize efficacy by randomized group within transplant-status, biopsy-availability, and disease subgroups, and examine treatment interactions descriptively. Rationale: these analyses assess consistency; assumption: subgroup data are available and estimates may be imprecise.

**[Proposed]** Describe week-52 outcomes by original randomized group and treatment sequence without claiming a concurrent placebo-controlled effect. Rationale: both groups receive active treatment in the OLP; assumption: week-52 data are available.

**[Clarification Required]** Define any additional prespecified subgroups and supportive analyses, including how week-48 24-hour urine results contribute to week-52 evaluation, because their interpretation and timing are not otherwise determined.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Proposed]** Summarize adverse events, serious adverse events, deaths, discontinuations due to adverse events, and clinically relevant laboratory abnormalities by period and actual treatment received, with participant counts and exposure. Rationale: this describes safety across different exposure durations; assumption: events, exposure, and laboratory data are collected.

**[Clarification Required]** Safety collection windows, event coding, severity and causality rules, and adverse events of special interest require specification for consistent reporting.

### 5.7 Software and References

**[Clarification Required]** Specify and validate the statistical software and versions before analysis because reproducibility depends on the implementation.
