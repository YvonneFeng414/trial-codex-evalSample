# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

[Provided] This phase IIb primary-prevention trial tests whether sustained oral insulin exposure during the usual period of initiation of beta-cell autoimmunity induces immune tolerance and reduces beta-cell autoimmunity and diabetes in genetically at-risk infants.

### 1.2 Objectives

[Provided] **Primary:** Determine whether daily oral insulin from age 4–7 months to age 3 years reduces the cumulative incidence of beta-cell autoantibodies and diabetes in childhood.

[Provided] **Secondary:** Compare development of any persistent confirmed beta-cell autoantibody, persistent confirmed IAA, persistent confirmed GADA, and dysglycaemia or diabetes.

[Provided] **Exploratory:** Assess treatment effects in subgroups and in longitudinal immunologic, transcriptomic, metabolic, and viral-antibody measures; assess type 1 diabetes and progression from seroconversion to clinical type 1 diabetes.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

[Provided] The study is investigator-initiated, multicentre, multinational, randomized, double-blind, and placebo-controlled. Allocation is 1:1 to oral insulin or placebo.

[Provided] Parents administer one oral capsule daily, preferably between 07:00 and 10:00. Treatment ends the day before the age-3 visit or upon clinical diabetes.

[Provided] Oral insulin is given at 7.5 mg for 2 months, 22.5 mg for the next 2 months, and 67.5 mg thereafter until age 3 years. Capsules contain human recombinant insulin crystals and microcrystalline cellulose to a total weight of 200 mg in a 0.5-mL hard gelatin capsule.

[Provided] Placebo is a matching microcrystalline-cellulose capsule administered daily until age 3 years.

[Clarification Required] Procedures for treatment interruption, resumption, adherence assessment, and emergency unblinding require specification because they affect participant management and interpretation of exposure.

### 2.2 Randomization

[Provided] The study-drug manufacturer performs package-number randomization. A separate randomization list assigns multiples to the same arm.

[Clarification Required] Stratification, blocking, minimization, allocation concealment procedures, and the unit and mechanics of assignment for multiples are not specified. They matter for reproducibility and valid inference.

### 2.3 Sample Size

[Provided] The original design projected 1,040 randomized infants; completed enrollment was 1,050, allocated 1:1.

[Provided] The original calculation concerned time to persistent confirmed multiple beta-cell autoantibodies or diabetes. It assumed an exponential distribution, a placebo event probability of 7.5% at 3.5 years and hazard of 0.02227 per year, and a halved treatment hazard of 0.011135 per year.

[Provided] The target was a 50% risk/hazard-rate reduction, with 80% power and a two-sided overall significance level of 0.05 for a log-rank test of equal hazard rates.

[Derived] A 50% reduction in the exponential hazard does not imply exactly a 50% reduction in cumulative risk at a fixed time.

[Provided] The calculation required 832 children with complete follow-up, assuming uniform randomization over 3.5 years and another 3.5 years of follow-up. A 20% dropout allowance yielded 1,040 randomized children. All randomized children, including those observed until dropout, contribute to the primary analysis.

[Provided] An October 2021 blinded update used observed dropout, accrual, and event-rate information. It used a placebo hazard of 0.02456 per year and a halved treatment-hazard scenario of 0.01228 per year, projecting 81.3% power under the observed total hazard and halved treatment hazard.

[Provided] The update extrapolated 12% dropout, with a 95% confidence interval of 8–15%; used completed accrual of 3.17 years and 3.25 years of follow-up; and used PROC POWER in SAS 9.4 based on the Lakatos formula. No power issue was judged to prevent trial end six months after last treatment.

[Provided] The required number of events is not specified. A non-inferiority or equivalence margin is not applicable to the superiority framework. The sample-size information states that design effect or clustering is not applicable to this individually randomized trial.

[Clarification Required] The implications of assigning multiples to the same arm for the primary test and variance require resolution: correlated outcomes or dependent assignment could affect inference despite the stated sample-size assumption.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

[Provided] Primary and secondary endpoints use a superiority framework. The primary confirmatory comparison is oral insulin versus placebo for persistent confirmed multiple beta-cell autoantibodies or diabetes; the primary null compares equal hazard rates.

[Proposed] Assuming no efficacy interim testing changes the significance threshold, use a two-sided test at 0.05 and conclude superiority only if the test is significant and the estimated effect favors insulin. This preserves the stated two-sided framework while requiring benefit for a superiority claim.

[Clarification Required] The relationship between the broader primary objective’s cumulative incidence of beta-cell autoantibodies and diabetes in childhood and the confirmatory composite requires specification, including the intended reporting horizon. This determines which outcome and period support the primary conclusion.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

[Provided] The October 2021 sample-size update was blinded.

[Clarification Required] Whether any unblinded efficacy interim analysis occurred or is planned, and any associated stopping boundaries or alpha expenditure, is not specified. This matters for the final confirmatory significance threshold.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

[Provided] The observation period ends six months after the last participant’s last treatment day; this was expected in June 2024. Final analysis is after that period, final data export, and quality control. Database lock was October 23, 2024.

[Provided] The schedule includes eight intervention visits from baseline through age 3 years and up to nine post-age-3 follow-up visits. Blood for autoantibodies is collected at every visit. Persistent-autoantibody-positive children undergo OGTTs from age 3 years at six-month intervals.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

[Provided] Inclusion requires age 4–7 months, greater than 10% risk of developing multiple beta-cell autoantibodies by age 6 years, introduction of solid food, and parental written consent.

[Provided] Without a first-degree type 1 diabetes history, inclusion requires DR3/DR4-DQ8 or DR4-DQ8/DR4-DQ8 plus a genetic-risk score greater than 14.4.

[Provided] With a first-degree type 1 diabetes history, inclusion requires HLA DR4 and DQ8 without DRB1\*1501 or DQB1\*0503.

[Provided] Exclusions are interfering disease or treatment, a condition associated with poor compliance, an unsafe medical condition, diabetes at recruitment, or participation in another clinical trial.

[Clarification Required] Operational definitions for the clinical exclusions and genetic-risk assessment require specification because they determine consistent eligibility decisions.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

[Proposed] Assuming randomization date is available and endpoint dates are defined, measure time from randomization to the first qualifying primary composite event and compare assigned groups using a two-sided log-rank test. This follows the original time-to-event testing framework.

[Proposed] Assuming proportional hazards is a reasonable summary, estimate the insulin-to-placebo hazard ratio with a Cox model and a 95% confidence interval; inspect proportional-hazards departures. This quantifies the treatment contrast while checking model suitability.

[Proposed] Assuming censoring and competing-event handling are appropriately specified, report group-specific cumulative event estimates at prespecified times and numbers at risk. This describes absolute outcomes alongside the hazard ratio.

[Proposed] Assuming family identifiers and sufficient data are available, use a family-cluster-robust Cox analysis as a sensitivity analysis. This assesses the effect of within-family outcome correlation.

[Clarification Required] The primary test’s calibration under the assignment of multiples to the same arm, and whether a cluster-adjusted or design-based primary test is required, must be resolved before confirmatory inference.

[Proposed] Assuming secondary event definitions and sufficient events, analyze secondary time-to-event outcomes descriptively using analogous group-specific estimates and hazard ratios. This provides consistent comparisons without asserting confirmatory significance.

[Proposed] Assuming longitudinal measures and visit times are available, analyze suitable continuous repeated measures with models accounting for within-child correlation and treatment-by-time differences. This uses longitudinal information while allowing measurement patterns to differ.

[Proposed] Assuming seroconversion dates are defined, describe progression from seroconversion to clinical type 1 diabetes among children who seroconvert. Interpret between-group comparisons cautiously because seroconversion is a post-randomization selection event.

### 5.4 Missing Data

[Proposed] Assuming follow-up dates are available, summarize missing visits, dropout, and reasons by group and censor event-free children at their last adequate outcome assessment. This uses observed time while making the censoring rule explicit.
