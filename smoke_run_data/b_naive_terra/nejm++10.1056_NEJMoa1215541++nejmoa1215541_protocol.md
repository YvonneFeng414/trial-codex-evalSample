# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

[Provided] This Phase III study evaluates once-daily dolutegravir (DTG; GSK1349572) plus abacavir/lamivudine (ABC/3TC) versus efavirenz/emtricitabine/tenofovir (Atripla) in ART-naïve adults with HIV-1. It aims to establish non-inferior antiviral activity and characterize longer-term safety, tolerability, resistance, and health outcomes. DTG 50 mg once daily was selected from Phase 2b dose-ranging data.

### 1.2 Objectives

[Provided] **Primary:** Demonstrate antiviral activity of DTG plus ABC/3TC fixed-dose combination once daily versus Atripla over 48 weeks.

[Provided] **Secondary:** Demonstrate antiviral activity through Week 96; evaluate antiviral activity through Week 144; compare tolerability, long-term safety, antiviral and immunologic activity over time; and assess resistance in subjects experiencing virologic failure.

[Provided] **Secondary:** Assess change in symptom bother count; evaluate incidence of HIV-associated conditions over time; explore response by gender, race and/or HIV-1 subtype; and explore change in utility and health-related quality of life.

[Provided] No exploratory objectives are reported. All stated objectives are designated primary or secondary; exploratory work is described as analyses.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

[Provided] The study is Phase III, multicentre, randomized, stratified, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled, parallel-group, and fully powered for non-inferiority. Approximately 800 subjects will be randomized 1:1, approximately 400 per arm.

[Provided] The double-blind randomized phase runs from Day 1 through Week 96. Amendment 05 provides an open-label randomized phase through Week 144 and a DTG continuation phase thereafter.

[Provided] Treatment is once daily, preferably fasting/at bedtime:

- [Provided] **DTG plus ABC/3TC:** DTG 50 mg tablet, ABC/3TC 600/300 mg tablet, and Atripla placebo.
- [Provided] **Atripla:** EFV/FTC/TDF 600/200/300 mg tablet, plus DTG and ABC/3TC placebos.

[Clarification Required] The continuation-phase eligibility, treatment procedures, duration, and follow-up need specification because they determine exposure and interpretation after Week 144.

### 2.2 Randomization

[Provided] After confirmation of entry criteria, subjects are centrally randomized using a computer-generated schedule created with validated GSK RANDALL/RandAll software. A fixed block size is used and remains confidential. Minimization is not specified.

[Provided] Randomization is stratified by screening plasma HIV-1 RNA ≤100,000 versus >100,000 c/mL and screening CD4+ count ≤200 versus >200 cells/mm³, producing four strata.

[Proposed] Retain randomized treatment and recorded randomization strata in the analysis data. This supports treatment comparisons by assigned arm and adjustment for stratification; it assumes these records are retrievable.

### 2.3 Sample Size

[Provided] Approximately 800 subjects will be randomized, with 394 evaluable subjects required per arm. The calculation uses the proportion with plasma HIV-1 RNA <50 c/mL at Week 48.

[Provided] Planning assumes a 75% response in the Atripla arm and the same response in the DTG arm, a −10 percentage-point non-inferiority margin for DTG minus Atripla, one-sided alpha 2.5%, and 90% power. Assessment assumes comparison of the lower bound of a two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) with −10%.

[Provided] The calculation is binomial; no separate variance assumption, required event count, numerical dropout/non-adherence inflation, or calculation software is specified. It is not an event-driven or cluster design. No sample-size re-estimation will be performed.

[Provided] The 75% planning response incorporates historical approximately 15% drug-related and 14% non-drug-related failure context. The assumptions draw on historical Atripla-controlled and GSK trials, including an approximate control response of 71%, a recent EFV-arm range of 71%–82%, and anticipated better non-drug compliance.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

[Provided] For response proportions \(p_{\mathrm{DTG}}\) and \(p_{\mathrm{Atripla}}\), the hypotheses are \(H_0:p_{\mathrm{DTG}}-p_{\mathrm{Atripla}}\leq-10\%\) versus \(H_1:p_{\mathrm{DTG}}-p_{\mathrm{Atripla}}>-10\%\). Non-inferiority requires the lower two-sided 95% CI bound to exceed −10%.

[Provided] If both ITT-E and per-protocol (PP) analyses demonstrate non-inferiority, ITT-E superiority is tested two-sided at 5% and declared if the lower 95% CI bound exceeds zero.

[Provided] Conditional on non-inferiority, key secondary superiority tests use a generalized Wilcoxon test for time to suppression, a repeated-measures mixed model for Week-48 CD4, and ANCOVA for Week-4 SDM bother, in a specified fallback sequence.

[Clarification Required] The actual fallback order, alpha allocation, and any relationship between the primary superiority test and secondary tests are not stated. These decisions are necessary before confirmatory secondary conclusions can be made.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

[Clarification Required] Whether any interim efficacy or futility analyses, stopping rules, or independent monitoring reviews are planned is not stated. This matters for trial conduct and any adjustment to confirmatory error rates.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

[Provided] The final analysis occurs once all subjects have completed or discontinued; Week 48 is primary. RNA assessments in the final amended protocol occur at screening, Day 1, Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40 and 48, and every 12 weeks thereafter through Week 144. Actual dates allocate analysis windows.

[Provided] The complete assessment schedules for CD4/CD8, SDM, EQ-5D, and safety are not specified. Week-48 CD4 and Week-4 SDM bother are identified for key secondary tests.

[Clarification Required] The completion milestone for the final analysis, timing of the Week-48 primary analysis relative to later phases, cutoff dates, database locks, and data-readiness criteria need specification. These determine which observations are available for each analysis.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

[Provided] The primary non-inferiority assessment uses a two-sided 95% CI and a −10 percentage-point margin; conditional ITT-E superiority uses a two-sided 5% test.

[Proposed] Present treatment differences as DTG minus Atripla, with 95% CIs, and report nominal p-values for analyses outside a confirmed testing hierarchy. This gives a consistent direction of effect; it assumes no additional multiplicity procedure is specified.

[Clarification Required] The multiplicity procedure for the fallback tests and other secondary outcomes must be finalized because nominal significance alone may not support confirmatory claims.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

[Proposed] Document deviations and classify major deviations before unblinding the relevant analysis. This reduces data-driven population selection; it assumes adequate deviation records.

[Clarification Required] Major-deviation definitions and their effect on PP inclusion are not supplied. They matter because PP non-inferiority is required for the superiority sequence.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

[Provided] ITT-E and PP analyses are required in the non-inferiority and superiority decision sequence.

[Clarification Required] ITT-E, PP, safety, and any other analysis-population definitions are not supplied. In particular, treatment exposure requirements, exclusions, treatment assignment for analysis, and handling of major deviations must be decided to make the analyses reproducible.

[Proposed] Summarize disposition and population membership by randomized arm, including reasons for exclusion. This makes analysis selection transparent; it assumes reasons are recorded.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

[Provided] One re-screen with a new number and one repeat test per analyte/assessment are permitted. Genotype retesting is not permitted.

[Proposed] Summarize numbers screened, re-screened, eligible, and randomized, and reasons for screening failure where available. This describes selection into the trial; it assumes screening records permit these counts.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

[Provided] Inclusion requires ability to understand and comply, expected completion as planned, suitability for oral investigational therapy, HIV-1 infection, age ≥18 years, screening HIV-1 RNA ≥1,000 c/mL, ART-naïve status defined as ≤10 prior ART days after diagnosis, HLA-B*5701 negativity, and dated written informed consent.

[Provided] Women must be non-child-bearing—post-menopausal with 12 months’ amenorrhea and age ≥45 years, or documented sterilization—or have negative screening and Day-1 pregnancy tests and consistently use protocol-specified contraception from two weeks before investigational product (IP) through two weeks after it. Permitted methods include abstinence, double barrier, a qualifying IUD or other method with <1% failure, or hormonal contraception plus a barrier method.

[Provided] Enrollment in France additionally requires social-security affiliation or beneficiary status.

[Provided] Exclusions include breastfeeding; active CDC Category C disease except cutaneous Kaposi not requiring systemic therapy; hepatic impairment; HBsAg positivity or anticipated HCV therapy; recent GI bleed; and allergy or intolerance to study drugs or class. Historical or current CD4 <200 cells/mm³ is not exclusionary.

[Provided] Exclusions also include recent or ongoing malignancy except specified skin/Kaposi exceptions; recent HIV immunotherapeutic vaccine, radiation, cytotoxic chemotherapy, immunomodulator, HIV-active agent, or experimental drug/vaccine, including a France-specific restriction; and primary resistance.

[Provided] Further exclusions are a verified Grade-4/acute disqualifying laboratory abnormality, ALT/bilirubin thresholds whose numerical values are not specified, and creatinine clearance <50 mL/min by Cockcroft–Gault.

[Clarification Required] Numerical ALT/bilirubin thresholds, relevant “recent” intervals, the precise malignancy and France-specific exceptions, disqualifying laboratory criteria, and the contraception end reference require specification for consistent eligibility decisions.

### 4.3 Recruitment

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

[Proposed] Record treatment discontinuation and study withdrawal separately, with dates and reasons, and retain available subsequent assessments. This distinguishes treatment exposure from outcome follow-up; it assumes continued assessment is permitted and consented.

[Clarification Required] Withdrawal procedures, post-discontinuation assessment requirements, and permitted follow-up after consent withdrawal are not specified. These affect outcome ascertainment and missing-data interpretation.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

[Provided] The primary objective concerns antiviral activity over 48 weeks; the sample-size outcome is the proportion with plasma HIV-1 RNA <50 c/mL at Week 48.

[Clarification Required] The operational Week-48 response algorithm, visit window, choice among multiple RNA results, and treatment of discontinuation, regimen changes, intercurrent events, and missing RNA are not specified. These decisions define the estimand and can materially affect the non-inferiority conclusion.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

[Provided] Secondary outcomes are antiviral activity through Weeks 96 and 144; tolerability, long-term safety, antiviral and immunologic activity over time; resistance in subjects with virologic failure; change in symptom bother count; incidence of HIV-associated conditions over time; response by gender, race and/or HIV-1 subtype; and change in utility and health-related quality of life.

[Clarification Required] Virologic failure and suppression definitions, resistance assessments, HIV-associated condition definitions, instruments and scoring, and the precise CD4 and SDM analysis variables are not fully specified. These are needed to define denominators, endpoints, and comparisons.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

[Proposed] For each analysis population, estimate the Week-48 response-rate difference across the four randomization strata, standardized to pooled randomized-stratum proportions in ITT-E, and calculate a two-sided 95% CI using an unpooled stratified variance. This accounts for randomization strata and provides a common comparison; it assumes response and population rules are finalized and each treatment is represented in each stratum.

[Proposed] Use the corresponding lower CI bounds for the supplied non-inferiority and conditional superiority decisions. This aligns estimation and testing; it assumes the proposed CI method is accepted before analysis.

[Clarification Required] A prespecified method for empty or sparse strata and the definitive CI method are needed because they can affect the primary decision.

[Provided] The key secondary methods are generalized Wilcoxon for time to suppression, repeated-measures mixed model for Week-48 CD4, and ANCOVA for Week-4 SDM bother.

[Clarification Required] Time origin, suppression and censoring rules, model covariates, repeated-measures covariance structure, SDM outcome definition, and fallback testing details are not stated. These choices affect estimates, tests, and confirmatory interpretation.

[Proposed] Summarize other binary outcomes by arm and visit and continuous outcomes using observed values and changes from baseline where applicable. This describes longitudinal outcomes without asserting unprovided confirmatory methods; it assumes endpoint and baseline definitions are finalized.

[Proposed] Summarize resistance among subjects meeting the finalized virologic-failure definition, showing both failures assessed and results available. This makes selection into resistance testing visible; it assumes test availability is recorded.

### 5.4 Missing Data

[Clarification Required] Primary and secondary missing-data rules, including how missing RNA interacts with discontinuation and other intercurrent events, are not supplied. These rules are necessary to define response rates and assess bias.

[Proposed] After the primary estimand and response algorithm are specified, perform sensitivity analyses under alternative defensible assumptions about missing outcomes. This assesses robustness; it assumes sufficient information is available to characterize missingness.

[Proposed] Report missingness and reasons by arm, outcome, and visit where available. This supports interpretation of sensitivity analyses; it assumes reasons are recorded.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

[Proposed] Explore treatment effects within gender, race, and HIV-1 subtype categories and assess treatment-by-subgroup interaction where data permit. This addresses the stated subgroup objective; it assumes subgroup data and adequate sample sizes, and results should be interpreted as exploratory.

[Proposed] Compare longer-term outcomes by randomized arm through Week 144 and describe continuation-phase outcomes separately. This preserves the randomized comparison while distinguishing later follow-up; it assumes phase and treatment dates are available.

### 5.6 Harms

[Proposed] Summarize adverse events, serious adverse events, discontinuations due to adverse events, and available laboratory abnormalities by treatment and phase. This supports the safety and tolerability objective; it assumes these events and laboratory results are collected.

[Clarification Required] Safety assessment schedules, event collection periods, grading rules, coding conventions, and the safety population are not specified. These determine comparable denominators and harm summaries.

[Clarification Required] Analysis software and version are not specified. They are needed to reproduce the statistical analyses.
