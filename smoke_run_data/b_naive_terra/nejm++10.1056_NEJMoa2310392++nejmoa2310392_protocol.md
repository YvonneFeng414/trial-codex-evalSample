# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** The trial evaluates whether intravenous tenecteplase improves clinical outcomes versus placebo in imaging-selected acute ischemic stroke presenting 4.5–24 hours after last-known-well time. The rationale is earlier reperfusion in patients with salvageable tissue and ICA or MCA occlusion, including patients needing transfer for endovascular therapy (EVT).

### 1.2 Objectives

**[Provided]** The primary objective is to compare tenecteplase with placebo using ordinal Day-90 modified Rankin Scale (mRS).

**[Provided]** Secondary objectives are to evaluate Day-90 functional independence, angiographic reperfusion, NIHSS, Barthel Index, Glasgow Outcome Scale, 24-hour reperfusion and recanalization, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), adverse events, mortality, and PH2.

**[Provided]** Exploratory objectives are to evaluate final infarct volume and growth, mRS subgroups, outcomes when thrombectomy is not performed, 30-day readmission, one-pass EVT, Neuro-QoL, and Day-90 survival.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a phase III, prospective, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial in imaging-eligible patients with acute ischemic stroke and ICA or MCA M1/M2 occlusion. Allocation is 1:1.

**[Provided]** Both groups receive guideline-based standard care. ICA/M1 patients may receive adjuvant EVT if guideline criteria are met.

**[Provided]** Tenecteplase is administered as a single intravenous bolus of 0.25 mg/kg, maximum 25 mg, over 5 seconds. Placebo is administered as a single bolus injection over 5 seconds.

**[Proposed]** Use procedures that conceal treatment assignment from patients, treating teams, outcome assessors, and analysts until the appropriate unblinding point; document any emergency unblinding. Rationale: this protects the double-blind comparison. Assumption: treatment preparation can be operationally masked.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify whether the 4.5–24-hour interval applies to presentation alone or also to randomization or administration. This determines eligibility and treatment timing.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** An interactive web response system (IWRS) randomizes eligible, consented patients and assigns a treatment kit. Treatment is given as soon as possible.

**[Provided]** Permuted blocks provide approximately 1:1 balance within strata defined by age ≤70 versus >70 years, ICA/M1 versus M2 occlusion, baseline NIHSS ≤15 versus >15, and ECC versus nECC.

**[Clarification Required]** Define ECC and nECC and their classification procedures. This is necessary to implement stratification and reproduce the analysis.

**[Proposed]** Retain the randomization-recorded strata for the primary analysis and reconcile discrepancies against source data before unblinding. Rationale: this preserves the implemented allocation structure. Assumption: IWRS strata are retrievable.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** Planned enrollment is 456 patients, approximately 228 per group, with 432 evaluable after 5% dropout. The calculation uses seven-level Day-90 mRS and a two-sided significance level of 0.049 after adjustment for one efficacy interim analysis.

**[Provided]** The assumed control-arm mRS 0–6 distribution is 13%, 13%, 12%, 14%, 18%, 12%, and 18%; the corresponding treatment-arm distribution is 17%, 20%, 16%, 15%, 13%, 7%, and 12%.

**[Provided]** The target common odds ratio is at least 1.76 for tenecteplase versus placebo. The Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney test was assumed for the calculation; planned power is at least 90%. Alternative historical scenarios gave >99% and >88% power. The distributions and alternative scenarios were based on prior stroke studies.

**[Provided]** The calculation used EAST 6.4.1. The design is individually randomized, not event-driven or clustered, and is not a non-inferiority or equivalence design. Sample-size re-estimation or amendments are not specified.

**[Derived]** Applying 5% dropout directly to 456 gives 433.2 expected evaluable patients; 432 evaluable patients represent approximately 94.7% of enrollment.

**[Clarification Required]** Reconcile the stated 432 evaluable patients with the nominal 5% dropout assumption. This matters for reproducing the calculation.

**[Derived]** The supplied distributions do not yield exactly the same cumulative odds ratio at every mRS cutpoint; for example, the odds ratios for mRS 0 versus 1–6 and mRS 0–1 versus 2–6 are approximately 1.37 and 1.67, respectively.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify how the target common odds ratio and assumed distributions were combined in the sample-size calculation, and whether any re-estimation is planned. This matters for reproducibility and trial operating characteristics.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** All efficacy endpoints are assessed for superiority. For the primary endpoint, the null hypothesis is a common odds ratio of 1 and the two-sided alternative is a common odds ratio not equal to 1. The odds ratio describes the odds of lower, better mRS for tenecteplase versus placebo.

**[Derived]** A two-sided primary test can detect a difference in either direction; an odds ratio greater than 1 favors tenecteplase under the stated orientation.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** One efficacy interim analysis is reflected in the stated significance-level adjustment.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the interim timing or information fraction, efficacy boundary, stopping authority, and whether futility or safety stopping rules apply. These decisions determine how the interim is conducted and interpreted.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the overall type I error target and whether 0.049 is the final nominal boundary or another adjusted level, together with the alpha-spending or other sequential procedure. Without these decisions, the complete significance rule cannot be established.

**[Proposed]** Restrict unblinded interim results to an independent review body and authorized support personnel, with documented recommendations and access controls. Rationale: this protects trial integrity. Assumption: such a body and support process will be established.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** Final analysis occurs after all patients complete or discontinue, all data are in the database, and the database is locked.

**[Provided]** Assessment windows are: 24-hour imaging and NIHSS, 18–30 hours; 72–96-hour NIHSS, 60–96 hours; 72–96-hour imaging, 60–120 hours; Day-5 mRS, Glasgow Outcome Scale and Barthel Index, 4–6 days; Day-30 clinical outcomes, 15–60 days; and Day-90 clinical outcomes, 61–120 days.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the timing of each individual secondary and exploratory endpoint where it is not otherwise defined, including how assessments outside the stated windows are handled. This is needed for endpoint derivation and analysis.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The sample-size calculation uses a two-sided significance level of 0.049 after adjustment for one efficacy interim analysis.

**[Proposed]** Report effect estimates and two-sided confidence intervals alongside p-values. For secondary and exploratory analyses, report nominal 95% intervals and identify them as unadjusted unless a multiplicity procedure is adopted. Rationale: estimates convey magnitude and precision. Assumption: these analyses are not used for unadjusted confirmatory claims.

**[Clarification Required]** Prespecify the primary confidence-interval method compatible with the sequential design and any multiplicity procedure for secondary endpoints. This determines error control and the interpretation of claims.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Record deviations, their timing, treatment assignment masking, and potential impact; classify important deviations before unblinding using prespecified rules. Rationale: consistent review limits outcome-dependent exclusions. Assumption: sufficient deviation data are collected.

**[Clarification Required]** Define important deviations and their effect on any supportive analysis population. This is needed for reproducible population assignment.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Proposed]** Define the primary intention-to-treat population as all randomized patients analyzed according to assigned treatment, regardless of treatment received or subsequent EVT. Rationale: this preserves randomization and reflects the treatment strategy. Assumption: all randomized patients can be identified.

**[Proposed]** Define the safety population as patients receiving any study drug, analyzed according to treatment received; describe randomized patients who receive no study drug. Rationale: safety summaries should reflect exposure. Assumption: administration records are reliable.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify handling of partial administration or receipt of both study treatments in safety summaries, and whether a per-protocol population is required. These decisions affect denominators and interpretability.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Maintain a screening log with eligibility decisions, consent status, reasons for nonrandomization, and counts screened, randomized, treated, and followed. Rationale: this permits assessment of recruitment and selection. Assumption: screening data may be collected under applicable privacy requirements.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are pre-stroke mRS 0–2; NIHSS ≥5 at baseline and immediately before randomization; qualifying CTA/MRA plus CTP/MRP target mismatch with core <70 mL, ratio ≥1.8, and mismatch ≥15 mL; and capacity to comply.

**[Provided]** Exclusion categories are bleeding, coagulopathy or anticoagulants; prior thrombolysis; relevant intracranial pathology; uncontrolled hypertension; glucose extremes; prior recent stroke or surgery; pregnancy; and prohibitive imaging findings.

**[Clarification Required]** Define the operational thresholds, time intervals, medications, and imaging findings within the exclusion categories, and how capacity to comply is assessed. These details are necessary for consistent eligibility decisions.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Proposed]** Document recruitment and consent procedures, including procedures for patients needing transfer, and monitor enrollment against the planned total. Rationale: consistent procedures support timely and equitable enrollment. Assumption: participating sites can implement the approved consent process.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify participating settings, recruitment period, consent arrangements, and applicable transfer procedures. These are needed to operationalize enrollment.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Proposed]** Record treatment discontinuation separately from withdrawal of study follow-up, including reasons and dates; seek remaining outcome and safety data when permitted. Rationale: stopping treatment need not imply loss of outcome data. Assumption: consent and applicable requirements permit continued collection.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify what data may be collected after each type of withdrawal and the endpoint-specific follow-up obligations. This affects missingness and safety ascertainment.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Proposed]** Summarize demographics, pre-stroke mRS, baseline NIHSS, occlusion, imaging mismatch measures, and randomization strata by assigned group, using descriptive statistics without baseline significance testing. Rationale: these summaries describe the randomized population. Assumption: the variables are available and consistently recorded.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is ordinal Day-90 mRS with seven levels, 0–6.

**[Proposed]** Use a treatment-policy comparison in the intention-to-treat population, retaining outcomes regardless of study-treatment discontinuation or subsequent EVT. Rationale: this estimates the effect of assignment within the trial’s care strategy. Assumption: outcome collection remains permissible after these events.

**[Proposed]** Code a patient known to have died by Day 90 as mRS 6. Rationale: mRS uses level 6 for death. Assumption: death by Day 90 can be reliably ascertained.

**[Clarification Required]** Define the target assessment and handling of death or assessment between Day 90 and the end of the Day-90 window. This matters because the window extends from 61 to 120 days.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** Secondary outcomes comprise Day-90 functional independence, angiographic reperfusion, NIHSS, Barthel Index, Glasgow Outcome Scale, 24-hour reperfusion, 24-hour recanalization, sICH, adverse events, mortality, and PH2.

**[Clarification Required]** Define functional-independence thresholds, imaging and hemorrhage criteria, endpoint denominators, assessment times, and handling of death before each assessment. These definitions are required to derive and interpret the secondary outcomes.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** As the primary model, fit a proportional-odds logistic regression for cumulative probabilities of lower mRS, including treatment and the randomization strata. Report the common odds ratio for tenecteplase versus placebo, its confidence interval, and the two-sided treatment test. Rationale: this directly estimates the stated common odds ratio. Assumption: the proportional-odds model is an acceptable primary model.

**[Clarification Required]** Confirm the primary test and model, including how the Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney test used for sample-size planning relates to the primary common-odds analysis. This affects the prespecified decision rule and reproducibility.

**[Proposed]** Assess proportional-odds fit and present the observed mRS distribution by group; conduct a prespecified alternative ordinal or distributional analysis if fit is inadequate. Rationale: a single common odds ratio may not describe all cutpoints. Assumption: the alternative method and its inferential role are fixed before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** Analyze binary secondary outcomes with treatment-group counts, proportions, and comparative estimates; analyze continuous or ordinal outcomes with methods appropriate to their distributions; analyze survival with time-to-event summaries and a treatment comparison. Rationale: methods should match outcome scale. Assumption: endpoint definitions and observation periods are established.

**[Clarification Required]** Prespecify the models, covariates, denominators, censoring rules, and any hierarchy for each secondary endpoint. These choices determine the estimands and interpretation.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Summarize missingness by treatment, endpoint, reason, and assessment time. Rationale: the extent and pattern of missingness inform interpretation. Assumption: reasons are recorded where available.

**[Proposed]** For missing primary mRS, use multiple imputation incorporating treatment, baseline strata, and available prognostic information, with estimates combined across imputations. Rationale: this permits an intention-to-treat analysis under a missing-at-random assumption. Assumption: the imputation model is adequately specified and observed information explains missingness.

**[Proposed]** Perform prespecified sensitivity analyses that vary the assumed outcomes of patients with missing mRS, including less favorable assumptions by treatment group. Rationale: missing-at-random cannot be verified from observed data. Assumption: the sensitivity scenarios are clinically interpretable and specified before unblinding.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify imputation details, the primary handling of missing data for each secondary endpoint, and sensitivity scenarios. These choices can materially affect estimates and conclusions.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Provided]** Exploratory outcomes include final infarct volume and growth, mRS subgroups, outcomes when thrombectomy is not performed, 30-day readmission, one-pass EVT, Neuro-QoL, and Day-90 survival.

**[Proposed]** Present exploratory subgroup estimates with interaction tests and confidence intervals, without treating within-subgroup significance as proof of treatment-effect differences. Rationale: subgroup comparisons are often imprecise. Assumption: subgroup definitions and tests are prespecified.

**[Proposed]** Treat comparisons restricted to patients who do not undergo thrombectomy as descriptive or exploratory, because thrombectomy status occurs after randomization. Rationale: conditioning on a post-randomization event can introduce selection bias. Assumption: no additional causal identification strategy is established.

**[Clarification Required]** Define exploratory subgroups, infarct-growth calculation, thrombectomy-not-performed and one-pass denominators, readmission window, Neuro-QoL measures, and survival censoring. These definitions are needed for reproducible analyses.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Provided]** Safety outcomes include sICH, adverse events, mortality, and PH2.

**[Proposed]** Summarize adverse events and serious adverse events by treatment received, with counts and proportions, and separately summarize sICH, PH2, and deaths. Rationale: both overall and clinically important harms require visibility. Assumption: events and exposure are systematically recorded.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify adverse-event collection periods, severity and causality rules, serious-event reporting procedures, and sICH and PH2 definitions. These determine safety ascertainment and comparability.

### 5.7 Software and References

**[Provided]** EAST 6.4.1 was used for sample-size calculation.

**[Proposed]** Document the analysis software and version, retain validated programs, and archive analysis datasets and outputs. Rationale: this supports reproducibility. Assumption: the selected software can implement the finalized methods.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the software for final statistical analyses and any required analysis standards. This is needed to finalize the reproducibility plan.
