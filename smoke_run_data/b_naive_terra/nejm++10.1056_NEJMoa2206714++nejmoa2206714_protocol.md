# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

[Provided] This phase 3 study compares lebrikizumab monotherapy with placebo in adolescents and adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD).

[Provided] The planned sample was increased beyond the efficacy calculation to collect safety information and provide sufficient Week-16 responders for maintenance.

### 1.2 Objectives

[Provided] **P1:** Evaluate the safety and efficacy of lebrikizumab versus placebo. The FDA primary endpoint is Week-16 IGA 0/1 with at least a 2-point improvement; the EMA co-primary endpoints are that IGA endpoint and Week-16 EASI-75.

[Provided] **S1:** Evaluate induction and maintenance efficacy, health outcomes and quality of life, rescue use, and serum lebrikizumab concentration.

[Provided] **S2:** Provide supportive analyses of additional efficacy and patient-reported outcomes and descriptive modified SQAAQ acceptability and tolerability results in participating EU adolescents.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

[Provided] This is an international, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group phase 3 study. It comprises screening, 16-week double-blind induction, 36-week maintenance, escape treatment where applicable, and approximately 12 weeks of safety follow-up after the last injection for early terminators or participants not entering the extension.

[Provided] Induction allocation is 2:1 to lebrikizumab 250 mg every 2 weeks (LEB250Q2W) or placebo. Eligible Week-16 responders are rerandomized 2:2:1 to LEB250Q2W, lebrikizumab 250 mg every 4 weeks (LEB250Q4W), or placebo.

[Provided] All injections are subcutaneous. Induction lebrikizumab is 500 mg at baseline and Week 2, described as two PFS-NSD loading doses, followed by 250 mg every 2 weeks through Week 14. Induction placebo is 4 mL at baseline and Week 2, then 2 mL every 2 weeks through Week 14.

[Provided] Maintenance responders receive LEB250Q2W, LEB250Q4W, or placebo every 2 weeks; placebo injections maintain blinding.

[Provided] Week-16 nonresponders, induction rescue users, and rerandomized participants losing EASI-50 at Weeks 24, 32, 40, or 48 receive open-label lebrikizumab 250 mg every 2 weeks through Week 52. Participants without EASI-50 after 8 escape weeks discontinue.

[Clarification Required] The precise Week-16 IGA/EASI response rule for maintenance eligibility, including how the measures are combined and how missing responses are handled, must be specified because it determines rerandomization and escape eligibility.

[Clarification Required] The handling of an 8-week escape assessment that would occur after Week 52, including for escape beginning at Week 48, must be specified because the stated escape treatment ends at Week 52.

### 2.2 Randomization

[Provided] After eligibility is established, electronic data capture (EDC) allocates treatment at baseline and rerandomizes maintenance participants according to Week-16 IGA/EASI response and induction rescue use. Protocol Amendment 1 replaced earlier IWRS wording with EDC.

[Provided] Specified stratification factors are geographic region (US, EU, rest of world), age (12 to <18 versus ≥18 years), and baseline disease severity (IGA 3 versus 4). Minimization or blocking is not specified.

[Clarification Required] Specify whether the listed stratification factors apply to both randomizations and how the allocation sequence and concealment are implemented; these affect analysis and operational reproducibility.

### 2.3 Sample Size

[Provided] Approximately 400 participants are planned, randomized 2:1 to lebrikizumab and placebo. The calculation using 96 and 48 participants, respectively, was increased to approximately 400 for safety information and sufficient Week-16 maintenance responders.

[Provided] For FDA, assumed Week-16 IGA 0/1 rates are 34.7% and 7.7%, respectively. With 96 and 48 participants, a two-group continuity-corrected chi-square test at two-sided α=0.05 has stated power >95%.

[Provided] For EMA, the same allocation has stated power >95% at two-sided α=0.05 for each co-primary endpoint. Assumed EASI-75 rates are 48.0% and 11.5%, respectively; stated overall power for both co-primary endpoints is ≥90%.

[Provided] The stated target differences are 27.0 percentage points for IGA 0/1 and 36.5 percentage points for EASI-75. The binary-proportion assumptions come from Phase 2b rescue-medication nonresponse sensitivity results; no separate variance parameter is specified.

[Provided] Event accrual and a non-inferiority or equivalence margin are not applicable to this fixed-time binary superiority calculation. The design is individually randomized and non-clustered.

[Clarification Required] Dropout or non-adherence inflation, sample-size re-estimation or amendments, and calculation software are not specified. Confirm these details to make the calculation reproducible.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

[Provided] The induction superiority null is equality between LEB250Q2W and placebo for Week-16 IGA 0/1 with at least a 2-point improvement. EMA additionally tests equality for Week-16 EASI-75.

[Provided] The FDA induction graphical family contains IGA01 at Week 16 followed by EASI-75 at Week 16, EASI-90 at Week 16, Pruritus NRS-4 at Weeks 16, 4, and 2, IGA01 at Week 4, adult IGA01 at Week 16, and sleep-loss response at Week 16.

[Provided] The EMA induction graphical family starts with co-primary IGA01 and EASI-75 at Week 16 and includes EASI-90 at Weeks 16 and 4, EASI percent change at Week 16, pruritus percent change and response at Weeks 16, 4, and 2, DLQI response and change at Week 16, and sleep-loss response and change at Week 16.

[Provided] For EMA maintenance, each of Q2W and Q4W follows the ordered sequence: persistence of EASI-75; persistence of IGA response; persistence of at least a 4-point pruritus response; and percent EASI change at Week 52. Comparisons are LEB-responder Q2W versus placebo and LEB-responder Q4W versus placebo.

[Clarification Required] Specify the graphical initial weights, transition weights, testing rules, and relationship between induction and maintenance families, including control across Q2W and Q4W. Endpoint lists alone do not determine adjusted confirmatory decisions.

[Clarification Required] Specify the induction treatment history of the maintenance placebo comparator and the exact persistence definitions and assessment time points. These determine the maintenance estimands and comparisons.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

[Clarification Required] Whether any interim analysis, its timing, access to unblinded data, decision boundaries, and any independent monitoring arrangements are planned is not specified. These decisions matter for operational integrity and, if applicable, error control.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

[Provided] Final database lock (DBL) follows safety follow-up, early discontinuation, or extension enrollment; it may be combined with Week-52 DBL depending on the regulatory timeline.

[Provided] Induction assessments are at baseline and Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16, with ±3-day windows. Maintenance assessments are at Weeks 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48, and 52, with ±5-day windows.

[Provided] IGA, EASI, and BSA are scheduled through Week 16. IGA, EASI, BSA, SCORAD, and intermittent patient-reported outcome and safety assessments continue during maintenance, with Week-52 end-of-study/early termination visit assessments and 12-week safety follow-up when required.

[Provided] Pruritus NRS and sleep-loss daily diaries are mapped to baseline and Weeks 1, 2, and 4, then scheduled visits through Week 52. Baseline requires at least 4 of 7 daily reports; postbaseline weekly values are prorated if at least one report is available. Out-of-window assessments are retained unless stated otherwise.

[Clarification Required] Specify planned induction and Week-52 analysis and lock dates, the diary-to-visit mapping and proration formula, and rules for multiple or conflicting assessments. These are needed for reproducible endpoint derivation and analysis timing.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

[Provided] The sample-size calculations use two-sided α=0.05. The EMA calculation states power for each co-primary endpoint and for both endpoints jointly.

[Proposed] Report two-sided p-values and 95% confidence intervals, identifying intervals not adjusted for multiplicity as nominal. This supports interpretation of effect size; it assumes the final analysis plan retains the stated two-sided significance level.

[Clarification Required] Confirm the regulatory decision rules and completed multiplicity graphs before confirmatory testing; nominal p-values alone do not establish significance within the graphical families.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

[Proposed] Document deviations and classify important deviations before unblinding, without automatically excluding participants from randomized analyses. This limits selection bias; it assumes deviations can be assessed without knowledge of treatment assignments.

[Clarification Required] Define important deviation categories and any consequences for supportive populations because these criteria were not supplied.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

[Proposed] Analyze induction efficacy in all baseline-randomized participants according to assigned treatment. This preserves the randomized comparison; it assumes the intended primary estimand includes all randomized participants.

[Proposed] Analyze safety in participants receiving at least one injection, grouped by treatment received, with open-label escape exposure identified separately. This describes actual exposure; it assumes treatment administration records permit reliable classification.

[Proposed] Analyze maintenance efficacy among eligible rerandomized participants according to maintenance assignment, separately identifying induction treatment history. This preserves the maintenance randomization within its selected responder population; it assumes the relevant comparator and eligibility rules are confirmed.

[Clarification Required] Define the populations for serum concentration and modified SQAAQ analyses and any additional efficacy populations; their eligibility and denominators affect interpretation.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

[Proposed] Summarize numbers screened, eligible, randomized, and not randomized, with recorded reasons for screen failure. This supports assessment of selection; it assumes screening outcomes and reasons are captured.

[Clarification Required] Define the screening period and required screening assessments and their timing because they determine operational eligibility.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

[Provided] No prospective enrollment waiver is permitted.

[Provided] Inclusion requires an adult or an adolescent aged 12 to <18 years weighing ≥40 kg; AD by American Academy of Dermatology criteria for ≥1 year; baseline EASI ≥16, IGA ≥3/4, and AD BSA ≥10%; and inadequate topical response or medical inadvisability.

[Provided] Inclusion also requires stable nonmedicated moisturizer twice daily for ≥7 days, ≥4 of 7 baseline pruritus/sleep diary entries, ability to comply, specified highly effective contraception or abstinence for WOCBP during treatment plus 18 weeks, male barrier contraception for 18 weeks, and consent or assent.

[Provided] Exclusions include prior lebrikizumab; anaphylaxis; and recent topical AD treatment, dupilumab or tralokinumab, systemic immunomodulator or phototherapy, investigational product or biologic, prescription moisturizer, tanning, or live vaccine.

[Provided] Exclusions also include uncontrolled chronic disease or severe asthma; recent or active infection, hepatitis or cirrhosis, parasitic infection risk, immunosuppression, or HIV; significant screening laboratory findings; and assessment-interfering skin disease.

[Provided] Malignancy within 5 years is excluded except fully treated in-situ cervical cancer or resolved nonmetastatic basal or squamous skin cancer. Severe illness or psychological condition, pregnancy, breastfeeding, or pregnancy planning are also exclusions.

[Clarification Required] Specify the exact recent-treatment and infection look-back periods, laboratory thresholds, contraception details, and operational definitions of medical inadvisability and other qualitative exclusions. These are necessary for consistent screening.

[Clarification Required] Confirm whether the ≥40 kg requirement applies to adults as well as adolescents and whether the diary minimum applies separately to each diary. These affect eligibility determinations.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

[Provided] The FDA primary outcome is Week-16 IGA 0/1 with at least a 2-point improvement. EMA co-primary outcomes are this IGA endpoint and Week-16 EASI-75.

[Derived] EASI-75 denotes at least a 75% improvement in EASI from baseline.

[Proposed] For induction, estimate the randomized-group difference in Week-16 response proportions, lebrikizumab minus placebo, under a strategy counting participants who use induction rescue as nonresponders. This targets efficacy without rescue; it assumes that strategy is accepted for the primary estimand.

[Clarification Required] Confirm the primary estimands, including treatment discontinuation, rescue, death, and other intercurrent-event handling. These choices determine what treatment effect is estimated.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

[Provided] Secondary outcomes cover induction and maintenance efficacy, health outcomes and quality of life, rescue use, and serum lebrikizumab concentration.

[Provided] Exploratory outcomes include additional efficacy and patient-reported outcomes and modified SQAAQ acceptability and tolerability in participating EU adolescents.

[Provided] The FDA and EMA graphical families and the EMA maintenance sequence are specified in Section 2.4.

[Clarification Required] Specify endpoint derivations and denominators for IGA01 beyond Week 16, adult IGA01, EASI-90, pruritus response and percent change, DLQI response and change, sleep-loss response and change, maintenance persistence, rescue use, and serum concentration summaries. These are needed for reproducible analyses and testing.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

[Proposed] For induction binary endpoints, compare randomized groups using a stratified test incorporating the specified baseline stratification factors, and report response counts, proportions, the unadjusted risk difference, and a nominal 95% confidence interval. This uses randomization information and gives an interpretable effect; it assumes adequate stratum information and that the finalized test is accepted.

[Clarification Required] Specify the exact stratified test, confidence-interval method, and prespecified treatment of sparse strata. These affect reproducibility and may differ from the continuity-corrected chi-square test used for the sample-size calculation.

[Proposed] Compare maintenance binary persistence outcomes by maintenance assignment within the confirmed eligible responder population, reporting counts, proportions, risk differences, and confidence intervals. This reflects maintenance randomization; it assumes the placebo comparator and persistence rules are confirmed.

[Proposed] Analyze continuous change outcomes using a repeated-measures model including treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline outcome, and appropriate randomization factors. This uses available longitudinal measurements; it assumes a suitable model and missing-at-random mechanism conditional on included information.

[Proposed] Summarize serum concentrations descriptively by sampling time and relevant treatment, rescue use by treatment group, and modified SQAAQ responses descriptively in participating EU adolescents. This matches the stated objectives; it assumes sampling and questionnaire data are available.

[Clarification Required] Specify the continuous-outcome model details, maintenance intercurrent-event strategies, and serum sampling schedule. These affect the target effects and reproducibility.

### 5.4 Missing Data

[Provided] Baseline diary values require at least 4 of 7 daily reports; postbaseline weekly values are prorated if at least one report is available.

[Proposed] For the primary binary analysis, classify participants with missing Week-16 response as nonresponders and report missingness and rescue use by group. This provides a conservative operational approach; it assumes nonresponse imputation is acceptable despite possible misclassification.

[Proposed] Perform observed-data and alternative missing-data sensitivity analyses, including analyses varying assumptions for missing responses. This assesses robustness; it assumes sufficient observed data to support the chosen alternatives.

[Clarification Required] Specify the alternatives, their parameters, and handling of missing components of composite responses and missing data after escape or withdrawal. These decisions can materially affect conclusions.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

[Proposed] Summarize efficacy by age, region, and baseline IGA severity, presenting subgroup effects and uncertainty without treating unprespecified subgroup findings as confirmatory. This assesses consistency; it assumes subgroup sample sizes permit informative estimates.

[Proposed] Describe participant disposition, exposure, rescue, escape, and discontinuation by treatment and period. This contextualizes efficacy and safety; it assumes the relevant dates and reasons are recorded.

[Clarification Required] Specify any prespecified subgroup interaction tests, supportive estimands, and the role of out-of-window measurements in selecting visit values. These determine interpretation and avoid inconsistent analyses.

### 5.6 Harms

[Provided] Safety evaluation is part of P1, and safety assessments continue into maintenance and required follow-up.

[Proposed] Summarize adverse events, serious adverse events, discontinuations due to adverse events, and available safety assessments by treatment and study period, distinguishing blinded treatment from open-label escape. This supports exposure-specific safety interpretation; it assumes events and treatment dates are captured.

[Clarification Required] Specify adverse-event collection periods, coding, treatment-emergent definitions, severity and causality rules, and planned safety laboratory and other summaries. These determine safety denominators and attribution.
