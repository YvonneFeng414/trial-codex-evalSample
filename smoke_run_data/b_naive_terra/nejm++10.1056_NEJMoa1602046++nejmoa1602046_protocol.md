# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

[Provided] This pivotal Phase III evaluation addresses the need for woman-controlled HIV prevention independent of sexual activity. It compares a monthly dapivirine vaginal ring with an indistinguishable placebo ring.

### 1.2 Objectives

[Provided] **P1:** Determine the efficacy of dapivirine ring use every 4 weeks for approximately 24 months (104 weeks) in preventing HIV-1 infection among healthy, sexually active HIV-negative women.

[Provided] **P2:** Assess the safety of dapivirine ring use on the same schedule.

[Provided] **S1:** Compare HIV-2 incidence, curable STI incidence and vaginal-flora changes, and determine pregnancy incidence.

[Provided] **S2:** Assess reported 4-week ring-use adherence and acceptability.

[Provided] **S3:** Assess the frequency and type of HIV-1 resistance in women acquiring HIV-1 while using investigational product (IP).

[Provided] **S4, exploratory:** Evaluate HSV-2/HIV-1 association; relationships among contraception method, pregnancy and HIV seroconversion; drug concentrations versus HIV-1 seroconversion or viral resistance; and drug concentrations versus self-reported adherence.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

[Provided] This is a multicentre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase III safety-and-efficacy trial with 2:1 allocation to dapivirine and placebo.

[Provided] Both groups use a ring continuously, with self-insertion and replacement every 4 weeks for approximately 104 weeks. Exit follow-up occurs 6 weeks after ring discontinuation.

[Provided] Ring-004 is a silicone-elastomer vaginal matrix ring containing 25 mg dapivirine. The comparator is a placebo vaginal ring.

### 2.2 Randomization

[Provided] An automated interactive voice/web response system assigns a unique participant identification number and treatment. A master randomization list links identification numbers to assignments. Randomization is stratified by research centre using a prespecified block size.

[Provided] Minimization is not specified.

[Clarification Required] The block size, sequence-generation procedures and emergency unblinding procedures are not supplied. These decisions matter for allocation concealment, reproducibility and participant safety.

[Proposed] Restrict access to the allocation list and document any emergency unblinding. This protects blinding, assuming the system can maintain an access and unblinding audit trail.

### 2.3 Sample Size

[Provided] Planned enrollment was approximately 1,950 women: 1,300 assigned dapivirine and 650 assigned placebo. Enrollment reached 1,959 and accrual closed.

[Provided] Amendment 3 increased planned enrollment from 1,650 to approximately 1,950 to mitigate product nonadherence and preserve power after high nonadherence at one research centre. This was not a prospectively described re-estimation algorithm.

[Provided] The calculation used HIV-1 seroconversion incidence rates under proportional hazards, a two-sided log-rank test at 0.05, average annual placebo incidence greater than 4% allowing for risk-reduction counselling, microbicide efficacy of at least 50%, and early discontinuation or loss to follow-up of at most 10%.

[Provided] The target was a 50% reduction in the HIV-1 seroconversion rate; 40% and 60% efficacy scenarios were also evaluated.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

[Provided] The primary efficacy null hypothesis is no difference between groups in HIV-1 seroconversion rate or population survival curves. The efficacy design tests superiority; the primary safety analysis is descriptive.

[Derived] Under the stated proportional-hazards assumption, no treatment difference corresponds to a dapivirine-to-placebo hazard ratio of 1; a hazard ratio below 1 favours dapivirine.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

[Clarification Required] Whether formal interim efficacy or safety analyses, an independent monitoring body, or stopping rules were planned is not specified. These decisions matter for trial oversight and, for unblinded efficacy testing, type I error control.

[Proposed] If unblinded interim efficacy testing is intended, prespecify its timing, boundaries and final-test adjustment before such results are accessed. This protects the overall error rate, assuming interim testing is feasible and authorized.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

[Provided] Final analyses occur only after database lock. Blinded review, including deviation classification, occurs before lock; treatment codes are released after lock.

[Provided] Screening lasts up to 28 days. IP use is approximately 104 weeks, with exit 6 weeks after last product use. On-IP visit windows are ±7 days.

[Provided] HIV and pregnancy testing, adverse-event and concomitant-medication review, diary/adherence data collection and ring exchange occur every 4 weeks.

[Provided] Pelvic, STI and safety laboratory assessments occur every 12 weeks. Acceptability is assessed at week 4 and every 24 weeks from week 24. Cervical cytology occurs at screening, week 52 and the last product-use visit.

[Clarification Required] The follow-up schedule after early product discontinuation is not specified. It matters for safety surveillance and unbiased assessment of infection outcomes.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

[Provided] The sample-size calculation used a two-sided significance level of 0.05.

[Proposed] Use a two-sided 0.05 test for the final primary efficacy analysis and report two-sided 95% confidence intervals. This aligns the analysis with the calculation, assuming no interim efficacy adjustment is required.

[Proposed] Treat secondary and exploratory inferential results as supportive, with unadjusted confidence intervals and no confirmatory multiplicity claims. This limits overinterpretation, assuming no additional confirmatory testing strategy is specified.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

[Provided] Deviation classification is reviewed blinded before database lock.

[Proposed] Document deviations and classify their potential impact before unblinding; summarize them by randomized group and category. This supports transparent interpretation, assuming deviation information is available.

[Clarification Required] Material-deviation criteria, including which deviations would exclude participants from any adherence-focused population, are not supplied. These criteria matter for reproducibility and avoidance of post-unblinding selection.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

[Proposed] Define the intention-to-treat population as all randomized participants, analysed by assigned group regardless of adherence or subsequent product discontinuation. This preserves randomization for efficacy, assuming randomization records are complete.

[Proposed] Define the safety population as participants who used at least one ring, analysed by product actually received. This describes exposure-related safety, assuming product receipt and use can be identified.

[Proposed] Describe participants with no post-randomization HIV assessment separately; retain them in intention-to-treat accounting without inventing infection outcomes. This makes missing follow-up visible, assuming randomized participants can be linked to assessment records.

[Clarification Required] A per-protocol population and its role are not specified. A decision is needed before any such analysis because exclusions based on post-randomization behaviour can introduce bias.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

[Provided] Eligible participants are women aged 18–45 years who can consent and attend procedures; are sexually active, defined as at least one penetrative penile-vaginal act per month in the prior 3 months; are HIV-negative at screening/enrolment; use stable contraception unless postmenopausal or surgically sterilized; are asymptomatic for genital infection at enrolment; are locatable; and are not participating in conflicting investigational studies.

[Provided] Exclusions include current or recent intended pregnancy or breastfeeding; specified injection-drug, research or vaccine histories; untreated significant urogenital infection or a grade ≥2 pelvic finding; specified genital, medical or allergy conditions; clinically important baseline laboratory abnormalities; disqualifying cytology; and investigator-judged risk or inability to adhere.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

[Provided] The primary outcomes are HIV-1 infection prevention and safety with dapivirine ring use every 4 weeks for approximately 24 months (104 weeks).

[Proposed] For efficacy, analyse time from randomization to first confirmed HIV-1-positive assessment, with participants without an event censored at their last documented HIV-1-negative assessment. This uses observed infection status without imputing unobserved events, assuming confirmation and assessment dates are available.

[Proposed] Include assessments through the exit visit in the primary intention-to-treat analysis, regardless of product discontinuation. This targets the effect of assignment over observed follow-up, assuming post-discontinuation assessments are available.

[Clarification Required] HIV-1 confirmation procedures, handling of indeterminate results and the intended efficacy follow-up endpoint are not specified. These decisions matter for event classification and person-time.

[Proposed] Describe safety using adverse events, laboratory findings and other available safety assessments by treatment group. This addresses the descriptive safety objective, assuming assessments can be linked to exposure.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

[Proposed] Estimate first HIV-2 infection incidence and compare groups using event counts, person-time and, if sufficient events permit, a time-to-event analysis. This addresses incidence while accounting for follow-up, assuming HIV-2 confirmation and negative-assessment dates are available.

[Proposed] Summarize curable STI incidence and vaginal-flora changes by group; report STI type and repeated episodes where recorded. This addresses both outcomes, assuming relevant tests and visit dates are available.

[Proposed] Estimate pregnancy incidence using first observed pregnancy and person-time at risk, with counts and rates by group. This addresses pregnancy occurrence, assuming pregnancy dates and periods at risk can be determined.

[Proposed] Summarize reported 4-week ring-use adherence and acceptability by group and scheduled assessment, including response denominators. This describes use over time, assuming questionnaires and diaries are available.

[Proposed] Among women acquiring HIV-1 while using IP, summarize resistance frequency and type by group, including the number with evaluable resistance results. This addresses S3 without treating an incomplete assay set as fully observed, assuming resistance testing is available.

[Clarification Required] STI definitions, flora measurements, pregnancy dating and risk periods, adherence and acceptability instruments, and resistance assays and eligibility for testing are not specified. These decisions affect outcome definitions and denominators.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

[Proposed] Compare HIV-1 time-to-event distributions using a two-sided log-rank test stratified by research centre; declare primary efficacy if the adjusted-for-any-interim test is significant at 0.05 and the estimated effect favours dapivirine. This follows the calculation and randomization stratification, assuming sufficient information within centres and a finalized interim plan.

[Proposed] Estimate the dapivirine-to-placebo hazard ratio and 95% confidence interval using a centre-stratified Cox model; express estimated efficacy as \(100(1-\text{hazard ratio})\%\). This provides effect magnitude, assuming proportional hazards and an estimable model.

[Proposed] Report group-specific event counts, person-time, incidence rates and survival curves; assess proportional hazards graphically and with residual-based diagnostics. This makes the treatment comparison interpretable, assuming event and follow-up dates are available.

[Proposed] If proportional hazards is doubtful, report the diagnostics and a prespecified descriptive time-specific survival comparison without replacing the primary test after unblinding. This aids interpretation, assuming a clinically meaningful supported time point can be chosen before unblinding.

[Clarification Required] The handling of sparse centre strata and the time point for any alternative survival summary are not specified. Prespecification matters for model stability and avoidance of data-driven choices.

### 5.4 Missing Data

[Proposed] Report missing assessments, incomplete follow-up and reasons by group and visit. This exposes possible differential missingness, assuming reasons are recorded.

[Proposed] For the primary analysis, censor event-free participants at their last documented HIV-1-negative assessment; do not impute infection status after that date. This avoids asserting unobserved outcomes, assuming censoring is sufficiently non-informative for the primary estimate.

[Proposed] Conduct prespecified sensitivity analyses for departures from non-informative censoring and for alternative plausible event times within the last-negative-to-first-positive interval. This assesses robustness, assuming adequate assessment dates and defensible scenarios are available.

[Clarification Required] Specific sensitivity-analysis scenarios and rules for participants lacking post-randomization HIV testing are not supplied. These choices matter because infection times are interval-observed and missing follow-up may be informative.
