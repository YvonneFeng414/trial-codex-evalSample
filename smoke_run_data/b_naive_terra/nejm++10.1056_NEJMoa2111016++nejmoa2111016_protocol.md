# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** This phase 3b trial compares edoxaban with a vitamin K antagonist (VKA) in people with atrial fibrillation (AF) requiring chronic oral anticoagulation after transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI).

**[Provided]** The final statistical analysis plan (SAP), version 3.0, applies where it adds to or differs from the final protocol.

### 1.2 Objectives

**[Provided]** The primary objectives are to compare the regimens for net adverse clinical events (NACE) and International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) major bleeding.

**[Provided]** NACE comprises all-cause death, myocardial infarction (MI), ischemic stroke, systemic thromboembolism (SEE), valve thrombosis, or ISTH major bleeding.

**[Provided]** Secondary objectives are to compare alternative NACE definitions using TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO; MACE; MACCE; cardiovascular mortality; stroke subtypes; SEE; MI; valve thrombosis; and repeat target-lesion revascularization.

**[Provided]** Further secondary objectives are to compare ISTH, TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO bleeding endpoints; intracranial, life-threatening, and fatal bleeding; mortality; adverse events (AEs); laboratory parameters; ECG; and vital signs.

**[Provided]** Exploratory objectives are to compare cardiovascular hospital admissions of at least 24 hours, length of stay, ICU days, PACT-Q treatment satisfaction, EQ-5D-5L quality of life, and optional biomarkers.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** The study is multinational, multicentre, prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded endpoint evaluation (PROBE), and parallel-group. An independent clinical endpoint committee (CEC) adjudicates events, and an independent data and safety monitoring board (DSMB) monitors safety.

**[Provided]** Participants are allocated 1:1 to edoxaban-based or VKA-based regimens. Randomized treatment starts within 24 hours of randomization and lasts up to 36 months, with a minimum of 12 months except for the last enrolled participants, whose minimum is 6 months. Treatment is followed by 30 days of observational follow-up.

**[Provided]** Edoxaban is given at 60 mg once daily, or 30 mg once daily when dose-reduction criteria in the locally approved label are met.

**[Provided]** The locally approved VKA is investigator-adjusted to an INR of 2.0–3.0; in Japan, the target is 1.6–2.6 for participants aged at least 70 years and 2.0–3.0 for those younger than 70 years.

**[Provided]** Antiplatelet co-therapy is predeclared at randomization: no antiplatelet therapy (APT) or single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) up to 90 days without another indication; SAPT indefinitely after PCI, with dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) only up to 3 months after each stent. Aspirin 75–100 mg/day or clopidogrel 75 mg/day is preferred.

**[Clarification Required]** The operational rules for antiplatelet therapy, including how the indefinite SAPT instruction applies during a DAPT period, need confirmation; they matter for consistent treatment and deviation assessment.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** An interactive response system (IXRS) assigns a unique identifier and treatment. An independent biostatistician generates the schedule under IXRS-vendor oversight. Crossover is not permitted.

**[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by TAVI procedure with stenting, yes or no, and need for edoxaban dose adjustment under the local label, yes or no.

**[Provided]** The schedule contains a randomization block number. Block size and minimization method are not specified.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** Approximately 1,400 participants are planned for randomization in an event-driven design. The calculation uses time to first NACE and an edoxaban-to-VKA hazard ratio (HR).

**[Provided]** Assumptions are a VKA annual NACE rate of 14%, an observed HR of 0.95, a noninferiority margin of 1.38, anticipated median follow-up of two years, alpha of 0.05, and 80% power for NACE noninferiority. At least 320 NACE events across both arms are required.

**[Provided]** Noninferiority is assessed by comparing the upper bound of a two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) for the HR with 1.38. The Executive Committee reviewed the margin’s acceptability.

**[Provided]** A separate variability assumption and numerical dropout or non-adherence inflation are not specified. Sample size or follow-up duration may be adjusted for actual versus expected endpoint and dropout rates.

**[Provided]** A clustering design effect is not applicable to this individually randomized parallel-group design. Sample-size software is not specified.

**[Clarification Required]** The procedure, decision authority, timing, and information used for any sample-size or follow-up adjustment need specification; they matter for operational control and preservation of statistical validity.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** Four confirmatory hypotheses are tested in sequence: NACE noninferiority; ISTH major-bleeding noninferiority; ISTH major-bleeding superiority; and NACE superiority. Failure at any step stops the sequence.

**[Provided]** For each noninferiority step, the upper bound of the two-sided 95% HR CI must be strictly less than 1.38. For each superiority step, it must be strictly less than 1.00.

**[Derived]** For either endpoint, the noninferiority null is HR ≥1.38 versus HR <1.38; the superiority null is HR ≥1.00 versus HR <1.00. HRs compare edoxaban with VKA.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** The DSMB monitors safety.

**[Clarification Required]** Whether any formal interim efficacy, futility, or safety analysis is planned, and its timing, access rules, and stopping boundaries, must be decided; these affect trial conduct and any required error control.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** The main endpoint analysis is intention-to-treat (ITT) over the overall study period: from randomization, or first dose for non-ITT sets, to Month 36/end of treatment (EOT), last visit, or death, whichever occurs first. Incomplete follow-up is censored at last known status.

**[Provided]** Assessments occur at baseline/randomization; Months 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30; and EOT/Month 36, with a ±14-day window. Post-treatment follow-up occurs at one month, ±7 days, followed by six-monthly follow-up contacts.

**[Provided]** PACT-Q and EQ-5D-5L are assessed at baseline, Month 3, and Month 12 or EOT, whichever occurs first.

**[Clarification Required]** The relationship between EOT, subsequent follow-up contacts, and the main endpoint censoring date needs operational definition; it determines which post-treatment events enter the main analysis.

**[Clarification Required]** The data cutoff, database-lock criteria, and event-adjudication readiness requirements need specification; they determine the data available for final analysis.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The confirmatory rules use two-sided 95% HR CIs and the sequential thresholds in Section 2.4.

**[Derived]** A two-sided 95% CI corresponds to a one-sided 2.5% test at each stated boundary.

**[Proposed]** Report two-sided 95% CIs for treatment-effect estimates and nominal p-values for nonconfirmatory comparisons. Rationale: this makes precision and exploratory evidence interpretable; assumption: no additional multiplicity procedure is mandated for those comparisons.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Document deviations, classify their potential impact on eligibility, treatment, and endpoint assessment, and finalize analysis-set rules without using comparative outcome results. Rationale: consistent classification limits selective exclusions; assumption: sufficient deviation information is collected.

**[Clarification Required]** The deviations that warrant exclusion from any per-protocol analysis need prespecification; they affect the interpretation of noninferiority sensitivity analyses.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Proposed]** Define the ITT population as all randomized participants analyzed according to assigned treatment. Rationale: this preserves the randomized comparison; assumption: all randomizations are retained in the analysis dataset.

**[Proposed]** Define a safety population as participants receiving at least one dose, analyzed according to treatment received. Rationale: this describes treatment exposure and harms; assumption: received-treatment data are reliable.

**[Proposed]** Conduct a supportive per-protocol analysis using prospectively defined eligibility, treatment, and follow-up criteria. Rationale: adherence-sensitive evidence can support noninferiority interpretation; assumption: deviations can be classified consistently.

**[Clarification Required]** The definitive non-ITT population definitions and handling of treatment received contrary to assignment need specification; they determine denominators and attribution.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Summarize numbers screened, eligible, ineligible, and randomized, with reasons for screening failure where recorded. Rationale: this describes selection into the trial; assumption: screening records are available.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Eligible adults have successful TAVI: correct single-valve position, gradient <20 mmHg, peak velocity <3.0 m/s, regurgitation ≤2, and no overt stroke or uncontrolled bleeding.

**[Provided]** Participants have pre-existing or new AF lasting >30 seconds on ECG and requiring chronic oral anticoagulation.

**[Provided]** Exclusions include high bleeding risk; contraindication to either treatment; prolonged DAPT requirement; recent stroke, MI, or major surgery; mechanical valve; severe renal or hepatic disease; instability; active malignancy; specified laboratory abnormalities; pregnancy; investigator-assessed inability to comply; prior randomization; substance dependence; and life expectancy <6 months.

**[Clarification Required]** The definitions and thresholds for the qualitative exclusions, including “recent,” high bleeding risk, severe disease, instability, specified laboratory abnormalities, and prolonged DAPT requirement, need specification; they determine reproducible eligibility decisions.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** Recruitment is multinational and multicentre, with approximately 1,400 randomized participants planned.

**[Clarification Required]** Recruitment dates, countries, sites, and recruitment procedures are not specified; these are needed to operationalize and report enrollment.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Proposed]** Record treatment discontinuation, study withdrawal, loss to follow-up, dates, and available reasons separately; seek outcome follow-up after treatment discontinuation when permitted. Rationale: treatment cessation and loss of outcome data have different implications; assumption: participant consent and applicable requirements allow follow-up.

**[Clarification Required]** Withdrawal procedures, permitted use of previously collected data, and the distinction between withdrawal from treatment and withdrawal from all follow-up need specification; they affect ascertainment and censoring.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Proposed]** Summarize baseline characteristics by randomized group, including age, the randomization strata, relevant medical history, and baseline treatment. Use counts and percentages for categorical variables and suitable descriptive statistics for continuous variables. Rationale: these summaries characterize the randomized population; assumption: the variables are collected.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The first primary outcome is time to first NACE: all-cause death, MI, ischemic stroke, SEE, valve thrombosis, or ISTH major bleeding.

**[Provided]** The second primary outcome is ISTH major bleeding. The confirmatory comparison uses an HR and the sequential rules in Section 2.4.

**[Proposed]** Analyze time to first ISTH major bleeding using the same time origin and main analysis window as the ITT NACE analysis. Rationale: aligned windows support comparison; assumption: bleeding dates are available.

**[Clarification Required]** The event-date conventions, adjudication rules for unresolved events, and handling of simultaneous component events need specification; they affect endpoint classification and time-to-event analysis.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** Secondary outcomes include alternative NACE definitions using TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO; MACE; MACCE; cardiovascular mortality; stroke subtypes; SEE; MI; valve thrombosis; and repeat target-lesion revascularization.

**[Provided]** Secondary safety outcomes include ISTH, TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO bleeding endpoints; intracranial, life-threatening, and fatal bleeding; mortality; AEs; laboratory parameters; ECG; and vital signs.

**[Provided]** Exploratory outcomes include cardiovascular hospital admissions of at least 24 hours, length of stay, ICU days, PACT-Q treatment satisfaction, EQ-5D-5L quality of life, and optional biomarkers.

**[Clarification Required]** Component definitions for MACE, MACCE, alternative NACE, and the specified bleeding and stroke categories need confirmation, including any version-specific criteria; they determine which events count.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** Estimate primary HRs and two-sided 95% CIs using Cox proportional-hazards models with randomized treatment and the two randomization stratification factors. Rationale: this accommodates time-to-event data and the allocation design; assumption: strata and event dates are available and the model is estimable.

**[Proposed]** Summarize event counts, participants with events, follow-up, and Kaplan–Meier estimates by group; assess proportional hazards and report relevant departures. Rationale: these complement the HR; assumption: censoring is sufficiently noninformative for the intended interpretation.

**[Proposed]** Treat death without prior ISTH major bleeding as censoring in the primary bleeding Cox analysis and provide a competing-risk sensitivity analysis. Rationale: death precludes later bleeding; assumption: the sensitivity estimand and follow-up window are prespecified.

**[Proposed]** Analyze secondary time-to-first-event outcomes using analogous descriptive and time-to-event methods where appropriate; summarize continuous, questionnaire, resource-use, and biomarker outcomes by group and assessment time. Rationale: methods should match the outcome type; assumption: sufficient data are available.

**[Clarification Required]** The estimands and analysis windows for secondary and exploratory outcomes, questionnaire scoring, recurrent admissions, and biomarker selection need specification; these choices affect interpretation and comparability.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Provided]** Incomplete endpoint follow-up is censored at last known status.

**[Proposed]** Summarize missingness and reasons by group and outcome, and perform sensitivity analyses for potentially informative endpoint loss. Rationale: censoring may depend on prognosis; assumption: adequate information exists to characterize missingness.

**[Clarification Required]** Rules for missing or partial event dates, missing stratification factors, questionnaire items, and other non-time-to-event measurements need specification; they affect analysis eligibility and bias.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Summarize NACE components and conduct supportive analyses by the randomization strata and in the per-protocol population, without treating subgroup findings as confirmatory. Rationale: these assess consistency and adherence sensitivity; assumption: event counts support estimation.

**[Clarification Required]** Any additional prespecified subgroups and sensitivity analyses need selection before comparative results are reviewed; they affect multiplicity and interpretation.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Proposed]** Summarize AEs, serious AEs, bleeding, deaths, and relevant laboratory, ECG, and vital-sign findings by treatment received, including event counts and affected participants where appropriate. Rationale: this provides a structured safety assessment; assumption: severity and seriousness are recorded.

**[Clarification Required]** AE collection periods, treatment-emergent definitions, attribution rules, and coding conventions need specification; they determine safety denominators and comparability.

### 5.7 Software and References

**[Provided]** Sample-size software is not specified.

**[Proposed]** Record statistical software, versions, analysis programs, and validation procedures before reporting results. Rationale: this supports reproducibility; assumption: the analysis environment is documented.
