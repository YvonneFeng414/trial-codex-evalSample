# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

[Provided] Oral factor Xa (FXa) inhibitors are widely used, but acute intracranial hemorrhage (ICrH) during anticoagulation has high mortality and disability. Preventing hematoma expansion is a key treatment goal.

[Provided] Andexanet alfa is a recombinant, catalytically inactive FXa analogue intended to reverse FXa inhibition.

### 1.2 Objectives

[Provided] **Primary:** Compare andexanet with usual care for effective hemostasis at 12 hours after randomization.

[Provided] **Secondary:** Compare percent change in anti-fXa activity from baseline to nadir during the first 2 hours after randomization.

[Provided] **Exploratory efficacy:** Assess thrombin generation, neurologic deterioration, mRS, NIHSS/GCS, imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa/hemostasis correlation, EQ-5D-5L, and utility-weighted mRS.

[Provided] **Exploratory safety:** Assess thrombotic events (TEs), mortality, invasive procedures, hospital use, adverse events (AEs), vital signs, and immunogenicity.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

[Provided] This is a phase 4, prospective, multicenter, randomized, open-label trial. The event adjudication committee (EAC) and imaging core laboratory are blinded. Allocation is 1:1 to andexanet or usual care.

[Provided] Usual care is investigator-selected treatment other than andexanet, including no treatment, initiated within 3 hours.

[Clarification Required] Specify the time origin for the 3-hour usual-care initiation window; it determines compliance with the intervention definition.

[Provided] Andexanet is given without crossover. The low dose is a 400-mg intravenous bolus over about 15 minutes, followed by 480 mg at 4 mg/min for 120 minutes. The high dose is an 800-mg intravenous bolus over up to about 30 minutes, followed by 960 mg at 8 mg/min for 120 minutes.

[Provided] Regimen selection uses the preceding FXa inhibitor, its last dose, and its timing. Participants qualifying through local anti-fXa activity >100 ng/mL with a last dose >15 hours ago or at an unknown time receive high-dose andexanet. Andexanet should start no later than 30 minutes after randomization and preferably within 2 hours of baseline brain imaging.

[Clarification Required] Supply the remaining drug-, dose-, and timing-specific regimen-selection rules and clarify the scope of the no-crossover rule, including treatment given as rescue. These decisions govern administration and deviation classification.

### 2.2 Randomization

[Provided] A sponsor-generated randomization scheme is used after consent, eligibility and baseline assessments, and documentation of intended usual care.

[Provided] Randomization is stratified by intended usual-care agent—plasma-derived coagulation-factor concentrate versus other—and symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time: <180 versus ≥180 minutes. Minimization or blocking is not specified.

[Proposed] Use a secure centralized allocation system and retain an audit trail of assignments and stratification entries. Rationale: this protects allocation integrity; it assumes the required system can be implemented at all sites.

### 2.3 Sample Size

[Provided] Approximately 900 participants are planned, 450 per group. A preplanned interim sample size re-estimation (SSR) may increase total enrollment to 1200.

[Provided] The calculation uses binary effective-hemostasis rates of 70% for usual care and 80% for andexanet: a 10-percentage-point difference and a 33% reduction in the risk of not achieving effective hemostasis. The andexanet rate derives from prior andexanet efficacy results; the 10% difference was considered clinically meaningful.

[Provided] The target is approximately 90% power at an overall two-sided 0.05 alpha, accounting for the interim analysis, using a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel (CMH) comparison stratified by symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time. The calculation accounts for early discontinuation, but no separate numerical inflation is specified.

[Provided] A separate variability assumption, required time-to-event count, non-inferiority or equivalence margin, and cluster design effect are not applicable to the stated calculation. Calculation software is not specified.

[Provided] At interim, conditional power of 30%–90% permits a DSMB-recommended increase to a total of up to 1200. No increase is permitted if conditional power is <30% or >90%.

[Clarification Required] Specify the conditional-power calculation, interim information used, and rule for selecting an increased sample size. These are needed to implement SSR and verify its operating characteristics.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

[Provided] For effective-hemostasis proportions, the primary hypothesis is \(H_0:\pi_{\mathrm{UC}}-\pi_{\mathrm{andexanet}}=0\) versus \(H_A:\pi_{\mathrm{UC}}-\pi_{\mathrm{andexanet}}\ne0\).

[Provided] The primary objective succeeds when the andexanet proportion is higher under the prespecified alpha-spending thresholds. The secondary endpoint is tested only after a significant primary endpoint.

[Clarification Required] Specify whether a significant primary result favoring usual care opens the secondary gate, and specify the secondary test’s alpha and interim treatment. These decisions determine confirmatory interpretation.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

[Provided] A preplanned interim analysis includes SSR, with DSMB recommendations governed by the stated conditional-power ranges. Alpha spending accounts for interim analysis.

[Clarification Required] Specify the interim timing and information fraction, alpha-spending function and efficacy boundaries, any stopping rules, and the method preserving overall type-I error after SSR. Without these, interim decisions and confirmatory inference cannot be implemented.

[Proposed] Restrict unblinded interim results to the DSMB and independent supporting statisticians; preserve EAC and imaging-core blinding. Rationale: this limits operational bias; it assumes those parties can be separated from trial conduct.

[Clarification Required] Define the DSMB’s membership, access, recommendation process, and decision authority. These matter for independent oversight and implementation of an enrollment change or stopping recommendation.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

[Provided] Final analyses occur after study completion and final database lock. If the study stops after interim, DCO1 supports confirmatory efficacy and DCO2 captures all participants for safety and efficacy sensitivity analyses.

[Provided] Anti-fXa is assessed at baseline and 1 and 2 hours; thrombin generation at baseline and 1 and 12 hours; and TFPI at baseline, 1, 2 and 12 hours and Day 30. CT/MRI is performed at baseline and about 12 hours.

[Provided] NIHSS and GCS are assessed at baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 72 hours; the 12- and 24-hour NIHSS assessments are blinded. Premorbid mRS is assessed at baseline; mRS and EQ-5D-5L at Day 30 or early termination.

[Provided] Follow-up includes Day 7 and 14 calls and a Day 30 (+7) follow-up. Participants with positive anti-andexanet antibodies have an additional approximately Day 120 visit.

[Clarification Required] Define DCO1 and DCO2 cutoffs, locking procedures, and the handling of participants or outcomes incomplete at each cutoff. These determine the confirmatory and sensitivity datasets.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

[Provided] Overall two-sided alpha is 0.05 for the primary design, accounting for interim analysis; primary success also requires a higher andexanet effective-hemostasis proportion.

[Proposed] Report treatment estimates with two-sided 95% confidence intervals, alongside stage-specific test results. Rationale: intervals convey precision; this assumes ordinary intervals are clearly distinguished from any intervals adjusted for interim monitoring or adaptation.

[Clarification Required] Specify the boundary-adjusted confidence-interval and p-value procedures after interim monitoring or SSR. These are needed for inference consistent with the final decision rule.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

[Proposed] Record deviations, their timing, cause, and impact; classify important deviations before unblinding analysis data. Rationale: consistent classification supports interpretable sensitivity analyses; this assumes a prespecified deviation review process.

[Clarification Required] Define important deviations, including regimen, timing, eligibility, and prohibited-treatment violations, and their analysis consequences. These decisions affect supportive populations and interpretation.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

[Proposed] Use all randomized participants, analyzed by assigned group, for the primary intention-to-treat analysis. Rationale: this preserves the randomized comparison; it assumes outcomes and missing-data rules are defined for every randomized participant.

[Proposed] Define a supportive per-protocol population using prospectively specified important deviations, and analyze by assigned group. Rationale: it assesses robustness to major departures; it assumes deviation classification is made without using comparative outcomes.

[Proposed] Include all randomized participants in safety summaries, with exposure to andexanet and other treatment recorded. Rationale: this retains participants receiving no treatment and permits exposure-based review; it assumes treatment and timing are reliably recorded.

[Clarification Required] Specify safety grouping for participants who receive treatment different from assignment, including any crossover or no treatment. This affects attribution and denominators.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

[Proposed] Maintain a screening log with consent status, eligibility results, enrollment, and reasons for nonrandomization, subject to applicable privacy rules. Rationale: it supports assessment of selection and recruitment; it assumes these data may lawfully be collected.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

[Provided] Eligible participants are adults with acute intracerebral bleeding of specified volume, CT/MRI within 2 hours before randomization, and symptom onset to baseline scan <6 hours.

[Provided] Qualifying anticoagulation is oral apixaban, rivaroxaban, or edoxaban at protocol-specified doses within 15 hours. Randomization outside 15 hours or after an unknown last-dose time requires standard-of-care local anti-fXa >100 ng/mL within 2 hours before consent and mandates high-dose andexanet.

[Provided] Consent may be obtained from the participant or LAR/proxy, or by emergency consent where lawful.

[Provided] Exclusions include planned hematoma-affecting surgery within 12 hours; GCS <7, with a specified non-neurologic sedation exception; NIHSS >35; hematoma volume ≤0.5 or ≥60 mL; inability to use the same CT/MRI modality; and expected survival <1 month.

[Provided] Other exclusions include recent thrombotic/cardiovascular events, cardiogenic shock, severe sepsis/septic shock, pregnancy or lactation, restricted anticoagulants or blood products, prior or planned andexanet, investigational drug, tumor-related bleeding, and hypersensitivity. Pregnancy testing and contraception precautions are required.

[Clarification Required] Specify qualifying drug doses, the sedation exception, lookback periods and definitions for recent events and restricted treatments, pregnancy testing and contraception requirements, and the precise hypersensitivity and investigational-drug restrictions. These are necessary for reproducible eligibility decisions.

### 4.3 Recruitment

[Proposed] Recruit consecutively where feasible and report screening, randomization, and enrollment by site and group. Rationale: this helps assess recruitment and selection; it assumes site screening records are sufficiently complete.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

[Provided] Follow-up consists of the specified Day 7 and 14 calls, Day 30 (+7) follow-up, and the approximately Day 120 visit for positive anti-andexanet antibodies.

[Proposed] Document withdrawal reason, date, treatment discontinuation, and whether outcome follow-up remains permitted; distinguish treatment discontinuation from withdrawal of all follow-up. Rationale: this supports retention and missing-data assessment; it assumes consent and applicable law permit continued collection.

[Clarification Required] Specify procedures for withdrawal, loss to follow-up, early termination, and permissible collection after withdrawal. These determine which outcomes may be analyzed.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

[Proposed] Summarize demographics, hemorrhage and imaging characteristics, baseline neurologic measures, anticoagulant and last-dose timing, anti-fXa activity, relevant medical history, and randomization strata by assigned group. Rationale: these describe comparability and prognosis; this assumes the variables are collected.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

[Provided] The primary outcome is effective hemostasis at 12 hours after randomization.

[Clarification Required] Define effective hemostasis, including its components, thresholds, imaging and clinical assessment rules, adjudication, assessment window, and treatment of death or rescue therapy. These are essential to determine each participant’s outcome and perform the primary analysis.

[Proposed] Report the number and percentage achieving effective hemostasis in each group and the andexanet-minus-usual-care absolute difference. Rationale: these make the treatment effect interpretable; this assumes the binary outcome has been defined.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

[Provided] The secondary endpoint is percent change in anti-fXa activity from baseline to nadir during the first 2 hours after randomization.

[Proposed] Calculate percent change as \(100\times(\text{nadir}-\text{baseline})/\text{baseline}\). Rationale: this expresses change relative to baseline; it assumes a positive, quantifiable baseline value and that negative values represent reductions.

[Clarification Required] Specify the samples and windows eligible for the nadir, whether baseline can contribute, assay and quantification rules, and handling of incomplete samples or zero/unquantifiable baseline values. These decisions affect the endpoint and its comparability.

[Provided] Exploratory efficacy outcomes include thrombin generation, neurologic deterioration, mRS, NIHSS/GCS, imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa/hemostasis correlation, EQ-5D-5L, and utility-weighted mRS.

[Clarification Required] Define exploratory outcome thresholds, rescue therapy, imaging-only hemostasis, utility weights, and any time points not specified in the assessment schedule. These are needed for reproducible analyses.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

[Provided] The planned primary comparison is a CMH comparison of effective-hemostasis proportions stratified by symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time.

[Proposed] Use the randomized time stratum for the primary CMH test and report the group proportions and absolute difference; examine intended usual-care stratum in a supportive analysis. Rationale: this retains the specified primary stratification while considering the other allocation factor; it assumes recorded strata are available.

[Clarification Required] Specify how incorrect or missing stratification entries, sparse strata, and missing primary outcomes enter the CMH analysis, including any pooling across imputations. These choices affect the test and its type-I error.

[Proposed] Compare secondary percent change using a model including assigned group, baseline anti-fXa, and imaging-time stratum; report the adjusted group contrast and interval. Rationale: baseline adjustment can improve precision; it assumes model diagnostics support the analysis.

[Proposed] Summarize repeated neurologic and laboratory measures by group and time; analyze Day 30 mRS as an ordinal outcome, and summarize rescue therapy and imaging-only hemostasis as proportions. Rationale: these approaches match the measurement scales; they assume adequate data and finalized endpoint definitions.

[Proposed] Treat exploratory comparisons as hypothesis-generating and report estimates and intervals without confirmatory claims. Rationale: numerous outcomes are assessed; it assumes no additional confirmatory multiplicity plan is established.

### 5.4 Missing Data

[Proposed] Summarize missingness by group, outcome, visit, and reason. For missing binary primary outcomes, consider multiple imputation using assigned group, randomization stratum, and relevant baseline predictors, with a missing-at-random assumption. Rationale: this uses observed prognostic information; validity depends on that assumption and a prespecified model.

[Proposed] Assess robustness to departures from missing at random using treatment-group-specific unfavorable-outcome scenarios and report complete-case results as supportive. Rationale: missingness may be informative; this assumes the scenarios are defined before comparative analysis.

[Clarification Required] Prespecify the primary missing-data method, imputation variables and pooling, and how death, treatment discontinuation, rescue, and unavailable imaging are distinguished from missing outcomes. These decisions determine the estimand and confirmatory test.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

[Proposed] Examine treatment effects by imaging-time stratum, intended usual-care category, and qualifying FXa inhibitor, reporting subgroup estimates and interaction assessments. Rationale: these may reveal heterogeneity; it assumes subgroup data and sample sizes are adequate.

[Proposed] Analyze anti-fxa/hemostasis association descriptively and evaluate TFPI over scheduled visits. Rationale: these support mechanistic interpretation; they assume assays are comparable over time and exploratory analyses are not interpreted as causal.

### 5.6 Harms

[Provided] Safety objectives include TEs, mortality, invasive procedures, hospital use, AEs, vital signs, and immunogenicity.

[Proposed] Summarize participants with each event, event counts, timing, and denominators by group; list serious events and deaths, and summarize antibody results and additional follow-up. Rationale: this describes both frequency and clinical detail; it assumes event collection and adjudication rules are defined.

[Clarification Required] Specify AE and TE definitions, collection windows, seriousness and attribution rules, adjudication procedures, and analysis of incomplete follow-up. These are necessary for comparable safety summaries.
