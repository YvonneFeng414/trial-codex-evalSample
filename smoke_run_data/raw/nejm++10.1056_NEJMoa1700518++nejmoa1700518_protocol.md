# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

After initial anticoagulation for DVT or PE, recurrent VTE remains clinically important. Long-term VKA therapy is effective but carries bleeding, monitoring, and dose-adjustment burden; ASA has modest recurrence-prevention efficacy. This study evaluates whether reduced-dose rivaroxaban can maintain efficacy while improving safety, using ASA as comparator. *(Source: Protocol §2.1–2.6, pages 15–18)*

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- Evaluate superiority of rivaroxaban 10 mg and 20 mg versus ASA 100 mg for fatal or nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE. *(Source: Protocol §3, page 19; final SAP §2, page 75)*

**Secondary Objectives**

- Evaluate superiority of each rivaroxaban dose versus ASA for fatal/nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE, MI, ischemic stroke, or systemic non-CNS embolism. *(Source: Protocol §3, page 19; final SAP §2, page 75)*

**Safety Objectives**

- **Principal safety objective:** Document incidence of major bleeding. *(Source: Protocol §3, page 19; final SAP §2, page 75)*
- **Secondary safety objective:** Document incidence of CRNM bleeding. *(Source: Protocol §3, page 19; final SAP §2, page 75)*

**Exploratory Objectives**

The protocol labels the following as **Additional study objectives** (not exploratory objectives): *(Source: Protocol §3, page 19; final SAP §2, page 75)*

- a. Evaluate the composite of nonfatal symptomatic VTE and all-cause mortality. *(Source: Protocol §3, page 19; final SAP §2, page 75)*
- b. Evaluate the composite of major bleeding and recurrent VTE. *(Source: Protocol §3, page 19; final SAP §2, page 75)*
- c. Evaluate the composite of major bleeding, recurrent VTE, MI, ischemic stroke, and systemic non-CNS embolism. *(Source: Protocol §3, page 19; final SAP §2, page 75)*

Exploratory analyses describe composites of the primary or secondary efficacy outcome with symptomatic venous thrombosis in other locations and net clinical benefit. *(Source: final SAP §§3.1, 6.2.7, pages 76, 97)*

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Phase III, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, active-comparator, event-driven trial; all efficacy and safety events are adjudicated centrally and final analyses use adjudication results. *(Source: Protocol §4, page 19; final SAP §3, pages 75–76)*
- **Allocation Ratio:** 1:1:1. *(Source: final SAP §9.4.3, page 109)*
- **Intervention Summary:** Oral once-daily treatment with food; nominal duration 12 months, although final randomized patients may receive 6 months only. *(Source: Protocol §§4, 4.4.1, pages 19, 21; final SAP §3, pages 75–76)*
  - Rivaroxaban 10 mg once daily.
  - Rivaroxaban 20 mg once daily.
  - ASA 100 mg once daily.
  - Double-dummy treatment supplies one rivaroxaban-or-placebo tablet and one ASA-or-placebo tablet daily. *(Source: Protocol §4.4.1, page 21)*
  - First dose is ≥24 hours after previous VTE treatment. With VKA, INR >3.0 delays randomization; INR 2.5–3.0 permits randomization but delays first intake to the next day; INR <2.5 permits dosing. A missed dose may be made up only on the same day; no double dose is allowed. *(Source: Protocol §4.4.1.1, page 21)*
  - Six months’ medication is initially supplied; IxRS decides at visit 4 whether a further six months is supplied. Temporary interruption may restart if total treatment does not exceed 12 months; last randomized patients receive no second supply. *(Source: Protocol §§4, 4.7, pages 19–20, 22)*

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** Central interactive voice/web response system (IxRS); a unique randomization number identifies treatment allocation. *(Source: Protocol §§4.4, 4.6, pages 21–22)*
- **Method:** The investigator provides IxRS with study-center identification and DVT/PE presentation; detailed assignment procedures are in the IxRS manual. *(Source: Protocol §4.4, page 21)*
- **Stratification Factors:** Country and investigator-reported index event: DVT only versus PE with or without DVT. *(Source: Protocol §4.4, page 21; final SAP §6.2.2, page 92)*
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1–111 reviewed)*

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** Approximately 950 patients per arm; 2,850 total, subject to adjustment using observed overall primary-event incidence. *(Source: Protocol §6.8, page 29; final SAP §9.4.3, page 109)*
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Confirmed primary efficacy outcome: fatal or nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE. *(Source: Protocol §§5.1, 6.8, pages 24, 29)*
- **Outcome Type:** Time-to-first-event survival outcome. *(Source: Protocol §6.8, page 29; final SAP §9.4.3, pages 109–110)*
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** ASA event rate 5% per year, modeled exponentially. *(Source: final SAP §9.4.3, page 109)*
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** HR 0.30 for rivaroxaban 20 mg versus ASA and HR 0.40 for rivaroxaban 10 mg versus ASA. *(Source: Protocol §6.8, page 29; final SAP §§9.4.1–9.4.2, pages 108–109)*
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** HR 0.30 (20 mg) and 0.40 (10 mg) versus ASA. *(Source: Protocol §6.8, page 29)*
- **Variability Assumption:** Not reported as a separate variability parameter; simulation used exponential event, discontinuation, and withdrawal processes. *(Source: final SAP §9.4.3, pages 109–110; pages 1–111 reviewed)*
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Two-sided 0.05 for the sequential superiority tests. *(Source: Protocol §6.4.1, page 28; final SAP §6.2.2, page 92)*
- **Power:** 90%; the 10,000-trial simulation estimated joint Cox-regression power 0.901. *(Source: Protocol §6.8, page 29; final SAP §§9.4.3, pages 109–111)*
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Stratified Cox proportional-hazards superiority analysis with upper 95% CI bound below 1; stratified log-rank testing is supportive. *(Source: Protocol §§6.4.1, 6.8, pages 28–29; final SAP §6.2.2, pages 92–93)*
- **Required Number of Events:** At least 80 confirmed primary efficacy outcomes. *(Source: Protocol §4, page 19; final SAP §3, page 76)*
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable; the planned comparisons are superiority tests. *(Source: Protocol §6.4.1, page 28)*
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** Simulations assume exponential annual 10% treatment discontinuation and 1% loss to follow-up/withdrawn consent; last subjects receive 6 months’ treatment. After simulated discontinuation, simulated event time uses inferior-control treatment; losses before simulated event are censored. *(Source: final SAP §9.4.3, page 109)*
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable; this is an individually randomized, non-clustered trial. *(Source: Protocol §4, page 19)*
- **Basis for Assumptions:** The 20-mg assumption derives from an adjusted indirect comparison of EINSTEIN Extension and ASA pooled evidence; the 10-mg assumption reflects orthopedic rivaroxaban and apixaban extended-treatment evidence. *(Source: final SAP §§9.4.1–9.4.2, pages 108–109)*
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** Enrollment number is adjusted from blinded pooled projection based on observed primary-event incidence. Projection iterates total N until ≥80 total events has probability ≥0.50, also presenting alternatives such as probability about 0.80. *(Source: Protocol §6.8, page 29; final SAP §9.2, pages 105–106)*
- **Software Used for Calculation:** SAS Macro by Shih and three-arm simulations. *(Source: Protocol §6.8, page 29)*
- **Sample Size Rationale:** The event-driven design targets 80 events to demonstrate superiority of both doses. Simulations run 10,000 trials, apply administrative censoring at one year/end of trial, select trials/observation time reaching target, fit Cox and log-rank tests, and define joint power as both 20-mg/ASA and 10-mg/ASA HR 95% CI upper bounds ≤1 (0.901). The 20-mg indirect estimate was HR 0.264 (95% CI 0.12–0.59) from placebo-controlled rivaroxaban and ASA evidence; 0.30 was conservative. For 10 mg, 0.40 (60% reduction) was set versus an expected 0.30 from reduced-dose rationale. *(Source: Protocol §6.8, page 29; final SAP §§9.4.1–9.4.3, pages 108–111)*

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

The primary framework is a fixed sequence controlling familywise error: first H0: HR(20 mg/ASA)=1 versus two-sided H1: HR≠1; only if significant, test H0: HR(10 mg/ASA)=1 versus H1: HR≠1. Each test is two-sided at 0.05; 20 mg is superior if its HR 95% CI upper bound is <1, and 10 mg is superior only if 20 mg is superior and its upper bound is <1. *(Source: Protocol §6.4.1, page 28; final SAP §6.2.2, page 92)*

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** No formal interim efficacy or futility analysis is planned. *(Source: Protocol §6.7, page 29; final SAP §4.4, pages 78–79)*
- **Stopping Guidelines:** The DMC regularly reviews SAEs, confirmed efficacy outcomes, and bleeding and may recommend stopping recruitment or treatment for safety. *(Source: Protocol §5.5.3, page 27; final SAP §4.4, page 79)*
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** Not applicable to formal interim analysis; primary fixed-sequence testing controls familywise error. *(Source: final SAP §§4.4, 6.2.2, pages 79, 92)*

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** SAP revisions are issued before database lock; final analyses use adjudicated outcomes through each patient’s individual intended treatment limit. Standard limits are day 353, visit day if day 353–367, or day 367; defined administrative shortening rules also apply. *(Source: Protocol §6.1, page 27; final SAP §6.2.1, pages 89–91)*
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Baseline; Day 1; Day 30±7; Day 90±7; Day 180±7; Day 270±7; Day 360±7; and a 30±7-day post-treatment observation visit. Potential outcomes/AEs are elicited at all contacts. *(Source: Protocol flow chart, page 12; Protocol §5, pages 23–24)*
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Visit 7 may be telephone or clinic and occurs early (30±7 days after stopping) after premature discontinuation. Other visits continue after premature treatment stop unless participation is refused; investigators may arrange additional visits. *(Source: Protocol §5, pages 23–24)*

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** Two-sided 0.05 for each primary fixed-sequence comparison. *(Source: Protocol §6.4.1, page 28)*
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** Fixed-sequence hierarchy controls primary familywise error; secondary, additional, and safety p-values have no multiplicity adjustment. *(Source: Protocol §§6.4.1–6.5, pages 28–29; final SAP page 81)*
- **Confidence Intervals:** Cox HRs have 95% CIs for each active-versus-ASA and 20-mg-versus-10-mg comparison across primary, secondary, additional, and safety outcomes. Kaplan–Meier cumulative-risk absolute differences and 95% CIs use the Com-Nougue/Rodary/Patte method with Greenwood variance. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.2–6.2.4, 6.4, pages 92–93, 97)*

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** Overall active-medication compliance is tablets taken divided by active treatment duration; categories are missing, <50%, 50% to <80%, and ≥80%. *(Source: final SAP §6.1.6, page 88)*
- **Adherence Assessment:** Unused medication is returned/counted at visits 4 and 6 or premature discontinuation; interruptions and their reasons are summarized. *(Source: Protocol §4.11, page 23; final SAP §6.1.6, page 88)*
- **Adherence Assessment:** Exposure is summarized as missing, <1 week, ≥1 week–<1 month, ≥1–<3, ≥3–<6, ≥6–<9, ≥9–<12, and ≥12 months; interruption days are excluded from compliance if VKA, non-topical heparin/LMWH, direct thrombin inhibitor, apixaban, edoxaban, fondaparinux, or rivaroxaban prophylaxis occurred. *(Source: final SAP §6.1.6, page 88)*
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** PPS excludes subjects with compliance <80% or other major protocol deviations; further detail is in the Protocol Deviation Document and Validity Review Report. *(Source: final SAP §5.1, pages 80–81)*
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Investigators must explain and document deviations. Final analysis-set decisions and relevant evaluation decisions are documented at Validity Review Meeting in the Validity Review Report; the Protocol Deviation Document also governs PPS details. *(Source: Protocol §11.1, page 34; final SAP §§4.6, 5.1, pages 80–81)*

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** No population is labeled intention-to-treat. The FAS is the primary analysis set: all randomized patients receiving ≥1 study-medication dose. *(Source: Protocol §§6.2, 6.4, pages 27–28; final SAP §5.1, page 80)*
- **Per Protocol:** PPS excludes patients with <80% compliance or other major protocol deviations. *(Source: final SAP §5.1, pages 80–81)*
- **Other Populations:** FAS excludes untreated randomized patients only where objectively measured pre-randomization eligibility violation and/or the decision not to start was uninfluenced by allocation; final set assignment is made at Validity Review Meeting. *(Source: final SAP §5.1, page 80)*

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

Screening collects consent, demographics, VTE risk factors, medical history, pregnancy testing when applicable, pre-randomization anticoagulation, index-event imaging/adjudication package, creatinine, Hb, platelets, and INR for VKA switching. Creatinine/Hb/platelets may be assessed within 1 month before randomization. *(Source: Protocol flow chart, page 12; Protocol §5, page 23)*

At all visits/contacts, new/ongoing concomitant medication and potential outcomes/AEs are elicited. Only SAEs, efficacy/safety outcomes, pregnancy/outcomes, Hb <10 g/dL, white cells <3.0×10^9/L and/or neutrophils <0.5×10^9/L, platelets <50×10^9/L, and possibly related allergic skin/systemic reactions require reporting; the latter events require reporting within 24 hours. *(Source: Protocol flow chart, page 12; Protocol §7.1, page 30)*

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

Eligible patients have confirmed symptomatic DVT and/or PE, received 6–12 months of anticoagulation without interruption >1 week, and provide written consent. *(Source: Protocol §4.2, page 20)*

Exclusions are: *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*

1. Country-specific legal lower age limit. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
2. Indication for therapeutic-dose anticoagulants. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
3. Hypersensitivity to investigational or comparator therapy. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
4. Any contraindication in local investigational/comparator labeling. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
5. Indication for antiplatelet therapy or conventional NSAID. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
6. Hepatic disease with coagulopathy and clinically relevant bleeding risk. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
7. Calculated CrCl <30 mL/min. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
8. Active bleeding or anticoagulant-contraindicating high bleeding risk. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
9. Life expectancy <6 months. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
10. Systemic strong dual CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors: HIV protease inhibitors, ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, or posaconazole. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
11. Childbearing potential without proper contraception, pregnancy, or breastfeeding. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*
12. Investigational drug/device study participation within 30 days before randomization. *(Source: Protocol §4.3, pages 20–21)*

### 4.3 Recruitment

Approximately 230 centers in over 20 countries are planned to recruit 2,850 patients over about 27 months; mean site enrollment is 5.5 patients/year. *(Source: Protocol synopsis, pages 7–11)*

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Treatment may stop at patient request, investigator judgment, pregnancy, or exceptional Bayer request; untreated randomizations are not replaced. *(Source: Protocol §§4.1, 8, pages 20, 32)*
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** Patients stopping treatment continue planned visits to their individual intended end unless consent is withdrawn; consent withdrawal prompts a request for outcome/AE follow-up or at least vital status. Data collected before withdrawal are retained. *(Source: Protocol §8, page 32; final SAP §4.2, page 77)*
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** Refusal of further outcome/AE/vital-status collection is respected and documented. Post-study anticoagulant treatment is collected at visit 7; all patients have one-month post-treatment observation. *(Source: Protocol §§5, 8, pages 23–24, 32)*
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Reason is recorded in eCRF and medical records; counts/reasons for premature study/treatment discontinuation are displayed by treatment, with KM plots of time to treatment/observation end. *(Source: Protocol §8, page 32; final SAP §4.2, page 77)*
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Temporary treatment discontinuation can restart only if total duration does not exceed the intended 12 months; permanently discontinued treatment is thereafter at investigator discretion. No prematurely stopped randomized patient is replaced. *(Source: Protocol §§4.1, 8, pages 20, 32)*

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Demographics, history, index-event presentation/risk factors, baseline laboratory values, concomitant medication, and pretreatment are summarized. Medical history uses MedDRA SOC/HLT/PT. *(Source: Protocol §5, page 23; final SAP §§6.1.1–6.1.5, pages 81–88)*
- **Summary Methods:** By treatment and overall, continuous data: nonmissing/missing n, mean, SD, minimum, quartiles, median, maximum; categorical data: n (%). No inferential treatment-group comparisons are performed. *(Source: final SAP §§4.1, 6.1.1, pages 77, 81)*
- **Summary Methods:** Closest pre-randomization lab value is used; if absent, creatinine/weight/height through day 90 after randomization, and Hb/platelets through 30 days after first dose only if above lower reference limit, are eligible. Cockcroft–Gault is used for CrCl; if weight is missing but age, sex, and race are available, CKD-EPI is used. *(Source: final SAP §4.5.1, page 79)*

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**Outcome: Fatal or nonfatal symptomatic recurrent VTE** *(Source: Protocol §5.1, pages 24–25; final SAP §3.1, page 76)*

- **Definition:** Composite includes recurrent symptomatic PE/DVT and fatal PE; final SAP clarifies unexplained death where PE cannot be ruled out. PE and DVT require specified objective imaging findings, with therapeutic anticoagulation >48 hours/thrombolysis supporting confirmation if objective testing is absent. *(Source: Protocol §5.1, pages 24–25; final SAP §3.1, page 76)*
- **Definition:** PE confirmation requires new segmental-or-proximal CT filling defect; new/extended defect or sudden cutoff in >2.5-mm vessels on angiography; ≥75% segment perfusion defect with normal ventilation; or inconclusive PE imaging plus lower-extremity DVT by CUS/venography. DVT requires abnormal CUS or venographic defect when no prior study, or new abnormal compression/substantial (≥4 mm) thrombus-diameter increase or new/extended venographic defect when prior study exists. Fatal PE is objectively/autopsy confirmed PE or unexplained death where PE cannot be excluded. *(Source: Protocol §5.1, page 24)*
- **Measurement Units:** First adjudicated event; HR, 95% CI, KM cumulative risk and absolute risk difference are reported. *(Source: final SAP §6.2.2, page 92)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Time from randomization to first event through individual intended treatment limit; usual limits are day 353–367, with defined administrative-shortening paths. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.1–6.2.2, pages 89–92)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** CIAC adjudication defines events. Censoring applies at the defined last visit/intended limit for event-free patients, loss, non-VTE death, consent withdrawal, or administrative censoring; special rules apply after permanent discontinuation for antiplatelet/NSAID indication. *(Source: final SAP §6.2.1, pages 89–91)*

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**Outcome: Secondary efficacy composite** *(Source: Protocol §5.1, pages 24–25; final SAP §3.1, page 76)*

- **Definition:** Primary efficacy outcome plus MI, ischemic stroke, or systemic non-CNS embolism. MI, including PCI/CABG-specific biomarker/Q-wave criteria; ischemic stroke; and systemic non-CNS embolism are centrally adjudicated using protocol definitions. *(Source: Protocol §5.1, pages 24–25)*
- **Definition:** Non-PCI/CABG MI requires ischemic symptoms with troponin/CK-MB >ULN, new pathological Q waves in ≥2 contiguous ECG leads, or autopsy confirmation. PCI MI requires CK-MB/CK >3×ULN within 24 hours with normal baseline, ≥50% rise from stable/decreasing elevated baseline, or new Q waves; CABG MI requires CK-MB/CK >5×ULN plus Q waves or >10×ULN with/without Q waves within 24 hours. Ischemic stroke is new sudden focal deficit of presumed cerebrovascular cause demonstrated by brain imaging, not reversible within 24 hours and not tumor/seizure, or autopsy confirmed. Systemic non-CNS embolism is abrupt vascular insufficiency with clinical/radiological arterial occlusion in the absence of another plausible mechanism. *(Source: Protocol §5.1, pages 25–26)*
- **Measurement Units:** First adjudicated event; Cox HR and 95% CI. *(Source: final SAP §6.2.3, page 93)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Time to first event during individual intended treatment period. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.1, 6.2.3, pages 89–93)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Composite is derived from adjudicated component events; component incidence is described overall and by randomization stratum. *(Source: final SAP §6.2.3, page 93)*

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** FAS time-to-first-event analysis uses stratified Cox regression (index event stratum) with randomized three-level treatment; KM risks and absolute risk differences are provided. Stratified log-rank is supportive. Components are descriptive by treatment, stratum, region and country. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.2, pages 92–93)*
- **Primary Analysis Methods:** The three treatment levels are ASA, rivaroxaban 10 mg, and rivaroxaban 20 mg; HR/95% CI also compares 20 mg with 10 mg. The stratified SAS implementation uses PROC PHREG, Efron ties, treatment class and index-event STRATA; PROC LIFETEST supplies stratified log-rank testing. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.2, pages 92–93; Appendix §9.3.1, pages 106–107)*
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Primary model includes categorical treatment and stratifies by index event. Subgroup models include subgroup covariates and may estimate adjusted HRs. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.2, 6.2.5, pages 92–96)*
- **Assumption Checks:** Proportional hazards is checked by log(-log) plots, scaled Schoenfeld residual plots, and treatment-by-log(time) interaction. *(Source: Protocol §6.4.1, page 28; final SAP §6.2.2, page 92)*
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** For sparse/nonconvergent subgroup or safety Cox models, use an unstratified model and Firth penalized likelihood; if still nonconvergent, no HR is presented. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.5, 6.4, pages 93, 97–98)*
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Supportive on-treatment analysis through 2 days post-last intake (not beyond intended limit) is performed in FAS and PPS. For potentially informative dropout, Bayesian exponential MCMC analyses use IMHR 1.5 and 2.0 for rivaroxaban versus 1 for ASA. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.2, 6.2.6, pages 93, 97)*
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Efficacy subgroups include sex, age, race/ethnicity, weight/BMI, cardiac/pulmonary disease, index event/extent, region, thrombophilia, cancer, VTE history/risk profile, renal function, pretreatment duration, fragility, and diabetes. Wald interaction tests are used; p<0.05 triggers two-sided Gail–Simon qualitative-interaction testing. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.5, pages 93–96)*
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Age cutoffs are 60, 65 and 75 years; weight <90/≥90 kg, BMI <30/≥30 kg/m², CrCl ≥80, 50–<80, <50 mL/min; fragility is age >75, weight ≤50 kg, or CrCl <50 mL/min. Index-event extent, regions, unprovoked/provoked and five-/three-category CIAC risk profiles, cancer, thrombophilia, and diabetes use the prespecified Table 6–7 levels. Separate level-specific models require ≥1 event per treatment group/subgroup. Wald interaction degrees of freedom equal number of modeled subgroup levels minus one. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.5, pages 93–96)*
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Safety subgroup analyses cover sex, age, weight, cancer, renal function, race/ethnicity, fragility, and RIETE low/intermediate/high bleeding-risk score; the same adjusted Cox, level-specific, interaction, simplified-model and Firth rules apply. *(Source: final SAP §§6.4.1, pages 98–100)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Menstrual bleeding presence, duration, intensity relative to pre-anticoagulation, and actions are summarized by visit/arm. Treatment-emergent low laboratory abnormalities and special-interest AEs are tabulated for Hb, white-cell/neutrophil, platelet, and allergic events. ISTH CRNM and major-or-CRNM analyses use stratified Cox with unstratified/Firth fallback. *(Source: final SAP §§6.4, 6.4.2, pages 97–100)*

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** Primary time-to-event censoring assumes censoring due to event-free status, loss, other-cause death, consent withdrawal, and administrative censoring is independent of outcome. Potentially informative dropout is addressed in the stated IMHR sensitivity analyses. *(Source: Protocol §6.4.1, page 28; final SAP §§6.2.1, 6.2.6, pages 89–91, 97)*
- **Handling Methods:** Missing baseline/demographic values are counted by arm. Predefined deterministic rules impute incomplete index-event, pretreatment, efficacy/safety/AE, medication start, and medication-stop dates; an incomplete event date uses randomization/first-dose and available event/diagnostic-date constraints and never exceeds death date. *(Source: final SAP §§4.3, pages 77–79)*
- **Handling Methods:** All missing/partial values are listed as recorded. For index DVT/PE, a missing day uses complete pretreatment start date or day 15; missing month/year uses complete pretreatment start date or remains missing. Missing pretreatment start is the later of first available month/year (or randomization year−1) and complete index-event date; stop date with month/year is the earlier of randomization−7 and first day of reported month. *(Source: final SAP §4.3, pages 77–78)*
- **Handling Methods:** For efficacy/safety/AE dates, missing day uses the maximum of randomization, first dose, and first day of reported month; missing year/complete event date uses first day of year, or (for recurrent VTE/vascular event) diagnostic test date−1 when complete, in each case not after death. Missing medication start date/time becomes randomization date/time; an unclarified pre-randomization start becomes randomization. Stop day becomes day 15 capped at last pre-observational visit/death; missing day/month or complete stop date becomes last pre-observational visit/death. *(Source: final SAP §4.3, pages 78–79)*

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

Three additional composites use Cox/log-rank methods with HR/95% CI and unadjusted multiplicity: nonfatal VTE/all-cause mortality; major bleeding/primary efficacy outcome; and major bleeding/secondary efficacy outcome. Exploratory work describes venous thrombosis-in-other-location composites, calculates net clinical benefit using primary efficacy benefit and major-bleeding harm on an absolute-risk-difference scale, and applies Andersen–Gill robust-variance intensity modeling for recurrent major/CRNM bleeding. *(Source: final SAP §§6.2.4, 6.2.7, 6.4.3, pages 93, 97, 100–101)*

Additional safety outcomes are major-or-CRNM bleeding, ISTH-defined CRNM bleeding, major-or-ISTH-CRNM bleeding, and all confirmed bleeding categorized as major, CRNM, or trivial under both protocol and ISTH definitions. All confirmed treatment-emergent bleeding categories are tabulated; KM and time-to-first Cox analyses are provided for ISTH nonmajor/major-or-CRNM outcomes. *(Source: final SAP §§3.1, 6.4.2–6.4.3, pages 76, 100–101)*

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** AE is any untoward medical occurrence after pharmaceutical product, regardless of causality; SAE includes death, life-threatening event, hospitalization/prolongation, congenital anomaly/birth defect, persistent/significant disability, or another important medical event. Major bleeding is overt bleeding with ≥2 g/dL Hb fall, ≥2-unit transfusion, critical site, or contribution to death; protocol CRNM is nonmajor overt bleeding associated with >14-day interruption. *(Source: Protocol §§5.2, 7.2, pages 25–26, 30–31)*
- **Adverse Event Definitions:** Trivial bleeding is any other overt bleeding not meeting clinically relevant bleeding criteria. All SAEs go to Global Pharmacovigilance within 24 hours; non-serious AEs causing permanent discontinuation are captured in eCRF, while other non-serious AEs need not be captured. Efficacy/safety outcomes are captured in CRF but exempt from expedited individual AE reporting. Pregnancy/outcome and specified laboratory/allergic events are captured/reported within 24 hours. *(Source: Protocol §§5.2, 7.1, pages 25–26, 30)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** FAS summaries include bleeding during treatment or within 2 days after stop. Time-to-first principal/secondary safety outcomes use stratified Cox, HR/95% CI and KM; all comparisons have no multiplicity adjustment. AEs are MedDRA-described, including treatment-emergent events through 2 days after stop, relation, intensity, outcome and treatment action. *(Source: final SAP §6.4, pages 97–98)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Intensity is mild, moderate, or severe; causality is reasonable suspected relationship assessed from available clinical information. Seriousness, duration, relationship, action and outcome are documented. *(Source: Protocol §§7.2–7.2.3, pages 30–31)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Actions are withdrawn, interrupted, dose reduced, unchanged/not increased, not applicable, or unknown; outcomes are resolved, resolving, resolved with sequelae, not resolved, fatal, or unknown. Causality considers temporal sequence, exposure duration, dechallenge/rechallenge, alternative disease/medications, and pharmacology/pharmacokinetics. *(Source: Protocol §§7.2.2–7.2.3, pages 30–31)*
