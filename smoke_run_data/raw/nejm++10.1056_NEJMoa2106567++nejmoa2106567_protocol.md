# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale
EFC14153 evaluates subcutaneous dupilumab in children aged 6 to <12 years with uncontrolled persistent asthma receiving background controller therapy. *(Source: SAP 1.1, page 468)*

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**
- Evaluate efficacy of dupilumab in children aged 6 to <12 years with uncontrolled persistent asthma. *(Source: SAP 1.2.1, page 468)*

**Secondary Objectives**
- Assess safety/tolerability; PROs including HRQoL; systemic exposure and anti-drug antibodies; and the association of treatment with pediatric immune responses to tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis and/or seasonal trivalent/quadrivalent influenza vaccination. *(Source: SAP 1.2.2, page 468)*

**Exploratory Objectives**
- Explore biomarker prediction/association with response and optional genetic profiles; evaluate ICS/second-controller step-up and additional PROs. *(Source: SAP 1.2.3, page 469)*

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design
- **Design Type:** Multinational, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study; screening is 4±1 weeks, treatment 52 weeks, with a 12-week post-treatment period for participants not entering the one-year extension. *(Source: SAP 1.1 and 1.4, pages 468-470)*
- **Allocation Ratio:** Dupilumab:matching placebo 2:1. *(Source: SAP 1.1, page 468)*
- **Intervention Summary:** Subcutaneous Q2W dupilumab or matching placebo on background medium-dose ICS plus a second controller, or high-dose ICS alone/with a second controller.
  - Baseline weight ≤30 kg: dupilumab 100 mg Q2W/matching placebo (0.67 mL).
  - Baseline weight >30 kg: dupilumab 200 mg Q2W/matching placebo (1.14 mL); investigators and patients are unblinded to dose level because volumes differ. *(Source: SAP 1.1 and 1.4, pages 468-470)*

Treatment is for a maximum 52 weeks. Background controllers continue; temporary ICS increase up to fourfold for ≤10 days is allowed, while permanent controller step-up may follow at least two severe exacerbations. Scheduled visits use a ±5-day window; ETD/EOT assessment and telephone follow-up exceptions are specified. *(Source: protocol sections 1.1 and 7.1, pages 5-6, 14-15, 101-102; SAP 2.1.2.1, page 479)*

### 2.2 Randomization
- **Method:** Central IVRS/IWRS permuted-block randomization after screening. *(Source: SAP 1.1, page 468)*
- **Stratification Factors:** Screening ICS dose (medium/high), screening eosinophils (<0.3/≥0.3 Giga/L), and region: Latin America (Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Chile, Mexico); Eastern Europe (Poland, Hungary, Romania, Lithuania, Russia, Ukraine, Turkey); Western Countries (Australia, Canada, Italy, South Africa, Spain, USA). These strata are distinct from pooled-country regional analysis. *(Source: SAP 1.1 and 2.5.6, pages 468, 542)*
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Permuted blocks; minimization is not reported in protocol. *(Source: SAP 1.1, page 468; pages 1-588 reviewed)*

### 2.3 Sample Size
**Outcome: Annualized severe-exacerbation rate over 52 weeks**
- **Planned Sample Size:** At least 402 overall (268 dupilumab, 134 placebo): approximately 345 Type 2 phenotype (230/115), 327 eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L (218/109), and 255 eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L (170/85). *(Source: SAP 1.3, pages 468-470)*
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Annualized rate of severe exacerbations during 52 weeks. *(Source: SAP 1.3 and 2.1.3.1, pages 469, 480-481)*
- **Outcome Type:** Negative-binomial count/rate. *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** 0.7/year (Type 2 and ≥0.15) and 0.8/year (≥0.3). *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** 0.322/year (Type 2 and ≥0.15) and 0.32/year (≥0.3). *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** Relative-risk reduction 54% (Type 2 and ≥0.15) or 60% (≥0.3). *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Variability Assumption:** Dispersion parameter 1.5. *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** Two-tailed 0.05. *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Power:** 94% (Type 2), 93% (≥0.15), 96% (≥0.3). *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Dupilumab-placebo comparison of a negative-binomial severe-exacerbation rate. *(Source: SAP 1.3 and 2.4.4.1, pages 469, 502-503)*
- **Required Number of Events:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-588 reviewed)*
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** Not applicable; this is a superiority comparison. *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** Linear discontinuation 20% at one year; average exposure assumed 0.9 year. *(Source: SAP 1.3, page 469)*
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable; individually randomized trial. *(Source: SAP 1.1, page 468)*
- **Basis for Assumptions:** Relative-risk reductions based on Phase 3 EFC13579 (QUEST); projected prevalences were 86% Type 2, 81% eosinophils ≥0.15, and 64% ≥0.3. *(Source: SAP 1.3, pages 469-470)*
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** Amendment 2 increased sample size from 294 to 471; Amendment 3 revised the primary populations and final calculation to 402. *(Source: SAP 1.5, pages 471-474)*
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-588 reviewed)*
- **Sample Size Rationale:** Sizing assured target power across three biomarker-defined populations under 2:1 allocation. *(Source: SAP 1.3, pages 469-470)*

The three powered populations, retained separately, are: (1) Type 2 inflammatory phenotype (eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L or FeNO ≥20 ppb), n=345 (230/115), placebo rate 0.7, dupilumab rate 0.322, 54% reduction, 94% power; (2) eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, n=327 (218/109), rates 0.7/0.322, 54%, 93%; and (3) eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L, n=255 (170/85), rates 0.8/0.32, 60%, 96%. Each assumes dispersion 1.5, 2:1 allocation, 20% one-year linear discontinuation and two-sided alpha 0.05. *(Source: SAP 1.3, pages 469-470)*

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework
Superiority hypotheses compare dupilumab with placebo for severe-exacerbation rate and the sequentially tested efficacy endpoints; the final SAP uses regional hierarchical sequences to control two-sided Type I error at 5%. *(Source: SAP 1.5 and 2.4.4.4, pages 473-474, 520-522)*

US/reference sequence (orders 1-24): severe-exacerbation rate in ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2; Week-12 pre-BD FEV1 in ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2; Week-24 ACQ-7 in ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2; the same three endpoints in FeNO ≥20; severe-exacerbation rate and FEV1 in full ITT; severe-exacerbation rate in high-ICS ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2, FeNO ≥20, full ITT; then Week-12 FeNO in ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2, FeNO ≥20, full ITT. EU/reference uses the same ordered endpoint groups but starts the first three, FEV1 and ACQ-7 sequences Type 2, ≥0.15, ≥0.3 and retains that order for high-ICS and FeNO. *(Source: SAP Tables 9-10, pages 520-522)*

The complete regional hypothesis framework is repeated verbatim as the full US/reference and EU/reference 24-test sequence under **3.1 Multiplicity Adjustment**, which is part of this SAP's formal hypothesis framework. *(Source: SAP Tables 9-10, pages 520-522)*

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals
- **Alpha Level:** Formal hierarchy is two-sided 5%. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.4, page 520)*
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** Sequential hierarchy: US/reference begins ≥0.3 eosinophils, then ≥0.15, then Type 2; EU/reference begins Type 2, then ≥0.15, then ≥0.3, followed by ordered FEV1, ACQ-7, FeNO and high-ICS/full-ITT tests. A hypothesis is tested only after its predecessor is significant. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.4, pages 520-522)*
- **Confidence Intervals:** Two-sided 95% CIs are reported for adjusted rates/rate ratios, LS-mean differences and hazard ratios as applicable. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1-2.4.4.3, pages 503, 511, 518)*

For US/reference countries, order 1-24 is severe-exacerbation rate in ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2; Week-12 pre-BD FEV1 in ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2; Week-24 ACQ-7 in ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2; severe-exacerbation rate, FEV1, ACQ-7 in FeNO ≥20; severe-exacerbation rate and FEV1 in full ITT; severe-exacerbation rate in high-ICS ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2, FeNO ≥20 and full ITT; then Week-12 FeNO in ≥0.3, ≥0.15, Type 2, FeNO ≥20 and full ITT. *(Source: SAP Table 9, pages 520-521)*

For EU/reference countries, order 1-24 is severe-exacerbation rate in Type 2, ≥0.15, ≥0.3; Week-12 pre-BD FEV1 in Type 2, ≥0.15, ≥0.3; Week-24 ACQ-7 in Type 2, ≥0.15, ≥0.3; severe-exacerbation rate, FEV1, ACQ-7 in FeNO ≥20; severe-exacerbation rate and FEV1 in full ITT; severe-exacerbation rate in high-ICS Type 2, ≥0.15, ≥0.3, FeNO ≥20 and full ITT; then Week-12 FeNO in Type 2, ≥0.15, ≥0.3, FeNO ≥20 and full ITT. *(Source: SAP Table 10, pages 521-522)*

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations
- **Adherence Definition:** IMP compliance is compliant administrations/administrations planned in the treatment epoch; controller compliance requires actual dose at least prescribed dose, with LABA spirometry-test days excluded. *(Source: SAP 2.4.2.1.1 and 2.4.3.2, pages 500-502)*
- **Adherence Assessment:** Electronic diary records controller use; exposure/compliance summaries include <80% compliance, above- and under-planned dosing. *(Source: SAP 2.4.2.1.1 and 2.4.3.2, pages 500-502)*
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Critical/major deviations are identified; randomization/drug-dispensing irregularities include ineligible or incorrectly stratified/randomized participants and incorrect kit dispensing. *(Source: SAP 2.2-2.2.1, pages 495-496)*
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Critical/major deviations, including COVID-19-related deviations, are summarized by category and treatment; irregularities are documented in the clinical study report. *(Source: SAP 2.2-2.2.1, pages 495-496)*

IMP dosing summaries include <80% compliance, above-/under-planned dosing, overdose (at least twice intended dose in <11 days), and dosing irregularities. Irregularities include incorrect randomization/stratum, double randomization, incorrect/no IRT kit transaction, erroneous kit, unavailable kit and site switch. *(Source: SAP 2.2.1 and 2.4.3.2, pages 495-496, 501-502)*

### 3.3 Analysis Populations
- **Intention-to-Treat:** Full ITT is all randomized participants, analyzed as randomized; efficacy also uses Type 2 (eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L or FeNO ≥20 ppb), eosinophils ≥0.3, eosinophils ≥0.15, and FeNO ≥20 subpopulations. *(Source: SAP 2.3.1, pages 496-497)*
- **Per Protocol:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: SAP 2.3, pages 496-498; pages 1-588 reviewed)*
- **Other Populations:** Safety is all who received ≥1 dose/part-dose, analyzed as treated; PK requires safety plus ≥1 serum concentration; ADA requires safety plus ≥1 post-dose ADA result; biomarkers use exposed safety population. *(Source: SAP 2.3.2-2.3.3, pages 497-498)*

All efficacy endpoints are analyzed in Type 2 and ≥0.3 populations; selected endpoints are additionally analyzed in full ITT, ≥0.15 and FeNO ≥20 populations. *(Source: SAP 2.3.1 and 2.4.4, pages 496-497, 502)*

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary
Children aged 6 to <12 years had uncontrolled persistent asthma and stable ≥3-month medium-dose ICS plus second controller or high-dose ICS alone/with second controller, with stable dose ≥1 month before screening; third controller was not permitted. Protocol inclusion criteria I01-I05 enumerate consent/assent, age/weight, asthma duration and uncontrolled-asthma, lung-function/reversibility, exacerbation and background-therapy thresholds. Exclusions E01 onward enumerate biologic/immunotherapy and prohibited-therapy restrictions, comorbidity, malignancy and other safety exclusions. *(Source: protocol eligibility criteria, pages 39-45 and subsequent exclusion subsections; SAP 2.1.2.1, page 479)*

- **Inclusion I01-I05:** age 6 to <12 years; physician-diagnosed persistent asthma ≥12 months; medium ICS+one controller or high ICS regimen stable as specified; pre-BD FEV1 ≤95% predicted or FEV1/FVC <0.85 at screening/baseline; ≥10% reversibility (with stated alternatives); prior-year SCS-treated exacerbation or hospital/emergency care; screening uncontrolled-asthma evidence (ACQ-5 ≥1.5, reliever use, sleep awakening, or symptoms); ability to comply, caregiver comprehension, required assent/consent and questionnaire completion. *(Source: protocol 7.1, pages 39-40)*
- **Exclusion E01-E29:** outside age/weight limits; chronic lung disease/life-threatening asthma; interfering comorbidity/malignancy/procedure inability; recent biologic, immunosuppressant, immunotherapy, investigational therapy or prohibited medications; prior dupilumab trial/related site staff; <80% background compliance or prohibited SCS/topical steroid; major medical disease; pregnancy/contraception restriction, parasite/HIV/infection/autoimmune/immunosuppression/hypersensitivity/viral-hepatitis risk; specified liver/laboratory abnormalities; consent withdrawal or enrollment stop. *(Source: protocol 7.2, pages 40-43)*

  - E01-E07: age <6/≥12, weight <16 kg, chronic lung disease, life-threatening asthma, interfering disease, malignancy, or inability to follow procedures.
  - E08-E13: timing-restricted omalizumab/other biologic or immunosuppressant, allergen immunotherapy, investigational medicines, prohibited concomitants, prior dupilumab study, or site-staff relationship.
  - E14-E16: <80% diary-recorded background adherence, specified corticosteroid exposure, or clinically significant illness (including stated tuberculosis provisions).
  - E17-E27: pregnancy/contraception, parasite/HIV/infection/immunosuppression/autoimmune disease, biologic hypersensitivity, hepatitis, liver injury, or stated CPK/platelet/eosinophil/creatinine thresholds.
  - E28-E29: consent withdrawal or study-level enrollment cessation. *(Source: protocol 7.2.1-7.2.4, pages 40-43)*

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes
**Outcome: Annualized rate of severe exacerbation events during the 52-week placebo-controlled treatment period** *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.1, pages 480-481)*
- **Definition:** Deterioration requiring systemic corticosteroids ≥3 days, or hospitalization/emergency visit for asthma requiring systemic corticosteroids; events are distinct when start dates are ≥28 days apart. *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.1, pages 480-481)*
- **Measurement Units:** Events per patient-year. *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.1, page 481)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Onset during the 52-week treatment period, including off-treatment events through Week 52 for participants remaining in follow-up. *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.1, page 481)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Annualized rate = event count/on-study observation duration; log duration is regression offset. *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.1 and 2.4.4.1, pages 481, 502-503)*

The treatment-policy estimand includes events after treatment discontinuation through Week 52 or study withdrawal, whichever occurs first; for study withdrawal, observation is randomization to last contact and unobserved events are not imputed in the primary analysis. Gross annualized rates and mean cumulative-function plots are descriptive outputs. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1, pages 502-504)*

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

- **Measurement Units:** Percent predicted FEV1. *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.2, page 481)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Week 12; measurements through Week 12 feed MMRM. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.2.1, pages 510-511)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Change from baseline; percent-predicted value from spirometry. *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.3 and 2.4.4.2.1, pages 482, 510-511)*

**Outcome: Other secondary efficacy endpoints** *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.2, pages 481-482)*

The retained endpoint-level groups are pre-BD percent-predicted FEV1 (Weeks 2,4,8,12,24,36,52), absolute/relative FEV1, FVC, FEF25-75%, post-BD FEV1, AM/PM PEF, time to first severe exacerbation, time to first LOAC, HCRU, AM/PM symptom NRS, ACQ-IA, reliever use, nocturnal awakenings, PAQLQ(S)-IA (only participants ≥7 years at randomization) and FeNO. ### 5.3 Analysis Methods
- **Primary Analysis Methods:** Negative-binomial regression of primary event count with log observation duration offset; covariates include treatment, age, weight, region, eosinophils, FeNO, ICS level and prior-year exacerbations (remove defining biomarker in its restricted population). Reports adjusted rates, rate ratio, 95% CI and P value. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1, pages 502-504)*
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Key FEV1 MMRM includes treatment, weight, region, ethnicity, baseline eosinophils, FeNO, ICS level, visit, treatment-by-visit, baseline FEV1 and baseline-by-visit; restricted populations omit their defining biomarker. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.2.1, pages 510-511)*
- **Assumption Checks:** Negative-binomial nonconvergence is addressed by default, LAPLACE, then QUAD algorithms; subtype interactions use nominal threshold 0.05 and Gail-Simon qualitative-interaction test. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1 and 2.4.4.2.1, pages 503-504, 515)*
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** Replace continuous prior-exacerbation covariate with categories (1, 2, >2) if needed; report model adjustment in output footnote. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1, pages 503-504)*
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Primary endpoint: PMM multiple imputation, control-based PMM and tipping-point analyses for post-withdrawal events; FEV1: on-treatment MMRM and two systemic-corticosteroid censoring rules. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1-2.4.4.2.1, pages 505-506, 512-514)*
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Primary rate and Week-12 FEV1 use prespecified demographic, clinical, biomarker and Eos-FeNO-quadrant subgroups, interactions and forest plots; biomarker analyses model categorical/continuous FeNO/eosinophils and interactions. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1-2.4.4.2.1, pages 507-510, 514-516)*

Supportive treatment-adherence severe-exacerbation estimand excludes events after permanent IMP discontinuation or first permanent controller step-up and uses a negative-binomial model with the same covariates and shorter treatment/regimen-change offset in Type 2 and ≥0.3 populations. Key FEV1 MMRM uses visits through Week 12, unstructured covariance, REML/Newton-Raphson and Kenward-Roger inference; secondary continuous endpoints use MMRM through Week 52; time-to-event endpoints use Cox/Kaplan-Meier; ACQ-7/ACQ-5/AQLQ responders use logistic regression with MCID ±0.5 rules; post-BD FEV1 slope uses linear mixed effects. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1-2.4.4.3, pages 505, 510-519)*

For key FEV1, MMRM populations are Type 2, ≥0.3, ≥0.15, FeNO ≥20 and full ITT; covariates are treatment, baseline weight, region, ethnicity, baseline eosinophils, FeNO, ICS level, visit, treatment-by-visit, baseline FEV1 and baseline-by-visit, omitting the defining biomarker. ACQ-7/FeNO MMRM includes age and the corresponding baseline endpoint; responder analyses occur Weeks 12,24,36,52 and use treatment, age, weight, pooled country, biomarkers, ICS and baseline score. Biomarker interaction models separately use categorical and continuous FeNO/eosinophils; nominal interaction P<0.15 is specified, and Gail-Simon is used after nominal P<0.05 subgroup interaction. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.2.1-2.4.4.3.2, pages 510-519)*

### 5.4 Missing Data
- **Missing Data Assumptions:** Primary treatment-policy analysis includes off-treatment data; withdrawal censors observation and does not impute unobserved events. FEV1 primary MMRM uses observed data under its latent MAR imputation framework. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1-2.4.4.2.1, pages 502-506, 510-513)*
- **Handling Methods:** Primary rate sensitivity analyses use PMM-MI logistic models, control-based PMM and tipping point; ACQ missing >1 item is missing, one item is prorated; missing binary responder data are nonresponse; time-to-event analyses censor at discontinuation. *(Source: SAP 2.1.3.5 and 2.4.4.1-2.4.4.3, pages 485-486, 505-506, 517-519)*

For primary events, PMM-MI imputes monthly post-withdrawal event status, control-based PMM uses placebo observations, tipping point alters treatment-specific odds, and COVID-specific PMM is specified. FEV1 has PMM/control-based/tipping-point sensitivity analyses plus two SCS censoring methods; MMRM primary inference is under MAR. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.1-2.4.4.2.1 and Appendix D, pages 505-506, 512-514, 561-572)*

### 5.5 Additional Analyses
Additional analyses include PRO MMRM, systemic-corticosteroid exposure (days, courses, time to first use), annualized LOAC negative-binomial analysis, PK/ADA, biomarkers/genomics, vaccine response and health economics. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.5 and 2.4.6-2.4.8, pages 523-524; SAP contents, pages 465-466)*

SCS courses separated by <7 days are continuous; course count is negative-binomial and time to first use uses time-to-event methods. Additional PROs use the key-secondary MMRM; LOAC uses the primary count-model framework. Biomarkers have below-LLQ handling; vaccine analyses summarize eligible participants' pre/post titers; exploratory DNA/RNA analysis is deferred to a separate document. *(Source: SAP 2.4.4.5-2.4.8, pages 523-534)*

### 5.6 Harms
- **Adverse Event Definitions:** TEAEs develop, worsen or become serious during treatment epoch through last IMP+98 days/rollover; pretreatment and post-treatment events are separately defined. *(Source: SAP 2.1.4-2.1.4.1, pages 488-490)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Safety analyses are descriptive in as-treated safety population; summarize TEAEs/serious TEAEs, discontinuations, AESIs and selected groupings by MedDRA SOC/HLGT/HLT/PT, with n (%). *(Source: SAP 2.4.5-2.4.5.1, pages 524-525)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** PCSA thresholds (May 2014 Appendix A) identify medically important laboratory, vital-sign and ECG abnormalities; AESI algorithms include MedDRA searches and blinded review for systemic hypersensitivity. *(Source: SAP 2.4.5 and 2.1.4.1, pages 489-490, 524-525)*

Safety outputs separate pretreatment and treatment-emergent events, use conservative onset-date imputation, and report exposure-adjusted incidence, severity (maximum mild/moderate/severe), relationship, corrective treatment, outcome, discontinuation/interruption and deaths. Missing relationship is treated as related in frequency tables; missing severity uses remaining occurrences. AESI/injection-site algorithms and follow-up are protocol specified. PCSA uses treatment-emergent windows and assessed-patient denominators. *(Source: SAP 2.1.4-2.1.4.1, pages 488-490; SAP 2.4.5-2.4.5.1 and 2.5.3-2.5.4, pages 524-540)*
