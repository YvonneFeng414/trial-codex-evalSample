# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

FOXTROT is a phase IIa study of BAY 1213790, a fully human IgG1 anti-factor XIa antibody that binds FXIa near its catalytic center, in patients having elective primary unilateral total knee arthroplasty (TKA). TKA carries substantial VTE risk, whereas established thromboprophylaxis entails bleeding risk; selective FXIa inhibition is being assessed for VTE prevention with a potentially favorable bleeding profile. Dose selection (0.3-1.8 mg/kg) was informed by preclinical work, Phase 1 population PK/PD, and systems pharmacology. *(Source: Sections 3 and 5, pages 24-27 and 34-35; final SAP, pages 117-119)*

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

- Assess safety and efficacy of different BAY 1213790 doses versus enoxaparin in elective primary unilateral TKA. *(Source: Section 4, page 28)*

**Secondary Objectives**

- Compare safety and efficacy of BAY 1213790 with apixaban. *(Source: Section 4, page 28)*

**Exploratory Objectives**

- Assess BAY 1213790 dose-response for efficacy and safety. *(Source: Section 4, page 28)*
- Characterize BAY 1213790 PK/PD and their relationship. *(Source: Section 4, page 28)*
- **Other protocol objectives:** Further characterize BAY 1213790 by evaluating additional PD markers, and investigate thrombosis mechanisms using additional PD markers. (These are labeled “Other objective” in the protocol.) *(Source: Section 4, page 28)*

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Randomized, active-comparator-controlled, multicenter phase IIa study; open-label for treatment assignment, observer-blinded for BAY 1213790 dose, with blinded CIAC adjudication. Part A administers BAY after surgery; sequential Part B administers BAY before surgery, with no parallel recruitment. *(Source: Section 5, pages 28-34)*
- **Allocation Ratio:** 5:1:1 pooled BAY 1213790:enoxaparin:apixaban, adaptable after arm closure/opening; initial target 700, maximum 900. *(Source: Sections 5 and 7.3, pages 29 and 42)*
- **Intervention Summary:**
  - **Part A post-surgery:** BAY 0.3, 0.6, or 1.2 mg/kg IV once after adequate hemostasis on the day after TKA (preferably morning, ≤24 h after wound closure); a 1.8-mg/kg arm may open. Enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily begins pre-TKA evening or 6-8 h after TKA and continues ≥10 days to venography; apixaban 2.5 mg orally twice daily begins after TKA (≤24 h from closure) and continues ≥10 days to venography. *(Source: Sections 5 and 7.4, pages 29 and 42-44)*
  - **Part B pre-surgery:** BAY 0.3 or 1.2 mg/kg IV once in the second half of pre-surgery Day 1; IDMC/SC may select 0.3/0.6/1.2/1.8 mg/kg or add an arm. Comparator regimens are the same. Part B begins only after Part A completion and review of ≥75% Part A primary assessments. *(Source: Sections 5 and 7.4, pages 30, 33, and 42-43)*
  - BAY recipients are assigned 1:1:1 among Part A doses and 1:1 in Part B, subject to adjustment after arm closure/opening. *(Source: Section 7.3, page 42)*

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** IxRS randomization to BAY, enoxaparin, or apixaban, followed by IxRS randomization of BAY recipients to dose arm. *(Source: Section 7.3, page 42)*
- **Stratification Factors:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-137 reviewed)*
- **Minimization or Blocking:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-137 reviewed)*

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** 700 initially (500 BAY across five intended arms and 100 each enoxaparin/apixaban), extendable to 900; target 100 per BAY and comparator arm, with Part A comparator enrollment at least 60 and Part B at least 40. *(Source: Sections 5 and 10.4, pages 29-30 and 97-98)*
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Primary efficacy composite VTE endpoint at Day 12+3. *(Source: Section 10.4, pages 97-98; Section 10.3.1, page 93)*
- **Outcome Type:** Binary event proportion. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2 and 10.4, pages 94-98)*
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** Enoxaparin 25%; apixaban 10% in the examples. *(Source: Table 10-1, page 98)*
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** BAY 10% and 4% in the respective calculation scenarios. *(Source: Table 10-1, page 98)*
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** Risk difference; NI margin 5%. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2 and 10.4, pages 94 and 97-98)*
- **Variability Assumption:** Not applicable; calculation is for binary event proportions. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2 and 10.4, pages 94-98)*
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** One-sided 5%. *(Source: Table 10-1, page 98)*
- **Power:** Enoxaparin scenarios: 81% superiority/97% NI for 10% vs25%, and 99%/99% for 4% vs25%; apixaban scenarios are descriptive calculation context (0%/29% and 43%/88%). *(Source: Table 10-1, page 98)*
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** One-sided NI and superiority tests using lower bound of two-sided 90% normal-approximation CI for risk difference. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2 and 10.4, pages 94-98)*
- **Required Number of Events:** Not applicable; powered on proportions, not an event-driven target. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2 and 10.4, pages 94-98)*
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** 5% NI margin. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2 and 10.4, pages 94 and 97-98)*
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** Approximately 20% unavailable/non-evaluable venograms (quality or dropout), yielding 80 evaluable per arm. *(Source: Section 10.4, pages 97-98)*
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable; individually randomized, non-clustered study. *(Source: Sections 5 and 7.3, pages 28-30 and 42)*
- **Basis for Assumptions:** Event-rate scenarios and 20% non-evaluable venography assumption. *(Source: Section 10.4, pages 97-98)*
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** After ~75% Part A Visit 7, pooled unavailable venography >30% may trigger sample increase to regain 80% superiority power; arm/dose enrollment may also change on IDMC/SC advice, within 900. *(Source: Sections 5, 10.4 and 10.5, pages 29-30 and 97-99)*
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-137 reviewed)*
- **Sample Size Rationale:** Phase II comparisons aim to characterize dose safety/efficacy; examples demonstrate NI/superiority precision versus enoxaparin. *(Source: Sections 5 and 10.4, pages 34-35 and 97-98)*

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

For each BAY dose/time arm versus enoxaparin, test NI first then superiority, each one-sided at global α=5%; the fixed sequence stops after the first unrejected null. NI requires lower 90% CI bound for BAY minus enoxaparin to be below 5%; superiority requires it below 0. No hypothesis testing is planned versus apixaban; provide descriptive proportions and 90% CIs. *(Source: Section 10.3.3.2, pages 94-95)*

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** IDMC continuously monitors safety. After 75% Part A completes Visit 7, IDMC/SC review supports Part B initiation/dose selection but is explicitly not a formal interim analysis. Potential efficacy/safety analyses occur after Part A Visit 7, Part A Visit 10, and both-part Visit 7 completion, with no formal report. *(Source: Section 10.5, pages 98-99)*
- **Stopping Guidelines:** No early efficacy stopping; IDMC/SC can advise continuing/stopping recruitment or opening/closing BAY dose arms for safety/efficacy assessment. *(Source: Sections 5 and 10.5, pages 29-30 and 98-99)*
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** No group-sequential/adaptive design and no multiplicity adjustment for interim analyses. *(Source: Section 10.5, page 99)*

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** Primary completion is all patients assessed at Visit 7; study end is last patient’s last visit. Database blind lasts through final database lock after LPLV and authorization of release. *(Source: Sections 5 and 7.5.1, pages 36 and 44)*
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** Screening Day -13 to1; randomization Day1; TKA Day2; treatment visits Days3,4,6±1,12+3; bilateral venography/primary outcomes Visit7 Day12+3; post-treatment visits Day30±5, Day90±7, Day150±7. *(Source: Sections 5 and 9.2, pages 34 and 58)*

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** One-sided global α=5% for each BAY arm’s NI/superiority sequence. *(Source: Section 10.3.3.2, pages 94-95)*
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** Fixed NI then superiority sequence within each arm; no adjustment across five BAY arms, apixaban comparisons descriptive only, and no interim adjustment. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2 and 10.5, pages 94-95 and 98-99)*
- **Confidence Intervals:** Two-sided 90% normal-approximation CIs for risk differences; 90% CIs also for descriptive comparisons and time-to-event estimates. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2-10.3.3.3, pages 94-96; final SAP, pages 130-132)*

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** Comparator adherence is assessed through patient instruction, return of all packaging/unused drug, and reconciliation of discrepancies; accountability log is completed for every patient. *(Source: Section 7.7, pages 45-46)*
- **Adherence Assessment:** Drug accountability, source documentation, and IxRS/CRF records; materially protocol-deviant dose may exclude PPS/PK/PD analysis. *(Source: Section 7.7, page 45; final SAP, pages 127-128)*
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Important deviations or validity findings affecting primary efficacy exclude PPS; specified examples are prior DVT, weight >135 kg, materially deviating dose, and prohibited anticoagulation/dual antiplatelets in treatment phase. *(Source: Section 10.2, page 93; final SAP, page 127)*
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Deviations must be explained and documented by investigator; final list of important deviations, validity findings, and analysis-set allocation follows data review; further details are deferred to the Protocol Deviation Document. *(Source: Section 13.3, page 105; final SAP, pages 126-127)*

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** No named ITT set. SAF efficacy analyses use randomized treatment following the ITT principle; SAF contains all patients receiving ≥1 study-drug dose. *(Source: Section 10.2, page 93; final SAP, page 127)*
- **Per Protocol:** PPS: ≥1 dose, valid Day12+3 venography or objectively confirmed symptomatic VTE before Day12+3, and no important primary-efficacy deviation/validity finding; analyze as treated. Primary efficacy uses PPS. *(Source: Section 10.2, page 93; final SAP, page 127)*
- **Other Populations:** mITT: ≥1 dose plus valid venography/early symptomatic VTE; sensitivity/descriptive efficacy. SAF is primary safety, as treated. PK set: BAY treated, ≥1 valid concentration and no interfering deviation; PD set: ≥1 dose, ≥1 valid PD sample and no interfering deviation. *(Source: Sections 10.2 and 10.3, pages 93-94; final SAP, pages 127-128)*

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

Consent precedes study procedures. Screening includes demographics, medical/prior medication history, weight/height, physical examination, BP/pulse, supine ECG, pregnancy test where applicable, local aPTT/PT/creatinine/ALT/bilirubin/platelet/Hb testing, AE review, and eligibility. Screening failures retain minimum demographics, consent date, relevant eligibility criteria, discontinuation reason, and last-visit date; SAE cases additionally retain SAE-related data. *(Source: Sections 9.2.1.1, 9.2.2.1 and 11.1, pages 58-59, 66, and 100)*

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

Eligible patients provide consent, are ≥18 years with elective primary unilateral TKA, meet reproductive/contraception requirements, and can follow instructions. Exclusions include clinically significant bleeding risk (specified hematologic/coagulation/hepatic/hypertension/surgery/bleeding-disorder conditions), prior DVT, weight >135 kg, CrCl <60 mL/min by MDRD, recent MI/stroke, active cancer exceptions specified, comparator contraindication, full anticoagulation/dual antiplatelets, compression/stimulator use, contrast allergy, prior assignment, recent investigational treatment, or site affiliation. Part A excludes postoperative epidural analgesia; Part B requires general anesthesia and excludes regional anesthesia/epidural analgesia. *(Source: Sections 6.1-6.2, pages 36-38)*

### 4.3 Recruitment

The study is multicenter. Screening spans Day -13 to Day 1; eligible patients randomize on the final screening day (Day 1). Planned enrollment is 700, extendable to 900 (detailed in Section 2.3); actual accrued recruitment and the number of sites are not reported in protocol. *(Source: Sections 5 and 9.2, pages 28-30 and 58; pages 1-137 reviewed)*

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Stop treatment if continued treatment is harmful; enoxaparin must stop for confirmed platelet decline below 50% baseline. Treatment may stop for investigational drug use, suspected drug-related AE/SAE, or emergent exclusion. Study withdrawal may be patient/representative request, harm, or sponsor request for noncompliance/safety. Pre-randomization termination is screening failure; randomized premature discontinuation is dropout; no patients replaced. *(Source: Section 6.3, pages 38-40)*
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** Continue scheduled visits/health-status follow-up where possible; PDC occurs as soon as possible and collects medications, AEs, vital signs, central safety, PK/PD/ADA/D-dimer as applicable, accountability, and outcome assessment. Collected/pre-withdrawal material data remain analyzable unless the patient objects to post-withdrawal processing. *(Source: Sections 6.3, 9.2 and 13.4, pages 38-40, 65, 74, and 106)*
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Record reason in CRF and medical record; summarize premature discontinuation by reason and treatment arm. *(Source: Section 6.3, page 40; final SAP, page 124)*

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Age/year of birth, sex, race/ethnicity, weight, height, BMI, smoking, alcohol, relevant history, creatinine clearance; randomization vital signs, otherwise pre-TKA closest nonmissing value. *(Source: Sections 9.3 and 9.6.3.4, pages 74-75 and 89; final SAP, pages 126 and 128)*
- **Summary Methods:** SAF and PPS descriptive summaries by treatment and overall; continuous mean, SD, median, quartiles, minimum/maximum and categorical counts/percentages; no between-group tests. Summarize reasons for exclusion from analysis populations. *(Source: Section 10.3.3.1, page 94; final SAP, page 128)*

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**Outcome: Primary efficacy composite VTE** *(Source: Sections 9.4 and 10.3.1, pages 75 and 93)*
- **Definition:** Incidence of asymptomatic DVT detected by mandatory bilateral venography, objectively confirmed symptomatic DVT, nonfatal PE, fatal PE, or unexplained death for which PE cannot be excluded; positively adjudicated events only. *(Source: Sections 9.4 and 10.3, pages 75-76 and 93-94)*
- **Measurement Units:** Patient-level event incidence/proportion and risk difference. *(Source: Section 10.3.3.2, pages 94-95)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Through Visit 7, Day 12+3. Bilateral venography is required for asymptomatic patients; early objectively confirmed symptomatic DVT/PE obviates it. *(Source: Sections 5 and 9.4, pages 34 and 75-76)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Composite is derived from component events. Contrast venography uses standardized bilateral deep-vein visualization and blinded CIAC assessment. *(Source: Section 9.4, pages 75-76)*

**Outcome: Primary safety composite bleeding** *(Source: Sections 9.6 and 10.3.2, pages 77 and 94)*
- **Definition:** Incidence of positively adjudicated composite major plus clinically relevant non-major (CRNM) bleeding. Major bleeding includes bleeding contributing to death; symptomatic critical-organ bleeding; extra-surgical-site bleeding with ≥2 g/dL hemoglobin fall or ≥2 units transfused within 24-48 h; surgical-site bleeding requiring a second open/arthroscopic/endovascular intervention; hemarthrosis large enough to delay mobilization or wound healing, prolong hospitalization, or cause deep wound infection; or unexpected/prolonged/large surgical-site bleeding with hemodynamic instability and associated ≥2 g/dL hemoglobin fall or ≥2 units transfused within 24 h. CRNM bleeding is overt acute/subacute bleeding not major that causes admission, physician-guided medical/surgical treatment, or a change in antithrombotic treatment. *(Source: Section 9.6, pages 77-78)*
- **Measurement Units:** Patient-level event incidence/proportion and risk difference. *(Source: Section 10.3.3.3, page 96)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Randomization through Visit 7, Day 12+3; include events regardless of onset relative to first study drug. *(Source: Section 10.3.3.3, page 96; final SAP, page 134)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Composite and its major/CRNM components. *(Source: final SAP, page 134)*

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**Outcome: Secondary efficacy composite VTE** *(Source: Sections 9.4 and 10.3.1, pages 75 and 93-94)*
- **Definition:** Symptomatic DVT/nonfatal PE, fatal PE, or unexplained death for which PE cannot be excluded through Visit 10, plus objectively confirmed asymptomatic DVT through Visit 7; positively adjudicated events only. *(Source: Sections 9.4 and 10.3, pages 75 and 93-94)*
- **Measurement Units:** Incidence/proportion, risk difference, and time to first event. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2, pages 95-96; final SAP, pages 130-131)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Symptomatic/death components Day150±7; asymptomatic DVT Day12+3. *(Source: Section 9.4, page 75)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Composite has mixed windows; analyze first-event time from randomization. *(Source: Sections 9.4 and 10.3.3.2, pages 75 and 95-96)*

**Outcome: Secondary safety composite bleeding** *(Source: Sections 9.6 and 10.3.2, pages 77 and 94)*
- **Definition:** Positively adjudicated major plus CRNM bleeding. *(Source: Sections 9.6 and 10.3.2, pages 77-78 and 94)*
- **Measurement Units:** Incidence/proportion, risk difference, and time to event. *(Source: final SAP, pages 134-135)*
- **Timing of Measurement:** Through Visit 10, Day150±7. *(Source: Section 9.6, page 77)*
- **Transformations / Derived Variables:** Composite and components; other safety includes any/nonmajor/major postoperative bleeding 6 hours after surgery through Visit 7. *(Source: final SAP, pages 123 and 135)*

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** Analyze primary efficacy separately by BAY dose/time arm versus enoxaparin on PPS as treated, applying NI then superiority CI decision rules. Provide proportions and 90% CIs versus apixaban descriptively. Primary/secondary efficacy and safety also evaluate: each Part A BAY dose versus first 60 Part A comparator patients; each Part B BAY dose versus last 40 Part B comparator patients; pooled post-surgery BAY versus comparators; pooled pre-surgery BAY versus comparators; pooled post- versus pre-surgery BAY; and post-surgery dose versus corresponding pre-surgery dose. Analyze primary/secondary safety on as-treated SAF descriptively with 90% normal-approximation CIs by dose and comparator. *(Source: Sections 10.2-10.3, pages 93-96; final SAP, pages 128-130 and 134-135)*
- **Covariate Adjustment:** No covariate-adjusted primary analysis. Logistic regression for dose-response uses dose as explanatory variable; MI logistic model uses treatment and baseline characteristics. *(Source: Sections 10.3.3.2 and 10.3.3.3, pages 95-96; final SAP, pages 132-133)*
- **Assumption Checks:** Dose-response logistic regression includes Pearson goodness-of-fit, dose effect, and regression curve. No other primary-analysis assumption check is reported. *(Source: final SAP, page 132; pages 1-137 reviewed)*
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1-137 reviewed)*
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Primary/secondary efficacy SAF with MI for missing primary endpoint; mITT descriptive primary/secondary analyses; PPS secondary outcome restricted to observation phase. Secondary time-to-event runs from randomization to first secondary event through Day150±7; no event, loss, non-DVT/PE death, consent withdrawal, or administrative end is censored at last contact. Conservative SAF analysis censors those cases no earlier than the later of last contact and Day12+3; primary time-to-event runs to Day12+3 using liberal censoring. For primary time-to-event, compare pooled post-surgery BAY with enoxaparin and apixaban, pooled pre-surgery BAY with enoxaparin and apixaban, pooled post- versus pre-surgery BAY, and post-surgery BAY dose(s) with corresponding pre-surgery dose(s). Kaplan-Meier estimates/curves and Cox treatment-model hazard ratios with 90% CIs are provided. *(Source: Sections 10.2-10.3, pages 93-96; final SAP, pages 130-133)*
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Primary/secondary incidence and 90% risk-difference CIs/forest plots by age <60/≥60, sex, race, ethnicity, weight <90/≥90 kg, BMI <30/≥30 kg/m², country, Part A anesthesia, thrombophilia history, and prophylactic pre-TKA tranexamic acid. *(Source: final SAP, page 132)*

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** Primary PPS requires valid venography/early confirmed event, so no imputation. For SAF primary endpoint, protocol states short study duration makes dropout unrelated to outcome and venogram readability independent of missing read, therefore missing at random. *(Source: Section 10.3.3.2, page 95; final SAP, pages 132-133)*
- **Handling Methods:** SAF primary outcome: logistic regression on complete cases with treatment and baseline factors/covariates; predict missing-event probability, repeatedly draw Bernoulli imputations, analyze each complete set and combine. Only scheduled-visit values are used in visit-based analyses; unscheduled data are listed. General missing/partial CRF data are listed. Missing AE/clinical-event day with month/year uses the latest of randomization, first study medication, or first day of month; year-only uses the latest of randomization, first medication, or 1 January, not after death. Missing study-drug start date/time uses randomization; unclarified earlier start uses randomization. Missing BAY stop date uses start date; missing comparator stop date uses specified mid-month/last-visit/death minimum rules. *(Source: Section 11.4, page 101; final SAP, pages 124-126 and 132-133)*

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

PK concentrations are summarized by actual dose/visit (geometric/arithmetic mean, %CV, median/range); calculate means only when ≥2/3 values are measured and above LLOQ, substituting half LLOQ otherwise and flagging such means. Plot geometric mean concentration-time and naive-pooled concentrations on linear/semilog scales. PD aPTT, PT seconds/INR, D-dimer, FXI activity/concentration, and exploratory markers use descriptive statistics and baseline changes. Population PK/exposure-response and exploratory PGt analyses may be reported separately. Other efficacy outcomes are DVT (total/proximal/distal), bilateral DVT (total/proximal/distal), symptomatic VTE (total/DVT/PE), asymptomatic VTE (total/DVT/PE), and death (total/fatal PE/unexplained death where PE cannot be excluded/non-PE death), analyzed like the secondary efficacy outcome across randomization-to-Visit 7 and randomization-to-Visit 10 windows. Repeat primary safety analysis for treatment-emergent events and each major/CRNM component; apply this approach to secondary safety and postoperative bleeding. *(Source: Sections 9.5 and 10.3.3.4, pages 76-77 and 96-97; final SAP, pages 130-135)*

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** AE is any untoward occurrence after consent, whether or not related; SAEs meet death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, congenital anomaly, or medically important-event criteria. AEs are collected from consent through post-treatment observation, with no active post-observation collection. *(Source: Section 9.6.1, pages 79-83)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** CIAC adjudicates bleeding; SAF uses descriptive proportions, 90% risk-difference CIs, pooled analyses, components, postoperative bleeding and time-to-event secondary safety. Treatment-emergent AEs are events from treatment start through no later than five months, coded MedDRA and tabulated by SOC/PT, seriousness, AESI, causal relationship to study drug and procedures (venography/TKA), maximum intensity, death, and permanent discontinuation. Produce additional AE summaries for screening, pre-TKA, treatment, and observation phases. At screening, Day1, Day2 before/after surgery, Day3 before treatment, Day4, Day6±1, Day12+3, Day30±5, Day90±7, Day150±7 and PDC, summarize BP/heart rate and change from baseline. Use centrally analyzed laboratories only; summarize values/change from baseline at Visit2 Day1, Visit4 Day3 before treatment, Visit6 Day6±1, Visit7 Day12+3, Visit8 Day30±5, Visit9 Day90±7, Visit10 Day150±7, and PDC, with high/low abnormality summaries. *(Source: Sections 9.6 and 10.3.3.3, pages 77-78 and 96; final SAP, pages 134-136)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Investigator assigns mild/moderate/severe intensity and yes/no reasonable causal relationship separately per study treatment/procedure. AESIs are platelet decline below LLN and hypersensitivity/infusion reactions; report within 24 hours. Hypersensitivity reactions use NCI CTCAE v4.0; SAEs are reported within 24 hours and followed to resolution/stabilization. *(Source: Section 9.6.1, pages 80-86)*
