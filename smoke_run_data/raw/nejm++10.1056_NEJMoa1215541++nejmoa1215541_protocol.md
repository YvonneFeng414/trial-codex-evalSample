# Statistical Analysis Plan

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

ING114467 is a Phase III study in antiretroviral therapy (ART)-naïve adults with HIV-1. It evaluates once-daily dolutegravir (DTG; GSK1349572) plus abacavir/lamivudine (ABC/3TC) versus the fixed-dose efavirenz/emtricitabine/tenofovir regimen Atripla, to establish non-inferior antiviral activity and characterize longer-term safety, tolerability, resistance, and health outcomes. DTG 50 mg once daily was selected from Phase 2b dose-ranging data. *(Source: Protocol §§1.1–1.3, pages 114–120; RAP §§1–3, pages 235–239)*

### 1.2 Objectives

**Primary Objectives**

To demonstrate antiviral activity of DTG plus ABC/3TC FDC once daily versus Atripla over 48 weeks in HIV-1 infected, ART-naïve subjects. *(Source: Protocol §2.1, page 120; RAP §2.1, page 235)*

**Secondary Objectives**

- Demonstrate antiviral activity through Week 96. *(Source: Protocol §2.2, page 121; RAP §2.1, page 235)*
- Evaluate antiviral activity through Week 144. *(Source: Amendment 05, pages 212–225)*
- Compare tolerability, long-term safety, antiviral and immunologic activity over time. *(Source: Protocol §2.2, page 121; RAP §2.1, page 235)*
- Assess development of viral resistance in subjects experiencing virologic failure. *(Source: Protocol §2.2, page 121; RAP §2.1, pages 235–236)*
- Assess change in symptom bother count. *(Source: Protocol §2.2, page 121; RAP §2.1, pages 235–236)*
- Evaluate incidence of HIV-associated conditions over time. *(Source: Protocol §2.2, page 121; RAP §2.1, pages 235–236)*
- Explore the impact of gender, race and/or HIV-1 subtype on response. *(Source: Protocol §2.2, page 121; RAP §2.1, pages 235–236)*
- Explore change in utility and health-related quality of life. *(Source: Protocol §2.2, page 121; RAP §2.1, pages 235–236)*

**Exploratory Objectives**

Not reported in protocol; the protocol lists all stated objectives as primary or secondary. Exploratory analyses are described as analyses rather than objectives. *(Source: Protocol §2, pages 120–121; RAP §§11–15, pages 269–289; pages 1–307 reviewed)*

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **Design Type:** Phase III, multicentre, randomized, stratified, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled, parallel-group, fully powered non-inferiority study. The double-blind randomized phase runs from Day 1 through Week 96; Amendment 05 provides an open-label randomized phase through Week 144 and a DTG continuation phase thereafter. *(Source: Protocol §3.1, pages 121–124; Amendment 05, pages 212–225)*
- **Allocation Ratio:** 1:1; approximately 800 randomized subjects, approximately 400 per arm. *(Source: Protocol §3.1, page 121; RAP §3, page 237)*
- **Intervention Summary:** Once daily, double-dummy, preferably fasting/at bedtime.
  - DTG + ABC/3TC: DTG 50 mg tablet plus ABC/3TC 600/300 mg tablet and Atripla placebo.
  - Atripla: EFV/FTC/TDF 600/200/300 mg tablet plus DTG and ABC/3TC placebos. *(Source: Protocol §5.1.3, pages 133–134)*

### 2.2 Randomization

- **Method:** Central computer-generated randomization after entry criteria confirmation; the GSK validated RANDALL/RandAll randomization software generates the schedule. *(Source: Protocol §5.2, page 134; RAP §3.2, page 238)*
- **Stratification Factors:** Screening plasma HIV-1 RNA ≤100,000 versus >100,000 c/mL and screening CD4+ count ≤200 versus >200 cells/mm3, creating four strata. *(Source: Protocol §3.1.2, page 122; RAP §3.2, page 238)*
- **Minimization or Blocking:** A fixed block size is used and remains confidential; minimization is not reported in protocol. *(Source: RAP §3.2, page 238; pages 1–307 reviewed)*

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **Planned Sample Size:** Approximately 800 randomized (~400/arm); 394 evaluable subjects/arm required. *(Source: Protocol §4.1, page 125; §8.2.1, pages 164–166; RAP §5, pages 240–241)*
- **Outcome Used for Calculation:** Proportion with plasma HIV-1 RNA <50 c/mL at Week 48. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, page 164; RAP §5.1, page 240)*
- **Outcome Type:** Binary virologic response rate. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, page 164; RAP §5.1, page 240)*
- **Assumed Control-Arm Value:** 75% Week-48 response in the Atripla (TDF/FTC/EFV) arm. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, pages 164–166; RAP §5.1, pages 240–241)*
- **Assumed Treatment-Arm Value:** Same 75% response rate is the non-inferiority planning assumption; a distinct DTG value is not reported in protocol. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, pages 164–166; RAP §5.1, pages 240–241; pages 1–307 reviewed)*
- **Target Difference / Effect Size:** Non-inferiority margin of −10 percentage points for DTG minus Atripla response rate. *(Source: Protocol §8.1, page 164; RAP §2.3, page 237)*
- **Variability Assumption:** Binomial response-rate calculation; no separate variance assumption is reported in protocol. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, pages 164–166; pages 1–307 reviewed)*
- **Alpha (Significance Level):** One-sided 2.5%. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, page 166; RAP §5.1, page 241)*
- **Power:** 90%. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, page 166; RAP §5.1, page 241)*
- **Statistical Test Assumed:** Non-inferiority assessment of the difference in proportions using the lower bound of a two-sided 95% CI against −10%. *(Source: Protocol §§8.1–8.2, pages 164–166; RAP §§2.3,5, pages 237,240–241)*
- **Required Number of Events:** Not applicable; this is powered on a binary response rate, not an event-driven time-to-event design. *(Source: Protocol §8.2, pages 164–166)*
- **Non-Inferiority / Equivalence Margin:** −10%. *(Source: Protocol §8.1, page 164; RAP §2.3, page 237)*
- **Dropout / Non-Adherence Inflation:** No numerical inflation is specified. The 75% planning response incorporates historical approximately 15% drug-related and 14% non-drug-related failure context. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, pages 164–166; RAP §5.1, pages 240–241)*
- **Design Effect / Clustering:** Not applicable; individual randomized parallel-group trial with no cluster design. *(Source: Protocol §3.1, page 121)*
- **Basis for Assumptions:** Historical Atripla-controlled and GSK trials: approximate control response 71%, recent EFV-arm range 71%–82%, and anticipated better non-drug compliance. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, pages 164–166; RAP §5.1, pages 240–241)*
- **Sample Size Re-Estimation / Amendments:** No sample-size re-estimation will be performed. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.3, page 166; RAP §5.3, page 241)*
- **Software Used for Calculation:** Not reported in protocol. *(Source: pages 1–307 reviewed)*
- **Sample Size Rationale:** 394 evaluable per arm provides 90% power for 75% control response and a 10% NI margin at one-sided 2.5%; the recruitment target is approximately 800. *(Source: Protocol §8.2.1, page 166; RAP §5.1, page 241)*

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

The primary hypotheses are H0: pDTG−pAtripla ≤−10% versus H1: pDTG−pAtripla >−10%. DTG is non-inferior if the lower bound of the two-sided 95% CI for the response-rate difference is greater than −10%. If ITT-E and PP analyses demonstrate NI, ITT-E superiority is tested two-sided at 5% and declared if the lower 95% CI bound exceeds 0. Conditional on NI, key secondary superiority tests are generalized Wilcoxon (time to suppression), repeated-measures mixed model (Week-48 CD4), and ANCOVA (Week-4 SDM bother), in the specified fallback sequence. *(Source: Protocol §§8.1,8.3.3, pages 164,167–168; RAP §§2.3,7, pages 237,244–245)*

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **Interim Analyses Planned:** Week-48 primary analysis after all complete Week 48 and Week-96 secondary analysis after all complete Week 96; Amendment 05 adds Week-144 analysis. Further cuts may support submissions/publications. An external SDAC provides unblinded data to the IDMC for periodic high-viral-load efficacy and targeted safety review under its charter. *(Source: Protocol §§3.1.7,8.3.4, pages 124,168; Amendment 05, page 225; RAP §§3.6,4.1, pages 239–240)*
- **Stopping Guidelines:** IDMC operating details and any stopping guidance are in the IDMC charter; not included in this document. *(Source: Protocol §§3.1.7,9.8, pages 124,173; pages 1–307 reviewed)*
- **Alpha Spending or Adjustment:** No adjustment for repeated Week-48/96 (and amended Week-144) evaluation because Week 96/144 analyses are secondary. *(Source: Protocol §8.3.4, pages 168–169; Amendment 05, page 225; RAP §4.1, page 240)*

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **Final Analysis Timing:** Final analysis occurs once all subjects have completed or discontinued; Week 48 is primary. *(Source: RAP §§4.1–4.2, page 240)*
- **Outcome Assessment Schedule:** The final amended protocol schedules RNA at screening, Day 1, Weeks 2,4,8,12,16,24,32,40,48 and every 12 weeks thereafter through Week 144; CD4/CD8, SDM, EQ-5D and safety assessments follow Table 1. Amendment 05 documents the Week-144 open-label/continuation conduct; actual dates allocate analysis windows. *(Source: Protocol §§3.1–3.1.4, pages 121–124; §6.1, pages 139–145; Amendment 05, pages 212–225; RAP §9.3, pages 261–262)*

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **Alpha Level:** The primary NI comparison is one-sided 2.5%; corresponding CIs are two-sided 95%. All CIs are two-sided. *(Source: Protocol §§8.1,8.3.3, pages 164,167; §8.3.5, page 168)*
- **Multiplicity Adjustment:** Gatekeeping permits superiority only after non-inferiority; no adjustment applies solely to secondary Week-96/144 repeated endpoint evaluation. The three key secondary tests use fallback alpha: time to suppression 1%, Week-48 CD4 change 3%, and Week-4 SDM bother change 1%. Rejection passes accumulated alpha forward; non-rejection burns it. Thus P1 rejection makes P2 4%; P1/P2 rejection makes P3 5%; if P1 fails, P2 is 3% and its rejection makes P3 4%; otherwise P3 remains 1%. The sequence controls overall type I error after primary NI. *(Source: Protocol §§8.3.3–8.3.4, pages 167–169; RAP §§7.2,8.4, pages 244–248)*
- **Confidence Intervals:** Stratified CMH adjusted risk differences use Sato variance estimation and two-sided 95% CIs; unadjusted response differences also receive 95% CIs. *(Source: Protocol §8.3.5.1, pages 168–169; RAP §11.1, pages 269–272)*

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **Adherence Definition:** Compliance is evaluated by pill counts of unused investigational product at every visit through withdrawal or completion. *(Source: Protocol §5.5, page 135)*
- **Adherence Assessment:** Pill-count data are recorded in the CRF; IP interruptions are recorded in the CRF. *(Source: Protocol §§5.5,6.4.2, pages 135,146)*
- **Protocol Deviation Definition:** Deviations affecting the PP population directly affect HIV RNA efficacy or indirectly cause permanent IP discontinuation/withdrawal and missingness. *(Source: RAP §10.2.2, page 265)*
- **Protocol Deviation Reporting:** Summarize number/percentage with eligibility deviations and PP-exclusion deviations and list subjects/criteria. PP review occurs before database freeze/unblinding at Week-48 and Week-96 analyses. *(Source: RAP §§10.2.1–10.2.2, pages 265–266)*

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **Intention-to-Treat:** ITT-E comprises randomized subjects receiving at least one IP dose, analyzed by randomized treatment regardless of treatment received; it is primary for efficacy. *(Source: Protocol §8.3.1.1, page 166; RAP §6.1.4, page 242)*
- **Per Protocol:** PP is ITT-E excluding prespecified efficacy-impacting deviations before the relevant timepoint; PP supports the primary endpoint. Deviations include eligibility departures, wrong IP >10% on-treatment, non-treatment-related interruption >10%, relevant prohibited medicines, and withdrawal for protocol deviation. *(Source: Protocol §8.3.1.2, page 167; RAP §§6.1.5,10.2.2, pages 242,265–266)*
- **Other Populations:** Safety is exposed subjects analyzed by actual treatment (majority exposure if treatments differ) and is primary for safety. All Screened, Randomized, and PDVF genotype/phenotype populations are used as specified for listings, disposition, and resistance analyses. *(Source: Protocol §8.3.1.3, page 167; RAP §§6.1.1–6.1.3,6.1.6, pages 242–243)*

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

Written consent precedes screening. The screening period is 21 days and may extend to 28; screen data include eligibility, full/targeted medical history, demographics, CDC class, prior ART, HIV risk/mode, concomitant medicines, plasma RNA/genotype, HLA-B*5701, laboratory safety assessments, hepatitis serologies and pregnancy testing. Screening failures and reasons are summarized/listed. *(Source: Protocol §§6.1–6.2, pages 139–143; RAP §10.1, page 264)*

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

Eligible subjects must be able to understand and comply, be expected to complete the study as planned, and be appropriate candidates for oral investigational therapy; they are HIV-1 infected adults ≥18 years, have screening RNA ≥1000 c/mL, are ART-naïve (≤10 prior ART days after diagnosis), HLA-B*5701 negative, and provide dated written consent. One re-screen (new number) and one repeat test per analyte/assessment are permitted; genotype retesting is not permitted. *(Source: Protocol §§4.2–4.3, pages 125–128)*

Women must be non-child-bearing (post-menopausal: 12 months amenorrhea and ≥45 years; or documented sterilization) or have negative screening and Day-1 pregnancy tests and use protocol-specified abstinence, double barrier, qualifying IUD/other <1%-failure method, or hormonal contraception plus barrier method, consistently from two weeks before IP through two weeks after it. French enrollment also requires social-security affiliation/beneficiary status. *(Source: Protocol §4.2, pages 125–127)*

Exclusions retain the protocol’s numbered structure: breastfeeding; active CDC Category C disease except cutaneous Kaposi not needing systemic therapy (historical/current CD4 <200 is not exclusionary); hepatic impairment; HBsAg positivity or anticipated HCV therapy; recent GI bleed; allergy/intolerance to study drugs/class; recent/ongoing malignancy except specified skin/Kaposi exceptions; recent HIV immunotherapeutic vaccine, radiation, cytotoxic chemotherapy, immunomodulator, HIV-active agent, or experimental drug/vaccine (including France-specific restriction); primary resistance; verified Grade-4/acute disqualifying laboratory abnormality; ALT/bilirubin thresholds; and CrCl <50 mL/min by Cockcroft–Gault. *(Source: Protocol §§4.3–4.4, pages 127–129)*

### 4.3 Recruitment

Sufficient subjects are screened to randomize approximately 800 (~400/arm); the protocol projects approximately 1150 screened. Subjects are recruited from multiple geographic regions. *(Source: Protocol §4.1, page 125)*

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **Withdrawal Levels:** Permanent randomized-treatment discontinuation before Week 96, or open-label discontinuation before commercial drug supply, is study withdrawal. Subjects may discontinue voluntarily; the investigator may discontinue at discretion; and withdrawal may occur for subject/investigator/sponsor request, noncompliance, prohibited therapy, confirmed virologic failure, pregnancy, required substitution/dose reduction, specified QTc/liver/renal toxicity, or causally related Grade 4 clinical AE. *(Source: Protocol §4.5, pages 128–130; §4.6, pages 130–131)*
- **Withdrawal Data Collection:** Perform and record Withdrawal and Follow-up procedures before discharge, record essential withdrawal evaluations and CRF reason, and make every effort to follow withdrawing subjects. Ongoing clinical/laboratory AEs trigger a medically appropriate follow-up visit approximately four weeks after last dose. *(Source: Protocol §§4.5,3.1.5–3.1.6, pages 129–130,123–124)*
- **Reasons for Withdrawal:** Subjects need not state a reason; the investigator documents the stated reason or absence of reason in the Completion/Withdrawal CRF. Subjects are not replaced. *(Source: Protocol §4.5, page 130)*

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **Baseline Variables:** Summarize gender, age, ethnicity/race, height, weight, hepatitis status, CDC HIV class, HIV risk/mode, current/past medical conditions, cardiovascular risk, screening/baseline RNA and CD4, and factors affecting completion. Baseline is the last pre-treatment value unless otherwise specified. *(Source: Protocol §§6.1–6.2, pages 139–143; RAP §§9.2.2,10.3, pages 253,266–267)*
- **Summary Methods:** Present counts/percentages for categorical characteristics and descriptive summaries for continuous values by treatment; list subject-level data. *(Source: RAP §10.3, pages 266–267)*

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **Primary Analysis Methods:** In ITT-E, compare Week-48 MSDF response with a CMH stratified adjusted common risk difference across RNA/CD4 strata, Sato variance and two-sided 95% CI. NI is declared if lower CI >−10%. *(Source: Protocol §§8.3.3,8.3.5, pages 167–169; RAP §§11.1.1–11.1.2, pages 269–271)*
- **Covariate Adjustment:** Primary CMH uses baseline RNA and CD4 strata. CD4 MMRM includes baseline RNA/CD4, visit and interactions; SDM/EQ-5D ANCOVA includes primary categorical covariates, gender, race, age and baseline score; bone/lipid models use stated baseline covariates. *(Source: RAP §§11.1–11.2, pages 269–275; §§12.7,13, pages 280–287)*
- **Assumption Checks:** Test CMH homogeneity using Fleiss weighted least-squares chi-square at one-sided 10%. Assess ANCOVA normality/homoscedasticity by normal-probability and standardized-residual plots and assess influential observations. *(Source: Protocol §8.3.5.1, page 169; RAP §§11.1.3.2.2,13, pages 272,284)*
- **Alternative Methods if Assumptions Fail:** Explore/report stratum-specific effects for material heterogeneity; combine sparse subgroups or remove baseline factors if logistic convergence fails; conduct additional health-outcome sensitivity analysis if model assumptions fail. *(Source: RAP §§11.1.3,13, pages 271–272,284)*
- **Sensitivity Analyses:** Repeat primary comparison in PP and with the alternate continuity-corrected CMH variance estimator; conduct TRDF and ERDF Kaplan–Meier sensitivity analyses; CD4 MI, LOCF and missing=baseline; SDM/EQ-5D OC; and lipid LOCF/OC where stated. TRDF/ERDF, subgroup and homogeneity analyses are supportive and have no multiplicity adjustment. *(Source: RAP §§6.2,7.2,11.1.3,11.2,13, pages 243–245,270–275,283–287; Appendix §17.5, pages 306–307)*
- **Subgroup Analyses:** Explore treatment effects using logistic regression with treatment-by-subgroup interactions; prespecified strata are baseline RNA and CD4, with gender, race, subtype and other listed descriptors. Interaction p-values use 5%; sparse groups can be combined. *(Source: Protocol §2.2, page 121; RAP §§8.3,11.1.3, pages 246–247,271–272)*

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **Missing Data Assumptions:** MSDF is a virology-first algorithm rather than a stated MAR assumption. CD4 MMRM/MI uses MAR; a treatment-related withdrawal adjustment assumes 3 CD4 cells/week decline off treatment. No general assumption is reported for observed-case data. *(Source: RAP §§9.1.1–9.1.5,11.2.2.2, pages 250–252,273–275)*
- **Handling Methods:** MSDF classifies missing/switch/discontinuation at RNA endpoints; CD4 uses MMRM, 1000 MI, LOCF and missing=baseline; health outcomes use LOCF and OC; other laboratory, RNA continuous, CD8, vitals and ECG analyses use observed data without imputation. For SDM, fewer than 10% missing items (up to two) are treated as symptom absent; if >2 are missing, LOCF is applied at individual symptom level and OC aggregate scores are missing. Lipid LOCF excludes baseline lipid-lowering users, replaces later values after initiation with the last fasted on-treatment value before initiation, and does not impute other missing lipid values. Partial birth dates use day 15 or June 30 as specified; partial AE dates use first/last day of month. *(Source: RAP §§6.2,9.1, pages 243–244,250–253)*

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

Additional analyses include Kaplan–Meier time-to-suppression and ACTG failure; longitudinal snapshot response plots; CDC disease-progression and HIV-associated-condition summaries; resistance prevalence at PDVF; exploratory ANCOVA of bone biomarkers and lipids; ECG, Framingham-risk, hepatitis, PGx and detailed data listings. *(Source: RAP §§9.2,11–15, pages 254–289)*

### 5.6 Harms

- **Adverse Event Definitions:** An AE is any untoward medical occurrence temporally associated with a medicinal product, regardless of causality. SAE includes death, life-threatening event, hospitalization/prolongation, disability, congenital anomaly, medically important events, Hy’s Law events, and suspected ABC hypersensitivity. *(Source: Protocol §§6.4.4.1–6.4.4.2, pages 156–158)*
- **Safety Analysis Methods:** Safety population; summarize exposure, post-baseline AEs by MedDRA SOC/preferred term/severity/relatedness/discontinuation/seriousness, labs/vitals/ECG by visit and treatment, and grade 2–4/grade 3–4 AEs. Safety uses observed-case data; data are summarized for randomized phase and specified long-term DTG outputs. Also list exposure duration and dose changes/interruptions; deaths, SAEs, neurologic/psychiatric AEs, pregnancies, suspected ABC hypersensitivity, and liver events. Summarize laboratory toxicity grades/ranges and shifts, NCEP lipid-category changes, ECG QTc categories (≤450, >450–≤480, >480–≤500, >500 msec) and change categories (≤30, >30–≤60, >60 msec), vital signs, and liver stopping-criterion/RUCAM follow-up information. *(Source: Protocol §8.3.5.2, page 169; RAP §§6.2.6,12.1–12.8.4, pages 244,277–283)*
- **Grading Scales and Causality:** Investigators grade AEs using DAIDS Table for Grading the Severity of Adult and Pediatric AEs, Version 1.0 (December 2004), clarification August 2009; relationship is recorded and treatment-related categories are summarized. AEs are collected from first IP through follow-up; related SAEs from consent through follow-up. *(Source: Protocol §§6.4.2,6.4.9, pages 146,160; Appendix 2, page 180; RAP §12.2, pages 277–278)*
