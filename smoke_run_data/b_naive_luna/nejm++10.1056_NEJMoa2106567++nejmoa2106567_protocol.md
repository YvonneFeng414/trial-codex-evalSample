# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1\. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

- **[Provided]** EFC14153 evaluates subcutaneous dupilumab in children aged 6 to <12 years with uncontrolled persistent asthma who are receiving background controller therapy.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **Primary objective (P1):** Evaluate the efficacy of dupilumab in children aged 6 to <12 years with uncontrolled persistent asthma. The objective-level outcome is efficacy.
- **[Provided]** **Secondary objective (S1):** Assess safety and tolerability; patient-reported outcomes (PROs), including health-related quality of life (HRQoL); systemic exposure; anti-drug antibodies; and the association of treatment with pediatric immune responses to tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis, and/or seasonal trivalent/quadrivalent influenza vaccination.
- **[Provided]** **Exploratory objective (S2):** Explore biomarker prediction or association with response and optional genetic profiles; evaluate inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)/second-controller step-up and additional PROs.

## 2\. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **[Provided]** This is a multinational, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study. Screening lasts 4±1 weeks, and treatment lasts a maximum of 52 weeks.
- **[Provided]** Participants not entering the one-year extension have a 12-week post-treatment period.
- **[Provided]** Participants are randomized 2:1 to subcutaneous dupilumab or matching placebo, on background medium-dose ICS plus a second controller, or high-dose ICS alone or with a second controller.
- **[Provided]** At baseline weight ≤30 kg, the assigned regimen is dupilumab 100 mg Q2W or matching placebo (0.67 mL). At baseline weight >30 kg, it is dupilumab 200 mg Q2W or matching placebo (1.14 mL).
- **[Provided]** Investigators and patients are unblinded to dose level because the dose volumes differ.
- **[Provided]** Background controllers continue. Temporary ICS increases of up to fourfold for ≤10 days are allowed. Permanent controller step-up may follow at least two severe exacerbations.
- **[Provided]** Scheduled visits use a ±5-day window. Exceptions apply to end-of-treatment discontinuation (ETD)/end-of-treatment (EOT) assessments and telephone follow-up; the specific exceptions are not stated.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the exact ETD/EOT assessment and telephone follow-up procedures. These determine the assessments and follow-up expected after treatment discontinuation and at study end.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **[Provided]** Central IVRS/IWRS permuted-block randomization occurs after screening, with a 2:1 allocation to dupilumab and matching placebo.
- **[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by screening ICS dose (medium/high), screening eosinophils (<0.3/≥0.3 Giga/L), and the following region categories:
  - **[Provided]** Latin America: Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Chile, Mexico.
  - **[Provided]** Eastern Europe: Poland, Hungary, Romania, Lithuania, Russia, Ukraine, Turkey.
  - **[Provided]** Western Countries: Australia, Canada, Italy, South Africa, Spain, USA.
- **[Provided]** These randomization strata are distinct from pooled-country regional analysis.
- **[Provided]** Minimization is not specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify block sizes or whether they vary, and any additional allocation-concealment procedures beyond central IVRS/IWRS. These details affect reproducibility and assessment of allocation concealment.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** The planned study size is at least 402 participants overall: 268 assigned to dupilumab and 134 to placebo.
- **[Provided]** The powered populations and assumptions are:
  - **[Provided]** Type 2 inflammatory phenotype (eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L or FeNO ≥20 ppb): approximately 345 participants (230 dupilumab/115 placebo); assumed placebo rate 0.7/year, dupilumab rate 0.322/year, relative-risk reduction 54%, and power 94%.
  - **[Provided]** Eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L: 327 participants (218/109); assumed placebo rate 0.7/year, dupilumab rate 0.322/year, relative-risk reduction 54%, and power 93%.
  - **[Provided]** Eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L: 255 participants (170/85); assumed placebo rate 0.8/year, dupilumab rate 0.32/year, relative-risk reduction 60%, and power 96%.
- **[Provided]** Calculations assumed the annualized rate of severe exacerbations during 52 weeks as a negative-binomial count/rate outcome, a negative-binomial comparison between dupilumab and placebo, dispersion parameter 1.5, 2:1 allocation, two-sided alpha 0.05, linear discontinuation of 20% at one year, and average exposure of 0.9 year.
- **[Provided]** Projected prevalences were 86% for Type 2 phenotype, 81% for eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, and 64% for eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L. Relative-risk reductions were based on Phase 3 EFC13579 (QUEST).
- **[Provided]** Sizing was intended to assure target power across the three biomarker-defined populations under 2:1 allocation. The required number of events was not specified. A non-inferiority/equivalence margin is not applicable because this is a superiority comparison; design effect/clustering is not applicable because this is an individually randomized trial. Calculation software was not specified.
- **[Provided]** Amendment 2 increased the sample size from 294 to 471. Amendment 3 revised the primary populations and final calculation to 402.
- **[Clarification Required]** Confirm whether 402 is the final enrollment target or minimum, and how it relates operationally to the earlier 471 target. This is needed to interpret recruitment targets and final sample-size planning.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

- **[Provided]** The framework uses superiority hypotheses comparing dupilumab with placebo for severe-exacerbation rate and sequentially tested efficacy endpoints. The final SAP uses regional hierarchical sequences to control two-sided Type I error at 5%.
- **[Provided]** The US/reference sequence is specified as orders 1–24:
  - **[Provided]** Orders 1–3: severe-exacerbation rate in eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L, eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, and Type 2 phenotype, in that order.
  - **[Provided]** Orders 4–6: Week-12 pre-bronchodilator (pre-BD) FEV1 in eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L, eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, and Type 2 phenotype, in that order.
  - **[Provided]** Orders 7–9: Week-24 ACQ-7 in eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L, eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, and Type 2 phenotype, in that order.
  - **[Provided]** Orders 10–12: the same three endpoints—severe-exacerbation rate, Week-12 pre-BD FEV1, and Week-24 ACQ-7—in the FeNO ≥20 ppb population.
  - **[Provided]** Orders 13–14: severe-exacerbation rate and FEV1 in the full ITT population.
  - **[Provided]** Orders 15–19: severe-exacerbation rate in the high-ICS populations with eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L, eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, Type 2 phenotype, FeNO ≥20 ppb, and full ITT, in that order.
  - **[Provided]** Orders 20–24: Week-12 FeNO in the populations with eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L, eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, Type 2 phenotype, FeNO ≥20 ppb, and full ITT, in that order.
- **[Provided]** The EU/reference sequence uses the same ordered endpoint groups, starts the first three, FEV1, and ACQ-7 sequences with Type 2, eosinophils ≥0.15 Giga/L, and eosinophils ≥0.3 Giga/L, and retains that order for high-ICS and FeNO.
- **[Clarification Required]** Confirm the complete EU/reference order for every population and endpoint, including the FeNO ≥20 ppb groups and any full-ITT groups. The supplied description does not unambiguously assign every sequence position, which could affect multiplicity-controlled conclusions.
- **[Proposed]** Implement the stated hierarchy as a fixed-sequence procedure: test each hypothesis at the specified two-sided alpha only if all preceding hypotheses in the applicable sequence reject. **Rationale:** This is a direct operational implementation of sequential testing and hierarchical Type I error control. **Assumption:** No alpha recycling, branching, or other multiplicity procedure is intended.
- **[Clarification Required]** Confirm whether the fixed-sequence assumption is the intended rule and specify how the US/reference and EU/reference sequences are assigned and used. This determines which confirmatory claims are supported.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **[Provided]** The originally planned two database locks were integrated into one final lock.
- **[Clarification Required]** State whether any formal interim efficacy, safety, or futility analyses are planned. The integration of database locks does not establish whether interim analyses occur, and their presence could affect oversight and Type I error control.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **[Provided]** The final database lock occurs after every randomized participant completes Week 52 or discontinues earlier. Analyses through that lock are final CSR analyses.
- **[Provided]** Treatment of post-lock data is decided for the CSR/addendum; no calendar date is specified.
- **[Provided]** The primary rate assessment covers the period through Week 52. Key FEV1 is assessed at Week 12. Spirometry is performed at screening, randomization, and subsequent visits.
- **[Provided]** Post-bronchodilator assessments include Weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, 52, and 64.

## 3\. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **[Provided]** The sample-size calculations use a two-sided alpha of 0.05, and the hierarchical framework controls two-sided Type I error at 5%.
- **[Proposed]** Report treatment effects with two-sided 95% confidence intervals and corresponding p-values for confirmatory comparisons. **Rationale:** This aligns inference reporting with the supplied two-sided 5% framework. **Assumption:** No different confidence level is specified in the final SAP.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the confidence-interval method for each endpoint and whether confidence intervals are multiplicity-adjusted. This affects interpretation of estimates outside the confirmatory testing sequence.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **[Proposed]** Define important deviations prospectively, including deviations affecting eligibility, randomized treatment, study medication exposure, background-controller requirements, endpoint assessment, or follow-up. Document deviations before database lock and apply the definitions consistently. **Rationale:** Prespecified classification supports reproducible analysis-population decisions. **Assumption:** The final protocol/SAP will identify which deviations are considered major or analysis-relevant.
- **[Clarification Required]** Provide the protocol-specific deviation categories and rules for determining major deviations. These determine any per-protocol population and sensitivity analyses.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **[Provided]** The efficacy hierarchy includes a full ITT population.
- **[Proposed]** Define the full ITT population as all randomized participants, analyzed according to randomized assignment. **Rationale:** This preserves the randomized comparison. **Assumption:** No protocol-specific ITT exclusions are intended.
- **[Proposed]** Define a safety population as randomized participants who receive at least one dose of study treatment, analyzed according to treatment received. **Rationale:** This permits safety summaries to reflect actual exposure. **Assumption:** At least one dose is the intended safety-population criterion.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the membership and analysis rules for the full ITT population, safety population, and any per-protocol or other analysis populations. These rules affect the population represented by each estimate.

## 4\. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

- **[Provided]** Screening information includes age, asthma history and control, background controller regimen, pre-BD FEV1 and FEV1/FVC, reversibility, prior-year exacerbation or hospital/emergency care, screening ACQ-5 and other uncontrolled-asthma evidence, eosinophils, FeNO, and diary-recorded background adherence.
- **[Provided]** Screening ICS dose and screening eosinophil category are randomization stratification factors.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the full screening data collection schedule, screening failure categories, and handling of repeat or out-of-window screening assessments. These details affect participant disposition and the population available for analysis.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

- **[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are age 6 to <12 years; physician-diagnosed persistent asthma for ≥12 months; uncontrolled persistent asthma; and medium-dose ICS plus one controller, or high-dose ICS alone or with a second controller, for ≥3 months with a stable dose for ≥1 month before screening. A third controller is not permitted.
- **[Provided]** Inclusion criteria also require pre-BD FEV1 ≤95% predicted or FEV1/FVC <0.85 at screening/baseline; ≥10% reversibility with stated alternatives; a prior-year systemic corticosteroid (SCS)-treated exacerbation or hospital/emergency care; and screening evidence of uncontrolled asthma consisting of ACQ-5 ≥1.5, reliever use, sleep awakening, or symptoms.
- **[Provided]** Participants must be able to comply, have caregiver comprehension, provide required assent/consent, and complete questionnaires.
- **[Provided]** Exclusion criteria include age <6 or ≥12 years; weight <16 kg; chronic lung disease or life-threatening asthma; interfering disease, malignancy, or inability to follow procedures; timing-restricted omalizumab/other biologic or immunosuppressant use, allergen immunotherapy, investigational medicines, or prohibited concomitants; prior dupilumab study participation or a site-staff relationship; and <80% diary-recorded background adherence.
- **[Provided]** Exclusions also include specified corticosteroid exposure, including prohibited SCS/topical steroid; clinically significant illness, including stated tuberculosis provisions, or major medical disease; pregnancy/contraception restrictions; parasite, HIV, infection, immunosuppression, autoimmune disease, biologic hypersensitivity, or viral-hepatitis risk; liver injury or specified liver/laboratory abnormalities, including CPK, platelet, eosinophil, and creatinine thresholds; consent withdrawal; or study-level enrollment cessation.
- **[Clarification Required]** Provide the exact reversibility alternatives, specified corticosteroid exposure restrictions, tuberculosis provisions, pregnancy/contraception requirements, laboratory thresholds, and timing windows for restricted therapies. The summary does not provide these operational definitions, and they determine eligibility.
- **[Clarification Required]** Define the required screening evidence of uncontrolled asthma beyond ACQ-5 ≥1.5, including the reliever-use, sleep-awakening, and symptom criteria. These definitions affect consistent enrollment decisions.

### 4.3 Recruitment

- **[Provided]** The study is multinational and multicenter, with an overall planned sample size of at least 402 participants.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **[Provided]** Participants receive treatment for a maximum of 52 weeks. Participants not entering the one-year extension have a 12-week post-treatment period.
- **[Provided]** Every randomized participant is followed to Week 52 or earlier discontinuation for the final database-lock criterion. ETD/EOT assessment and telephone follow-up exceptions apply, but their details are not supplied.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify withdrawal categories, whether participants who stop study treatment should continue scheduled assessments, and the follow-up requirements after withdrawal of treatment versus withdrawal of consent. - **[Provided]** The primary objective-level outcome is efficacy; a more specific primary-outcome label is not supplied.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **[Proposed]** Analyze severe-exacerbation rate using a negative-binomial regression, reporting the dupilumab-versus-placebo rate ratio, confidence interval, and p-value. **Rationale:** This matches the negative-binomial outcome and comparison assumed in the supplied sample-size calculations. **Assumption:** The final SAP confirms a negative-binomial model for the primary analysis.
- **[Proposed]** Use the log of observed follow-up time as an offset in the rate model and consider adjustment for the randomization stratification factors. **Rationale:** The offset accounts for unequal observed follow-up, while stratification-factor adjustment reflects the randomization design. **Assumption:** Follow-up time is validly measured and the final SAP accepts these model terms.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the primary model covariates, treatment of regional factors, dispersion estimation, and rules for participants with no post-randomization follow-up. These choices can affect rate estimates and inference.
- **[Proposed]** For Week-12 pre-BD FEV1, use a baseline-adjusted treatment comparison, with the exact analysis metric and covariates prespecified. **Rationale:** This provides an adjusted comparison for a continuous lung-function endpoint. **Assumption:** The endpoint is the Week-12 pre-BD FEV1 measure identified in the hierarchy.
- **[Proposed]** For Week-24 ACQ-7 and Week-12 FeNO, use endpoint-appropriate treatment comparisons with baseline value adjustment when available. **Rationale:** Baseline adjustment can account for initial endpoint levels. **Assumption:** The final SAP confirms continuous endpoint scales and available baseline measurements.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the exact FEV1 metric, ACQ-7 scoring and change definition, FeNO analysis scale or transformation, and models for these endpoints. These choices determine the estimands and reported treatment effects.
- **[Proposed]** Apply the multiplicity procedure specified for the applicable regional hierarchy to confirmatory efficacy tests; report other efficacy and objective-related analyses as exploratory unless otherwise designated. **Rationale:** This distinguishes multiplicity-controlled claims from additional analyses. **Assumption:** No other confirmatory testing family is specified.
