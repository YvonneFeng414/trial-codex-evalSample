# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1\. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

- **[Provided]** IPM 027 is a pivotal Phase III evaluation of whether a 25-mg dapivirine silicone-elastomer vaginal matrix ring (Ring-004), replaced every 4 weeks, safely prevents HIV infection in healthy HIV-negative women compared with an indistinguishable placebo ring.
- **[Provided]** The program addresses the need for woman-controlled HIV-prevention options and evaluates a monthly product intended for sustained use independent of sexual activity.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary efficacy:** Determine the efficacy of dapivirine ring use every 4 weeks for approximately 24 months (104 weeks) in preventing HIV-1 infection among healthy, sexually active HIV-negative women. The outcome is HIV-1 infection prevention on this schedule.
- **[Provided]** **P2—Primary safety:** Assess the safety of dapivirine ring use every 4 weeks for approximately 24 months (104 weeks). The outcome is safety on this schedule.
- **[Provided]** **S1—Secondary:** Compare HIV-2 incidence; curable STI incidence and vaginal-flora changes; and determine pregnancy incidence. Outcomes are HIV-2 incidence, curable STI incidence, vaginal-flora changes, and pregnancy incidence.
- **[Provided]** **S2—Secondary:** Assess reported 4-week ring-use adherence and acceptability.
- **[Provided]** **S3—Secondary:** Assess the frequency and type of HIV-1 resistance in women acquiring HIV-1 while using investigational product (IP).
- **[Provided]** **S4—Exploratory:** Evaluate the HSV-2/HIV-1 association; relationships between contraception method, pregnancy and HIV seroconversion; drug concentrations versus HIV-1 seroconversion or viral resistance; and drug concentrations versus self-reported adherence.

## 2\. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **[Provided]** This is a multi-centre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pivotal Phase III safety-and-efficacy trial with a 2:1 allocation ratio of dapivirine to ring placebo.
- **[Provided]** In both arms, participants use a ring continuously and self-insert and replace it at 4-week intervals for approximately 104 weeks. Exit follow-up is 6 weeks after ring discontinuation.
- **[Provided]** The dapivirine arm uses Ring-004, a silicone-elastomer vaginal matrix ring containing 25 mg dapivirine. The control arm uses a placebo vaginal ring.
- **[Provided]** Screening may last up to 28 days. On-IP visit windows are ±7 days. Every 4 weeks, visits include HIV and pregnancy testing, AE and concomitant-medication review, diary/adherence data, and ring exchange. Every 12 weeks, pelvic, STI, and safety laboratory assessments are scheduled. Acceptability is assessed at week 4 and every 24 weeks from week 24. Cervical cytology is collected at screening, week 52, and the last product-use visit.
- **[Clarification Required]** The number and locations of research centres and the specific procedures at the 6-week exit visit are not stated. These are needed to define site-level conduct and the complete follow-up schedule.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **[Provided]** An automated IXRS (Interactive Voice/Web Response System) assigns each participant a unique identification number and treatment. A master randomization list links participant identification numbers to assignments.
- **[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by research centre, and a pre-specified block size is used. Minimization is not specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** The block size and any operational details for implementing allocation concealment are not stated. These details are needed to document and reproduce the allocation procedure.
- **[Clarification Required]** The roles included in the double-blind and any circumstances or procedures for unblinding are not specified. These matter for maintaining the blind and interpreting potentially bias-sensitive assessments.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** Planned enrollment was approximately 1,950 women: 1,300 assigned to dapivirine and 650 to placebo. A total of 1,959 women were enrolled, and accrual closed.
- **[Provided]** Amendment 3 increased planned enrollment from 1,650 to approximately 1,950 to mitigate product nonadherence and preserve power after high nonadherence at one research centre. This was an amendment, not a prospectively described sample-size re-estimation algorithm.
- **[Provided]** The calculation concerned HIV-1 seroconversion incidence in the two treatment arms, using a time-to-event incidence-rate framework under proportional hazards. The assumed average annual placebo-arm HIV incidence was greater than 4%, allowing for reduction from risk-reduction counselling. The assumed treatment-arm microbicide efficacy was at least 50%.
- **[Provided]** The target effect was a 50% reduction in HIV-1 seroconversion rate; scenarios also evaluated 40% and 60% efficacy. The calculation used a two-sided significance level of 0.05 and a two-sided log-rank test. Early discontinuation or loss to follow-up of no more than 10% was assumed.
- **[Provided]** The expected-event and power scenarios were: 50% efficacy, 81% power and 96 expected HIV-1 seroconversions; 40% efficacy, 61% power and 104 expected seroconversions; and 60% efficacy, 94% power and 88 expected seroconversions. These are expected events, not a specified required-event target.
- **[Provided]** With 1,950 participants, the trial also has a 95% probability of detecting an AE occurring at a frequency of at least 0.3% among participants assigned IP.
- **[Provided]** A non-inferiority or equivalence margin is not applicable because this is a superiority efficacy trial. A design effect or clustering adjustment is not applicable because randomization is individual, with centre stratification rather than cluster randomization.
- **[Clarification Required]** The sample-size calculation software is not specified. This matters for reproducibility of the calculation.
- **[Clarification Required]** Actual numbers randomized to each arm are not given. The planned 1,300:650 allocation should not be treated as the actual allocation counts.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

- **[Provided]** The primary efficacy null hypothesis is that HIV-1 seroconversion rates or population survival curves do not differ between dapivirine and placebo. The design tests superiority.
- **[Provided]** The primary safety analysis is descriptive.
- **[Proposed]** Use a two-sided superiority test for the primary efficacy comparison, with a lower HIV-1 seroconversion rate in the dapivirine arm supporting efficacy. **Rationale:** this operationalizes the stated superiority design and the two-sided calculation. **Assumption:** the calculation’s two-sided 0.05 level is intended to guide the primary efficacy analysis.
- **[Clarification Required]** The inferential status of the secondary outcomes and the multiplicity strategy across outcomes are not specified. This matters for controlling interpretation of multiple comparisons and for distinguishing confirmatory from exploratory findings.

## 3\. Statistical Principles

- **[Provided]** The sample-size calculation used a two-sided significance level of 0.05.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **[Provided]** Deviations are reviewed and classified while treatment remains blinded and before database lock.
- **[Proposed]** Finalize deviation definitions and the rules for identifying major deviations before treatment codes are released; summarize deviations by randomized arm after unblinding. **Rationale:** prespecification and blinded classification reduce the risk that treatment knowledge affects deviation assessment. **Assumption:** deviation data are collected in a form that permits classification.
- **[Clarification Required]** The criteria for deviation categories and the consequences of each category for analysis populations are not specified. These matter for consistent classification and interpretation of any per-protocol or sensitivity analyses.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **[Proposed]** Define the primary efficacy population as all randomized participants analyzed according to assigned treatment, regardless of ring adherence or discontinuation. **Rationale:** this preserves randomized comparisons and addresses the assigned-treatment effect. **Assumption:** randomization status and assigned treatment are available for all participants.
- **[Proposed]** Define a safety population as randomized participants who use at least one ring, analyzed according to the treatment actually received. **Rationale:** safety summaries should reflect exposure. **Assumption:** ring initiation and actual treatment received are recorded.
- **[Proposed]** Use any per-protocol or adherence-restricted efficacy analyses only as supportive analyses and define their eligibility rules before unblinding. **Rationale:** adherence is a study objective and may inform interpretation, but post-randomization selection can affect comparability. **Assumption:** such analyses are desired and the required adherence and deviation data are available.
- **[Clarification Required]** The protocol-specified analysis populations and any handling of participants who are randomized but never initiate ring use are not stated. These decisions affect denominators and interpretation of efficacy and safety results.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

- **[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are: women aged 18–45 years; able to consent and attend procedures; sexually active, defined as at least one penetrative penile-vaginal act per month in the prior 3 months; HIV-negative at screening/enrolment; using stable contraception unless postmenopausal or surgically sterilized; asymptomatic for genital infection at enrolment; locatable; and not participating in conflicting investigational studies.
- **[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are: current or recent intended pregnancy or breastfeeding; specified injection-drug, research, or vaccine histories; untreated significant urogenital infection or a grade ≥2 pelvic finding; specified genital, medical, or allergy conditions; clinically important baseline laboratory abnormalities; disqualifying cytology; or investigator-judged risk or inability to adhere.
