# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** CAPiTA is a phase 4 trial of 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (13vPnC) to prevent vaccine-serotype pneumococcal community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) in older adults in the Netherlands. The final controlling protocol is Amendment 9, dated 01 October 2013.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1 — Primary:** Demonstrate 13vPnC efficacy in prevention of a first episode of confirmed vaccine-type (VT) pneumococcal CAP. **Outcome:** First episode of confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP.
- **[Provided]** **S1 — Secondary:** Demonstrate efficacy against first confirmed nonbacteremic/noninvasive (NB/NI)-VT pneumococcal CAP and first VT-IPD. **Outcomes:** First confirmed NB/NI-VT pneumococcal CAP and first VT-IPD.
- **[Provided]** **S2 — Exploratory:** Evaluate prevention of all confirmed VT-CAP; first confirmed pneumococcal, NB/NI pneumococcal, CAP, and IPD; first/all confirmed VT-CAP by individual 13vPnC serotype; first/all NVT and pneumococcal CAP; first/all NB/NI-VT-CAP and NB/NI-VT-CAP by individual serotype; first/all culture-confirmed VT, pneumococcal, and NVT CAP; and first/all probable VT, pneumococcal, NVT and possible pneumococcal CAP. **Outcomes:** These same event categories.
- **[Provided]** **S3 — Exploratory:** Evaluate all CAP; all VT-IPD, first/all NVT-IPD, all IPD; deaths from confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP/VT-IPD, pneumococcal CAP/IPD and all causes; hospitalization with CAP plus influenza A/B and specified respiratory viruses; age efficacy trends and economic effect. **Outcomes:** These same event categories and evaluations.
- **[Provided]** **S4 — Exploratory:** Evaluate VT, all, NVT and individual-serotype *S. pneumoniae* carriage and *H. influenzae*, *M. catarrhalis*, and *S. aureus* carriage at 6, 12, and 24 months; safety objectives are SAE incidence, subset reactogenicity/AEs and deaths; immunogenicity objective is serotype IgG/OPA by age and sex at baseline and approximately months 1, 12, and 24. **Outcomes:** These same carriage, safety, and immunogenicity measures.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **[Provided]** Phase 4, parallel-group, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial conducted at multiple sites/regions in the Netherlands by the Julius Center.
- **[Provided]** Allocation is 1:1 to 13vPnC or placebo. Participants receive one 0.5-mL intramuscular deltoid dose at Visit 1.
- **[Provided]** 13vPnC contains serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, and 23F conjugated to CRM197; 2 micrograms each except 6B at 4 micrograms; pH 5.8, 5 mM succinate, 0.85% sodium chloride, 0.02% polysorbate 80, and 0.125 mg aluminum phosphate.
- **[Provided]** Placebo contains pH 5.8, 5 mM succinate, 0.15 M sodium chloride, 0.02% polysorbate 80, and 0.125 mg aluminum per 0.5 mL.
- **[Clarification Required]** The operational procedures that maintain double-blinding, including syringe appearance, preparation, and emergency unblinding, are not specified. They matter for implementation and assessment of bias.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **[Provided]** A sponsor/designee-generated randomization schedule is used. Eligible, consented subjects receive the next available syringe, whose PAC identifies allocation. A randomized block design randomly orders vaccine groups within blocks, with several blocks assigned at a time.
- **[Provided]** The immunogenicity subset is age-stratified into 65–69, 70–79, and ≥80 years, with at least 300 participants in each stratum.
- **[Provided]** Trial-wide randomization stratification is not specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** The meaning of PAC, the block sizes, and the operational handling of the allocation schedule are not supplied. These details matter for reproducibility and evaluation of allocation concealment.
- **[Clarification Required]** Whether any trial-wide stratification is intended remains unresolved; the input specifies age stratification only for the immunogenicity subset. This matters for randomization implementation and any stratified analysis.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** Planned enrollment is approximately 85,000 participants (42,500 per group), including an immunogenicity subset of 2,000 (1,000 per group).
- **[Provided]** The calculation outcome is first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP; supplementary power statements address NB/NI-VT-CAP and VT-IPD. The outcome is time to first confirmed clinical event, analyzed as incidence rate/relative risk.
- **[Provided]** Assumed control-arm rates are VT-CAP incidence 1.2/1,000/year and placebo CAP, confirmed pneumococcal CAP, and confirmed VT-CAP rates of 10, 2.5, and 1.2/1,000/year, respectively.
- **[Provided]** Assumed true VT-CAP efficacy is at least 52%; specificity-adjusted efficacy is 48.4% in the revised model. Test specificity was assumed to be at least 97% initially; SSUAD was subsequently validated at 98%.
- **[Provided]** The design uses a two-sided 0.05 experiment-wise alpha. Planned power is 90% for primary VT-CAP, at least 80% for NB/NI-VT-CAP under the stated assumptions, and at least 90% for VT-IPD if true efficacy is at least 90%.
- **[Provided]** The immunogenicity-subset IgG precision target is 0.10–0.16 for 95% CI differences.
- **[Provided]** The revised primary event target was derived using a Breslow-Day conditional binomial approach; analysis uses exact conditional confidence intervals with an O’Brien-Fleming adjustment. The original target was 156 primary events; revised targets are 65 interim and 130 final first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events. A DMC administrative review led to revision after observed incidence was lower than predicted.
- **[Provided]** The stated VT-IPD event requirement is 20 events. A non-inferiority/equivalence margin is not applicable because the framework is efficacy/superiority.
- **[Provided]** A 5% loss to follow-up was assumed. Design effect/clustering is not applicable because the trial is individually randomized and non-clustered.
- **[Provided]** Assumptions were based on clinical feasibility, one enrollment season, at least 2 years of follow-up, incidence assumptions, assay specificity, DMC review, and an external consultant-supported revised model. The 85,000 participants were selected to accrue required primary events; the 2,000-person subset was based on six-month safety regulatory requirements and feasibility.
- **[Clarification Required]** The calculation software is not specified. This matters for reproducibility of the sample-size derivation and analysis.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

- **[Provided]** The primary inference is vaccine efficacy = 1 − relative risk for first confirmed VT-CAP. Primary success requires the adjusted two-sided 95% CI lower bound to exceed 0.
- **[Provided]** Interim clinical significance requires a VT-CAP lower confidence bound greater than 0.20.
- **[Provided]** NB/NI-VT-CAP is tested hierarchically only following clinically significant primary efficacy.
- **[Provided]** VT-IPD is evaluated once at study conclusion and is considered efficacious if its two-sided 95% CI exceeds 0.
- **[Clarification Required]** The precise definition of “clinically significant primary efficacy” for activating the NB/NI-VT-CAP test is not specified beyond the interim lower-bound threshold. This matters for the testing sequence and multiplicity control.
- **[Clarification Required]** The confidence interval and decision rule to be used for final primary efficacy and VT-IPD, including how the interim adjustment applies to each, require specification to ensure the stated error control is implemented consistently.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **[Provided]** Revised event targets are 65 interim and 130 final first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events. The exact conditional confidence interval analysis uses an O’Brien-Fleming adjustment.
- **[Provided]** An interim efficacy declaration uses the interim dataset for inference; overrunning data are supplementary.
- **[Clarification Required]** The interim review schedule, data cutoff, stopping boundaries beyond the stated interim clinical-significance threshold, and DMC operating procedures are not specified. These affect implementation of interim monitoring and interpretation of the error-control procedure.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **[Provided]** If efficacy is not declared at interim, final efficacy inference occurs after investigator sign-off of at least 130 per-protocol first confirmed VT-CAP events, data cleaning, unblinding, and database freeze.
- **[Provided]** CAP/IPD surveillance continues through the case-acquisition stopping rule.
- **[Provided]** Immunogenicity blood collection is at baseline and months 1, 12, and 24. Carriage assessments are at baseline and months 6, 12, and 24.
- **[Provided]** Safety assessments include a 7-day reactogenicity window and 28-day unsolicited AE window. SAE assessment windows are not specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** The case-acquisition stopping rule and the calendar timing of interim and other analyses are not provided. They matter for defining follow-up completion and analysis cutoffs.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **[Provided]** The experiment-wise alpha is two-sided 0.05. Exact conditional confidence interval analysis with an O’Brien-Fleming adjustment is specified for the revised primary event target.
- **[Provided]** Primary efficacy is defined as 1 − relative risk, with success when the adjusted two-sided 95% CI lower bound exceeds 0. The interim clinical-significance threshold for VT-CAP is a lower bound greater than 0.20.
- **[Clarification Required]** The confidence-interval construction and multiplicity strategy for the full set of secondary and exploratory outcomes are not specified. This matters when interpreting nominal statistical significance across multiple outcomes.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **[Proposed]** Document protocol deviations before treatment-code access and classify them by nature and potential impact on participant safety, endpoint ascertainment, and analysis-population membership. **Rationale:** Prespecified, blinded classification supports transparent analysis. **Assumption:** The study has a process for reviewing deviations without access to treatment assignment.
- **[Clarification Required]** The protocol’s deviation categories, review process, and criteria for major deviations are not supplied. These decisions matter particularly to defining the per-protocol population used for the primary event target.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **[Provided]** The sample-size and primary event targets refer to first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events.
- **[Proposed]** Use the per-protocol population for the primary efficacy analysis, with a randomized-population analysis as a sensitivity analysis. **Rationale:** This aligns the primary analysis with the stated event target while checking robustness. **Assumption:** Randomized participants can be analyzed according to assigned group and endpoint follow-up is available.
- **[Proposed]** Define a safety population as randomized participants who receive study product, analyzed according to product received; define immunogenicity and carriage analysis sets from participants with the relevant baseline and follow-up measurements. **Rationale:** These sets match the supplied safety, immunogenicity, and carriage objectives. **Assumption:** Receipt of product and specimen availability are recorded.
- **[Clarification Required]** Formal population definitions, including handling of participants with eligibility deviations, incomplete dosing, and missing endpoint follow-up, are not supplied. These choices affect efficacy estimates and their interpretation.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

- **[Provided]** Inclusion criteria: adults aged ≥65 years at vaccination; registered with a referring GP; able to meet study requirements; and, for subset subjects, able to complete e-diary procedures.
- **[Provided]** Exclusion criteria: prior pneumococcal vaccination; institutional/semiskilled care; vaccine contraindication; recent investigational product; severe vaccine reaction; defined immune deficiency/suppression; and, for subset subjects, influenza vaccination in the preceding 7 days.
- **[Provided]** Acute febrile illness ≥38.0°C or recent acute illness delays enrollment.
- **[Clarification Required]** Operational definitions for “recent” investigational product, “severe” vaccine reaction, “defined” immune deficiency/suppression, and “recent” acute illness are not supplied. These matter for consistent eligibility decisions across sites.

### 4.3 Recruitment

- **[Provided]** The trial is conducted at multiple sites/regions in the Netherlands by the Julius Center, and participants must be registered with a referring GP.
- **[Provided]** One enrollment season is part of the planning assumptions underlying sample size.
- **[Clarification Required]** Recruitment methods, site counts, recruitment dates, and participant approach procedures are not specified. These matter for operational planning and assessing representativeness.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **[Provided]** CAP/IPD surveillance runs through the case-acquisition stopping rule; 5% loss to follow-up was assumed for planning.
- **[Clarification Required]** Withdrawal criteria, withdrawal procedures, follow-up duration for individual participants, and collection of outcomes after study-product discontinuation are not specified. These affect exposure time, endpoint ascertainment, and handling of withdrawals in analyses.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **[Provided]** Primary efficacy is expressed as 1 − relative risk for first confirmed VT-CAP. The revised primary event-target derivation uses a Breslow-Day conditional binomial approach, and exact conditional confidence intervals with an O’Brien-Fleming adjustment are specified.
- **[Proposed]** Report group-specific event counts and follow-up time, the relative risk and vaccine efficacy estimate, and its confidence interval for the primary outcome. **Rationale:** These outputs make the supplied incidence-rate/relative-risk framework interpretable. **Assumption:** Participant-level event and follow-up times are available.
- **[Clarification Required]** The precise estimator, time origin, censoring rules, and handling of competing events for the primary analysis are not specified. These choices determine the relative-risk estimate and confidence interval.
- **[Provided]** NB/NI-VT-CAP is hierarchically tested only after clinically significant primary efficacy. VT-IPD is evaluated once at study conclusion and is efficacious if its two-sided 95% CI exceeds 0.
- **[Proposed]** Present secondary and exploratory clinical outcomes with group-specific event counts and effect estimates with confidence intervals; treat exploratory comparisons as descriptive unless a formal testing procedure is specified. **Rationale:** This avoids implying confirmatory error control that was not supplied. **Assumption:** No additional multiplicity procedure is planned for exploratory objectives.
- **[Proposed]** Summarize carriage and immunogenicity measurements by visit and the specified organism/serotype, age, and sex categories. **Rationale:** This follows the stated S4 assessment dimensions. **Assumption:** The relevant assays and specimen results are available at the specified visits.
- **[Clarification Required]** Methods for age efficacy trends, serotype-specific comparisons, repeated carriage measurements, immunogenicity summaries, and economic evaluation are not specified. These decisions matter because the objectives involve multiple outcomes, visits, and subgroups.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **[Provided]** Sample-size planning assumed 5% loss to follow-up.
- **[Proposed]** Report missingness by group, visit, and outcome, and describe its extent and reasons where recorded; do not assume that missing outcomes are event-free. **Rationale:** This makes the impact of incomplete follow-up visible. **Assumption:** Reasons for missingness and follow-up status can be summarized.
- **[Clarification Required]** The approach to missing endpoint, safety, immunogenicity, and carriage data is not specified. This matters because different missing-data assumptions can change efficacy and ancillary outcome estimates.
