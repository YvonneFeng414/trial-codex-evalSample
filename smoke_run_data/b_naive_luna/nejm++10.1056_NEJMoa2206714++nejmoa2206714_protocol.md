# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

- **[Provided]** J2T-DM-KGAB (DRM06-AD04; ADvocate1) is a phase 3 study of lebrikizumab monotherapy versus placebo in adolescents and adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD).

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** The primary objective is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of lebrikizumab compared with placebo in moderate-to-severe AD. For FDA, the primary efficacy endpoint is Week-16 IGA 0/1 with at least a 2-point improvement. For EMA, the Week-16 IGA endpoint and EASI-75 are co-primary.
- **[Provided]** The secondary objective is to evaluate induction and maintenance efficacy, health outcomes and quality of life, rescue use, and serum lebrikizumab concentration.
- **[Provided]** Exploratory objectives are to provide supportive analyses of additional efficacy and patient-reported outcomes (PROs), and descriptive acceptability/tolerability results from the modified SQAAQ in participating EU adolescents.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **[Provided]** This is an international, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group phase 3 study comprising screening, 16-week double-blind induction, 36-week maintenance, escape treatment where applicable, and approximately 12-week safety follow-up after the last injection for early terminators or participants who do not enter the extension.
- **[Provided]** Induction allocation is 2:1 (LEB250Q2W:placebo). Eligible Week-16 responders are rerandomized 2:2:1 (LEB250Q2W:LEB250Q4W:placebo).
- **[Provided]** All injections are subcutaneous. Induction LEB250Q2W is 500 mg at baseline and Week 2 (two PFS-NSD loading doses), followed by 250 mg every 2 weeks through Week 14. Induction placebo is 4 mL at baseline and Week 2, followed by 2 mL every 2 weeks through Week 14.
- **[Provided]** Maintenance responders receive lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W, lebrikizumab 250 mg Q4W, or placebo Q2W; placebo injections maintain blinding.
- **[Provided]** Week-16 nonresponders, induction rescue users, and rerandomized patients who lose EASI-50 at Weeks 24, 32, 40, or 48 receive open-label lebrikizumab 250 mg Q2W to Week 52. Patients without EASI-50 after 8 weeks of escape treatment discontinue.
- **[Clarification Required]** The operational definition of Week-16 eligibility/response for maintenance rerandomization and the specific response criteria for “nonresponders” are not supplied. These definitions determine who enters maintenance and escape pathways.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **[Provided]** Baseline allocation is performed through electronic data capture (EDC) after eligibility is confirmed. EDC rerandomizes maintenance patients according to Week-16 IGA/EASI response and induction rescue use. Protocol Amendment 1 replaced earlier IWRS wording with EDC.
- **[Provided]** Stratification factors are geographic region (US, EU, rest of world), age (12 to <18 versus ≥18 years), and baseline disease severity (IGA 3 versus 4).
- **[Provided]** Minimization or blocking is not specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** The randomization sequence-generation and allocation-concealment procedures, including any block sizes or implementation details beyond EDC allocation, are not supplied. These details are important for assessing allocation integrity and reproducibility.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** Approximately 400 participants are planned, randomized 2:1 to lebrikizumab and placebo. The initial 96:48 calculation was increased to approximately 400 to collect safety information and ensure sufficient Week-16 responders for maintenance.
- **[Provided]** For FDA, the Week-16 IGA 0/1 calculation assumes response rates of 34.7% for LEB250Q2W and 7.7% for placebo. With 96 LEB250Q2W and 48 placebo participants, power is >95% using a two-group continuity-corrected chi-square test at two-sided α = 0.05.
- **[Provided]** For EMA, the same 96:48 allocation provides >95% power at two-sided α = 0.05 for each co-primary endpoint, Week-16 IGA 0/1 and EASI-75. Assumed EASI-75 response rates are 48.0% for lebrikizumab and 11.5% for placebo; overall power for both co-primary endpoints is ≥90%.
- **[Provided]** The stated target differences are 27.0 percentage points for IGA 0/1 and 36.5 percentage points for EASI-75, calculated from the stated Phase 2b assumptions. The calculation is based on fixed-time binary response proportions and does not require event accrual.
- **[Provided]** The stated basis for the assumptions is DRM06-AD01/J2T-DM-KGAF Phase 2b rescue-medication nonresponse sensitivity results. A separate variance parameter is not specified.
- **[Provided]** This is a superiority comparison; a non-inferiority/equivalence margin is not applicable. Design effect/clustering is not applicable for the individually randomized, non-clustered design. Dropout/non-adherence inflation, sample-size re-estimation/amendments, and calculation software are not specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** The target sample size for each maintenance arm and the anticipated number of Week-16 eligible responders are not supplied. These are needed to assess the precision and power of maintenance comparisons.
- **[Clarification Required]** The sample-size rationale does not specify how the approximately 400 participants are distributed across regions or other strata. This matters for planning enrollment and interpreting subgroup precision.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

- **[Provided]** The induction superiority null hypothesis is equality between LEB250Q2W and placebo for Week-16 IGA 0/1 with at least a 2-point improvement. EMA additionally tests equality for Week-16 EASI-75.
- **[Clarification Required]** Whether the EMA Week-16 IGA co-primary endpoint uses precisely the same IGA 0/1 and at least 2-point improvement definition as the FDA endpoint is not explicit. This affects endpoint specification and testing.
- **[Provided]** The FDA graphical family includes, in the stated sequence, IGA01 W16, EASI-75 W16, EASI-90 W16, Pruritus NRS-4 at Weeks 16, 4, and 2, IGA01 W4, adult IGA01 W16, and sleep-loss response W16.
- **[Provided]** The EMA induction graphical family starts with co-primary IGA01 W16 and EASI-75 W16 and includes EASI-90 W16/W4, EASI percent change W16, pruritus percent change and response at Weeks 16/4/2, DLQI response/change W16, and sleep-loss response/change W16.
- **[Provided]** For EMA maintenance, Q2W and Q4W each follow the prespecified ordered sequence: persistence of EASI-75; persistence of IGA response; persistence of at least a 4-point pruritus response; and percent EASI change at Week 52. The inferential comparisons are LEB-responder Q2W versus placebo and LEB-responder Q4W versus placebo.
- **[Clarification Required]** The graphical testing procedures’ starting alpha, weights, transfer rules, and overall type I error-control specifications are not supplied. These are necessary to define confirmatory inference across the listed endpoints.
- **[Clarification Required]** The assessment timepoints and operational definitions for “persistence” of the first three maintenance outcomes are not specified. These determine the endpoint data and analysis.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **[Clarification Required]** No interim analysis schedule, interim decision rule, or alpha-spending procedure is supplied. Whether any interim analyses are planned must be confirmed because interim testing can affect type I error and study conduct.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **[Provided]** The final database lock (DBL) follows safety follow-up, early discontinuation, or extension enrollment. The final DBL may be combined with the Week-52 DBL depending on the regulatory timeline.
- **[Provided]** Induction assessments are scheduled at baseline and Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16, with ±3-day windows. Maintenance assessments are scheduled at Weeks 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48, and 52, with ±5-day windows.
- **[Provided]** IGA, EASI, and BSA are scheduled through Week 16. IGA, EASI, BSA, and SCORAD, as well as intermittent PRO and safety assessments, continue during maintenance. Week-52 end-of-study/ETV assessments and a 12-week safety follow-up are conducted when required.
- **[Provided]** Pruritus NRS and sleep-loss daily diaries are mapped to baseline and Weeks 1, 2, and 4, then to scheduled visits through Week 52. Baseline requires at least 4 of 7 daily reports; postbaseline weekly values are prorated when at least 1 report is available.
- **[Provided]** Out-of-window assessments are retained unless otherwise stated.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **[Provided]** The sample-size calculations use two-sided α = 0.05.
- **[Proposed]** Report treatment-effect estimates with two-sided 95% confidence intervals for efficacy comparisons. Rationale: this aligns the interval level with the stated two-sided 0.05 calculation; assumption: no different confidence level is required by the finalized multiplicity procedure.
- **[Clarification Required]** The confirmatory significance level and confidence-interval approach for each endpoint, including multiplicity-adjusted inference, are not fully specified. This matters for interpreting statistical significance and coverage.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **[Proposed]** Define deviations prospectively and classify their impact on eligibility, treatment exposure, endpoint assessment, and analysis-population membership before unblinding. Rationale: consistent, blinded classification reduces analysis discretion; assumption: no deviation-classification rules have already been finalized.
- **[Clarification Required]** Study-specific definitions of major/important deviations and the consequences for analysis populations are not supplied. These decisions affect analysis inclusion and interpretability.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **[Proposed]** Define an induction efficacy population comprising all induction-randomized participants, analyzed according to randomized assignment. Rationale: this preserves the randomized comparison; assumption: the intended primary estimand is based on randomized assignment.
- **[Proposed]** Define a maintenance efficacy population comprising participants rerandomized at Week 16, analyzed according to maintenance assignment. Rationale: this reflects the maintenance randomization; assumption: the intended maintenance estimand is the effect among participants eligible and rerandomized at Week 16.
- **[Proposed]** Define a safety population comprising participants who receive at least one study injection, summarized according to treatment received. Rationale: safety summaries should reflect actual exposure; assumption: exposure is recorded sufficiently to classify participants.
- **[Clarification Required]** The protocol does not specify formal analysis-population definitions, including any per-protocol population or exclusions. These definitions should be finalized before analysis because they determine which participants contribute to each result.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

- **[Provided]** Screening eligibility data include age and weight, AD diagnosis/duration, baseline EASI, IGA and AD BSA, topical-treatment response or inadvisability, moisturizer use, baseline diary completion, ability to comply, contraception-related criteria, consent/assent, and the stated exclusion conditions including significant screening laboratory findings.
- **[Clarification Required]** The complete screening schedule, laboratory panel, screening-period duration, and handling of screening failures or repeat screening are not supplied. These details affect screening operations and participant disposition summaries.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

- **[Provided]** No prospective enrollment waiver is permitted.
- **[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are: adult or adolescent aged 12 to <18 years weighing at least 40 kg; AD by American Academy of Dermatology criteria for at least 1 year; baseline EASI ≥16, IGA ≥3/4, and AD BSA ≥10%; inadequate response to topical treatment or medical inadvisability of such treatment; stable nonmedicated moisturizer twice daily for at least 7 days; at least 4 of 7 baseline pruritus/sleep diary entries; ability to comply; specified WOCBP highly effective contraception/abstinence during treatment plus 18 weeks and male barrier contraception for 18 weeks; and consent/assent.
- **[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are: prior lebrikizumab; anaphylaxis; recent topical AD treatment, dupilumab/tralokinumab, systemic immunomodulator/phototherapy, investigational product/biologic, prescription moisturizer, tanning, or live vaccine; uncontrolled chronic disease/severe asthma; recent/active infection, hepatitis/cirrhosis, parasitic infection risk, immunosuppression, or HIV; significant screening laboratory findings; assessment-interfering skin disease; malignancy within 5 years except fully treated in-situ cervical or resolved nonmetastatic basal/squamous skin cancer; severe illness/psychological condition; pregnancy, breastfeeding, or pregnancy planning.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specific lookback intervals for “recent” exclusion criteria and the detailed contraception requirements are not supplied. These are needed for consistent eligibility determinations.

### 4.3 Recruitment

- **[Clarification Required]** Recruitment settings, countries/sites, recruitment methods, and enrollment dates or targets by site are not supplied. This information is needed to describe recruitment and assess the representativeness of enrolled participants.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **[Provided]** Early terminators and participants who do not enter the extension have approximately 12-week safety follow-up after their last injection. Week-52 end-of-study/ETV assessments and safety follow-up are conducted when required.
- **[Provided]** Patients who enter escape treatment and do not achieve EASI-50 after 8 escape weeks discontinue.
- **[Clarification Required]** Withdrawal criteria, procedures for discontinuing treatment while continuing assessments, and follow-up requirements after other forms of discontinuation are not specified. These decisions affect safety surveillance and missing outcome data.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **[Proposed]** Summarize baseline characteristics by randomized group using descriptive statistics, including age, geographic region, baseline IGA severity, and baseline disease measures available in the study. Rationale: these characteristics describe the randomized groups; assumption: the listed baseline measures are captured in the analysis dataset.
- **[Clarification Required]** The full baseline-characteristic variable set and any planned subgroup summaries are not specified. These should be defined to ensure consistent reporting.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

- **[Provided]** The primary objective covers safety and efficacy compared with placebo. For FDA, the primary efficacy endpoint is Week-16 IGA 0/1 with at least a 2-point improvement. For EMA, the Week-16 IGA endpoint and EASI-75 are co-primary.
- **[Clarification Required]** The primary safety outcome(s), safety observation period, and formal safety estimand are not specified. This matters because safety is a primary objective but no corresponding primary safety endpoint is defined.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

- **[Provided]** Secondary outcomes cover induction and maintenance efficacy, health outcomes/quality of life, rescue use, and serum lebrikizumab concentration.
- **[Provided]** Exploratory outcomes cover additional efficacy and PROs, plus descriptive acceptability/tolerability results from the modified SQAAQ in participating EU adolescents.
- **[Provided]** Outcomes listed in the FDA and EMA graphical testing families are specified in Section 2.4.
- **[Clarification Required]** The complete secondary and exploratory endpoint definitions, assessment timepoints beyond those stated for graphical families, and the specific health-outcome, quality-of-life, and serum concentration variables are not supplied. These are required to finalize endpoint-specific analyses.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **[Proposed]** For binary induction efficacy outcomes, estimate response proportions by group and the between-group difference with confidence intervals. Use the two-group continuity-corrected chi-square test as the primary comparison if consistent with the finalized analysis plan. Rationale: this matches the stated sample-size calculation; assumption: no prespecified covariate-adjusted or stratified primary test supersedes it.
- **[Proposed]** For continuous outcomes, summarize change from baseline and use a repeated-measures model if repeated measurements are available and model assumptions are considered reasonable. Rationale: this can use longitudinal measurements; assumption: the endpoint is measured repeatedly and the model is specified before unblinding.
- **[Proposed]** For maintenance binary outcomes, estimate and compare response proportions for each lebrikizumab maintenance regimen versus placebo within the rerandomized population. Rationale: this matches the stated maintenance comparisons; assumption: the final endpoint definitions and multiplicity procedure are prespecified.
- **[Clarification Required]** The specific estimands, covariates, stratification adjustments, statistical models, and endpoint-specific test procedures are not supplied. These choices affect the interpretation and reproducibility of efficacy estimates.
- **[Clarification Required]** The handling of rescue use, escape treatment, treatment discontinuation, and post-rescue observations in efficacy estimands is not specified. These intercurrent events can materially change treatment-effect estimates.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **[Provided]** Baseline pruritus/sleep diary values require at least 4 of 7 daily reports. Postbaseline weekly values are prorated when at least 1 daily report is available. Out-of-window assessments are retained unless otherwise stated.
- **[Proposed]** Prespecify endpoint-specific missing-data methods and sensitivity analyses; for binary outcomes, consider a missing-as-nonresponse analysis alongside appropriate sensitivity analyses. Rationale: missingness can affect binary response estimates; assumption: missing assessments can occur and this approach is not contradicted by the finalized estimand.
- **[Proposed]** For repeated continuous outcomes, consider a likelihood-based longitudinal analysis under a missing-at-random assumption, with sensitivity analyses if that assumption is not credible. Rationale: this uses available repeated measurements; assumption: the selected model is appropriate for the outcome and missingness process.
- **[Clarification Required]** Missing-data rules for non-diary outcomes, including outcomes after rescue, escape, or discontinuation, are not specified. This matters because missingness and intercurrent-event handling can alter efficacy conclusions.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

- **[Provided]** Supportive analyses of additional efficacy and PROs are exploratory objectives. Descriptive acceptability/tolerability results from the modified SQAAQ are planned in participating EU adolescents.
- **[Proposed]** Present descriptive summaries of rescue use and serum lebrikizumab concentration, and conduct supportive analyses only where endpoint definitions and data permit. Rationale: this addresses the stated secondary objectives without presuming an unprovided inferential model; assumption: these measures are collected as planned.
- **[Clarification Required]** The planned subgroup analyses, sensitivity analyses beyond the stated sample-size basis, and analyses of rescue use and serum concentrations are not specified. These decisions matter for controlling multiplicity and interpreting supportive results.

### 5.6 Harms

- **[Provided]** The study evaluates safety, includes intermittent safety assessments during maintenance, and provides approximately 12-week safety follow-up after the last injection for early terminators or participants not entering the extension.
- **[Proposed]** Summarize safety observations descriptively by treatment and exposure, including participant counts and event counts where collected. Rationale: descriptive summaries support the safety objective; assumption: relevant safety events and exposure data are recorded.
- **[Clarification Required]** Safety-event definitions, severity/relatedness classifications, collection windows, laboratory and other safety variables, and rules for summarizing events are not supplied. These are needed to standardize safety reporting.
