# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** Oral factor Xa (FXa) inhibitors are widely used. Acute intracranial hemorrhage (ICrH) during anticoagulation is associated with high mortality and disability, and preventing hematoma expansion is a key treatment goal.

**[Provided]** Andexanet alfa is a recombinant, catalytically inactive FXa analogue intended to reverse FXa inhibition. This study compares andexanet with usual care in this setting.

### 1.2 Objectives

**[Provided]** The primary objective is to compare andexanet with usual care for effective hemostasis at 12 hours post-randomization.

**[Provided]** The secondary objective is to compare percent change in anti-fXa activity from baseline to nadir during the first 2 hours post-randomization.

**[Provided]** Exploratory efficacy objectives are thrombin generation, neurologic deterioration, mRS, NIHSS/GCS, imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa/hemostasis correlation, EQ-5D-5L, and utility-weighted mRS.

**[Provided]** Exploratory safety objectives cover thrombotic events (TEs), mortality, invasive procedures, hospital use, adverse events (AEs), vital signs, and immunogenicity.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a Phase 4, prospective, multicenter, randomized, open-label clinical trial. The EAC and imaging core laboratory are blinded.

**[Provided]** Participants are allocated 1:1 to andexanet or usual care.

**[Provided]** Usual care is investigator-selected treatment, including no treatment, other than andexanet initiated within 3 hours.

**[Provided]** Andexanet has no crossover and is administered using one of two regimens:
- **[Provided]** Low dose: 400-mg IV bolus over about 15 minutes, followed by 480 mg at 4 mg/min for 120 minutes.
- **[Provided]** High dose: 800-mg IV bolus over up to about 30 minutes, followed by 960 mg at 8 mg/min for 120 minutes.

**[Provided]** Regimen selection uses the preceding FXa inhibitor, its last dose, and its timing. Participants qualifying through local anti-fXa >100 ng/mL with last dose >15 hours ago or of unknown time receive high dose.

**[Provided]** Andexanet should start no later than 30 minutes after randomization and preferably within 2 hours of baseline brain imaging.

**[Clarification Required]** The specific usual-care options, restrictions on co-interventions, and operational meaning of “other than andexanet initiated within 3 hours” require clarification. These decisions matter for treatment consistency and interpretation of the randomized comparison.

**[Clarification Required]** The protocol does not specify the FXa-inhibitor-specific dose-selection rules beyond the stated high-dose criterion. The complete rules are needed to ensure consistent regimen selection.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** After consent, eligibility and baseline assessments, and documentation of intended usual care, a sponsor-generated randomization scheme assigns participants to study group.

**[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by intended usual-care agent (plasma-derived coagulation-factor concentrate versus other) and symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time (<180 versus ≥180 minutes).

**[Provided]** Minimization or blocking is not specified.

**[Proposed]** Use a centralized system to implement the sponsor-generated allocation and restrict access to the allocation sequence. Rationale: this supports allocation concealment; assumption: a centralized system is operationally available.

**[Clarification Required]** The randomization block or minimization approach, allocation concealment procedures, and any site stratification require specification. These details matter for reproducibility and protection against foreknowledge of allocation.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** Planned enrollment is approximately 900 participants, 450 per group. A preplanned interim sample size re-estimation (SSR) could increase total enrollment to 1200.

**[Provided]** The sample size calculation uses the binary effective-hemostasis outcome, assuming a 70% control-arm rate and an 80% andexanet-arm rate. The target difference is 10 percentage points, representing a 33% reduction in the risk of not achieving effective hemostasis.

**[Provided]** The calculation targets approximately 90% power with an overall two-sided alpha of 0.05 accounting for the interim analysis. It assumes a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel (CMH) comparison of effective-hemostasis proportions stratified by symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time.

**[Provided]** No separate numerical inflation for dropout or non-adherence is specified; the calculation accounts for early discontinuation. The design is individually randomized, so no cluster design effect applies. A time-to-event event count and a non-inferiority or equivalence margin are not applicable.

**[Provided]** The assumed andexanet rate is based on prior efficacy results, and a 10-percentage-point difference was considered clinically meaningful. The stated rationale is to target a clinically meaningful difference while accounting for early discontinuation and interim type-I error.

**[Provided]** At interim, conditional power of 30%–90% permits a DSMB-recommended increase up to 1200 participants. No increase is made if conditional power is <30% or >90%. The software used for the sample size calculation is not specified.

**[Clarification Required]** The conditional-power calculation, its assumptions, and the precise interim information fraction are not specified. These are needed to reproduce the SSR decision and assess its operating characteristics.

**[Clarification Required]** The exact sample size procedure within the permitted range up to 1200, including how the DSMB recommendation is translated into a target, is not specified. This matters for implementing the re-estimation consistently.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** The primary hypothesis is H0: πUC−πandexanet=0 versus HA: πUC−πandexanet≠0. The primary objective succeeds when the andexanet effective-hemostasis proportion is higher under the prespecified alpha-spending thresholds.

**[Provided]** The secondary endpoint is tested only after a significant primary endpoint.

**[Proposed]** Test the primary hypothesis using a two-sided test, with the treatment contrast and confidence interval oriented to show andexanet minus usual care. Rationale: this retains the stated two-sided null while making the direction of the intended benefit explicit; assumption: the stated higher andexanet proportion is the direction of benefit.

**[Clarification Required]** The alpha-spending function, interim and final critical values, and exact decision thresholds are not supplied. These are required to determine statistical significance while controlling the stated overall two-sided alpha of 0.05.

**[Clarification Required]** The exact secondary endpoint test, multiplicity procedure beyond the primary-to-secondary gate, and confirmatory status of any exploratory comparisons are not specified. These decisions affect type-I error control and interpretation.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** A preplanned interim SSR uses conditional power: a value from 30% through 90% permits a DSMB-recommended increase up to 1200 participants; a value below 30% or above 90% does not permit an increase.

**[Provided]** If the study stops after interim, DCO1 supports confirmatory efficacy, while DCO2 captures all participants for safety and efficacy sensitivity analyses.

**[Clarification Required]** The interim analysis timing, data cutoff, personnel with access to unblinded data, and whether interim analyses include efficacy or safety stopping rules are not specified. These details matter for operational conduct and protection of the type-I error rate.

**[Clarification Required]** The procedures for separating SSR review from trial conduct and for recording the DSMB recommendation are not specified. These are needed to preserve trial integrity.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** Final analyses occur after study completion and final database lock. If the study stops after interim, DCO1 supports confirmatory efficacy and DCO2 captures all participants for safety and efficacy sensitivity analyses.

**[Provided]** Scheduled assessments are:
- **[Provided]** Anti-fXa: baseline, 1 and 2 hours.
- **[Provided]** Thrombin generation: baseline, 1 and 12 hours.
- **[Provided]** TFPI: baseline, 1, 2 and 12 hours, and Day 30.
- **[Provided]** CT/MRI: baseline and about 12 hours.
- **[Provided]** NIHSS: baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 72 hours; 12- and 24-hour assessments are blinded.
- **[Provided]** GCS: baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 72 hours.
- **[Provided]** Premorbid mRS: baseline.
- **[Provided]** mRS and EQ-5D-5L: Day 30 or early termination.
- **[Provided]** Follow-up: Day 7 and Day 14 calls and Day 30 (+7) follow-up; an additional approximately Day 120 visit for participants with positive anti-andexanet antibodies.

**[Clarification Required]** The allowed assessment windows around the scheduled time points and the exact definitions of DCO1 and DCO2 are not specified. These affect endpoint classification and the analysis data sets.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The sample-size calculation uses an overall two-sided alpha of 0.05 accounting for interim analysis, and the secondary endpoint is tested only after a significant primary endpoint.

**[Proposed]** Report treatment-effect estimates with two-sided 95% confidence intervals, using the alpha-spending-adjusted inference for the primary endpoint. Rationale: estimates and confidence intervals communicate effect size and precision alongside testing; assumption: the final alpha-spending procedure supports corresponding adjusted inference.

**[Clarification Required]** The precise confidence-interval levels and primary-endpoint inference method after alpha spending are not provided. They should be aligned with the prespecified interim and final testing boundaries.

**[Proposed]** Treat exploratory analyses as descriptive and report estimates with confidence intervals without unadjusted claims of confirmatory significance. Rationale: the exploratory objectives are numerous; assumption: no additional confirmatory multiplicity strategy is selected.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Define important protocol deviations before database lock, document their timing and impact, and review them without using outcome results to determine classification. Rationale: prespecified, auditable classification reduces outcome-dependent judgments; assumption: a deviation review process can be established.

**[Clarification Required]** The study-specific list of major deviations and the rules for excluding participants or observations from particular analyses are not supplied. These decisions matter for analysis-population membership and interpretation.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Proposed]** Use an intention-to-treat (ITT) population consisting of all randomized participants, analyzed according to assigned group, for the primary efficacy analysis. Rationale: this preserves the randomized comparison; assumption: randomization occurs only after eligibility and required baseline procedures.

**[Proposed]** Define a per-protocol population as randomized participants without prespecified major deviations that materially affect the primary endpoint, and use it for supportive analyses. Rationale: this assesses robustness to major deviations; assumption: deviation rules are finalized before outcome review.

**[Proposed]** Define the safety population as randomized participants who receive any study treatment, analyzed by treatment received. Rationale: this supports descriptive evaluation of exposure and harms; assumption: treatment exposure and any relevant subsequent treatment are reliably recorded.

**[Clarification Required]** The intended population for each efficacy and safety analysis, including handling of participants who receive no assigned treatment or receive other treatment, is not specified. This matters because the trial is open-label and usual care may include no treatment.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are:
- **[Provided]** Adults with acute intracerebral bleeding of specified volume.
- **[Provided]** CT/MRI within 2 hours before randomization.
- **[Provided]** Oral apixaban, rivaroxaban, or edoxaban at protocol-specified doses within 15 hours.
- **[Provided]** Symptom onset-to-baseline scan <6 hours.
- **[Provided]** Randomization outside 15 hours or after an unknown last-dose time requires standard-of-care local anti-fXa >100 ng/mL within 2 hours before consent and mandates high-dose andexanet.
- **[Provided]** Consent may be provided by the participant, LAR/proxy, or emergency consent where lawful.

**[Derived]** The hematoma volume must be >0.5 mL and <60 mL to avoid the stated exclusion thresholds of ≤0.5 mL and ≥60 mL, subject to all other eligibility criteria.

**[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are:
- **[Provided]** Planned hematoma-affecting surgery within 12 hours.
- **[Provided]** GCS<7, with a specified non-neurologic sedation exception.
- **[Provided]** NIHSS>35.
- **[Provided]** Hematoma volume ≤0.5 or ≥60 mL.
- **[Provided]** Inability to use the same CT/MRI modality.
- **[Provided]** Expected survival <1 month.
- **[Provided]** Recent thrombotic/cardiovascular events.
- **[Provided]** Cardiogenic shock.
- **[Provided]** Severe sepsis/septic shock.
- **[Provided]** Pregnancy/lactation, with required pregnancy testing and contraception precautions.
- **[Provided]** Restricted anticoagulants/blood products.
- **[Provided]** Prior/planned andexanet.
- **[Provided]** Investigational drug.
- **[Provided]** Tumor-related bleeding.
- **[Provided]** Hypersensitivity.

**[Clarification Required]** The exact bleeding types and volume measurement method, FXa-inhibitor dose list, definitions of recent thrombotic/cardiovascular events, restricted anticoagulants or blood products, hypersensitivity scope, and details of the sedation exception are not supplied. These are needed to apply eligibility consistently.

**[Clarification Required]** The specific pregnancy testing and contraception requirements and the permitted circumstances for emergency consent are not detailed. These decisions matter for participant protection and consistent enrollment.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is effective hemostasis at 12 hours post-randomization, comparing andexanet with usual care.

**[Provided]** Effective hemostasis is the binary outcome used for sample-size planning, with assumed rates of 70% for usual care and 80% for andexanet.

**[Clarification Required]** The operational definition of effective hemostasis, its component criteria, assessment window, handling of unevaluable imaging or clinical data, and adjudication process are not supplied. These decisions are essential to determine the primary outcome consistently.

**[Proposed]** Estimate the effective-hemostasis proportion in each randomized group and the between-group difference, with confidence intervals and the prespecified alpha-spending-adjusted test. Rationale: this directly addresses the primary comparison and reflects the binary endpoint; assumption: the outcome is ascertained for a sufficiently defined analysis set.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The secondary outcome is percent change in anti-fXa activity from baseline to nadir during the first 2 hours post-randomization. Anti-fXa is assessed at baseline, 1 hour, and 2 hours.

**[Proposed]** Summarize the anti-fXa outcome by group and compare groups using an appropriate two-group method for the observed distribution, reporting an effect estimate and confidence interval. Rationale: this addresses the randomized comparison while allowing for skewed laboratory data; assumption: the analysis method is selected after review of the prespecified measurement scale, without using treatment-effect results.

**[Clarification Required]** The anti-fXa assay, definition of nadir when one or both post-randomization measurements are missing, percent-change formula, and treatment of zero or below-quantification baseline values are not specified. These decisions affect the secondary outcome’s calculation and analysis.

**[Provided]** Exploratory efficacy outcomes are thrombin generation, neurologic deterioration, mRS, NIHSS/GCS, imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa/hemostasis correlation, EQ-5D-5L, and utility-weighted mRS.

**[Provided]** Thrombin generation is assessed at baseline, 1 hour, and 12 hours; NIHSS and GCS are assessed at baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24, and 72 hours; CT/MRI is assessed at baseline and about 12 hours. NIHSS assessments at 12 and 24 hours are blinded. mRS and EQ-5D-5L are assessed at Day 30 or early termination, and premorbid mRS is assessed at baseline.

**[Clarification Required]** Definitions, time points, scoring rules, and analysis windows for neurologic deterioration, imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa/hemostasis correlation, utility-weighted mRS, and the remaining exploratory outcomes are not provided. ### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Provided]** The sample-size calculation specifies a CMH comparison of effective-hemostasis proportions stratified by symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time. Randomization is stratified by intended usual-care agent and symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time.

**[Proposed]** Use the specified CMH approach for the primary outcome, stratified by symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time, and report the stratum-adjusted comparison. Rationale: this matches the stated sample-size test and preserves the stated analysis stratification; assumption: both strata are available and adequately populated.

**[Clarification Required]** Whether the primary analysis should additionally account for intended usual-care agent, which is a randomization stratification factor but not listed for the sample-size CMH test, is not specified. This matters for consistency between randomization and analysis.

**[Proposed]** Present unadjusted group-specific binary outcome proportions and a treatment-effect estimate alongside the primary stratified test. Rationale: these provide interpretable absolute outcome rates and effect size; assumption: the estimand and confidence-interval method are specified before analysis.

**[Proposed]** For repeated laboratory and clinical measurements, summarize observed values and changes from baseline by time point and group; use a repeated-measures model as a supportive analysis if its assumptions are reasonable. Rationale: this uses the scheduled longitudinal observations while allowing incomplete repeated measurements; assumption: the model structure, covariance assumptions, and analysis time points are prespecified.

**[Proposed]** For mRS, consider an ordinal analysis; for NIHSS and GCS, summarize the scheduled scores and changes over time; and for EQ-5D-5L and utility-weighted mRS, analyze the defined scores at the specified follow-up. Rationale: this respects the distinct scales and repeated assessments; assumptions: scoring and endpoint definitions are finalized and the chosen model is appropriate for each scale.

**[Clarification Required]** The specific models, covariates, estimands, score derivations, and treatment of repeated measures for secondary and exploratory outcomes are not specified. These choices affect interpretation and comparability of results.
