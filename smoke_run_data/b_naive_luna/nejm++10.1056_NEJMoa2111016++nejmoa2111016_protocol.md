# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

- **[Provided]** This is a phase 3b trial comparing edoxaban with a vitamin K antagonist (VKA) in people with atrial fibrillation (AF) who require chronic oral anticoagulation after transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI).
- **[Provided]** The final statistical analysis plan (SAP) v3.0 applies where it adds to or differs from the final protocol.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1 primary objective:** Compare edoxaban-based with VKA-based regimens for net adverse clinical events (NACE), defined as all-cause death, myocardial infarction (MI), ischemic stroke, systemic thromboembolism (SEE), valve thrombosis, or International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) major bleeding.
- **[Provided]** **P2 primary objective:** Compare the regimens for ISTH major bleeding.
- **[Provided]** **S1 secondary objective:** Compare alternative NACE definitions (TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO), MACE, MACCE, cardiovascular mortality, stroke subtypes, SEE, MI, valve thrombosis, and repeat target-lesion revascularization.
- **[Provided]** **S2 secondary objective:** Compare ISTH, TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO bleeding endpoints; intracranial, life-threatening, and fatal bleeding; mortality; adverse events (AEs); laboratory parameters; electrocardiograms (ECGs); and vital signs.
- **[Provided]** **S3 exploratory objective:** Compare cardiovascular hospital admissions of at least 24 hours, length of stay, intensive care unit (ICU) days, PACT-Q treatment satisfaction, EQ-5D-5L quality of life, and optional biomarkers.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **[Provided]** The study is a multinational, multicentre, prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded endpoint evaluation (PROBE), parallel-group phase 3b trial. An independent Clinical Events Committee (CEC) adjudicates events, and an independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) monitors safety.
- **[Provided]** Participants are allocated 1:1 to an edoxaban-based or VKA-based regimen. Randomized treatment starts within 24 hours of randomization and continues for up to 36 months, with a minimum treatment duration of 12 months except that the last-enrolled participants have a minimum of 6 months, followed by 30 days of observational follow-up.
- **[Provided]** The edoxaban-based regimen is edoxaban 60 mg once daily, or 30 mg once daily when dose-reduction criteria in the locally approved label are met.
- **[Provided]** The VKA-based regimen is a locally approved VKA, with investigator dose adjustment to an INR of 2.0–3.0; in Japan, the target is 1.6–2.6 for age ≥70 years and 2.0–3.0 for age <70 years.
- **[Provided]** Antiplatelet co-therapy is predeclared at randomization. No antiplatelet therapy (APT) or single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) is used up to 90 days without another indication; SAPT is used indefinitely after PCI, with dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) only up to 3 months after each stent. Preferred agents are ASA 75–100 mg/day or clopidogrel 75 mg/day.
- **[Clarification Required]** The operational definition of the 90-day antiplatelet period and how co-therapy is handled when another indication exists are not specified; these decisions affect treatment implementation and interpretation of outcomes.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **[Provided]** An IXRS assigns each participant a unique identifier and treatment. An independent biostatistician generates the schedule under IXRS-vendor oversight. Crossover is not permitted.
- **[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by TAVI procedure with stenting (yes/no) and need for edoxaban dose adjustment under the local label (yes/no). The schedule contains a randomization block number.
- **[Clarification Required]** Block size and whether minimization is used are not specified; these details are needed to document the allocation procedure and reproduce it.
- **[Proposed]** Preserve the treatment assignment as generated by IXRS in the analysis database and document any assignment errors before database lock. **Rationale:** This supports an auditable intention-to-treat analysis. **Assumption:** IXRS assignment records are available.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** Approximately 1400 participants are planned for randomization in an event-driven design. The outcome used for calculation is time to first NACE event: all-cause death, MI, ischemic stroke, SEE, valve thrombosis, or ISTH major bleeding.
- **[Provided]** The assumed annual VKA-arm NACE rate is 14%, and the assumed observed treatment hazard ratio (HR) is 0.95. The noninferiority margin is HR 1.38.
- **[Provided]** The calculation assumes alpha 0.05, 80% power for NACE noninferiority, anticipated median follow-up of two years, and at least 320 NACE events across both arms. Noninferiority is assessed using the upper bound of a two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) for the HR against 1.38.
- **[Provided]** The variability assumption and numerical dropout/non-adherence inflation are not specified. Sample size or follow-up duration may be adjusted for actual versus expected endpoint and dropout rates.
- **[Provided]** The design effect from clustering is not applicable to this individually randomized parallel-group trial.
- **[Clarification Required]** The procedure and decision criteria for any sample-size or follow-up adjustment are not specified; these are needed to ensure that any adjustment is operationally defined and does not compromise the planned inference.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

- **[Provided]** Four confirmatory hypotheses are tested sequentially: (1) NACE noninferiority, (2) ISTH major-bleeding noninferiority, (3) ISTH major-bleeding superiority, and (4) NACE superiority.
- **[Provided]** For steps 1 and 2, noninferiority is met when the upper bound of the two-sided 95% CI for the HR is <1.38. For steps 3 and 4, superiority is met when that upper bound is <1.00. Failure at any step stops the sequence.
- **[Proposed]** Define the treatment HR as edoxaban-based regimen divided by VKA-based regimen in the analysis specification. **Rationale:** A fixed direction is needed to interpret the stated upper-bound decision rules. **Assumption:** This direction is consistent with the intended comparison.
- **[Clarification Required]** Confirm whether the stated HR direction is edoxaban versus VKA; the direction must be unambiguous for applying the sequential decision rules.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **[Provided]** An independent DSMB monitors safety.
- **[Provided]** No formal interim efficacy analysis or interim decision boundary is specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** Confirm whether any interim efficacy, futility, or formal safety analyses are planned, including timing, access to unblinded data, and stopping boundaries; interim testing can affect interpretation and type I error control.
- **[Proposed]** Unless a formal interim plan is confirmed, conduct no interim efficacy hypothesis tests and retain DSMB safety monitoring under its independent procedures. **Rationale:** This avoids introducing unplanned repeated testing. **Assumption:** The confirmatory sequence is intended to be evaluated at final analysis only.
- **[Clarification Required]** The DSMB review schedule, charter, and any safety stopping rules are not specified; these are important for safety oversight and documentation.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **[Provided]** The main endpoint analysis is intention-to-treat (ITT) over the overall study period, from randomization to Month 36/end of treatment (EOT), last visit, or death, whichever occurs first. For non-ITT sets, the stated time origin is first dose. Incomplete follow-up is censored at last known status.
- **[Provided]** Assessments occur at baseline/randomization; Months 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30; and EOT/Month 36 (±14 days). Post-treatment follow-up is at one month (±7 days), followed by 6-monthly follow-up contacts.
- **[Provided]** PACT-Q and EQ-5D-5L assessments occur at baseline, Month 3, and Month 12 or EOT, whichever comes first.
- **[Clarification Required]** Data cutoff, database lock, and data-readiness criteria are not specified; these are needed to establish the final analysis date and ensure analysis data are complete and adjudicated.
- **[Clarification Required]** The handling of death as a censoring event for outcomes that do not include death is not specified; death may preclude observation of such outcomes and the approach affects their estimates.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **[Provided]** The sample-size calculation uses alpha 0.05, and confirmatory decisions use the upper bound of a two-sided 95% CI according to the sequential rules in Section 2.4.
- **[Proposed]** Report treatment-effect estimates with two-sided 95% CIs for outcomes where an estimate and interval are meaningful. **Rationale:** Estimates and precision support interpretation beyond threshold testing. **Assumption:** No alternative interval level is specified for nonconfirmatory outcomes.
- **[Proposed]** Treat only the four hypotheses in the specified sequence as confirmatory and interpret other analyses as secondary or exploratory. **Rationale:** This respects the supplied gatekeeping procedure without asserting additional multiplicity control. **Assumption:** No other confirmatory testing hierarchy is intended.
- **[Clarification Required]** No multiplicity procedure is specified for secondary or exploratory outcomes; this matters when interpreting nominal p-values across multiple comparisons.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **[Provided]** Treatment crossover is not permitted.
- **[Proposed]** Define major and minor protocol deviations before treatment-code access, and retain randomized participants in the ITT analysis regardless of deviations. **Rationale:** Prespecified deviation handling supports an unbiased primary comparison and a reproducible per-protocol sensitivity analysis. **Assumption:** Deviation documentation is available before database lock.
- **[Clarification Required]** Criteria for major deviations, their review process, and their impact on non-ITT analyses are not specified; these decisions affect analysis-population membership.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **[Provided]** The main endpoint analysis is ITT. The timing specification also refers to non-ITT sets, with time origin at first dose.
- **[Proposed]** Define the ITT population as all randomized participants analyzed according to randomized assignment. **Rationale:** This operationalizes the stated ITT analysis. **Assumption:** Randomized assignment and outcome follow-up can be retained for all randomized participants.
- **[Proposed]** Define a safety population as randomized participants who receive at least one dose, analyzed according to treatment received. **Rationale:** Exposure-based summaries are appropriate for safety. **Assumption:** Administration records identify treatment received and exposure.
- **[Proposed]** Define a per-protocol population using prespecified criteria for adherence and major deviations, and use it as a sensitivity population for noninferiority. **Rationale:** Noninferiority conclusions can be sensitive to deviations from assigned treatment. **Assumption:** Per-protocol criteria can be determined without post hoc selection.
- **[Clarification Required]** Names, membership rules, and endpoint coverage for the non-ITT analysis sets are not specified; they must be finalized, particularly because their time origin is first dose.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

- **[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are adults with successful TAVI, defined by correct single-valve position, gradient <20 mmHg, peak velocity <3.0 m/s, regurgitation ≤2, and no overt stroke or uncontrolled bleeding; and pre-existing or new AF (>30 seconds on ECG) requiring chronic oral anticoagulation (OAC).
- **[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are high bleeding risk; contraindication to either treatment; prolonged DAPT requirement; recent stroke, MI, or major surgery; mechanical valve; severe renal or hepatic disease; instability; active malignancy; specified laboratory abnormalities; pregnancy; investigator-assessed inability to comply; prior randomization; substance dependence; and life expectancy <6 months.
- **[Clarification Required]** Operational definitions and assessment windows for high bleeding risk, “recent,” severe disease, instability, specified laboratory abnormalities, and other broadly stated exclusions are not supplied; they are necessary for consistent eligibility decisions.

### 4.3 Recruitment

- **[Provided]** The trial is multinational and multicentre, with approximately 1400 participants planned for randomization.

- **[Proposed]** Report recruitment and randomization counts over time and, where recorded, by site and country. **Rationale:** This describes recruitment patterns across a multicentre trial. **Assumption:** Site, country, and date data are available.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **[Provided]** Treatment lasts up to 36 months, subject to the stated minimum durations, and is followed by 30 days of observational follow-up; follow-up contacts then occur every 6 months.
- **[Clarification Required]** Procedures for treatment discontinuation, withdrawal of consent, loss to follow-up, and continued outcome collection after treatment cessation are not specified; these decisions affect endpoint ascertainment and censoring.
- **[Proposed]** Where consent permits, continue outcome follow-up after study-treatment discontinuation and distinguish treatment discontinuation from withdrawal of study follow-up. **Rationale:** Continued follow-up supports the ITT comparison. **Assumption:** Consent and applicable study procedures permit continued contact.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

- **[Provided]** The P1 primary outcome is time to first NACE event: all-cause death, MI, ischemic stroke, SEE, valve thrombosis, or ISTH major bleeding.
- **[Provided]** The P2 primary outcome is ISTH major bleeding.
- **[Provided]** NACE noninferiority is assessed before ISTH major-bleeding noninferiority; the latter is followed by ISTH major-bleeding superiority and then NACE superiority, subject to the stopping rule in Section 2.4.
- **[Clarification Required]** The operational endpoint definitions, event-date rules, and treatment of recurrent events are not provided for all primary outcomes; these are needed for consistent adjudication and analysis.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

- **[Provided]** S1 outcomes are alternative NACE definitions using TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO criteria; MACE; MACCE; cardiovascular mortality; stroke subtypes; SEE; MI; valve thrombosis; and repeat target-lesion revascularization.
- **[Provided]** S2 outcomes are ISTH, TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO bleeding endpoints; intracranial, life-threatening, and fatal bleeding; mortality; AEs; laboratory parameters; ECGs; and vital signs.
- **[Provided]** S3 outcomes are cardiovascular hospital admissions of at least 24 hours, length of stay, ICU days, PACT-Q treatment satisfaction, EQ-5D-5L quality of life, and optional biomarkers.
- **[Clarification Required]** Definitions, assessment windows, and analysis scales for the secondary and exploratory outcomes are not specified; these are needed to determine event status and select suitable analyses.
- **[Clarification Required]** Biomarker types and which participants will provide optional biomarker measurements are not specified; this affects the analysis population and interpretation of biomarker comparisons.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **[Proposed]** For P1 and P2, estimate the treatment HR and two-sided 95% CI using a Cox proportional hazards model stratified by the two randomization factors. **Rationale:** This estimates the specified time-to-first treatment effect while accounting for the stated stratification. **Assumption:** The HR is the intended effect measure and proportional hazards is a reasonable primary model assumption.
- **[Proposed]** Apply the prespecified sequential noninferiority and superiority criteria to the relevant HR confidence bounds; report the HR, CI, and decision at each step reached. **Rationale:** This implements the stated confirmatory sequence. **Assumption:** The HR direction is confirmed as specified in Section 2.4.
- **[Proposed]** Describe time-to-event outcomes using event counts, follow-up, and Kaplan–Meier estimates, and assess the proportional-hazards assumption for Cox analyses. **Rationale:** These summaries help contextualize HR estimates and evaluate the model assumption. **Assumption:** Event dates and censoring dates are available.
- **[Proposed]** For component and other time-to-event outcomes, use prespecified time-to-first definitions and report effect estimates with CIs; for continuous outcomes, summarize change from baseline at scheduled assessments. **Rationale:** These approaches align the analysis with event and measurement types. **Assumption:** Endpoint definitions, measurement scales, and assessment times are finalized before analysis.
- **[Clarification Required]** The primary model’s handling of ties, missing covariates, and any covariate adjustment beyond the randomization strata is not specified; these choices affect reproducibility and potentially the estimates.
- **[Clarification Required]** The analysis approach for outcomes subject to competing events, particularly death for nonfatal outcomes, is not specified; this can affect estimates and their interpretation.
- **[Proposed]** For continuous repeated-measures outcomes, consider a repeated-measures model using the scheduled assessments and treatment-by-time effects, with an appropriate covariance structure selected in advance. **Rationale:** This can use available repeated measurements while accounting for within-participant correlation. **Assumption:** The outcome is measured on a scale suitable for such a model and missingness assumptions are addressed.
- **[Clarification Required]** The scoring rules and direction of benefit for PACT-Q and EQ-5D-5L, and the intended summary time point or contrast, are not specified; these decisions are necessary to interpret treatment comparisons.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **[Provided]** Incomplete follow-up is censored at last known status.
- **[Proposed]** Do not impute unobserved time-to-event outcomes in the primary analysis; apply the stated censoring rule and describe numbers and reasons for censoring by group. **Rationale:** This follows the specified approach and makes loss of observation visible. **Assumption:** Last known status can be determined reliably.
- **[Proposed]** For missing non-time-to-event measurements, report the amount and pattern of missingness and use a prespecified analysis appropriate to the outcome; consider sensitivity analyses under alternative missing-data assumptions. **Rationale:** Missing-data assumptions can influence non-event outcome comparisons. **Assumption:** Missingness reasons and relevant observed data are available.
- **[Clarification Required]** The missing-data strategy for repeated measures, incomplete endpoint assessments, and withdrawal-related missingness is not specified; this matters because different assumptions can change estimates and conclusions.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

- **[Proposed]** Conduct sensitivity analyses for the primary outcomes using the per-protocol population and alternative reasonable censoring or model specifications defined before database lock. **Rationale:** Sensitivity analyses assess whether conclusions depend on analysis-population or modeling choices. **Assumption:** The relevant definitions and alternatives are prespecified and data support them.
- **[Proposed]** Present secondary and exploratory analyses as supportive or descriptive unless a multiplicity strategy is specified. **Rationale:** This avoids overstating findings from multiple comparisons. **Assumption:** No additional confirmatory hierarchy applies.
- **[Clarification Required]** Planned subgroup analyses and their interaction tests are not specified; prespecification is needed to distinguish planned effect-modification analyses from post hoc exploration.

### 5.6 Harms

- **[Provided]** An independent DSMB monitors safety. Safety-related outcomes include AEs, laboratory parameters, ECGs, vital signs, bleeding endpoints, mortality, and intracranial, life-threatening, and fatal bleeding.
- **[Proposed]** Summarize harms by treatment received in the safety population, including participant counts and proportions with each reported event and descriptive summaries of laboratory, ECG, and vital-sign measures. **Rationale:** Exposure-based summaries support safety interpretation. **Assumption:** Treatment exposure and harm data are available.
