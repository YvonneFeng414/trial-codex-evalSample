# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** This international phase 3 study compares lebrikizumab monotherapy with placebo in adolescents and adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD).

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **Primary objective:** Evaluate the safety and efficacy of lebrikizumab compared with placebo. For FDA, the primary endpoint is Week-16 Investigator’s Global Assessment (IGA) 0/1 with ≥2-point improvement from baseline. For EMA, this IGA endpoint and Week-16 EASI-75 are co-primary endpoints.
- **[Provided]** **Secondary objective:** Evaluate induction and maintenance efficacy, health outcomes/quality of life, rescue use, and serum lebrikizumab concentration.
- **[Provided]** **Exploratory objective:** Provide supportive analyses of additional efficacy and patient-reported outcomes, and descriptive acceptability/tolerability results from the modified SQAAQ in participating EU adolescents.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** The study is randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, and parallel-group. It comprises screening, 16-week double-blind induction, 36-week maintenance, escape treatment where applicable, and approximately 12-week safety follow-up after the last injection for early terminators or participants not entering the extension.

**[Provided]** All injections are subcutaneous. Treatment groups and regimens are:

- **[Provided]** **Induction lebrikizumab, LEB250Q2W:** 500 mg at baseline and Week 2, described as two PFS-NSD loading doses, followed by 250 mg every 2 weeks through Week 14.
- **[Provided]** **Induction placebo:** 4 mL at baseline and Week 2, followed by 2 mL every 2 weeks through Week 14.
- **[Provided]** **Maintenance responders:** Lebrikizumab 250 mg every 2 weeks, lebrikizumab 250 mg every 4 weeks, or placebo every 2 weeks. Placebo injections maintain blinding.
- **[Provided]** **Escape treatment:** Week-16 nonresponders, induction rescue users, or rerandomized participants losing EASI-50 at Weeks 24, 32, 40, or 48 receive open-label lebrikizumab 250 mg every 2 weeks to Week 52. Participants without EASI-50 after 8 escape weeks discontinue.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the exact Week-16 responder algorithm, including whether IGA and EASI response criteria are joined by “and” or “or,” how missing assessments are handled, and how induction rescue use affects eligibility. These decisions determine maintenance entry and the population to which maintenance results apply.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the treatment pathway for induction-placebo responders and whether they enter the same rerandomization process. This is necessary to distinguish all maintenance participants from the lebrikizumab-responder population used for inferential comparisons.

**[Clarification Required]** Finalize maintenance dosing dates, matching-placebo schedules, and the last maintenance injection date. These details are needed to implement every-2-week and every-4-week dosing without compromising blinding.

**[Clarification Required]** Define rescue treatment, permitted concomitant AD treatments, restrictions on background treatment, and the timing of escape entry for induction rescue users. These decisions affect clinical management, treatment-failure classification, and efficacy estimands.

**[Clarification Required]** Resolve how the 8-week escape-response rule applies to escape beginning at Week 48, because its assessment would fall at Week 56, beyond treatment through Week 52. Also specify whether “discontinue” means treatment discontinuation, study withdrawal, or both.

**[Clarification Required]** Define extension eligibility, extension-entry procedures, and the transition from this study. These decisions affect end-of-study assessments and safety-follow-up requirements.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** Baseline allocation occurs through electronic data capture (EDC) after eligibility is established, using a 2:1 ratio of LEB250Q2W to placebo. Protocol Amendment 1 replaced earlier interactive web response system wording with EDC.

**[Provided]** Eligible Week-16 responders are rerandomized through EDC in a 2:2:1 ratio to LEB250Q2W, LEB250Q4W, or placebo, according to Week-16 IGA/EASI response and induction rescue use.

**[Provided]** Stratification factors are geographic region—US, EU, or rest of world—age—12 to <18 versus ≥18 years—and baseline disease severity—IGA 3 versus 4.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify sequence generation, blocking or minimization, allocation-concealment implementation, and the application of stratification at each randomization stage. These details are necessary for reproducibility and appropriate adjustment in analysis.

**[Proposed]** Restrict access to allocation lists and unblinded treatment information to authorized personnel, with documented emergency-unblinding procedures. Rationale: protect allocation concealment and blinded assessment. Assumption: EDC and treatment-supply systems can support role-based access.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** Approximately 400 participants are planned, randomized 2:1 to lebrikizumab versus placebo. The initial calculation of 96 lebrikizumab and 48 placebo participants was increased to approximately 400 to collect safety information and ensure sufficient Week-16 responders for maintenance.

**[Provided]** For the FDA calculation, assumed Week-16 IGA 0/1 response rates are 34.7% for LEB250Q2W and 7.7% for placebo. A sample of 96 versus 48 provides >95% power using a two-group continuity-corrected chi-square test at two-sided α = 0.05.

**[Provided]** For EMA, the same 96:48 allocation provides >95% power at two-sided α = 0.05 for each co-primary endpoint. Assumed EASI-75 rates are 48.0% versus 11.5%. Overall power for both co-primary endpoints is ≥90%.

**[Provided]** The stated treatment differences are 27.0 percentage points for IGA 0/1 and 36.5 percentage points for EASI-75. The assumptions are based on phase 2b rescue-medication nonresponse sensitivity results.

**[Provided]** The calculation concerns fixed-time binary response proportions with binomial variability; no separate variance parameter is specified. An event-accrual target is not applicable.

**[Provided]** This is an individually randomized, non-clustered superiority study. A clustering design effect and non-inferiority/equivalence margin are not applicable.

**[Clarification Required]** No numerical dropout/non-adherence inflation, sample-size re-estimation plan, or calculation software is specified. Confirm these elements to make the sample-size justification reproducible and distinguish the safety-driven increase from formal attrition inflation.

**[Clarification Required]** Expected maintenance responder numbers and maintenance-specific power assumptions are not supplied. These are needed to assess precision and power for the two maintenance comparisons without implying that induction power applies to maintenance.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** The induction superiority null hypothesis is equality of LEB250Q2W and placebo response proportions for Week-16 IGA 0/1 with ≥2-point improvement. EMA additionally tests equality for Week-16 EASI-75.

**[Derived]** Writing the corresponding response probabilities as \(p_L\) and \(p_P\), each endpoint-specific null is \(H_0:p_L=p_P\). A higher response probability with lebrikizumab is the direction of clinical benefit.

**[Provided]** FDA’s graphical family contains IGA01 at Week 16 followed by EASI-75 at Week 16, EASI-90 at Week 16, Pruritus NRS-4 at Weeks 16/4/2, IGA01 at Week 4, adult IGA01 at Week 16, and sleep-loss response at Week 16.

**[Provided]** EMA’s induction graphical family starts with co-primary IGA01 and EASI-75 at Week 16 and includes:

- **[Provided]** EASI-90 at Weeks 16 and 4.
- **[Provided]** EASI percent change at Week 16.
- **[Provided]** Pruritus percent change and response at Weeks 16, 4, and 2.
- **[Provided]** DLQI response and change at Week 16.
- **[Provided]** Sleep-loss response and change at Week 16.

**[Provided]** For EMA maintenance, each of LEB250Q2W and LEB250Q4W follows this prespecified ordered sequence:

1. **[Provided]** Persistence of EASI-75.
2. **[Provided]** Persistence of IGA response.
3. **[Provided]** Persistence of ≥4-point pruritus response.
4. **[Provided]** Percent EASI change at Week 52.

**[Provided]** Maintenance inferential comparisons are lebrikizumab-responder Q2W versus placebo and lebrikizumab-responder Q4W versus placebo.

**[Clarification Required]** Supply the complete FDA and EMA multiplicity graphs, including initial alpha weights, transition weights, endpoint-node definitions, and recycling rules. Listing family members does not determine an executable graphical testing procedure.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify alpha allocation between maintenance dose branches, any linkage between induction and maintenance families, and any FDA maintenance confirmatory framework. These decisions determine which findings can support multiplicity-controlled claims.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Clarification Required]** No statistical interim-analysis schedule, efficacy/futility boundary, alpha-spending rule, or independent monitoring arrangement is specified. Confirm whether any such procedures are intended, because unblinded repeated testing can affect error control and study conduct.

**[Proposed]** Do not introduce unblinded efficacy-driven early stopping or sample-size adaptation without a prospectively approved amendment and revised operating characteristics. Rationale: prevent unplanned inflation of false-positive risk. Assumption: no additional approved interim procedure exists.

**[Clarification Required]** Classify any Week-16 and Week-52 analysis cuts as stage-specific analyses, interim analyses, or final analyses for their respective hypotheses. This classification is needed to determine whether repeated testing of the same hypothesis requires adjustment.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** Final database lock follows safety follow-up, early discontinuation, or extension enrollment. It may be combined with the Week-52 database lock depending on the regulatory timeline.

**[Provided]** Induction assessments occur at baseline and Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16, with ±3-day windows.

**[Provided]** Maintenance assessments occur at Weeks 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48, and 52, with ±5-day windows.

**[Provided]** IGA, EASI, and body surface area (BSA) assessments are scheduled through Week 16. IGA/EASI/BSA/SCORAD and intermittent patient-reported outcome and safety assessments continue in maintenance. Week-52 end-of-study/early-termination-visit assessments and a 12-week safety follow-up are conducted when required.

**[Provided]** Daily pruritus NRS and sleep-loss diaries are mapped to baseline and Weeks 1, 2, and 4, then to scheduled visits through Week 52. Baseline requires ≥4 of 7 daily reports; postbaseline weekly values are prorated when ≥1 report is available.

**[Provided]** Out-of-window assessments are retained unless stated otherwise.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify database-cut dates, readiness criteria, the complete assessment schedule, and rules for assigning unscheduled, duplicate, early-termination, and out-of-window measurements to analysis visits. These rules affect endpoint availability and must not silently exclude assessments that are to be retained.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the diary reference periods and exact proration calculation. The stated minimum report counts alone do not fully determine reproducible weekly values.

**[Proposed]** Finalize and approve the statistical analysis plan and endpoint derivations before treatment-unblinded access for the applicable analysis. Rationale: limit outcome-informed analytical choices. Assumption: analysis datasets can be prepared and reviewed while treatment identities remain masked.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The sample-size calculations use two-sided α = 0.05 for the FDA primary endpoint and each EMA co-primary endpoint.

**[Proposed]** Use two-sided tests and 95% confidence intervals for primary treatment contrasts, with confirmatory significance governed by the finalized regulator-specific multiplicity procedure. Rationale: align estimation with the stated two-sided planning level while distinguishing nominal from multiplicity-controlled inference. Assumption: no alternative inferential level is approved.

**[Proposed]** Require both EMA co-primary endpoints to succeed under the finalized testing procedure before declaring EMA induction primary efficacy success. Rationale: retain the joint requirement implied by co-primary efficacy endpoints. Assumption: the co-primary claim requires both outcomes rather than either outcome.

**[Proposed]** Report nominal estimates and confidence intervals for all prespecified endpoints, but identify results outside an available multiplicity-controlled testing path as descriptive. Rationale: provide complete estimation without overstating confirmatory evidence. Assumption: the final multiplicity graph determines claim eligibility.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify whether confidence intervals used for confirmatory claims must be multiplicity-adjusted or whether nominal intervals will accompany adjusted testing decisions. This distinction affects interpretation of intervals that exclude the null.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Classify deviations before unblinding using prespecified categories, including eligibility violations, dosing errors, prohibited treatment, missed assessments, and deviations affecting blinding. Rationale: ensure consistent handling without outcome-driven decisions. Assumption: source records permit classification and dating.

**[Proposed]** Retain participants with deviations in randomized efficacy analyses; use exclusions only in a separately defined supportive per-protocol analysis. Rationale: preserve the randomized comparison. Assumption: consent permits use of the relevant data.

**[Clarification Required]** Finalize the major-deviation criteria and any per-protocol exclusion rules. Their absence prevents reproducible definition of the supportive population.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **[Proposed]** **Induction randomized population:** All initially randomized participants, analyzed according to baseline assignment, irrespective of treatment received or adherence. Rationale: preserve randomization for induction efficacy. Assumption: randomized participants can be identified reliably.
- **[Proposed]** **Maintenance randomized efficacy population:** Induction lebrikizumab responders eligible for, and receiving, rerandomization, analyzed according to maintenance assignment. Rationale: align maintenance inference with the specified lebrikizumab-responder comparisons. Assumption: the eligibility and rerandomization algorithms are finalized.
- **[Proposed]** **Safety population:** Participants receiving at least one injection, classified by actual exposure within induction, maintenance, and escape periods. Rationale: relate harms to treatment received. Assumption: administration records distinguish actual treatment and timing.
- **[Proposed]** **Concentration population:** Lebrikizumab-exposed participants with at least one interpretable serum concentration result, with exclusions documented. Rationale: avoid summarizing uninterpretable measurements. Assumption: assay-validity rules and sampling records are available.
- **[Proposed]** **Modified SQAAQ population:** Participating EU adolescents with available questionnaire data, summarized with eligible, participating, and evaluable denominators. Rationale: distinguish participation from questionnaire completeness. Assumption: these denominators are recorded.

**[Clarification Required]** Define analysis treatment for allocation errors, accidental treatment crossover, and any induction-placebo responders entering maintenance. These situations can otherwise produce inconsistent efficacy and safety group assignments.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Maintain a screening log containing consent status, eligibility outcome, principal exclusion reason, and whether randomization occurred, using privacy-protective identifiers. Rationale: support transparent participant accounting. Assumption: collection is permitted by consent and local requirements.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify screening duration, permitted rescreening, repeated eligibility testing, and handling of duplicate screening records. These decisions affect eligibility consistency and recruitment denominators.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** No prospective enrollment waiver is permitted.

**[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** Adult participants or adolescents aged 12 to <18 years weighing ≥40 kg.
- **[Provided]** AD diagnosed by American Academy of Dermatology criteria for ≥1 year.
- **[Provided]** Baseline EASI ≥16, IGA ≥3/4, and AD BSA ≥10%.
- **[Provided]** Inadequate topical response or medical inadvisability of topical treatment.
- **[Provided]** Stable nonmedicated moisturizer twice daily for ≥7 days.
- **[Provided]** ≥4 of 7 baseline pruritus/sleep diary entries.
- **[Provided]** Ability to comply.
- **[Provided]** Specified highly effective contraception or abstinence for women of childbearing potential during treatment plus 18 weeks, and male barrier contraception for 18 weeks.
- **[Provided]** Consent/assent.

**[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** Prior lebrikizumab.
- **[Provided]** Anaphylaxis.
- **[Provided]** Recent topical AD treatment, dupilumab/tralokinumab, systemic immunomodulator/phototherapy, investigational product/biologic, prescription moisturizer, tanning, or live vaccine.
- **[Provided]** Uncontrolled chronic disease or severe asthma.
- **[Provided]** Recent/active infection, hepatitis/cirrhosis, parasitic infection risk, immunosuppression, or HIV.
- **[Provided]** Significant screening laboratory findings.
- **[Provided]** Skin disease that interferes with assessment.
- **[Provided]** Malignancy within 5 years, except fully treated in-situ cervical cancer or resolved nonmetastatic basal/squamous skin cancer.
- **[Provided]** Severe illness or psychological condition.
- **[Provided]** Pregnancy, breastfeeding, or pregnancy planning.

**[Clarification Required]** Operationalize the IGA eligibility notation “≥3/4” and confirm whether the ≥40 kg threshold applies only to adolescents or to all participants. These decisions affect eligibility and cannot be resolved through enrollment waivers.

**[Clarification Required]** Supply treatment-specific washout intervals, definitions of recent infection and parasitic risk, laboratory exclusion thresholds, the scope of the anaphylaxis exclusion, and criteria for uncontrolled disease or severe illness. These details are required for consistent screening and participant safety.

**[Clarification Required]** Define women of childbearing potential, acceptable contraception/abstinence requirements, pregnancy-testing procedures, and the start/end anchors for the male 18-week requirement. These details determine eligibility and reproductive-safety obligations.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** Recruitment is international, with approximately 400 participants planned.

**[Clarification Required]** Countries, sites, recruitment dates, recruitment methods, and any enrollment targets by age or region are not specified. These decisions affect feasibility, representativeness, and implementation of stratification.

**[Proposed]** Track screened, eligible, randomized, treated, rerandomized, escaped, and completed participants by treatment stage and region. Rationale: identify recruitment bottlenecks and preserve transparent accounting across the two randomizations. Assumption: participant pathways can be linked across stages.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Provided]** Early terminators and participants not entering the extension undergo approximately 12-week safety follow-up after the last injection.

**[Proposed]** Distinguish stopping assigned treatment from withdrawing consent for further assessments, and continue scheduled outcome and safety collection after treatment cessation when permitted. Rationale: reduce avoidable missingness and characterize post-treatment outcomes. Assumption: participants consent to continued follow-up.

**[Proposed]** Record withdrawal date, treatment-stop date, last injection, reason, rescue/escape status, last completed assessment, and subsequent follow-up availability. Rationale: support consistent estimand and missing-data handling. Assumption: these details can be obtained without overriding participant preferences.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify participant- and investigator-initiated discontinuation criteria and mandatory stopping rules beyond the stated escape rule. These decisions affect safety management and classification of intercurrent events.

**[Clarification Required]** Define how reproductive-safety obligations extending to 18 weeks are supported when routine safety follow-up is approximately 12 weeks. This matters for contraception counseling and pregnancy reporting beyond the scheduled follow-up period.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Proposed]** Summarize induction baseline characteristics by initial assignment and maintenance-entry characteristics by rerandomized assignment. Include age, region, baseline IGA/EASI/BSA, diary scores, prior treatment, disease duration, and other collected demographic or clinical variables. Rationale: describe each randomized population without conflating induction and responder-selected maintenance cohorts. Assumption: listed variables are collected.

**[Proposed]** Report continuous variables using sample size, mean, standard deviation, median, and range; report categorical variables using counts and percentages, with missing counts shown. Do not use baseline significance tests to select adjustment variables. Rationale: provide descriptive balance assessment without data-driven modeling. Assumption: variable distributions and data types are verified.

**[Clarification Required]** Define baseline measurement selection and whether maintenance analyses reference original baseline, Week 16, or both. This affects change scores, response persistence, and model covariates.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The FDA primary outcome is the proportion achieving Week-16 IGA 0/1 with ≥2-point improvement from baseline. EMA uses this outcome and the proportion achieving Week-16 EASI-75 as co-primary outcomes.

**[Proposed]** Operationalize EASI-75 as a ≥75% reduction from original randomized baseline EASI; similarly operationalize EASI-90 and EASI-50 as ≥90% and ≥50% reductions. Rationale: provide reproducible percentage-response derivations consistent with endpoint terminology. Assumption: original randomized baseline is the intended reference.

**[Proposed]** For primary binary induction analyses, use a composite strategy under which protocol-defined rescue use or permanent assigned-treatment discontinuation before Week 16 constitutes nonresponse, regardless of later improvement. Rationale: estimate response achieved without treatment failure under the assigned regimen. Assumption: these intercurrent events are intended to count as failure; this strategy requires approval before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** Define the primary induction treatment contrast as the difference in Week-16 response probabilities between initial LEB250Q2W and placebo assignments in the induction randomized population. Rationale: provide an interpretable absolute efficacy effect aligned with randomization. Assumption: the composite strategy above is adopted.

**[Clarification Required]** Confirm the primary estimand, especially handling of rescue, treatment cessation, prohibited therapy, death, and use of measurements obtained after these events. These decisions are not established by the sample-size assumptions and materially affect interpretation.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** Secondary and exploratory outcomes include induction and maintenance efficacy, health outcomes/quality of life, rescue use, serum lebrikizumab concentration, additional efficacy and patient-reported outcomes, and modified SQAAQ acceptability/tolerability results in participating EU adolescents.

**[Provided]** The regulator-specific induction endpoint families and the ordered EMA maintenance endpoints are listed in Section 2.4.

**[Clarification Required]** Supply complete endpoint definitions, including Pruritus NRS-4 response rules and baseline eligibility, DLQI response thresholds, sleep-loss response thresholds, adult-subset definitions where applicable, and instrument scoring conventions. These details determine denominators and reproducible response classification.

**[Clarification Required]** Define maintenance “persistence” as response at a specified assessment or sustained response across multiple assessments, and identify the eligible denominator for each endpoint. These alternatives answer different clinical questions.

**[Clarification Required]** Confirm the reference baseline for Week-52 percent EASI change and the assessment time for each persistence endpoint. Only the percent EASI change endpoint is explicitly tied to Week 52 in the supplied maintenance sequence.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the complete secondary/exploratory endpoint catalogue, serum sampling schedule, modified SQAAQ version and scoring rules, and any instrument-specific missing-item rules. Objectives alone do not establish executable outcome definitions.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** Analyze binary efficacy endpoints using a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel comparison stratified by the applicable randomization factors. Report responder counts, denominators, response percentages, a stratification-adjusted risk difference, its 95% confidence interval, and the applicable p-value. Rationale: account for stratified allocation and quantify absolute benefit. Assumption: the final stratification implementation supports this analysis.

**[Proposed]** Prespecify a blinded sparse-stratum assessment and an exact or otherwise validated sensitivity analysis where asymptotic testing is unreliable; do not pool strata using observed treatment effects. Rationale: maintain stable inference without outcome-driven pooling. Assumption: sparse-data rules are fixed before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** Analyze repeated continuous outcomes separately for induction and maintenance using a mixed model with treatment, categorical visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, the endpoint’s baseline value, baseline-by-visit interaction, and applicable randomization factors. Rationale: use repeated observations and allow visit-specific treatment effects. Assumption: the selected outcome scale and conditional model are adequate.

**[Proposed]** Use an unstructured within-participant covariance matrix, with a prespecified compound-symmetry fallback for nonconvergence. Report adjusted visit-specific means, treatment differences, and 95% confidence intervals. Rationale: allow flexible correlations while retaining an executable fallback. Assumption: sufficient repeated data are available to estimate the selected covariance structure.

**[Proposed]** For continuous outcomes affected by rescue or permanent treatment cessation, use a hypothetical no-rescue/no-cessation analysis by excluding subsequent measurements from the principal model and addressing them through the missing-data framework. Rationale: estimate continuous disease outcomes absent these intercurrent events. Assumption: the corresponding counterfactual outcomes can be modeled credibly; this estimand differs from the proposed binary composite estimand.

**[Proposed]** Analyze maintenance contrasts according to rerandomized assignment, using the finalized persistence definitions and the specified within-dose testing sequence. Treat escape or permanent maintenance-treatment cessation as failure for binary persistence outcomes. Rationale: estimate maintenance success without escape. Assumption: the composite maintenance strategy and endpoint-specific denominators are approved.

**[Proposed]** Summarize serum lebrikizumab concentrations by assigned regimen, actual exposure, and scheduled sampling time using available valid observations. Rationale: describe concentration patterns without assuming an unprovided pharmacokinetic model. Assumption: sample timing and assay-validity information are available.

**[Proposed]** Summarize modified SQAAQ responses descriptively by item or approved score, showing respondent and missing-response counts. Rationale: match the descriptive acceptability/tolerability objective. Assumption: scoring and language-version rules are finalized.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the numerical implementation of adjusted risk differences, model convergence criteria, transformations for markedly non-normal outcomes, and below-quantification concentration handling. These choices are needed for reproducible programming.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** For binary primary outcomes, classify participants with unavailable Week-16 response assessments as nonresponders after applying the intercurrent-event rules. Rationale: provide a complete randomized denominator and a transparent primary rule. Assumption: nonresponse imputation is an acceptable principal missing-data strategy; it is not assumed automatically conservative for every treatment contrast.

**[Proposed]** Apply the same missing-as-nonresponse rule to binary maintenance endpoints after their timing and eligible denominators are finalized. Rationale: maintain consistent handling of unavailable response status. Assumption: the endpoint is an assessment-specific response or has a prespecified sustained-response algorithm.

**[Proposed]** For repeated continuous outcomes, use likelihood-based estimation under missing at random conditional on included covariates and observed history. Rationale: use available longitudinal information without single-value carry-forward. Assumption: residual missingness is explainable by modeled information.

**[Proposed]** Assess sensitivity using multiple imputation under missing at random and prespecified missing-not-at-random departures, including delta-adjusted or tipping-point analyses. Rationale: show how conclusions depend on unverifiable missing-data assumptions. Assumption: imputation models include treatment, stratification factors, baseline severity, prior outcomes, and relevant missingness predictors.

**[Provided]** Diary baseline values require ≥4 of 7 daily reports; postbaseline weekly values are prorated with ≥1 report.

**[Proposed]** Treat a postbaseline diary week with no reports as missing, while retaining weeks meeting the supplied ≥1-report rule. Rationale: avoid fabricating a weekly value or imposing an unsupported stricter completeness threshold. Assumption: diary proration rules are finalized.

**[Proposed]** Report missingness by treatment, visit, endpoint, and reason, separating missing assessments from observed data classified as failure after an intercurrent event. Rationale: distinguish data absence from estimand-defined outcomes. Assumption: assessment and intercurrent-event dates are captured.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify handling of missing baseline covariates and distinguish diary-based eligibility failures from later data corrections. These decisions affect model inclusion and eligibility-deviation reporting.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Conduct supportive subgroup analyses by region, age category, and baseline IGA severity using treatment-by-subgroup interactions and confidence intervals. Rationale: examine consistency across supplied stratification factors. Assumption: subgroup sample sizes permit estimation; results are exploratory unless included in an approved multiplicity procedure.

**[Proposed]** Perform a supportive per-protocol analysis and an available-outcome treatment-policy analysis retaining measurements after rescue or treatment cessation where collected. Rationale: assess sensitivity to adherence and intercurrent-event handling. Assumption: deviation definitions and post-event observations are available.

**[Proposed]** Summarize rescue frequency, timing, type, and duration separately for induction and maintenance; summarize escape entry and subsequent outcomes descriptively. Rationale: characterize additional treatment needs without treating selected escape participants as a new randomized comparison. Assumption: rescue and escape records are sufficiently complete.

**[Proposed]** Treat analyses of additional patient-reported outcomes, concentration–response associations, and other unlisted contrasts as exploratory. Rationale: avoid confirmatory claims outside the specified testing families. Assumption: these analyses do not alter the prespecified primary analysis.

**[Clarification Required]** Confirm any additional prespecified subgroups, concentration–response models, or exploratory endpoints before unblinding. Their definitions and analytical status are otherwise undetermined.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Provided]** Safety comparison is part of the primary objective, and the enrollment target was increased to collect additional safety information.

**[Proposed]** Summarize adverse events, serious adverse events, treatment discontinuations due to adverse events, deaths, laboratory findings, and other collected safety measurements by actual treatment and study period. Rationale: characterize harms while distinguishing induction, maintenance, and open-label escape exposure. Assumption: these data are collected under standardized procedures.

**[Proposed]** Report participant counts and percentages with at least one event, event severity, relationship assessments where collected, and exposure duration; supplement with exposure-adjusted rates when follow-up differs materially. Rationale: distinguish participant risk from unequal exposure opportunity. Assumption: event dates and exposure time are reliable.

**[Proposed]** Present randomized induction safety comparisons separately from responder-selected maintenance and nonrandomized escape summaries. Rationale: avoid attributing differences caused by selection or exposure history to treatment alone. Assumption: treatment-period boundaries are documented.

**[Proposed]** Review clinically significant findings promptly and use documented procedures for urgent clinical action and emergency unblinding. Rationale: prioritize participant protection independently of scheduled statistical analyses. Assumption: qualified investigators and an operational escalation pathway are available.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify adverse-event collection windows, treatment-emergent definitions, coding dictionary/version, severity grading, seriousness reporting timelines, laboratory thresholds, pregnancy follow-up, and any adverse events of special interest. These decisions are essential for consistent safety reporting.

**[Clarification Required]** Define safety oversight responsibilities, any independent monitoring committee, and individual or study-level safety stopping rules. These arrangements cannot be inferred from the presence of a safety objective.

### 5.7 Software and References

**[Clarification Required]** Statistical software, versions, packages, random-number seeds, and sample-size calculation software are not specified. These details are needed to reproduce calculations, multiplicity procedures, imputation, and final outputs.

**[Proposed]** Use validated statistical software with version-controlled programs, documented analysis specifications, retained execution logs, and independent verification of primary endpoints and multiplicity decisions. Rationale: support reproducibility and quality assurance. Assumption: an appropriately controlled programming environment is available.

Rationale: make the framework executable without undocumented analytical choices. Assumption: all clarification items are resolved and approved before the applicable unblinded analysis.
