# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

- **[Provided]** This pivotal Phase III study evaluates whether a vaginal ring containing 25 mg dapivirine safely prevents HIV infection in healthy HIV-negative women compared with an indistinguishable placebo ring.

- **[Provided]** The intervention addresses the need for woman-controlled HIV-prevention options. The monthly product is intended for sustained use independent of sexual activity.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary efficacy objective and outcome:** Determine the efficacy of dapivirine ring use every 4 weeks for approximately 24 months, or 104 weeks, in preventing HIV-1 infection among healthy, sexually active HIV-negative women.

- **[Provided]** **P2—Primary safety objective and outcome:** Assess the safety of dapivirine ring use every 4 weeks for approximately 24 months, or 104 weeks.

- **[Provided]** **S1—Secondary objective and outcomes:** Compare HIV-2 incidence, curable sexually transmitted infection (STI) incidence, and vaginal-flora changes, and determine pregnancy incidence.

- **[Provided]** **S2—Secondary objective and outcomes:** Assess reported 4-week ring-use adherence and acceptability.

- **[Provided]** **S3—Secondary objective and outcomes:** Assess the frequency and type of HIV-1 resistance in women acquiring HIV-1 while using investigational product (IP).

- **[Provided]** **S4—Exploratory objective and outcomes:** Evaluate the HSV-2/HIV-1 association; relationships between contraception method, pregnancy, and HIV seroconversion; drug concentrations versus HIV-1 seroconversion and viral resistance; and drug concentrations versus self-reported adherence.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **[Provided]** The study is a multicentre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pivotal Phase III safety-and-efficacy trial with a 2:1 dapivirine-to-placebo allocation ratio.

- **[Provided]** The dapivirine arm receives Ring-004, a silicone-elastomer vaginal matrix ring containing 25 mg dapivirine. The control arm receives an indistinguishable placebo vaginal ring.

- **[Provided]** Participants in both arms use a ring continuously, with self-insertion and replacement at 4-week intervals for approximately 104 weeks. Exit follow-up occurs 6 weeks after ring discontinuation.

- **[Clarification Required]** The identities and number of research centres, participating jurisdictions, and operational responsibilities are not supplied. These are needed to define site oversight, laboratory responsibilities, and applicable regulatory procedures.

- **[Proposed]** Document informed consent, ethics approval, confidentiality protections, and access to appropriate clinical care. Rationale: these are essential participant protections. Assumption: the applicable consent and approval requirements can be established from the participating jurisdictions and study records.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the risk-reduction counselling package and permitted concomitant HIV-prevention measures. These may influence HIV incidence and the interpretation of the intervention effect.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **[Provided]** An automated Interactive Voice/Web Response System (IXRS) assigns a unique participant identification number and treatment. A master randomization list links participant identification numbers to assignments.

- **[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by research centre and uses a prespecified block size.

- **[Clarification Required]** The numerical block size, sequence-generation procedure, and any use of minimization are not supplied. These details are needed to reproduce and audit allocation without assuming an unspecified randomization method.

- **[Proposed]** Restrict access to the allocation list to authorized personnel independent of routine participant assessment and blinded analysis. Rationale: allocation concealment and protected treatment codes reduce selection and assessment bias. Assumption: IXRS access controls and an auditable code-management process are available.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify which participant-facing, clinical, laboratory, and analysis personnel are blinded, together with emergency unblinding authority and procedures. “Double-blind” alone does not establish these operational arrangements.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** Planned enrollment was approximately 1,950 women: 1,300 assigned dapivirine and 650 assigned placebo. A total of 1,959 women were enrolled, and accrual closed.

- **[Provided]** Amendment 3 increased planned enrollment from 1,650 to approximately 1,950 to mitigate product nonadherence and preserve power following high nonadherence at one research centre. This was an amendment, not a prospectively described sample-size re-estimation algorithm.

- **[Provided]** The sample-size calculation concerns HIV-1 seroconversion incidence in the two treatment arms, treated as a time-to-event incidence-rate outcome under proportional hazards.

- **[Provided]** Assumptions include average annual placebo-arm HIV incidence greater than 4%, allowing for a reduction associated with risk-reduction counselling; microbicide efficacy of at least 50%; proportional hazards between groups; and early discontinuation or loss to follow-up of no more than 10%.

- **[Provided]** The target effect is a 50% reduction in the HIV-1 seroconversion rate. Calculations use a two-sided log-rank test at significance level 0.05, with additional scenarios evaluating 40% and 60% efficacy.

- **[Provided]** At 50% efficacy, reported power is 81%, with 96 expected HIV-1 seroconversions.

- **[Provided]** At 40% efficacy, reported power is 61%, with 104 expected HIV-1 seroconversions.

- **[Provided]** At 60% efficacy, reported power is 94%, with 88 expected HIV-1 seroconversions.

- **[Provided]** These seroconversion counts are expected events, not a specified required-event target.

- **[Provided]** No non-inferiority or equivalence margin applies because this is a superiority trial. A cluster-randomization design effect does not apply because randomization is individual, with research-centre stratification.

- **[Provided]** The additional safety rationale states that 1,950 participants provide a 95% probability of detecting an adverse event (AE) occurring at a frequency of at least 0.3% among those assigned IP.

- **[Clarification Required]** The complete calculation, including software, accrual assumptions, follow-up distribution, and the denominator underlying the AE-detection probability, is not supplied. These details are needed for reproducibility; the reported assumptions and results should not be replaced by an independently reconstructed calculation.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

- **[Provided]** The primary efficacy null hypothesis is no difference between dapivirine and placebo in HIV-1 seroconversion rates or population survival curves. The efficacy design tests superiority.

- **[Provided]** Primary safety analysis is descriptive.

- **[Derived]** Under the proportional-hazards assumption, define \(HR=\lambda_{\mathrm{dapivirine}}/\lambda_{\mathrm{placebo}}\). The efficacy null corresponds to \(HR=1\); benefit corresponds to \(HR<1\). Efficacy expressed as \(100(1-HR)\%\) gives target hazard ratios of 0.50, 0.60, and 0.40 for 50%, 40%, and 60% efficacy, respectively.

- **[Proposed]** Declare primary efficacy superiority only when the prespecified two-sided test rejects the null and the estimated effect favours dapivirine. Rationale: a statistically significant harmful effect must not be interpreted as prevention efficacy. Assumption: the final testing level remains valid after any approved interim-testing adjustments.

- **[Clarification Required]** No formal safety acceptability threshold or overall benefit–risk success rule is supplied. These must be specified if a binary safety or overall trial-success decision is intended; descriptive safety results alone do not define such a rule.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **[Clarification Required]** The existence, timing, and scope of interim efficacy, futility, or safety analyses are not supplied. Confirm any monitoring charter, information fractions, stopping boundaries, and decision authority because these determine whether final-test error control requires adjustment.

- **[Provided]** The enrollment increase under Amendment 3 was not a prospectively described re-estimation algorithm.

- **[Proposed]** Reconcile any historical unblinded reviews and analysis-affecting decisions before finalizing the statistical analysis plan (SAP). Rationale: undisclosed interim access can affect error control and interpretation. Assumption: monitoring and amendment records are available for review.

- **[Proposed]** Establish documented safety oversight and escalation procedures without inventing numerical stopping boundaries. Rationale: participant protection requires actionable oversight even when formal interim efficacy testing is absent. Assumption: an authorized clinical or independent oversight body can be identified.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **[Provided]** Final analyses occur only after database lock. Blinded review, including protocol-deviation classification, occurs before lock; treatment codes are released after lock.

- **[Provided]** Screening lasts up to 28 days. IP use lasts approximately 104 weeks, followed by exit 6 weeks after last product use. On-IP visit windows are ±7 days.

- **[Provided]** Every 4 weeks, assessments include HIV and pregnancy testing, AE and concomitant-treatment review, diary/adherence data, and ring exchange.

- **[Provided]** Every 12 weeks, assessments include pelvic examinations, STI assessments, and safety laboratory testing.

- **[Provided]** Acceptability is assessed at week 4 and every 24 weeks beginning at week 24.

- **[Provided]** Cervical cytology is performed at screening, week 52, and the last product-use visit.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the final data cutoff, required completion of exit follow-up, and handling of unresolved HIV diagnoses, pregnancies, and serious AEs at lock. Database lock alone does not define the observation period included in each analysis.

- **[Proposed]** Complete the SAP, endpoint derivation rules, analysis shells, and blinded data-quality review before code release. Rationale: prospective analytical decisions limit outcome-driven choices. Assumption: treatment-level comparative results remain unavailable during this process.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **[Provided]** The sample-size calculation uses a two-sided significance level of 0.05.

- **[Proposed]** Use a two-sided 0.05 level for the primary efficacy analysis and report a 95% confidence interval for the hazard ratio and corresponding efficacy estimate. Rationale: this aligns analysis with the design calculation. Assumption: no interim or other confirmatory testing requires adjustment.

- **[Derived]** If the hazard-ratio confidence interval is \((L,U)\), the corresponding efficacy interval is \(\bigl(100(1-U),\,100(1-L)\bigr)\%\).

- **[Proposed]** Treat the primary efficacy comparison as the single confirmatory statistical test; present safety descriptively and secondary/exploratory comparisons with nominal confidence intervals and clearly identified unadjusted \(p\)-values where useful. Rationale: this avoids unsupported confirmatory claims across many outcomes. Assumption: no additional confirmatory hierarchy has been specified.

- **[Proposed]** Report effect sizes, denominators, observation time, and uncertainty rather than interpreting statistical significance alone. Rationale: clinical interpretation requires magnitude and precision. Assumption: relevant denominators and follow-up dates can be reconstructed.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **[Provided]** Protocol deviations are classified during blinded review before database lock.

- **[Proposed]** Classify deviations by potential impact on eligibility, participant safety, treatment exposure, endpoint ascertainment, and analysis validity. Rationale: impact-based categories support consistent reporting and sensitivity analyses. Assumption: source records permit blinded adjudication.

- **[Clarification Required]** Define major deviations, important visit-window violations, prohibited concomitant treatments, and any adherence thresholds before unblinding. These definitions affect supportive analysis membership and must not be selected using treatment effects.

- **[Proposed]** Retain participants in the primary randomized comparison despite post-randomization nonadherence or deviations; evaluate exclusions only in prespecified supportive analyses. Rationale: exclusions can compromise randomization. Assumption: an incident HIV-1 outcome and usable follow-up can be established.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **[Proposed]** Define the screened population as all women undergoing screening and the randomized population as all women assigned a treatment through IXRS. Rationale: separate populations distinguish recruitment, enrollment, and allocation. Assumption: screening, enrollment, and IXRS records can be reconciled.

- **[Proposed]** Use an intention-to-treat primary efficacy population, analyzing randomized participants according to assigned treatment irrespective of subsequent adherence or product changes. Rationale: this estimates the effect of assignment while preserving randomization. Assumption: baseline HIV-negative status can be established for incident-infection analysis.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the treatment of infections subsequently shown to predate randomization and of indeterminate baseline HIV results. These decisions affect incident-event classification and whether a modified intention-to-treat population is necessary.

- **[Proposed]** Define the safety population as participants with documented use of at least one study ring, summarized by product actually received; also provide randomized-arm denominators. Rationale: exposure-based summaries support attribution while allocation-based denominators preserve transparency. Assumption: insertion and dispensing records adequately identify exposure.

- **[Proposed]** Define any per-protocol or adherence-restricted population as supportive only, using blinded, prespecified criteria. Rationale: adherence-based selection may introduce confounding. Assumption: sufficiently reliable exposure data exist.

- **[Proposed]** Define outcome-specific populations for pathogen incidence, pregnancy, resistance, questionnaires, and drug concentrations, reporting eligibility, availability, and exclusions separately. Rationale: these outcomes have different biological risk sets and measurement requirements. Assumption: their eligibility and ascertainment rules are finalized before analysis.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

- **[Proposed]** Summarize women screened, eligible, enrolled, randomized, exposed, completing product use, completing exit follow-up, and included in each analysis, with reasons for exclusion or discontinuation. Rationale: a participant-flow accounting identifies selection and attrition. Assumption: disposition records are available.

- **[Proposed]** Tabulate screening failures by criterion and research centre, distinguishing women from repeated screening episodes. Rationale: this avoids inflated recruitment denominators and describes eligibility barriers. Assumption: repeat screening can be identified without compromising confidentiality.

- **[Clarification Required]** Screening counts, rescreening rules, and the relationship between the 1,959 enrolled women and the randomized population are not supplied. These must be confirmed to produce an accurate participant-flow account.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

- **[Provided]** Inclusion requires women aged 18–45 years who can consent and attend study procedures.

- **[Provided]** Participants must be sexually active, defined as at least one penetrative penile-vaginal act per month during the preceding 3 months, and HIV-negative at screening and enrollment.

- **[Provided]** Participants must use stable contraception unless postmenopausal or surgically sterilized; be asymptomatic for genital infection at enrollment; be locatable; and not participate in conflicting investigational studies.

- **[Provided]** Exclusions include current/recent intended pregnancy or breastfeeding; specified injection-drug, research, or vaccine histories; untreated significant urogenital infection or a grade ≥2 pelvic finding; specified genital, medical, or allergy conditions; clinically important baseline laboratory abnormalities; disqualifying cytology; and investigator-judged risk or inability to adhere.

- **[Clarification Required]** The permitted contraceptive methods, required duration of contraceptive stability, pregnancy-related time windows, and definitions of menopause and surgical sterilization are not supplied. These are needed for reproducible eligibility assessment.

- **[Clarification Required]** The specified injection-drug/research/vaccine histories, genital/medical/allergy conditions, laboratory thresholds, cytology categories, and grading system are not supplied. These details are necessary to operationalize exclusions without inventing criteria.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify acceptable HIV tests and resolution algorithms, management of genital infection before enrollment, and whether treatment permits rescreening. These decisions determine eligibility and baseline disease classification.

### 4.3 Recruitment

- **[Provided]** Accrual has closed after enrollment of 1,959 women.

- **[Clarification Required]** Recruitment dates, recruitment methods, centre-specific enrollment, and actual treatment-arm counts are not supplied. They are needed to describe recruitment and assess implementation of the planned allocation.

- **[Proposed]** Reconcile final enrollment and allocation counts against IXRS and centre records rather than assuming the planned 1,300:650 counts were achieved. Rationale: planned and actual allocations are distinct. Assumption: final allocation records are complete.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **[Provided]** Exit follow-up is scheduled 6 weeks after ring discontinuation.

- **[Proposed]** Distinguish temporary product interruption, permanent product discontinuation, withdrawal from procedures, withdrawal of consent for further contact, loss to follow-up, and death. Rationale: these have different implications for exposure and outcome ascertainment. Assumption: dates and reasons can be documented.

- **[Proposed]** Continue consented assessments within the applicable follow-up schedule after product discontinuation whenever feasible, and do not automatically equate stopping the ring with withdrawal from study follow-up. Rationale: this reduces avoidable missing outcome data. Assumption: continued contact is permitted by consent and the finalized protocol.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify product interruption, discontinuation, restart, and follow-up rules following pregnancy, suspected or confirmed HIV infection, clinically important toxicity, or participant request. These rules directly affect safety, exposure, resistance assessment, and censoring.

- **[Proposed]** Provide prompt clinical assessment and referral for positive HIV or pregnancy tests and clinically important findings. Rationale: timely care protects participants. Assumption: referral pathways and confirmatory testing are available.

- **[Clarification Required]** Define permitted tracing procedures, the threshold for declaring loss to follow-up, and continued use of previously collected data after consent withdrawal. These affect participant rights and completeness of analysis.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **[Proposed]** Describe baseline characteristics by randomized arm and overall using counts and percentages for categorical variables and appropriate measures of centre and spread for continuous variables. Rationale: descriptive summaries characterize the randomized groups without testing whether randomization “worked.” Assumption: baseline measurements precede product exposure.

- **[Proposed]** Include age, research centre, sexual-activity measures, contraception, reproductive history, baseline STI and vaginal-flora findings, clinical/laboratory findings, and HSV-2 status where collected. Rationale: these variables support interpretation of the stated objectives. Assumption: only actually collected variables will be reported.

- **[Clarification Required]** The full baseline data collection set is not supplied. Its contents and timing must be confirmed before prespecifying adjusted models or subgroup analyses.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

- **[Provided]** The primary efficacy outcome is HIV-1 infection prevention during dapivirine ring use every 4 weeks for approximately 24 months, or 104 weeks.

- **[Proposed]** Operationalize efficacy as time from randomization to first confirmed incident HIV-1 infection, comparing dapivirine with placebo. Rationale: this matches the time-to-event design. Assumption: baseline infection status and a reproducible incident-infection algorithm are available.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the diagnostic confirmation algorithm, HIV-1/HIV-2 differentiation, handling of discordant results, and infection-date assignment. These determine both event counts and event times.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the primary efficacy observation window, including whether infections identified at the 6-week exit visit contribute and how early product discontinuation changes follow-up. Approximately 104 weeks of product use does not by itself define the efficacy censoring horizon.

- **[Proposed]** Retain observed HIV follow-up after product interruptions or discontinuation within the agreed efficacy window, analyzing participants as assigned. Rationale: this supports the randomized assignment comparison. Assumption: post-discontinuation observations are collected and remain eligible under the finalized window.

- **[Provided]** The primary safety outcome is safety of dapivirine ring use on the same 4-week replacement schedule for approximately 104 weeks; analysis is descriptive.

- **[Clarification Required]** The formal primary safety endpoint or endpoint set is not specified. Define whether safety is represented by all AEs, selected severity categories, serious AEs, genital findings, laboratory abnormalities, or another prespecified set; these choices determine the primary safety summaries.

- **[Proposed]** Pending that definition, organize safety summaries around treatment-emergent AEs, serious AEs, severity, relatedness, discontinuations, and relevant clinical and laboratory findings. Rationale: these complementary summaries characterize tolerability and clinically important toxicity. Assumption: coding, grading, and attribution conventions are established before unblinding.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

- **[Provided]** S1 outcomes are HIV-2 incidence, curable STI incidence, vaginal-flora changes, and pregnancy incidence.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify the curable STI organisms, diagnostic methods, baseline-clearance requirements, reinfection definitions, vaginal-flora assessment system, and pregnancy-confirmation rules. These are needed to distinguish prevalent conditions, incident events, recurrence, and change.

- **[Provided]** S2 outcomes are reported 4-week ring-use adherence and acceptability.

- **[Proposed]** Summarize reported ring use over each reporting interval and longitudinally, retaining the underlying reported days or interruptions rather than imposing an unsupported adherence threshold. Rationale: this preserves information and avoids arbitrary dichotomization. Assumption: diary or interview data identify the relevant reporting interval.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify adherence questions, diary reconciliation, acceptability instruments, scoring algorithms, and any prespecified adherence categories. These choices determine the meaning and comparability of the reported outcomes.

- **[Provided]** S3 outcomes are frequency and type of HIV-1 resistance among women acquiring HIV-1 while using IP.

- **[Clarification Required]** Define “while using IP,” resistance-testing specimens and timing, assay methods, mutation classification, and successful-test criteria. These decisions define the resistance population and distinguish absent resistance from unavailable testing.

- **[Provided]** S4 outcomes concern HSV-2/HIV-1 associations; contraception, pregnancy, and HIV-seroconversion relationships; drug concentrations versus HIV-1 seroconversion or viral resistance; and drug concentrations versus self-reported adherence.

- **[Clarification Required]** Drug-concentration specimen types, sampling schedule, assay quantification limits, HSV-2 assessment methods, and timing of contraception updates are not supplied. These are essential for valid temporal alignment and interpretation.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **[Proposed]** Use a research-centre-stratified, two-sided log-rank test for the primary efficacy comparison. Estimate the treatment hazard ratio using a Cox model stratified by research centre, with treatment as the explanatory variable. Rationale: this respects the allocation structure and proportional-hazards design. Assumption: event information is adequate for the prespecified stratification.

- **[Proposed]** Report HIV-1 event counts, person-time, incidence rates, Kaplan–Meier estimates, the hazard ratio, and efficacy \(100(1-HR)\%\), with corresponding uncertainty. Rationale: these summaries describe absolute incidence and relative treatment effect. Assumption: event and censoring dates are consistently derived.

- **[Proposed]** Use randomization as time origin, with observed event and visit dates rather than nominal visit numbers; use a prespecified tie-handling method in the Cox model. Rationale: actual dates preserve follow-up information. Assumption: reliable dates are available and the efficacy horizon has been finalized.

- **[Proposed]** For participants without confirmed infection, censor at the last evaluable negative HIV assessment within the agreed efficacy window and data cutoff. Rationale: infection-free time should not extend beyond documented ascertainment. Assumption: censoring is conditionally non-informative for the primary analysis.

- **[Proposed]** Assess proportional hazards using graphical and model-based diagnostics. If materially violated, supplement the primary analysis with time-varying treatment effects or restricted mean infection-free time over a prespecified supported horizon. Rationale: a single hazard ratio may conceal changing effects. Assumption: adequate follow-up supports these supplementary estimates.

- **[Proposed]** Analyze first HIV-2, STI, and pregnancy events using incidence rates and suitable time-to-event comparisons among participants at risk for each outcome; describe recurrent events separately where ascertainment permits. Rationale: first and recurrent events require different risk-set definitions. Assumption: incident-event and recurrence rules are established before analysis.

- **[Proposed]** Analyze vaginal-flora changes using baseline-to-follow-up transitions and longitudinal models appropriate to the finalized measurement scale. Rationale: changes may be categorical, ordinal, or continuous. Assumption: the assessment system and repeated-measure structure are known before model selection.

- **[Proposed]** Summarize adherence and acceptability by visit and arm; use longitudinal models appropriate to the observed response scale when comparative estimation is useful. Rationale: repeated observations within a participant are correlated. Assumption: instruments and scoring rules are finalized and model support is adequate.

- **[Proposed]** Report resistance frequency using both all eligible on-IP HIV-1 seroconverters and successfully tested seroconverters as explicit denominators; describe resistance types separately. Rationale: incomplete testing can otherwise produce misleading proportions. Assumption: resistance-test eligibility and availability are recorded.

- **[Proposed]** Prespecify handling of sparse strata, zero-event groups, non-estimable models, and convergence failures without treatment-effect-driven pooling. Rationale: sparse outcomes can invalidate routine estimation. Assumption: blinded data review can identify plausible analytical limitations.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **[Proposed]** Report missingness by arm, centre, visit, outcome, and reason, distinguishing uncollected, unavailable, invalid, and indeterminate results. Rationale: different missing-data mechanisms have different implications. Assumption: reason codes or source records permit classification.

- **[Proposed]** Do not treat a missed HIV test as a negative result or assign infection-free time beyond the last evaluable negative assessment. Rationale: absence of testing does not establish absence of infection. Assumption: preceding and subsequent test dates can be identified.

- **[Proposed]** Assess sensitivity to uncertain infection timing using the interval between the last negative and first positive assessments, including an interval-censored analysis where feasible. Rationale: periodic testing does not directly observe acquisition time. Assumption: interval boundaries and diagnostic classifications are reliable.

- **[Proposed]** Examine potentially informative loss to follow-up or early discontinuation using inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting and/or prespecified event-risk sensitivity scenarios. Rationale: differential attrition can bias efficacy estimates. Assumption: measured predictors adequately support weighting, or scenario assumptions are explicitly reported.

- **[Proposed]** For repeated questionnaire or clinical outcomes, use models that accommodate incomplete observations under a stated missing-at-random assumption, with multiple imputation where justified and sensitivity analyses for important departures. Rationale: complete-case analysis alone may be biased. Assumption: sufficient observed predictors support the chosen method.

- **[Proposed]** Do not equate missing drug concentrations, unreturned rings, or missing resistance tests with nonadherence, zero concentration, or absence of resistance. Rationale: these are measurement failures, not observed biological states. Assumption: missingness can be distinguished from valid below-quantification results.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify handling of below-quantification concentrations, partial dates, missing questionnaire items, and unresolved HIV results before analysis. These decisions materially affect derived outcomes and model inputs.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

- **[Proposed]** Conduct supportive adjusted efficacy analyses using a limited, prespecified set of collected baseline prognostic variables. Rationale: adjustment may improve precision and assess robustness. Assumption: variables are selected before unblinding and event numbers support the model.

- **[Proposed]** Explore treatment-effect heterogeneity by research centre and clinically relevant collected baseline factors, using interaction estimates rather than comparing subgroup-specific significance. Rationale: interaction analysis directly addresses heterogeneity. Assumption: subgroup definitions are prespecified and results are interpreted as exploratory.

- **[Proposed]** Keep all randomized centres in the primary analysis; any centre-exclusion analysis should be explicitly supportive and accompanied by its rationale. Rationale: excluding a centre because of adherence or observed outcomes can distort the randomized comparison. Assumption: no independently established data-integrity finding requires a different prespecified action.

- **[Proposed]** Present adherence-restricted, on-product, or exposure-associated analyses only as supportive associations, with adjustment for measured predictors where feasible. Rationale: adherence and exposure are post-randomization variables susceptible to selection and confounding. Assumption: exposure measures and relevant covariates are available.

- **[Proposed]** Evaluate HSV-2 and HIV-1 associations using appropriately timed HSV-2 status; evaluate contraception and pregnancy relationships using time-updated variables where recorded. Rationale: temporal alignment limits reverse-causation and immortal-time errors. Assumption: measurement dates support the proposed ordering.

- **[Proposed]** Relate drug concentrations to subsequent HIV-1 seroconversion, resistance, and contemporaneous or appropriately lagged self-reported adherence using prespecified exposure windows. Rationale: measurements after an outcome cannot establish preceding exposure. Assumption: sampling dates and sufficient pre-outcome specimens are available.

- **[Proposed]** Interpret resistance and concentration analyses among seroconverters cautiously because infection, specimen availability, and adherence can select the analyzed subset. Rationale: subset associations are not automatically causal treatment effects. Assumption: the relevant selection mechanisms cannot be fully eliminated.

- **[Proposed]** If deaths occur before HIV ascertainment, describe them separately and consider competing-risk sensitivity analyses. Rationale: death can preclude observation of HIV infection. Assumption: death status and dates are ascertainable.

### 5.6 Harms

- **[Proposed]** Define treatment-emergent AEs as events beginning or worsening after first ring use, with the reporting window extending through the agreed safety follow-up period. Rationale: a consistent exposure-linked window separates baseline conditions from emerging events. Assumption: onset, worsening, first-use, and follow-up dates are available.

- **[Clarification Required]** Confirm whether the safety reporting window includes the full 6-week post-discontinuation period and whether selected serious events require longer follow-up. The exit schedule alone does not establish every AE reporting obligation.

- **[Proposed]** Summarize participants with at least one AE and total events by treatment, event category, severity, seriousness, relatedness, and action taken; include interruptions and permanent discontinuations. Rationale: participant incidence and recurrent-event burden answer different safety questions. Assumption: consistent coding and grading conventions are adopted.

- **[Proposed]** Present safety laboratory and pelvic findings using baseline-to-follow-up changes, clinically important abnormalities, and grade shifts under the selected grading system. Rationale: change-based summaries complement AE reporting. Assumption: comparable baseline and follow-up measurements exist.

- **[Proposed]** Describe pregnancies and available pregnancy outcomes separately from other AEs, while retaining any associated events that meet the finalized AE or serious-AE definitions. Rationale: pregnancy incidence and pregnancy-associated harms are distinct outcomes. Assumption: consented pregnancy follow-up information is available.

- **[Proposed]** Provide descriptive between-arm risk differences or ratios with confidence intervals where useful, without interpreting nonsignificance as evidence of equal safety. Rationale: rare events may have imprecise estimates despite no apparent statistical difference. Assumption: denominators and observation windows are comparable or their differences are disclosed.

- **[Clarification Required]** Specify AE terminology, grading standards, seriousness definitions, causality categories, expedited reporting rules, and oversight responsibilities. These are necessary for consistent safety classification and participant protection.

### 5.7 Software and References

- **[Clarification Required]** Software used for the sample-size calculation and planned final analyses is not specified. Record software names, versions, packages, and relevant settings to make results reproducible.

- **[Proposed]** Use validated statistical software, version-controlled programs, an analysis-ready data dictionary, and independent checks of primary endpoint derivation and efficacy estimates. Rationale: reproducible programming and verification reduce implementation error. Assumption: analysis datasets, specifications, and audit records are retained.

- **[Proposed]** Maintain a dated SAP and a documented record of post-lock analytical departures, identifying whether decisions preceded or followed unblinding. Rationale: transparent change control distinguishes prespecified analyses from subsequent exploration. Assumption: decision dates and access to treatment information can be documented.
