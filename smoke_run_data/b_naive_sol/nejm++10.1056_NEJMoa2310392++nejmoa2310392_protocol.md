# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

[Provided] This study evaluates whether intravenous tenecteplase improves clinical outcomes compared with placebo in imaging-selected acute ischemic stroke presenting 4.5–24 hours after last-known-well time. The rationale is to achieve earlier reperfusion in patients with salvageable tissue and internal carotid artery (ICA) or middle cerebral artery (MCA) occlusion, including patients requiring transfer for endovascular therapy (EVT).

### 1.2 Objectives

- [Provided] **P1—Primary:** Compare tenecteplase with placebo using ordinal Day-90 modified Rankin Scale (mRS).
- [Provided] **S1—Secondary:** Evaluate efficacy using Day-90 functional independence, angiographic reperfusion, NIHSS, Barthel Index, Glasgow Outcome Scale, 24-hour reperfusion, and 24-hour recanalization; evaluate safety using symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), adverse events, mortality, and PH2.
- [Provided] **S2—Exploratory:** Evaluate final infarct volume/growth, mRS subgroups, outcomes when thrombectomy is not performed, 30-day readmission, one-pass EVT, Neuro-QoL, and Day-90 survival.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

[Provided] This is a Phase III, prospective, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled superiority trial in imaging-eligible acute ischemic stroke presenting 4.5–24 hours after last-known-well time, with ICA or MCA M1/M2 occlusion. Allocation is 1:1.

- [Provided] **Tenecteplase:** 0.25 mg/kg intravenously, maximum 25 mg, administered as one bolus over 5 seconds.
- [Provided] **Placebo:** One bolus injection over 5 seconds.
- [Provided] **Background treatment:** Both groups receive guideline-based standard care. Patients with ICA/M1 occlusion may receive adjuvant EVT if guideline criteria are met.

[Proposed] Use matched preparation and administration procedures, restrict access to treatment codes, and maintain masking of outcome assessors and the analysis team until authorized unblinding. Rationale: these measures reduce performance and assessment bias. Assumption: matching preparations and restricted code access are operationally feasible.

[Clarification Required] Specify placebo composition, dose preparation, weight determination, masking responsibilities, and emergency unblinding procedures. These decisions are necessary for reproducible administration and safe maintenance of the blind.

[Clarification Required] Identify the applicable standard-care and EVT guidelines, their versions, and the approach to EVT in M2 occlusion. Also clarify whether the 4.5–24-hour requirement applies only to presentation or additionally constrains randomization or dosing; this affects eligibility and treatment logistics.

### 2.2 Randomization

[Provided] An interactive web response system (IWRS) randomizes eligible, consented patients and assigns a treatment kit. Treatment should be administered as soon as possible.

[Provided] Randomization uses permuted blocks to provide approximately 1:1 treatment balance within strata defined by age ≤70 versus >70 years, ICA/M1 versus M2 occlusion, baseline NIHSS ≤15 versus >15, and ECC versus nECC.

[Proposed] Generate and retain the allocation schedule independently of recruiting staff, conceal future assignments within IWRS, and restrict access to block information. Rationale: allocation concealment prevents prediction or manipulation of assignments. Assumption: independent schedule generation and secure IWRS administration are available.

[Clarification Required] Define ECC and nECC, specify block sizes and whether they vary, and document how stratification values are established. Clarify which NIHSS assessment defines the baseline randomization stratum, because eligibility also requires an immediately pre-randomization NIHSS assessment.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- [Provided] Planned enrollment is 456 patients, approximately 228 per group, with 432 evaluable patients after an assumed 5% dropout.
- [Provided] The calculation uses Day-90 ordinal mRS with seven levels, 0–6.
- [Provided] The assumed control-arm distribution for mRS 0–6 is 13%, 13%, 12%, 14%, 18%, 12%, and 18%.
- [Provided] The assumed treatment-arm distribution for mRS 0–6 is 17%, 20%, 16%, 15%, 13%, 7%, and 12%.
- [Provided] The target effect is a common odds ratio of at least 1.76 for tenecteplase versus placebo.
- [Provided] The calculation assumes a Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney test, two-sided significance level 0.049 after adjustment for one efficacy interim analysis, and power of at least 90%.
- [Provided] Variability is represented by the assumed ordinal mRS distributions. Alternative historical scenarios produced power greater than 99% and greater than 88%.
- [Provided] The assumptions and alternative scenarios were based on prior stroke studies. The stated rationale is that 456 participants provide 90% power for the specified distribution difference after 5% dropout.
- [Provided] EAST 6.4.1 was used for sample-size calculations.
- [Provided] No required event count applies because the design is not event-driven. No non-inferiority or equivalence margin applies because this is a superiority study. No clustering design effect applies because randomization is individual rather than cluster-based.

[Derived] Applying 5% attrition directly to 456 participants gives 433.2 expected evaluable participants, rather than 432.

[Clarification Required] Reconcile the stated 432-evaluable target with the 456 enrollment target and 5% dropout assumption, including the rounding or inflation convention. This affects reproduction of the calculation; neither supplied target should be silently changed.

[Clarification Required] Specify whether sample-size re-estimation or enrollment amendments are permitted and under what governance. No such procedure is specified, and an unplanned change could affect operating characteristics.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

[Provided] All efficacy endpoints are assessed for superiority. For the primary endpoint, the null hypothesis is a common odds ratio of 1, versus the two-sided alternative that the common odds ratio differs from 1. The odds ratio represents the odds of lower, better mRS for tenecteplase versus placebo.

[Proposed] Interpret primary superiority as rejection of the null hypothesis under the prespecified testing rule together with an estimated common odds ratio greater than 1. Rationale: a statistically significant result favoring placebo would not establish tenecteplase benefit. Assumption: the primary model uses the stated favorable-outcome orientation.

[Clarification Required] Approve the primary inferential model and reconcile its operating characteristics with the Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney sample-size calculation. The common-odds-ratio hypothesis does not, by itself, fully specify the estimation and testing procedure.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

[Provided] The design includes one efficacy interim analysis, for which the stated final two-sided significance level is adjusted to 0.049.

[Clarification Required] Specify interim timing, information fraction, required endpoint maturity, efficacy boundary, overall type I error allocation, and any futility or safety stopping rules. The final significance level alone does not determine these design elements.

[Proposed] Use an independent monitoring committee supported by an unblinded statistical team, with recommendations communicated without disclosing comparative results to the blinded study team. Rationale: independent review permits monitoring while limiting operational bias. Assumption: committee membership, responsibilities, and access controls can be established before interim review.

[Clarification Required] Define who may recommend or authorize early termination and how follow-up and analysis would proceed after stopping. These decisions affect participant safety, endpoint completeness, and interpretation.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

[Provided] Final analysis occurs after all patients have completed or discontinued the study, all data are in the database, and database lock has occurred.

- [Provided] **24-hour imaging and NIHSS:** 18–30 hours.
- [Provided] **72–96-hour NIHSS assessment:** 60–96 hours.
- [Provided] **72–96-hour imaging assessment:** 60–120 hours.
- [Provided] **Day-5 mRS, Glasgow Outcome Scale, and Barthel Index:** 4–6 days.
- [Provided] **Day-30 clinical outcomes:** 15–60 days.
- [Provided] **Day-90 clinical outcomes:** 61–120 days.

[Clarification Required] Specify the reference clock for each window, selection rules for multiple assessments, treatment of out-of-window assessments, and endpoint-specific visit assignments. These details are needed to construct reproducible analysis datasets.

[Proposed] Finalize the statistical analysis plan, endpoint derivations, and blinded data-review decisions before treatment-code release for final analysis. Rationale: prospective specification reduces outcome-dependent analytical choices. Assumption: operational timelines allow these decisions before unblinding.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

[Provided] The stated final primary significance level is two-sided 0.049 after adjustment for one efficacy interim analysis.

[Proposed] Report the primary common odds ratio, its nominal two-sided 95.1% confidence interval, and the exact two-sided p-value. Separately identify any group-sequentially adjusted inference required by the finalized monitoring design. Rationale: the nominal interval corresponds to the stated final cutoff but is not automatically a repeated-testing-adjusted interval. Assumption: the approved interim design retains the supplied final cutoff.

[Clarification Required] Specify whether secondary endpoints have a confirmatory hierarchy or other multiplicity adjustment. Without this decision, secondary findings cannot be assigned a controlled family-wise confirmatory interpretation.

[Proposed] Until a confirmatory secondary testing strategy is approved, report secondary and exploratory effect estimates with nominal 95% confidence intervals and clearly identify p-values as descriptive. Rationale: this communicates uncertainty without implying multiplicity-controlled evidence. Assumption: only the primary endpoint currently has a specified confirmatory testing rule.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

[Proposed] Prospectively classify deviations involving eligibility, consent, randomization, dosing, prohibited treatment, masking, and endpoint assessment. Summarize counts and reasons by randomized group. Rationale: consistent classification supports transparent conduct reporting. Assumption: deviation review can be performed without access to comparative outcomes.

[Proposed] Do not exclude randomized participants from the primary analysis because of deviations; use a separately defined per-protocol analysis only as supportive evidence. Rationale: preserving randomized assignment reduces selection bias. Assumption: outcome collection remains permissible.

[Clarification Required] Approve the major-deviation definitions and any per-protocol exclusion rules before unblinding. Their absence prevents reproducible construction of a per-protocol population.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- [Proposed] **Intention-to-treat population:** All randomized participants, analyzed as assigned regardless of treatment receipt, subsequent EVT, or other post-randomization care. Rationale: this preserves the randomized comparison. Assumption: all randomized participants can be identified reliably.
- [Proposed] **Safety population:** All participants receiving any study injection, analyzed according to treatment received. Rationale: exposure-based grouping supports assessment of treatment-associated harms. Assumption: actual injection and treatment identity are documented.
- [Proposed] **Per-protocol population:** Randomized participants without prospectively defined major deviations, analyzed as assigned. Rationale: this provides a supportive assessment of adherence to the intended protocol. Assumption: exclusion rules are finalized before unblinding.
- [Proposed] **Endpoint-specific populations:** Identify participants eligible for each imaging or procedural endpoint and report both the eligible denominator and the number with evaluable measurements. Rationale: procedural eligibility and missing measurement are different issues. Assumption: eligibility can be determined independently of the endpoint result.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

[Proposed] Maintain a screening log recording eligibility assessment, reasons for exclusion, consent status, randomization status, and relevant treatment-window information. Rationale: this permits evaluation of recruitment and selection. Assumption: collection is permitted under applicable consent and privacy arrangements.

[Proposed] Present participant flow from screening through randomization, injection, follow-up, and analysis, with reasons for non-treatment and unavailable outcomes. Rationale: transparent denominators are essential for interpreting efficacy and safety. Assumption: participant disposition is systematically captured.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

[Provided] The study population comprises imaging-eligible acute ischemic stroke presenting 4.5–24 hours after last-known-well time with ICA or MCA M1/M2 occlusion.

#### Inclusion Criteria

- [Provided] Pre-stroke mRS 0–2.
- [Provided] NIHSS ≥5 at both baseline and immediately before randomization.
- [Provided] Qualifying CTA/MRA plus CTP/MRP target mismatch: core volume <70 mL, mismatch ratio ≥1.8, and mismatch volume ≥15 mL.
- [Provided] Capacity to comply.

#### Exclusion Criteria

- [Provided] Bleeding, coagulopathy, or anticoagulants.
- [Provided] Prior thrombolysis.
- [Provided] Relevant intracranial pathology.
- [Provided] Uncontrolled hypertension.
- [Provided] Glucose extremes.
- [Provided] Recent prior stroke or surgery.
- [Provided] Pregnancy.
- [Provided] Prohibitive imaging findings.

[Clarification Required] Specify operational definitions, thresholds, and look-back periods for the exclusion criteria, including anticoagulant exposure, blood pressure, glucose, recent stroke or surgery, intracranial pathology, and prohibitive imaging findings. These broad categories cannot be applied consistently without explicit rules.

[Clarification Required] Specify any age limits, pregnancy assessment requirements, imaging acquisition and processing standards, and interpretation of “capacity to comply.” These decisions determine eligibility; the age randomization strata do not establish age eligibility limits.

### 4.3 Recruitment

[Provided] Eligible, consented patients are randomized through IWRS and treated as soon as possible.

[Proposed] Integrate recruitment with emergency stroke assessment and transfer workflows without delaying indicated standard care. Rationale: treatment and transfer decisions are time-sensitive. Assumption: participating services can support parallel clinical and research activities.

[Clarification Required] Specify participating sites, recruitment duration, screening coverage, consent procedures for patients lacking decision-making capacity, and any permitted representative-consent arrangements. These decisions determine feasibility and lawful enrollment.

[Proposed] Obtain applicable ethics approvals and establish documented consent and privacy procedures before recruitment begins. Rationale: acute-stroke research requires appropriate participant protections. Assumption: the required approvals and operational resources are obtainable.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

[Proposed] Distinguish nonreceipt of study injection, withdrawal from study procedures, withdrawal of permission for further data collection, and loss to follow-up. Continue permitted outcome collection after nonreceipt of treatment or other deviations. Rationale: treatment exposure and follow-up participation are separate, and unnecessary outcome loss can bias results. Assumption: continued contact remains authorized.

[Proposed] Record dates and reasons for withdrawal and use documented contact attempts to obtain scheduled outcomes and vital status when permitted. Rationale: this supports transparent missing-data assessment. Assumption: approved contact methods are available.

[Clarification Required] Define permitted follow-up after consent withdrawal, acceptable external sources for vital status, and criteria for declaring loss to follow-up. These rules affect endpoint ascertainment and missing-data handling.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

[Proposed] Summarize age, the four randomization factors, pre-stroke mRS, baseline and immediately pre-randomization NIHSS, occlusion location, core and mismatch measurements, and relevant presentation, randomization, dosing, and transfer times. Include other collected demographic and clinical characteristics. Rationale: these describe prognosis and care pathways. Assumption: these variables are recorded with standardized definitions.

[Proposed] Present continuous variables using suitable distribution summaries and categorical variables using counts and percentages, with missing counts shown; do not use baseline significance tests to judge randomization success. Rationale: descriptive reporting is more informative than chance-driven baseline testing. Assumption: treatment assignment follows the randomized design.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

[Provided] The primary outcome is ordinal Day-90 mRS, with seven categories from 0 to 6, comparing tenecteplase with placebo. Lower mRS indicates a better outcome.

[Proposed] Target the effect of treatment assignment in the randomized population on Day-90 mRS, regardless of subsequent EVT, other care, or treatment nonreceipt. Rationale: this treatment-policy estimand addresses the overall randomized treatment strategy. Assumption: outcomes remain collectable after these events.

[Proposed] Represent death before the target Day-90 assessment time as mRS 6 rather than as missing functional outcome. Rationale: death is incorporated into the ordinal disability scale. Assumption: death dates and vital status are reliably ascertained and the target-time convention is finalized.

[Clarification Required] Define Day-90 mRS assessment procedures, assessor training and masking, acceptable interview modes, proxy assessments, and adjudication. Also reconcile the 61–120-day assessment window with deaths occurring around Day 90 or before a delayed assessment; otherwise disability and survival endpoints may use inconsistent time conventions.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

[Provided] Secondary efficacy outcomes are Day-90 functional independence, angiographic reperfusion, NIHSS, Barthel Index, Glasgow Outcome Scale, 24-hour reperfusion, and 24-hour recanalization. Secondary safety outcomes are sICH, adverse events, mortality, and PH2.

[Provided] Exploratory outcomes are final infarct volume/growth, mRS subgroup analyses, outcomes when thrombectomy is not performed, 30-day readmission, one-pass EVT, Neuro-QoL, and Day-90 survival.

[Clarification Required] Specify the mRS threshold defining functional independence, angiographic reperfusion scale and success threshold, and imaging definitions of 24-hour reperfusion and recanalization. These decisions determine binary endpoint classification and denominators.

[Clarification Required] Specify which visits and contrasts define NIHSS, Barthel Index, and Glasgow Outcome Scale endpoints, including whether analyses concern absolute scores, change, or categories. Assessment windows alone do not identify the intended statistical contrasts.

[Clarification Required] Define final infarct measurement timing and method, infarct-growth calculation, Neuro-QoL domains and scoring, readmission eligibility and qualifying causes, and the meaning and denominator of one-pass EVT. These choices are necessary for reproducible exploratory analyses.

[Clarification Required] Specify sICH and PH2 diagnostic criteria, ascertainment windows, adjudication rules, and the time horizon for the mortality endpoint. These definitions materially affect safety event rates.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

[Proposed] Analyze primary mRS using proportional-odds cumulative logistic regression adjusted for the four randomization factors. With tenecteplase coded as \(T=1\), use \(\operatorname{logit}\{P(Y\leq k)\}=\alpha_k+\beta T+\gamma^\top Z\), for \(k=0,\ldots,5\), and report \(\exp(\beta)\). Rationale: this directly estimates the stated favorable-outcome common odds ratio while accounting for stratification. Assumption: proportional odds and adequate model estimability are reasonable.

[Proposed] Present the full mRS distribution by treatment group alongside the model estimate. Rationale: the distribution reveals the pattern of disability outcomes that a single odds ratio may obscure. Assumption: category-level outcomes can be reported without compromising confidentiality.

[Proposed] Examine proportional-odds fit and provide threshold-specific treatment estimates and an unadjusted Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney sensitivity analysis without replacing the prespecified primary test based on diagnostics. Rationale: these analyses assess robustness and connect interpretation to the planning method. Assumption: sufficient observations support threshold-level summaries.

[Proposed] Prespecify an unadjusted proportional-odds fallback if the adjusted model remains nonestimable after documented numerical checks, and report the failure and fallback explicitly. Rationale: a deterministic fallback avoids outcome-driven model selection. Assumption: the unadjusted model is estimable.

[Proposed] Analyze binary efficacy outcomes using treatment comparisons that report event proportions, absolute differences, and odds ratios, with adjustment for randomization factors where estimable. Rationale: both absolute and relative effects aid clinical interpretation. Assumption: endpoint definitions and relevant denominators have been finalized.

[Proposed] Use ordinal models for ordered categorical scales and baseline-adjusted linear models for continuous outcomes where their interpretation is appropriate; supplement markedly skewed outcomes with prespecified rank-based summaries or analyses. Rationale: analysis should respect endpoint scale and distribution. Assumption: endpoint scoring and baseline counterparts are available where adjustment is intended.

[Proposed] Analyze Day-90 survival using Kaplan–Meier summaries and a treatment hazard ratio, censoring at the last known alive time or Day 90, whichever comes first. Assess proportional hazards before interpreting a single hazard ratio. Rationale: this uses available event timing. Assumption: death and follow-up dates are reliable and censoring is noninformative conditional on available information.

[Proposed] For 30-day readmission, report readmission and death separately and, if dates are available, estimate readmission cumulative incidence treating death as a competing event. Rationale: death prevents subsequent readmission and should not be treated as ordinary missing follow-up. Assumption: qualifying readmission and death dates are collected.

[Proposed] Do not adjust the primary treatment comparison for actual post-randomization EVT receipt. Rationale: EVT receipt may be affected by treatment and conditioning on it can distort the randomized comparison. Assumption: the primary objective concerns the overall treatment-assignment effect.

### 5.4 Missing Data

[Proposed] Describe missingness by treatment, endpoint, scheduled visit, and reason, distinguishing unperformed assessments, technically unevaluable imaging, withdrawal, and loss to follow-up. Rationale: different mechanisms require different interpretation. Assumption: reasons for unavailable data are recorded.

[Proposed] For missing primary mRS, use multiple imputation that preserves ordered categories and incorporates treatment, randomization factors, baseline prognostic measurements, and informative observed follow-up outcomes. Retain known deaths as observed mRS 6 under the approved timing convention. Rationale: this preserves the randomized analysis population without substituting death with imputed disability. Assumption: missingness is conditionally at random under the imputation model.

[Proposed] Conduct missing-not-at-random sensitivity analyses, including treatment-specific unfavorable shifts in imputed outcomes and a tipping-point assessment. Rationale: primary conclusions should be assessed against plausible departures from the missing-at-random assumption. Assumption: clinically interpretable shift ranges are prespecified before unblinding.

[Clarification Required] Specify the imputation implementation, number of imputations, pooling procedure, auxiliary variables, and sensitivity parameter ranges. These details affect reproducibility and the strength of missing-data assumptions.

[Proposed] Do not automatically classify unavailable imaging as failed reperfusion or carry the last observation forward. Report deaths and clinical instability that prevent imaging separately and apply endpoint-specific sensitivity analyses. Rationale: unavailable imaging may be informative without establishing the underlying imaging result. Assumption: reasons and timing of missing imaging are known.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

[Proposed] Explore mRS treatment effects within the four randomization factors using treatment-by-factor interactions and subgroup estimates with confidence intervals. Rationale: these factors are prospectively defined and potentially relevant to heterogeneity. Assumption: subgroup sizes support interpretable estimation; analyses are exploratory rather than separately powered tests.

[Clarification Required] Specify any additional mRS subgroups, their cut points, and interaction models before unblinding. The exploratory objective does not identify the complete subgroup list, and post hoc selection could exaggerate apparent heterogeneity.

[Proposed] Report outcomes among participants in whom thrombectomy was not performed and report one-pass EVT outcomes within the prespecified procedural denominator, with treatment-group counts and baseline descriptions. Rationale: these address supplied exploratory objectives while making selection visible. Assumption: procedure receipt and attempt details are recorded.

[Proposed] Interpret comparisons conditional on actual thrombectomy receipt or procedural success as descriptive, not randomized causal effects. Rationale: these are post-randomization selections. Assumption: no additional causal identification strategy has been prespecified.

[Proposed] Present supportive per-protocol, complete-case, and unadjusted primary analyses alongside—not instead of—the main analysis. Rationale: agreement or disagreement can illuminate sensitivity to analysis choices. Assumption: each population and method is fixed before unblinding.

### 5.6 Harms

[Provided] Safety assessment includes sICH, adverse events, mortality, and PH2.

[Proposed] Summarize participant-level event incidence, event counts, seriousness, severity, relationship to study treatment, and timing by actual treatment received; provide randomized-group summaries for major safety outcomes as supportive analyses. Rationale: exposure-based and assignment-based views answer complementary safety questions. Assumption: standardized event coding and exposure records are available.

[Proposed] Use injection through Day 90 as a provisional safety surveillance period and separately document relevant pre-injection events. Rationale: this aligns surveillance with the principal clinical follow-up horizon while distinguishing pre-exposure events. Assumption: this period is operationally feasible and accepted in the finalized safety plan.

[Clarification Required] Confirm safety surveillance start and end points, adverse-event coding conventions, severity and causality rules, reporting deadlines, and handling of events detected after scheduled follow-up. These decisions determine ascertainment completeness and reporting obligations.

[Proposed] Use blinded adjudication for sICH and PH2 where feasible, and permit emergency unblinding when knowledge of assignment is necessary for clinical management. Rationale: independent classification reduces bias while preserving patient safety. Assumption: timely adjudication and controlled emergency code access are available.

[Clarification Required] Specify individual-patient safety actions, aggregate safety monitoring rules, and escalation responsibilities. No numerical safety stopping boundaries are supplied, so none can be treated as established.

### 5.7 Software and References

[Provided] Sample-size calculations used EAST 6.4.1.

[Clarification Required] Select and document the statistical software, version, packages, and procedures for final analyses. These are not specified and are necessary to reproduce models, imputations, confidence intervals, and interim-adjusted inference.

[Proposed] Maintain version-controlled analysis programs, a data-definition specification, reproducible output generation, and independent checks of primary and major safety results. Rationale: these controls reduce programming errors and support auditability. Assumption: suitable statistical programming and validation resources are available.
