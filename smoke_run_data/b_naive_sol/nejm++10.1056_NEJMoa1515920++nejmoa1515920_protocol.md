# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1\. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** Empagliflozin (BI 10773) is being evaluated in this Phase III programme to establish long-term cardiovascular (CV) safety in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), insufficient glycaemic control, and increased CV risk. The study supports ongoing CV safety assessment and regulatory submission.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary:** Demonstrate non-inferiority of pooled empagliflozin 10 mg and 25 mg once daily versus placebo for time to first 3-point major adverse cardiovascular event (3P-MACE), using a hazard-ratio (HR) margin of 1.3.
- **[Provided]** **S1—Key secondary:** Assess the adjudicated 4-point composite of 3P-MACE plus hospitalisation for unstable angina within the confirmatory hierarchy.
- **[Provided]** **S2—Secondary:** Assess occurrence/time to silent myocardial infarction (MI), heart-failure hospitalisation, new albuminuria, new macroalbuminuria, new/worsening nephropathy, and the composite microvascular outcome.
- **[Provided]** **S3—Secondary:** Assess CV death, fatal/nonfatal MI and stroke, unstable-angina hospitalisation, all-cause mortality, transient ischaemic attack (TIA), and coronary revascularisation.
- **[Provided]** **S4—Secondary:** Assess changes from baseline in HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), weight, waist circumference, and blood pressure (BP); the HbA1c/systolic BP (SBP)/weight composite; and rescue-therapy use.
- **[Provided]** **S5—Exploratory:** Describe pharmacokinetic (PK) trough concentrations, adverse events (AEs), hypoglycaemia, adverse events of special interest (AESIs), laboratory data, electrocardiograms (ECGs), vital signs, and optional pharmacogenomics.
- **[Provided]** **S6—Exploratory:** Conduct TSAP/Publication-SAP further analyses of recurrent CV events, renal/microvascular endpoints, rescue therapy, risk factors, and correlations of SBP/HbA1c with urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR).

## 2\. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a Phase III, international, multicentre, randomised, parallel-group, double-blind, placebo-controlled, add-on-to-standard-care, event-driven CV-safety study.

**[Provided]** Screening is followed by a 2-week open-label placebo run-in, double-blind treatment until the required primary events, and 30-day follow-up. Planned treatment is approximately 6–8 years, up to approximately 420 weeks, conditional on accrual of the required events.

**[Provided]** Participants are allocated 1:1:1 to oral once-daily empagliflozin 10 mg, empagliflozin 25 mg, or matched placebo, in addition to background therapy.

**[Provided]** All patients undergo the 2-week open-label placebo run-in before randomisation. During the first 12 post-randomisation weeks, pre-consent background antidiabetic therapy should remain unchanged where possible; rescue is permitted by criteria. After week 12, background therapy may be adjusted for clinical care.

**[Clarification Required]** Rescue thresholds, permitted and contraindicated concomitant treatments, study-drug interruption or discontinuation criteria, and whether dose changes are permitted must be specified. These decisions determine safe treatment delivery and interpretation of adherence and efficacy.

**[Proposed]** Record study-drug dispensing, returns, interruptions, and background-treatment changes throughout follow-up. Rationale: these records support adherence assessment and interpretation of treatment exposure; assumption: reliable dispensing and concomitant-medication records can be maintained.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** A third-party telephone/web IVRS/IWRS assigns treatment using a computer-generated, pseudorandom, reproducible, non-predictable sequence. Randomisation is blocked and balanced within strata; no minimisation is reported.

**[Provided]** Randomisation uses the following stratification factors:

- **[Provided]** Screening HbA1c: <8.5% or ≥8.5%.
- **[Provided]** Randomisation BMI: <30 or ≥30 kg/m².
- **[Provided]** Region: North America, Latin America, Europe, Africa, or Asia.
- **[Provided]** Screening eGFR: ≥90, 60–89, or 30–59 mL/min.

**[Provided]** For the TSAP primary model, North America includes Australia and New Zealand. Analyses use actual case-report-form (CRF) information, with IXRS values only as a stated fallback for missing HbA1c/eGFR. Baseline-characteristic tables compare planned IVRS strata with actual CRF strata.

**[Proposed]** Restrict access to treatment codes and maintain an auditable emergency-unblinding process separate from routine study operations. Rationale: this protects the double-blind design while allowing urgent clinical decisions; assumption: the randomisation system supports controlled access and audit trails.

**[Clarification Required]** Sequence custody, block-size arrangements, dispensing implementation, emergency-unblinding responsibilities, and any permitted unblinded roles must be documented. These operational details are necessary to establish allocation concealment and maintain blinding.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** The planned sample size is 7000 patients: 2333 assigned to empagliflozin 10 mg, 2333 to empagliflozin 25 mg, and 2334 to placebo. Sample-size calculations pool the active arms, using a 2:1 active:placebo allocation.

**[Derived]** The planned pooled empagliflozin group therefore contains 4666 patients.

**[Provided]** The calculation concerns time to first 3P-MACE, assumes identical hazards for pooled empagliflozin and placebo under the non-inferiority planning scenario, and uses an HR non-inferiority margin of 1.3.

**[Provided]** Without interim adjustment, 690 primary events provide 90% non-inferiority power; the event table gives 516 and 690 events for 80% and 90% power, respectively. The interim-adjusted requirement is at least 691 adjudicated primary events.

**[Provided]** A separate superiority calculation states that 691 events provide at least 80% power to detect HR 0.785, corresponding to a 21.5% risk reduction.

**[Provided]** The calculation uses one-sided significance level .025, with .0001 spent at interim and .0249 remaining for final analysis. The assumed test is the log-rank test in EAST Software, version 5, stated to be equivalent to the Cox proportional-hazards score test.

**[Provided]** No fixed placebo event rate is used to determine the required event count. Duration scenarios use annual event rates of 1.0%, 1.5%, and 2.0%, with accrual over 2 or 2.5 years. Accrual of 7000 patients over 24 months at 3500 patients/year and a 1.5% annual event rate gives a duration just under 8 years.

**[Provided]** The required event count is independent of accrual, follow-up, and annual event rate; the number randomised and study duration depend on these quantities.

**[Provided]** No numeric dropout/non-adherence inflation factor is reported. A variability assumption is not applicable to this time-to-event event calculation, and a clustering/design effect is not applicable because randomisation is individual.

**[Provided]** Blinded review of the overall event rate may change the recruitment goal. Changes requiring additional recruitment or other alterations to the assumptions/accrual require a protocol amendment.

**[Provided]** The stopping basis is at least 691 adjudicated primary events, not 691 key-secondary events.

**[Clarification Required]** The clinical justification for selecting the non-inferiority margin of 1.3 is not supplied. It must be documented separately to support interpretation of the non-inferiority claim.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** Pooled empagliflozin versus placebo follows this fixed four-step hierarchy:

1. **[Provided]** Non-inferiority for primary 3P-MACE: H0: HR ≥1.3.
2. **[Provided]** Non-inferiority for key-secondary 4P-MACE: H0: HR ≥1.3.
3. **[Provided]** Superiority for primary 3P-MACE: H0: HR ≥1.
4. **[Provided]** Superiority for key-secondary 4P-MACE: H0: HR ≥1.

**[Provided]** Each hypothesis is specified for testing at one-sided alpha .025. Progression requires the preceding 95% confidence-interval (CI) upper bound to be strictly below 1.3 for non-inferiority or 1.0 for superiority.

**[Proposed]** Do not make confirmatory claims for hypotheses below a failed hierarchical step; report their estimates as descriptive. Rationale: this preserves the specified gatekeeping structure; assumption: no additional multiplicity procedure is subsequently authorised.

**[Clarification Required]** The relationship between the hierarchy’s nominal .025/95% CI rules and the final .0249 alpha allocation must be resolved before confirmatory testing. The two specifications are not numerically identical, and the operative boundaries affect statistical conclusions.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** The sample-size specification allocates .0001 alpha to an interim analysis and .0249 to the final analysis.

**[Clarification Required]** The formal interim timing, number of looks, information fraction, hypotheses evaluated, statistical boundaries, and resulting study actions are unspecified. These decisions are necessary to implement the stated alpha allocation.

**[Clarification Required]** Any interim futility or safety stopping rules, their binding status, and the membership, independence, access permissions, and charter of the reviewing body must be specified. No particular interim governance arrangement can be assumed.

**[Provided]** Blinded overall event-rate review may change the recruitment goal, with a protocol amendment required when assumptions/accrual necessitate additional recruitment or change.

**[Proposed]** Keep blinded event-rate reassessment operationally separate from any unblinded comparative interim analysis. Rationale: this limits disclosure of treatment effects and protects trial integrity; assumption: blinded aggregate data are sufficient for recruitment and duration reassessment.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** Final analysis occurs after at least 691 adjudicated primary events. Patients are followed to trial termination, and study end is last patient out.

**[Provided]** Post-randomisation visits occur at weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 28, 40, and 52, then every 14 weeks.

**[Provided]** HbA1c, FPG, weight, waist circumference, and BP are assessed at weeks 12 and 52, annually, and at end of study. Weight and waist are additionally assessed at 30-day follow-up.

**[Provided]** The final/end-of-study (EOS) visit includes drug collection/compliance, physical examination, laboratory assessment, and ECG.

**[Provided]** Visit 98 occurs 30 days after final/EOS and includes BP/pulse, weight/waist, blood/urine safety laboratory assessments, AEs/endpoints, and concomitant therapy.

**[Clarification Required]** The clinical data cutoff, event-eligibility cutoff, database-lock date, adjudication-completion requirements, and relationship between the event trigger, treatment termination, and last patient out are unspecified. They determine which observations enter final analyses.

**[Proposed]** Freeze the final analysis plan before comparative unblinding and lock the database after reconciliation of eligible endpoint adjudications, deaths, treatment exposure, and critical queries. Rationale: this limits analysis-driven decisions and ensures consistent datasets; assumption: the required reconciliation can be completed before final analysis.

## 3\. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The confirmatory hierarchy specifies one-sided alpha .025 and decisions based on 95% CI upper bounds below 1.3 or 1.0, as applicable. The sample-size specification separately reserves .0249 for final analysis after .0001 is spent at interim.

**[Clarification Required]** A single operational sequential-testing specification must reconcile the nominal hierarchy, interim expenditure, final alpha, and CI confidence levels. This includes whether confirmatory CIs are sequentially adjusted and how they control progression through the hierarchy.

**[Proposed]** Report two-sided 95% CIs for effect-size estimation, clearly distinguishing descriptive intervals from any adjusted intervals required for confirmatory decisions. Rationale: this supports interpretable estimation without obscuring the unresolved sequential-testing requirement; assumption: descriptive and confirmatory results can be separately identified.

**[Proposed]** Treat outcomes outside the four-step hierarchy, dose-specific comparisons, subgroup analyses, and further exploratory analyses as non-confirmatory unless a prospective multiplicity strategy is added. Rationale: no confirmatory error-control procedure is supplied for these analyses; assumption: the stated hierarchy is the complete supplied confirmatory family.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Classify deviations before comparative unblinding, including eligibility violations, treatment-assignment or dispensing errors, prohibited concomitant treatment, non-adherence, and major endpoint-follow-up failures. Rationale: prospective classification reduces outcome-driven exclusions; assumption: deviations can be identified from auditable records.

**[Proposed]** Retain randomised participants in the primary randomised analysis despite deviations, while evaluating important deviations in supportive analyses. Rationale: this preserves randomisation and reveals sensitivity to conduct problems; assumption: continued use of their data is permitted.

**[Clarification Required]** Definitions of major deviations, adherence thresholds, visit windows, and per-protocol exclusions must be approved before unblinding. They materially affect supportive non-inferiority analyses.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Proposed]** Define the primary full analysis population as all randomised participants, analysed according to assigned treatment, with the two empagliflozin groups pooled for confirmatory comparisons. Rationale: this preserves randomisation; assumption: endpoint follow-up and permission to analyse data are available.

**[Proposed]** Define a supportive per-protocol population using prospectively specified major-deviation and adherence rules. Rationale: non-adherence and treatment contamination can obscure differences in a non-inferiority study; assumption: exclusions can be determined without knowledge of comparative outcomes.

**[Proposed]** Define the safety population as participants receiving at least one dose of double-blind study medication, analysed by actual exposure. Rationale: harms should be linked to medication received; assumption: dose and exposure records permit classification.

**[Proposed]** Define PK and pharmacogenomic populations by evaluable samples, relevant exposure information, and applicable consent. Rationale: these analyses require usable specimens and permission; assumption: sample availability and consent status are recorded.

**[Clarification Required]** Rules for participants exposed to more than one study treatment, participants randomised but never dosed, and participants with unavailable consent or endpoint data must be finalised. These rules affect population counts and consistency across analyses.

## 4\. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Maintain a screening and disposition log recording assessment, run-in entry and completion, eligibility, randomisation, and reasons for exclusion or non-randomisation. Rationale: this permits transparent accounting of participant selection; assumption: collection complies with consent and privacy requirements.

**[Proposed]** Summarise participant flow by treatment after randomisation and summarise screening/run-in failures separately. Rationale: run-in exclusions occur before treatment assignment and should not be attributed to a randomised group; assumption: disposition categories can be reconciled.

**[Clarification Required]** The complete screening assessment schedule, acceptable assessment windows, and run-in completion/adherence criteria are not supplied. These are needed to operationalise eligibility and randomisation.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** T2DM before consent, with diet/exercise.
- **[Provided]** Drug-naïve status with no antidiabetic treatment for 12 weeks, or stable background therapy, including insulin dose within 10% for 12 weeks.
- **[Provided]** Screening HbA1c 7.0%–10.0%, or 7.0%–9.0% if drug-naïve.
- **[Provided]** Age ≥18 years generally, ≥20 years in Japan, and 18–65 years in India.
- **[Provided]** BMI ≤45 kg/m².
- **[Provided]** Consent.
- **[Provided]** Qualifying high CV risk: prior MI; specified multivessel/single-vessel coronary artery disease (CAD) or unstable angina; prior stroke; or peripheral arterial disease (PAD).
- **[Provided]** Acceptable contraception and periodic pregnancy testing for women of child-bearing potential.

**[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** Run-in fasting glucose >240 mg/dL, confirmed on a second day.
- **[Provided]** Liver enzymes/alkaline phosphatase >3× the upper limit of normal.
- **[Provided]** Planned cardiac procedure within 3 months.
- **[Provided]** eGFR <30.
- **[Provided]** Malabsorption surgery.
- **[Provided]** Dyscrasia/haemolysis.
- **[Provided]** Cancer within 5 years, except basal-cell carcinoma.
- **[Provided]** Contraindicated background therapy.
- **[Provided]** Unstable weight intervention.
- **[Provided]** Systemic steroids/uncontrolled endocrinopathy.
- **[Provided]** Pregnancy/nursing or unsuitable contraception.
- **[Provided]** Alcohol/drug abuse or poor compliance.
- **[Provided]** Recent/ongoing investigational trial.
- **[Provided]** Unsafe condition.
- **[Provided]** Acute coronary syndrome, stroke, or TIA within 2 months.

**[Provided]** Canada and South Africa have stated local exclusions, but their details are not specified.

**[Clarification Required]** Detailed qualifying CAD, unstable-angina, and PAD definitions; contraindicated treatments; investigational-trial washouts; and the Canadian and South African exclusions must be supplied. Without these, eligibility cannot be applied consistently.

**[Clarification Required]** Acceptable contraceptive methods, pregnancy-testing frequency, laboratory confirmation rules, and operational definitions of the broader exclusions must be specified. These affect participant protection and reproducibility of screening decisions.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** Recruitment is international and multicentre, with a planned total of 7000 participants. The principal duration scenario assumes 24-month accrual at 3500 participants/year.

**[Clarification Required]** Participating countries/sites, recruitment start and end dates, regional targets, and site-specific arrangements are not supplied. These affect feasibility, local implementation, and interpretation of recruitment assumptions.

**[Proposed]** Obtain applicable ethics approvals and consent before study-specific procedures, and monitor enrolment and run-in losses against the accrual assumptions. Rationale: this protects participants and identifies recruitment shortfalls; assumption: local approval and monitoring processes are available.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Provided]** Patients are followed to trial termination. The scheduled post-EOS follow-up occurs 30 days after final/EOS at Visit 98.

**[Proposed]** Distinguish study-drug discontinuation from withdrawal of study participation or permission for further data collection, and continue endpoint and vital-status follow-up after drug discontinuation when permitted. Rationale: stopping medication need not terminate outcome ascertainment; assumption: participants consent to continued follow-up.

**[Proposed]** Record dates and reasons for treatment discontinuation, withdrawal, loss to follow-up, and last successful endpoint assessment. Rationale: these data support disposition reporting and censoring decisions; assumption: reasons can be collected without overriding participant choices.

**[Clarification Required]** Mandatory discontinuation criteria, procedures after pregnancy or major clinical events, permissible remote or registry follow-up, and handling of consent withdrawal must be defined. These determine participant management and the completeness of long-term outcome collection.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Provided]** Baseline-characteristic tables compare planned IVRS strata with actual CRF strata. Analyses use actual CRF information, with IXRS fallback only as stated for missing HbA1c/eGFR.

**[Proposed]** Summarise baseline characteristics for each randomised group and pooled empagliflozin using counts/percentages and suitable continuous summaries. Include demographics, qualifying CV disease, diabetes history, background treatments, glycaemic measures, renal function, BMI, BP, and other collected risk factors. Rationale: these describe the study population and balance; assumption: the listed characteristics are collected.

**[Proposed]** Use descriptive baseline comparisons without significance testing. Rationale: randomisation, rather than baseline hypothesis tests, establishes the assignment mechanism; assumption: no separate baseline testing requirement exists.

**[Clarification Required]** Baseline selection rules and assessment windows must be specified, while retaining screening HbA1c/eGFR and randomisation BMI where required. They determine change-from-baseline values and model covariates.

## 5\. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is time to first 3P-MACE, comparing pooled empagliflozin 10 mg and 25 mg once daily with placebo. The non-inferiority HR margin is 1.3, and the final event requirement is at least 691 adjudicated primary events.

**[Clarification Required]** Exact 3P-MACE components, component definitions, event-date conventions, adjudication procedures, and rules for simultaneous or uncertain events must be specified. The composite name alone does not establish an executable endpoint definition.

**[Proposed]** Define the primary estimand around randomised assignment to pooled empagliflozin versus placebo, with time measured from randomisation to first qualifying event and follow-up retained after rescue therapy, background-treatment changes, or study-drug discontinuation. Rationale: this estimates the effect of assignment to an add-on treatment strategy; assumption: post-discontinuation and post-rescue events remain ascertainable.

**[Proposed]** Include all qualifying events through the approved analysis cutoff rather than truncating the dataset at the individual event that satisfies the minimum count. Rationale: this applies a common cutoff without selectively discarding eligible information; assumption: the final cutoff and adjudication rules are prospectively fixed.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The key secondary outcome is the adjudicated 4-point composite of 3P-MACE plus hospitalisation for unstable angina. It participates in both the non-inferiority and superiority stages of the hierarchy.

**[Provided]** Other secondary event outcomes include silent MI, heart-failure hospitalisation, new albuminuria, new macroalbuminuria, new/worsening nephropathy, the composite microvascular outcome, CV death, fatal/nonfatal MI and stroke, unstable-angina hospitalisation, all-cause mortality, TIA, and coronary revascularisation.

**[Provided]** Diabetes-related secondary outcomes include changes from baseline in HbA1c, FPG, weight, waist circumference, and BP; the HbA1c/SBP/weight composite; and rescue-therapy use.

**[Clarification Required]** Definitions and adjudication requirements for secondary events, albuminuria and nephropathy thresholds, confirmation requirements, and microvascular-composite components must be supplied. These determine event eligibility and reproducible analyses.

**[Clarification Required]** The HbA1c/SBP/weight composite thresholds and logical combination, rescue criteria, endpoint-specific assessment horizons, and whether each outcome is analysed as occurrence, time to event, or both must be specified. These choices cannot be recovered from the outcome names.

**[Clarification Required]** Baseline disease exclusions or risk-set restrictions for outcomes described as “new” must be defined. They determine which participants can experience an incident renal or microvascular outcome.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** Estimate the pooled empagliflozin-versus-placebo HR using a Cox proportional-hazards model with treatment and categorical adjustment for the supplied HbA1c, BMI, region, and eGFR factors. Rationale: this provides the required HR while accounting for randomisation factors; assumption: covariate adjustment, rather than a fully stratified baseline hazard, is acceptable and estimable.

**[Provided]** Actual CRF information is used for analysis; IXRS values are a stated fallback only for missing HbA1c/eGFR. North America includes Australia and New Zealand in the TSAP primary model.

**[Clarification Required]** The definitive Cox-model specification, including adjustment versus stratification, tie handling, inferential test, and any fallback for sparse categories, must be confirmed. The supplied sample-size test does not fully specify the final analysis model.

**[Proposed]** Apply the same prespecified modelling approach to the key secondary composite and implement the fixed hierarchy using the approved sequential-testing rules. Rationale: consistent methods facilitate interpretation of the two composites; assumption: both endpoints support the selected model.

**[Proposed]** Report participants with events, follow-up summaries, HRs, CIs, and event-free curves by treatment. For endpoints with competing deaths, additionally report cumulative incidence using a competing-risk method. Rationale: hazard contrasts and absolute event probabilities answer different questions; assumption: event and death dates are sufficiently complete.

**[Proposed]** For a cause-specific HR analysis, treat death that is not part of the endpoint as a competing event and end that participant’s event-free risk time at death. Rationale: participants cannot subsequently experience the nonfatal endpoint; assumption: cause-specific hazards are the intended primary contrast rather than a subdistribution-hazard estimand.

**[Proposed]** Assess proportional hazards using residual-based diagnostics and time-pattern displays, with non-proportional-hazards analyses treated as supportive rather than post hoc replacements for the confirmatory model. Rationale: this evaluates interpretability of a single HR without changing the primary test after seeing results; assumption: sufficient events support diagnostics.

**[Proposed]** Analyse other time-to-first-event secondary outcomes using corresponding Cox models and descriptive CIs. Rationale: this provides consistent treatment contrasts for event-time outcomes; assumption: outcome definitions, competing-event handling, and risk sets have been finalised.

**[Proposed]** Analyse repeated continuous efficacy measures with a baseline-adjusted longitudinal model containing treatment, visit, and treatment-by-visit effects, with within-participant correlation accounted for. Rationale: this uses repeated measurements and permits visit-specific contrasts; assumption: the measurement scale and selected covariance model are suitable.

**[Clarification Required]** The longitudinal covariance structure, visit windows, principal efficacy contrast, and estimand for measurements after rescue, discontinuation, or death must be fixed before analysis. These choices materially affect the interpretation of change from baseline.

**[Proposed]** Summarise binary composites and rescue-therapy occurrence using treatment-specific proportions and between-group contrasts at common prespecified horizons; analyse time to first rescue separately when defined. Rationale: common horizons avoid misleading comparisons under unequal follow-up; assumption: the required thresholds and horizons are approved.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Do not equate an absent endpoint report with confirmed event-free follow-up. Censor event-free participants at the last date on which endpoint status is adequately established, subject to the administrative cutoff. Rationale: this avoids overstating observed risk time; assumption: last ascertainment dates are reliably recorded.

**[Proposed]** Report missingness and reasons by treatment, visit, and outcome, including missing vital status and incomplete endpoint adjudication. Rationale: differential missingness can affect both non-inferiority and superiority conclusions; assumption: reasons and assessment status are available.

**[Provided]** Missing analysis HbA1c/eGFR may use the stated IXRS fallback; actual CRF information otherwise takes precedence.

**[Clarification Required]** Missing-data rules for BMI, region, other model covariates, partially dated events, and unresolved adjudications are not supplied. These decisions affect inclusion, event ordering, and model estimation.

**[Proposed]** Where appropriate, use a likelihood-based longitudinal analysis under missing at random, with multiple imputation for compatible missing covariates or outcomes when required. Rationale: these methods can use observed information without single-value substitution; assumption: the missing-at-random model is plausible and includes relevant predictors.

**[Proposed]** Assess informative loss to follow-up using prespecified sensitivity analyses, such as censoring-weighted event analyses and departures-from-missing-at-random analyses for continuous outcomes. Rationale: the main analyses depend on assumptions about unobserved data; assumption: collected predictors support the sensitivity models.

**[Proposed]** Do not impute post-death physiological measurements as though participants remained alive. Rationale: death is an intercurrent event rather than ordinary measurement missingness; assumption: the longitudinal estimand will specify how death is addressed.

**[Clarification Required]** Sensitivity-analysis scenarios, parameter ranges, and treatment of unavailable post-rescue or post-discontinuation measurements must be prespecified. These determine which departures from the main assumptions are meaningfully assessed.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Provided]** Further TSAP/Publication-SAP analyses include recurrent CV events, renal/microvascular endpoints, rescue therapy, risk factors, and correlations of SBP/HbA1c with UACR.

**[Proposed]** Present individual-dose estimates alongside the pooled comparison as exploratory analyses. Rationale: pooling addresses the confirmatory objective but does not describe dose-specific patterns; assumption: these comparisons are not assigned confirmatory alpha.

**[Proposed]** Assess treatment-effect heterogeneity across the supplied HbA1c, BMI, region, and eGFR categories using interaction terms and subgroup estimates. Rationale: these prespecified design factors offer interpretable exploratory subgroup analyses; assumption: event counts permit estimation without excessive instability.

**[Proposed]** Analyse recurrent CV-event burden using a method that accounts for multiple events per participant, with explicit assessment of terminal death and follow-up duration. Rationale: first-event analysis does not capture total event burden; assumption: repeat events and their dates are comprehensively collected.

**[Clarification Required]** Recurrent-event definitions, separation rules for distinct events, terminal-event handling, and the recurrent-event estimand/model must be selected prospectively. Different choices estimate different aspects of disease burden.

**[Proposed]** Treat risk-factor analyses and SBP/HbA1c–UACR associations as exploratory, accounting for repeated measurements where applicable and avoiding causal interpretation. Rationale: these associations are not protected by randomisation of the risk factors themselves; assumption: paired measurements and relevant covariates are available.

**[Provided]** PK trough concentrations and optional pharmacogenomics are exploratory outcomes.

**[Proposed]** Summarise evaluable trough concentrations by assigned dose and sampling visit, with variability and below-quantification results explicitly described. Rationale: descriptive summaries characterise exposure without assuming an unprovided PK model; assumption: sampling times, dose histories, and assay limits are recorded.

**[Clarification Required]** PK sampling schedules, acceptable trough timing, assay handling rules, and pharmacogenomic consent, analytes, quality-control procedures, and analysis plans are unspecified. These determine sample evaluability and the validity of exploratory findings.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Provided]** Safety assessments include AEs, hypoglycaemia, AESIs, laboratory data, ECGs, and vital signs. EOS includes laboratory assessment and ECG; the 30-day follow-up includes blood/urine safety laboratory assessments, BP/pulse, and AEs.

**[Proposed]** Summarise safety by actual treatment exposure for each dose, pooled empagliflozin, and placebo, including participants with events, serious events, discontinuations due to events, and deaths. Rationale: these summaries describe the frequency and clinical importance of harms; assumption: event seriousness, discontinuation reasons, and exposure are collected.

**[Clarification Required]** The treatment-emergent safety window and its relationship to last dose, EOS, and the 30-day visit must be defined. The supplied visit schedule does not itself determine which events enter on-treatment safety summaries.

**[Clarification Required]** AE coding conventions, seriousness and severity rules, relatedness assessment, hypoglycaemia definitions, and the AESI list are not supplied. These are necessary for reproducible classification and reporting.

**[Proposed]** Summarise laboratory and vital-sign changes and categorical shifts using prospectively defined clinical thresholds, with ECG abnormalities described by treatment. Rationale: changes and threshold crossings provide complementary safety information; assumption: reference ranges, thresholds, and assessment windows are agreed before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** Report participant incidence and, where follow-up differs materially, exposure-adjusted event rates, clearly distinguishing participants from event counts. Rationale: different denominators answer different safety questions; assumption: exposure duration is available and the chosen risk window is consistently applied.

**[Clarification Required]** Safety reporting responsibilities, escalation timelines, laboratory alert thresholds, and management procedures for important abnormalities must be specified. These are operational participant-protection requirements, not inferable from the endpoint list.
