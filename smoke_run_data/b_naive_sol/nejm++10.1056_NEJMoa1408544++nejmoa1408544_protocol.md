# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** This phase 4 trial evaluates 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (13vPnC) for prevention of vaccine-serotype pneumococcal community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) in older adults in the Netherlands.

**[Provided]** The controlling protocol is Amendment 9, dated 01 October 2013.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary:** Demonstrate efficacy of 13vPnC against a first episode of confirmed vaccine-type (VT) pneumococcal CAP. The primary outcome is the first episode of confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP.

- **[Provided]** **S1—Secondary:** Demonstrate efficacy against first confirmed nonbacteremic/noninvasive (NB/NI)-VT pneumococcal CAP and first VT-IPD. These are the two secondary efficacy outcomes.

- **[Provided]** **S2—Exploratory:** Evaluate all confirmed VT-CAP; first confirmed pneumococcal CAP, NB/NI pneumococcal CAP, CAP, and IPD; first/all confirmed VT-CAP by individual 13vPnC serotype; first/all non-vaccine-type (NVT) and pneumococcal CAP; first/all NB/NI-VT-CAP and NB/NI-VT-CAP by individual serotype; first/all culture-confirmed VT, pneumococcal, and NVT CAP; and first/all probable VT, pneumococcal, NVT, and possible pneumococcal CAP. The corresponding events constitute the S2 outcomes.

- **[Provided]** **S3—Exploratory:** Evaluate all CAP; all VT-IPD, first/all NVT-IPD, and all IPD; deaths from confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP/VT-IPD, pneumococcal CAP/IPD, and all causes; hospitalization with CAP plus influenza A/B and specified respiratory viruses; age efficacy trends; and economic effect. These constitute the S3 outcomes.

- **[Provided]** **S4—Exploratory carriage:** Evaluate VT, all, NVT, and individual-serotype *S. pneumoniae* carriage, together with *H. influenzae*, *M. catarrhalis*, and *S. aureus* carriage, at months 6, 12, and 24.

- **[Provided]** **S4—Safety:** Evaluate serious adverse event (SAE) incidence, subset reactogenicity and adverse events (AEs), and deaths.

- **[Provided]** **S4—Immunogenicity:** Evaluate serotype-specific IgG and opsonophagocytic activity (OPA) by age and sex at baseline and approximately months 1, 12, and 24.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a phase 4, parallel-group, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial conducted at multiple sites/regions in the Netherlands by the Julius Center. Allocation is 1:1 to 13vPnC or placebo.

**[Provided]** Each participant receives one 0.5-mL intramuscular injection into the deltoid at Visit 1.

- **[Provided]** **13vPnC:** Serotypes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, and 23F conjugated to CRM197; 2 micrograms of each serotype except 6B, which contains 4 micrograms; pH 5.8; 5 mM succinate; 0.85% sodium chloride; 0.02% polysorbate 80; and 0.125 mg aluminum phosphate.

- **[Provided]** **Placebo:** pH 5.8; 5 mM succinate; 0.15 M sodium chloride; 0.02% polysorbate 80; and 0.125 mg aluminum per 0.5 mL.

**[Proposed]** Maintain restricted access to treatment codes, document syringe accountability, and establish controlled emergency unblinding. Rationale: these measures protect masking and participant safety. Assumption: coded product handling can conceal allocation during routine trial operations.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify which participant-facing, clinical, laboratory, adjudication, and analysis personnel are blinded, how syringe appearance and PAC information preserve concealment, and who may authorize emergency unblinding. These details determine whether the stated double-blind design is operationally maintained.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** The sponsor or designee supplies the randomization schedule. Eligible, consented subjects receive the next available syringe, whose PAC identifies allocation. A randomized block design places vaccine groups in random order within blocks; several blocks are assigned at a time.

**[Provided]** Age stratification applies to the immunogenicity subset: 65–69, 70–79, and ≥80 years, with at least 300 participants in each stratum.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify block sizes, any variation in block size, block assignment across sites/regions, and safeguards against predicting allocation. These details are necessary to assess and reproduce allocation concealment.

**[Clarification Required]** Trial-wide randomization stratification is not specified. Confirm whether any strata beyond the immunogenicity subset apply, and whether the minimum of 300 in each immunogenicity age stratum applies overall or separately within each treatment group; these decisions affect allocation checks and adjusted analyses.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** Planned enrollment is approximately 85,000 participants, with 42,500 per treatment group.

- **[Provided]** The immunogenicity subset comprises 2,000 participants, with 1,000 per group.

- **[Provided]** The principal calculation concerns first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP. The outcome is a time-to-first confirmed clinical event analyzed using incidence rate/relative risk.

- **[Provided]** Assumed placebo rates are 10 CAP events, 2.5 confirmed pneumococcal CAP events, and 1.2 confirmed VT-CAP events per 1,000 participants per year. The control-arm VT-CAP incidence assumption is 1.2 per 1,000 per year.

- **[Provided]** Assumed true VT-CAP efficacy is at least 52%; the revised model uses specificity-adjusted efficacy of 48.4%.

- **[Provided]** Initial diagnostic specificity is assumed to be at least 97%; SSUAD was subsequently validated at 98% specificity.

- **[Provided]** The design uses two-sided experiment-wise alpha 0.05 and 90% power for primary VT-CAP efficacy.

- **[Provided]** Supplementary power is at least 80% for NB/NI-VT-CAP under the stated assumptions and at least 90% for VT-IPD if true efficacy is at least 90%. The stated VT-IPD power requires 20 events.

- **[Provided]** The IgG subset precision is 0.10–0.16 for differences expressed with 95% confidence intervals.

- **[Provided]** The revised primary event target uses a Breslow–Day conditional binomial derivation. Analysis uses exact conditional confidence intervals with O’Brien–Fleming adjustment.

- **[Provided]** The original target was 156 primary events. Revised targets are 65 interim and 130 final first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events. Revision followed a data monitoring committee (DMC) administrative review after observed incidence was lower than predicted.

- **[Provided]** The assumed loss to follow-up is 5%. No clustering/design effect applies because participants are individually randomized in a non-clustered design. Non-inferiority and equivalence margins are not applicable.

- **[Provided]** Planning assumptions include clinical feasibility, one enrollment season, at least 2 years of follow-up, incidence estimates, assay specificity, DMC review, and a consultant-supported revised model.

- **[Provided]** Approximately 85,000 participants were selected to accrue the required primary events. The 2,000-person subset was based on six-month safety regulatory requirements and feasibility.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the scale and contrast underlying IgG precision of 0.10–0.16, and the complete numerical inputs for the supplementary NB/NI-VT-CAP power calculation. Without these details, those calculations cannot be reproduced.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** Primary vaccine efficacy is \(VE=1-RR\), where \(RR\) is the relative risk for first confirmed VT-CAP. Primary efficacy succeeds when the adjusted two-sided 95% confidence interval lower bound for VE exceeds 0.

**[Derived]** The corresponding superiority hypotheses are \(H_0:VE\leq0\), equivalent to \(RR\geq1\), versus \(H_1:VE>0\), equivalent to \(RR<1\). If the confidence interval for RR is \([L_{RR},U_{RR}]\), the corresponding VE interval is \([1-U_{RR},1-L_{RR}]\).

**[Provided]** NB/NI-VT-CAP is tested hierarchically only after clinically significant primary efficacy.

**[Provided]** VT-IPD is evaluated once at study conclusion and is efficacious if its two-sided 95% confidence interval for VE is above 0.

**[Provided]** The interim clinical-significance criterion is a VT-CAP VE lower confidence bound greater than 0.20.

**[Clarification Required]** Define “clinically significant primary efficacy” for opening the NB/NI-VT-CAP hierarchy at each analysis, particularly at the final analysis. The supplied final primary success criterion exceeds 0, whereas the supplied interim clinical-significance criterion exceeds 0.20; these thresholds must not be treated as interchangeable.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** The revised interim target is 65 first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP events. The primary sequential analysis uses exact conditional confidence intervals with O’Brien–Fleming adjustment.

**[Provided]** At interim, the clinical-significance threshold requires the VT-CAP VE lower confidence bound to exceed 0.20.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the exact O’Brien–Fleming implementation, information fractions, nominal significance boundaries, adjusted confidence interval construction, and the complete interim efficacy-declaration rule. The event targets and clinical-significance threshold alone do not fully determine the sequential testing procedure.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify whether and how NB/NI-VT-CAP is tested at interim, how its repeated testing is controlled, and what data are available to the DMC. These decisions affect both the testing hierarchy and operational integrity.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify any futility or safety stopping rules, additional authorized looks, and the DMC’s decision authority. Their absence from the supplied details must not be interpreted as confirmation that no such rules exist.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** If efficacy is not declared at interim, final efficacy inference occurs after investigator sign-off of at least 130 per-protocol first confirmed VT-CAP events and completion of cleaning, unblinding, and database freeze.

**[Provided]** If efficacy is declared at interim, the interim dataset supplies the efficacy inference; overrunning data are supplementary.

**[Provided]** CAP/IPD surveillance continues through the case-acquisition stopping rule.

**[Provided]** Immunogenicity blood collection occurs at baseline and months 1, 12, and 24. Carriage assessments occur at baseline and months 6, 12, and 24.

**[Provided]** Reactogenicity is assessed over 7 days and unsolicited AEs over 28 days.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the operational case-acquisition stopping rule, including treatment of events occurring before the cutoff but confirmed afterward, event-date conventions, and surveillance completion. These details determine which events enter each analysis.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify visit windows, the time origin for scheduled assessments, and SAE ascertainment windows. The six-month safety rationale for subset size does not establish an SAE collection window.

**[Proposed]** Finalize the statistical analysis plan and programming specifications before access to comparative unblinded results, and retain versioned interim, final, and overrunning datasets. Rationale: this limits data-driven changes and preserves reproducibility. Assumption: analysis governance permits prospective specification before the relevant unblinding.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The experiment-wise significance level is two-sided 0.05. Primary efficacy inference uses an O’Brien–Fleming-adjusted two-sided 95% confidence interval and requires its VE lower bound to exceed 0.

**[Provided]** VT-IPD uses a two-sided 95% confidence interval at study conclusion.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify how the primary sequential procedure, hierarchical NB/NI-VT-CAP testing, and final VT-IPD test jointly maintain the stated experiment-wise alpha. The supplied information does not establish the complete multiplicity allocation or testing order.

**[Proposed]** Present exploratory efficacy, carriage, immunogenicity, and safety comparisons with descriptive two-sided 95% confidence intervals, without claiming multiplicity-controlled success. Rationale: numerous exploratory comparisons otherwise invite false-positive interpretation. Assumption: no additional confirmatory claims are intended for these endpoints.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Maintain a blinded deviation log covering eligibility, allocation, product administration, specimen timing and handling, outcome ascertainment, masking, and follow-up. Rationale: consistent documentation supports transparent population definitions and sensitivity analyses. Assumption: relevant deviations can be identified without comparative unblinded results.

**[Clarification Required]** Define major deviations and their endpoint-specific consequences before analysis. In particular, specify which deviations exclude participants or observation periods from per-protocol efficacy and immunogenicity analyses; these exclusions directly affect the primary result.

**[Proposed]** Summarize deviations by randomized group, report reasons for analysis exclusions, and compare primary per-protocol results with a randomized-population analysis. Rationale: this evaluates selection introduced by exclusions. Assumption: outcome ascertainment remains sufficiently available outside the per-protocol population.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Provided]** The primary event target and primary sample-size calculation concern first confirmed per-protocol VT-CAP.

**[Clarification Required]** Define the per-protocol efficacy population, qualifying follow-up periods, any postvaccination immune-response interval, and handling of newly arising exclusion conditions. These rules are essential to determine eligible events and person-time.

**[Proposed]** Define a supportive randomized full-analysis population containing all randomized participants, analyzed as assigned regardless of treatment receipt or deviations. Rationale: this preserves the protection of randomization. Assumption: permitted outcome follow-up is available for participants who do not receive the assigned dose.

**[Proposed]** Define the safety population as all participants receiving study injection, classified by the product actually received. Rationale: harms should be linked to actual exposure. Assumption: administration and product identity are reliably recorded.

**[Proposed]** Describe all designated immunogenicity and carriage subset participants, with endpoint analyses identifying those contributing valid samples at each visit. Rationale: this makes selection and changing denominators visible. Assumption: subset membership and specimen validity are recorded independently of treatment comparisons.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify subset selection, any immunogenicity/carriage per-protocol definitions, and overlap among immunogenicity, carriage, and reactogenicity/AE subsets. These relationships cannot be inferred solely from the 2,000-person immunogenicity sample size.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Maintain a screening log containing eligibility assessment, consent status, enrollment deferrals, exclusion reasons, randomization, and vaccination status. Report participant flow by group after randomization. Rationale: this documents selection and attrition. Assumption: screening information may be collected under applicable consent and privacy arrangements.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify whether pre-consent screening data and counts of individuals approached are available. These determine the completeness of recruitment and screening summaries.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are age ≥65 years at vaccination, registration with a referring general practitioner (GP), and ability to meet study requirements. Subset subjects must complete e-diary procedures.

**[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are prior pneumococcal vaccination; institutional/semiskilled care; vaccine contraindication; recent investigational product; severe vaccine reaction; and defined immune deficiency/suppression.

**[Provided]** For subset subjects, influenza vaccination during the preceding 7 days is exclusionary.

**[Provided]** Acute febrile illness with temperature ≥38.0°C or recent acute illness delays enrollment.

**[Provided]** Randomization is restricted to eligible, consented subjects.

**[Clarification Required]** Supply operational definitions for institutional/semiskilled care, vaccine contraindication, severe vaccine reaction, immune deficiency/suppression, “recent” investigational product, and “recent” acute illness. Specify when deferred participants may be reassessed; these definitions control consistent enrollment.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** Recruitment is conducted across multiple sites/regions in the Netherlands. One enrollment season is a sample-size planning assumption.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify actual recruitment dates, participating sites/regions, recruitment channels, and subset selection procedures. These are needed to assess enrollment feasibility and representativeness.

**[Proposed]** Coordinate eligibility confirmation and consent with referring GPs and apply consistent recruitment procedures across sites. Rationale: GP registration is required and standardized procedures reduce selection variability. Assumption: participating GPs can support recruitment within applicable permissions.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Proposed]** Distinguish withdrawal from study procedures, withdrawal from active contact, and withdrawal of permission for further data collection. Record dates and reasons separately. Rationale: these states have different implications for endpoint ascertainment and censoring. Assumption: consent permits these distinctions.

**[Proposed]** Continue endpoint and safety follow-up after procedural discontinuation when the participant permits it, without conditioning follow-up on treatment adherence. Rationale: continued observation reduces informative loss to follow-up. Assumption: follow-up remains ethically and operationally feasible.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify permitted record linkage, death ascertainment, handling of relocation, retention of previously collected data, and any participant replacement policy. These decisions affect denominators, missingness, and analysis validity.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Provided]** Participants are at least 65 years old at vaccination. Baseline assessments include immunogenicity blood sampling and carriage assessment in the relevant subsets.

**[Proposed]** Summarize age, sex, site/region, and available disease-risk characteristics by randomized group, separately describing relevant subsets. Use counts and percentages for categorical variables and suitable distribution summaries for continuous variables. Rationale: these summaries describe balance and applicability. Assumption: the listed variables are collected; uncollected characteristics will not be represented as available.

**[Proposed]** Present baseline characteristics descriptively without significance testing. Rationale: randomization, rather than baseline hypothesis tests, determines the interpretation of chance imbalance. Assumption: no protocol-mandated baseline testing is subsequently identified.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary endpoint is the first episode of confirmed VT pneumococcal CAP, with the principal event target defined in the per-protocol population.

**[Derived]** Each participant contributes at most one event to this first-episode endpoint. Subsequent qualifying episodes belong to applicable “all-event” exploratory outcomes rather than the primary event count.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the clinical, imaging, microbiological, serotyping, and SSUAD criteria required for confirmed VT-CAP, including specimen timing, conflicting results, and incomplete testing. Without an operational case definition, primary events cannot be classified reproducibly.

**[Clarification Required]** Define the primary relative-risk estimand precisely: incidence-rate ratio or cumulative-risk ratio, its follow-up horizon, and handling of death, withdrawal, subsequent vaccination, and immune suppression. The supplied description combines time-to-first events with incidence rate/relative risk and does not fully specify these elements.

**[Proposed]** Use standardized case review with treatment-blinded endpoint classification and a documented resolution process for uncertain cases. Rationale: consistent ascertainment protects against differential classification. Assumption: clinical and laboratory records permit blinded review.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The secondary efficacy endpoints are first confirmed NB/NI-VT pneumococcal CAP and first VT-IPD. NB/NI-VT-CAP testing is gated by clinically significant primary efficacy; VT-IPD is evaluated once at study conclusion.

**[Provided]** Exploratory clinical endpoints comprise all S2 and S3 first-event and all-event CAP/IPD categories, individual-serotype outcomes, disease-related and all-cause deaths, hospitalized CAP with influenza A/B or specified respiratory viruses, age efficacy trends, and economic effect.

**[Provided]** Exploratory carriage endpoints comprise the specified organisms and pneumococcal serotype categories at months 6, 12, and 24. Immunogenicity endpoints comprise serotype IgG and OPA by age and sex at baseline and approximately months 1, 12, and 24.

**[Clarification Required]** Define confirmed, culture-confirmed, probable, and possible disease; NB/NI status; VT/NVT classification; attribution of death; and distinct recurrent episodes. Specify whether first events are counted separately within each endpoint category. These decisions govern overlap and prevent inconsistent event counting.

**[Clarification Required]** Identify the respiratory viruses other than influenza A/B, diagnostic assays, and the interval linking viral detection to CAP hospitalization. These details are necessary to construct the hospitalized viral-associated CAP endpoints.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Provided]** Primary inference uses vaccine efficacy \(VE=1-RR\), exact conditional confidence intervals, and the specified O’Brien–Fleming adjustment.

**[Proposed]** Subject to confirmation that RR denotes an incidence-rate ratio, estimate \(RR=(E_V/T_V)/(E_P/T_P)\), where \(E\) denotes first qualifying events and \(T\) qualifying person-time in vaccine and placebo groups. Obtain an exact conditional interval by conditioning on the total event count, with allocation probability \(p=RR\,T_V/(RR\,T_V+T_P)\), and transform its limits to VE. Rationale: this implements the supplied conditional count-based framework while allowing unequal exposure. Assumptions: valid exposure definitions, comparable ascertainment, and an appropriate conditional event-count model.

**[Clarification Required]** Confirm whether the conditional analysis uses equal-exposure binomial allocation or person-time-adjusted allocation, and specify any stratification. This choice affects confidence intervals and cannot be settled from 1:1 randomization alone.

**[Proposed]** Report treatment-group event counts, participants analyzed, qualifying person-time, incidence rates, RR, VE, and applicable adjusted confidence limits. Retain exact interval procedures when one group has zero events; if neither group has events, report the absence of information rather than a numerical efficacy claim. Rationale: sparse outcomes require transparent reporting without arbitrary continuity corrections. Assumption: the chosen exact implementation supports boundary cases.

**[Proposed]** Apply the same endpoint-specific count/rate framework to first-event secondary and exploratory clinical outcomes, subject to the approved hierarchy and multiplicity rules. Rationale: consistent estimands facilitate comparison. Assumption: each outcome has compatible exposure and ascertainment definitions.

**[Proposed]** Analyze “all-event” outcomes using participant-level recurrent-event counts, with a negative-binomial model and log person-time offset where estimable; also report raw counts and rates. Rationale: this accommodates repeated episodes and between-participant heterogeneity. Assumptions: distinct episodes are defined, exposure is measured, and the model adequately represents count dispersion.

**[Proposed]** Summarize disease-related and all-cause mortality using deaths, denominators, follow-up, and comparative rates; describe viral-associated CAP hospitalizations using their defined event counts and rates. Rationale: these outcomes require explicit ascertainment and exposure denominators. Assumption: cause attribution and viral linkage rules are finalized before analysis.

**[Proposed]** At each carriage visit, report organism- and serotype-specific prevalence, evaluable denominators, and between-group prevalence differences or ratios with confidence intervals. Use a repeated-measures model if a longitudinal contrast is desired. Rationale: carriage is assessed repeatedly as a status rather than solely as an incident clinical event. Assumptions: specimen methods are comparable across groups and within-participant correlation is modeled appropriately.

**[Proposed]** Summarize IgG using geometric mean concentrations and OPA using geometric mean titers, with confidence intervals, by serotype, visit, treatment, age, and sex. Estimate postvaccination between-group geometric mean ratios using log-scale models, with corresponding baseline antibody values and relevant subset stratification factors where appropriate. Rationale: antibody measurements commonly require multiplicative summaries and baseline adjustment. Assumptions: measurements are positive after prespecified handling of assay limits and log-scale modeling is adequate.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify economic perspective, resource-use measures, costs, currency and price year, evaluation horizon, discounting, and the intended economic contrast. Without these decisions, an economic effect cannot be converted into a defined cost or cost-effectiveness estimand.

**[Proposed]** Pending an approved economic analysis plan, restrict economic reporting to available resource-use and cost summaries rather than an unsupported cost-effectiveness claim. Rationale: economic conclusions depend on the missing valuation and perspective decisions. Assumption: relevant resource or cost data were collected.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Tabulate missing endpoint ascertainment, incomplete laboratory testing, missed visits, and loss to follow-up by group, visit, and reason. Distinguish a missed assessment from a negative result. Rationale: different missingness mechanisms have different implications. Assumption: reasons and observation status are recorded.

**[Proposed]** For a person-time primary analysis, end observed at-risk time at the last date on which event status can be established, the qualifying first event, death, or the applicable study cutoff, according to the finalized estimand. Rationale: unobserved follow-up should not automatically contribute event-free exposure. Assumption: censoring dates are reliable and censoring is sufficiently noninformative for the main analysis.

**[Clarification Required]** Decide how suspected cases with incomplete confirmation are classified and whether they contribute to probable/possible endpoints. Missing diagnostic evidence must not silently be treated as evidence that disease was absent.

**[Proposed]** Use observed valid scheduled carriage and antibody measurements for primary descriptive summaries, report changing denominators, and avoid last-observation-carried-forward. Where missingness is material, supplement model-based analyses with multiple imputation or weighting under an explicitly stated missing-at-random assumption. Rationale: carrying observations forward can misrepresent evolving immune responses and carriage. Assumption: collected variables adequately explain missingness for the supplementary analysis.

**[Proposed]** Examine sensitivity to informative loss to follow-up using prespecified scenarios or tipping-point analyses for unobserved disease events and missed subset measurements. Rationale: missing-at-random or independent-censoring assumptions cannot be established from observed data alone. Assumption: clinically interpretable ranges can be agreed without reference to treatment effects.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify assay lower and upper limits, handling of values outside those limits, invalid samples, and repeat-test rules. These choices affect geometric means, ratios, and classification of evaluable observations.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Compare the primary per-protocol result with the randomized full-analysis result and describe reasons for any differences. Rationale: this assesses robustness to eligibility and adherence exclusions. Assumption: a comparable endpoint can be constructed in both populations.

**[Proposed]** Evaluate age efficacy trends through treatment-by-age interaction modeling, with descriptive age-group estimates; retain the specified 65–69, 70–79, and ≥80-year groups for immunogenicity summaries. Rationale: interaction analysis addresses variation in efficacy more directly than separate within-group significance tests. Assumptions: event counts support modeling and the age functional form is prespecified.

**[Proposed]** Treat individual-serotype efficacy estimates as exploratory and emphasize confidence interval width and event counts. Rationale: subdivision of rare events produces unstable estimates. Assumption: no serotype-specific confirmatory testing strategy is subsequently specified.

**[Proposed]** Supplement first-event rate analyses with time-to-event displays and competing-risk summaries treating death as a competing event where appropriate. Rationale: death can alter the probability of experiencing CAP/IPD in an older population. Assumption: event and death dates are available; these analyses do not replace the specified primary exact analysis.

**[Proposed]** Consider diagnostic-misclassification sensitivity analyses using 97% specificity as an initial-boundary scenario and 98% as the validated SSUAD scenario, only after specifying additional required diagnostic parameters. Rationale: specificity influenced planning and can affect measured efficacy for rare outcomes. Assumptions: validation is applicable to trial testing and sensitivity and misclassification structure can be justified.

**[Clarification Required]** Supply diagnostic sensitivity and any differential misclassification assumptions before attempting a numerical specificity correction. Specificity alone does not identify corrected vaccine efficacy.

**[Provided]** If interim efficacy is declared, overrunning observations are supplementary and do not replace the interim dataset for the efficacy declaration.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Provided]** Safety objectives include SAE incidence, subset reactogenicity/AEs, and deaths. Reactogenicity is assessed for 7 days and unsolicited AEs for 28 days.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify SAE and death collection periods, solicited reaction categories, severity grading, causal attribution, event coding, and reporting/escalation rules. These details are necessary to define comparable safety outcomes and protect participants.

**[Proposed]** Summarize participants with at least one event, event counts, severity, relatedness, and timing by actual treatment received, using the appropriate safety subset and collection window. Rationale: participant incidence and total burden answer different safety questions. Assumption: event-level dates and classifications are available.

**[Proposed]** Present descriptive between-group risk differences and confidence intervals for selected safety outcomes, without interpreting a nonsignificant comparison as proof of equivalent safety. Rationale: rare harms may be imprecisely estimated even in a large trial. Assumption: outcome definitions and ascertainment windows are comparable between groups.

**[Proposed]** Review serious events and deaths individually and in aggregate, with access to unblinded information restricted to authorized safety personnel when necessary. Rationale: participant protection must coexist with preservation of trial masking. Assumption: approved safety governance defines those roles.

### 5.7 Software and References

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the sample-size calculation software, analysis platform, package versions, and implementations of exact conditional inference and O’Brien–Fleming adjustment. These details are not supplied and are necessary for computational reproducibility.

**[Proposed]** Use validated statistical software, retain executable code and versioned datasets, and independently verify primary event counts, exposure, confidence limits, sequential boundaries, and success decisions. Rationale: confirmatory conclusions depend on reproducible implementation of relatively specialized methods. Assumption: independent statistical quality-control resources are available.
