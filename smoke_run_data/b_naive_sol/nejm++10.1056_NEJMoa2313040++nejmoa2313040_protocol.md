# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** Oral factor Xa (FXa) inhibitors are widely used. Acute intracranial hemorrhage during anticoagulation is associated with high mortality and disability, making prevention of hematoma expansion an important treatment goal.

**[Provided]** Andexanet alfa is a recombinant, catalytically inactive FXa analogue intended to reverse FXa inhibition. This study compares andexanet with usual care in patients with acute intracranial hemorrhage.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary:** Compare andexanet with usual care for effective hemostasis at 12 hours post-randomization.
- **[Provided]** **S1—Secondary:** Compare percent change in anti-fXa activity from baseline to nadir during the first 2 hours post-randomization.
- **[Provided]** **S2—Exploratory efficacy:** Evaluate thrombin generation, neurologic deterioration, modified Rankin Scale (mRS), NIHSS/GCS, imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa/hemostasis correlation, EQ-5D-5L, and utility-weighted mRS.
- **[Provided]** **S3—Exploratory safety:** Evaluate thrombotic events (TEs), mortality, invasive procedures, hospital use, adverse events (AEs), vital signs, and immunogenicity.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a phase 4, prospective, multicenter, randomized, open-label clinical trial. The EAC and imaging core laboratory are blinded. Allocation is 1:1 to andexanet or usual care.

**[Provided]** Usual care consists of investigator-selected treatment, including no treatment, other than andexanet, initiated within 3 hours.

**[Clarification Required]** The reference time for the usual-care 3-hour initiation requirement must be specified because it determines treatment compliance and protocol deviations.

**[Provided]** Crossover is not permitted. Andexanet is administered using one of two regimens:

- **[Provided]** **Low dose:** A 400-mg IV bolus over about 15 minutes, followed by 480 mg at 4 mg/min for 120 minutes.
- **[Provided]** **High dose:** An 800-mg IV bolus over up to about 30 minutes, followed by 960 mg at 8 mg/min for 120 minutes.

**[Provided]** Regimen selection depends on the preceding FXa inhibitor, its last dose, and its timing. Participants qualifying through local anti-fXa activity >100 ng/mL when the last dose was >15 hours earlier or its timing is unknown receive high-dose andexanet.

**[Provided]** Andexanet should start no later than 30 minutes after randomization and preferably within 2 hours of baseline brain imaging.

**[Clarification Required]** The complete drug-, dose-, and timing-based regimen-selection table and permitted concomitant treatments must be specified to support consistent treatment delivery and adherence assessment.

**[Proposed]** Obtain applicable ethics and regulatory approvals before enrollment and document lawful consent procedures at each site. Rationale: protect participants in an emergency-care trial; assumption: jurisdiction-specific procedures accommodate the permitted consent routes.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** Randomization follows a sponsor-generated scheme after consent, eligibility and baseline assessments, and documentation of intended usual care.

**[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by intended usual-care agent—plasma-derived coagulation-factor concentrate versus other—and symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time—<180 versus ≥180 minutes.

**[Clarification Required]** Blocking, minimization, allocation-concealment procedures, and implementation responsibilities are unspecified. These decisions matter for allocation integrity and reproducibility.

**[Proposed]** Use a restricted-access allocation system that releases assignment only after required assessments and intended usual care are recorded, with an audit trail. Rationale: prevent foreknowledge and post-assignment alteration of stratification information; assumption: suitable allocation infrastructure is available.

**[Proposed]** Remove treatment-identifying information from materials supplied to blinded assessors whenever feasible and record accidental unblinding. Rationale: preserve blinded outcome assessment despite open-label treatment; assumption: clinically necessary information can be retained without routinely revealing assignment.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** Planned enrollment is approximately 900 participants, with 450 per group. A preplanned interim sample size re-estimation (SSR) may increase total enrollment to 1200.

**[Provided]** The calculation uses the binary effective-hemostasis outcome, assuming 70% effective hemostasis with usual care and 80% with andexanet. The target difference is 10 percentage points, representing a 33% reduction in the risk of not achieving effective hemostasis.

**[Provided]** The design targets approximately 90% power at an overall two-sided alpha of 0.05, accounting for interim analysis. The assumed test is a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel (CMH) comparison stratified by symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time.

**[Provided]** The calculation accounts for early discontinuation, but no separate numerical dropout or non-adherence inflation is specified. No event-count target, separate variability parameter, non-inferiority/equivalence margin, or clustering adjustment applies to this individually randomized, binary-outcome superiority design.

**[Provided]** The assumed andexanet rate comes from prior andexanet efficacy results. A 10-percentage-point difference was considered clinically meaningful.

**[Provided]** At interim analysis, conditional power of 30%–90% permits a DSMB-recommended enrollment increase up to 1200. Enrollment is not increased when conditional power is <30% or >90%.

**[Clarification Required]** The original calculation software, discontinuation assumptions, information fraction, and detailed SSR algorithm are unspecified. These are needed to reproduce the sample size and verify operating characteristics.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** The primary hypothesis is \(H_0:\pi_{\mathrm{UC}}-\pi_{\mathrm{andexanet}}=0\), versus \(H_A:\pi_{\mathrm{UC}}-\pi_{\mathrm{andexanet}}\ne0\), where each proportion represents effective hemostasis.

**[Provided]** The primary objective succeeds when the andexanet effective-hemostasis proportion is higher and the comparison meets the prespecified alpha-spending threshold. The secondary endpoint is tested only after a significant primary endpoint.

**[Derived]** A statistically significant difference favoring usual care would not satisfy the stated primary success rule.

**[Clarification Required]** The secondary hypothesis, test, and alpha available at each analysis must be finalized. The hierarchical testing requirement alone does not specify multiplicity control across interim and final assessments.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** Interim analysis includes conditional-power-based SSR, with a DSMB recommendation permitting an increase to a maximum of 1200 participants under the specified 30%–90% conditional-power rule.

**[Clarification Required]** The number and timing of interim looks, information fraction, alpha-spending function, nominal efficacy boundaries, conditional-power assumptions, and method preserving type-I error after SSR must be specified. These decisions determine valid stopping and confirmatory inference.

**[Clarification Required]** Futility and safety stopping rules, their binding or nonbinding status, and DSMB decision authority require definition. The rule prohibiting an enrollment increase below 30% conditional power does not establish a futility-stopping rule.

**[Proposed]** Finalize a joint interim-monitoring and SSR procedure before access to comparative interim results, and verify its overall two-sided type-I error and power by calculation or simulation. Rationale: adaptation and repeated testing must operate coherently; assumption: the supplied SSR range and enrollment cap remain unchanged.

**[Proposed]** Restrict unblinded interim results to authorized independent personnel and the DSMB, with documented recommendations and implementation decisions. Rationale: reduce operational bias; assumption: independent analysis support is available.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** Final analyses occur after study completion and final database lock. If the study stops after interim analysis, DCO1 supports confirmatory efficacy analyses, while DCO2 includes all participants for safety and efficacy sensitivity analyses.

**[Clarification Required]** DCO1 and DCO2 dates, participant inclusion rules, follow-up maturity requirements, and treatment of enrollment occurring around the stopping decision must be defined to prevent inconsistent analysis populations.

- **[Provided]** **Anti-fXa activity:** Baseline, 1 hour, and 2 hours.
- **[Provided]** **Thrombin generation:** Baseline, 1 hour, and 12 hours.
- **[Provided]** **TFPI:** Baseline, 1 hour, 2 hours, 12 hours, and Day 30.
- **[Provided]** **CT/MRI:** Baseline and about 12 hours.
- **[Provided]** **NIHSS:** Baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24, and 72 hours; the 12- and 24-hour assessments are blinded.
- **[Provided]** **GCS:** Baseline and 2, 3, 6, 12, 24, and 72 hours.
- **[Provided]** **Premorbid mRS:** Assessed at baseline.
- **[Provided]** **mRS and EQ-5D-5L:** Day 30 or early termination.
- **[Provided]** **Follow-up:** Telephone calls on Days 7 and 14, and Day 30 (+7) follow-up.
- **[Provided]** **Additional immunogenicity follow-up:** An approximately Day 120 visit for participants with positive anti-andexanet antibodies.

**[Clarification Required]** Assessment windows, baseline-selection rules, handling of unscheduled or duplicate measurements, and time origins for hourly assessments must be specified. Nominal times alone do not determine which observations enter each endpoint.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** Confirmatory inference uses an overall two-sided significance level of 0.05, accounting for interim analysis. Secondary testing is conditional on primary significance.

**[Proposed]** Report effect estimates with 95% confidence intervals, identifying ordinary intervals as nominal and using design-adjusted intervals for confirmatory interpretation where required by the finalized interim/SSR procedure. Rationale: distinguish estimation from sequential decision thresholds; assumption: an appropriate design-adjusted method is available.

**[Proposed]** Treat exploratory analyses as descriptive, emphasizing effect sizes, uncertainty, and explicitly nominal p-values without additional success claims. Rationale: numerous exploratory outcomes do not have a supplied multiplicity strategy; assumption: only P1 and S1 are intended for confirmatory testing.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Record deviations involving eligibility, allocation, treatment selection or timing, dosing, prohibited crossover or cointerventions, assessment timing, and assessor blinding. Rationale: these may affect safety or endpoint interpretation; assumption: site records support reliable classification.

**[Proposed]** Finalize major-deviation classifications before comparative outcome review, retain affected participants in the principal randomized analysis, and use exclusions only in supportive per-protocol analyses. Rationale: preserve randomization while assessing adherence sensitivity; assumption: deviations can be classified without outcome-driven decisions.

**[Clarification Required]** Major-deviation definitions, timing tolerances, and per-protocol exclusion rules must be approved because they determine the composition and interpretation of supportive analyses.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Proposed]** **Full analysis population:** Include every randomized participant and analyze by assigned group, regardless of treatment receipt, adherence, or subsequent management. Rationale: estimate the effect of assignment and preserve randomization; assumption: no alternative confirmatory efficacy population is mandated.

**[Proposed]** **Safety population:** Include every randomized participant by assigned group, with additional exposure-based summaries distinguishing andexanet, usual-care treatments, and no treatment. Rationale: retain untreated participants while describing actual exposures; assumption: treatment receipt is adequately documented.

**[Proposed]** **Per-protocol population:** Include randomized participants without prospectively defined major deviations, analyzed by assignment. Rationale: assess robustness to important nonadherence; assumption: sufficiently sized groups remain and results are interpreted as supportive rather than randomized causal estimates.

**[Proposed]** Use endpoint-specific evaluable subsets for observed-data summaries, but retain missing participants in principal-analysis accounting. Rationale: prevent availability of laboratory or imaging data from silently redefining the randomized population; assumption: missingness is addressed as specified in Section 5.4.

**[Clarification Required]** Any intended efficacy restriction based on baseline anti-fXa activity or other post-randomization evaluability criteria requires explicit specification and justification because it changes the target population.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Maintain a screening and disposition log containing screening, eligibility, consent, randomization, treatment receipt, follow-up completion, and reasons for exclusion or withdrawal. Rationale: characterize selection and attrition; assumption: collection is permitted by applicable privacy and consent requirements.

**[Proposed]** Present participant flow overall and by randomized group, with denominators for each analysis population and endpoint. Rationale: make exclusions and missing outcomes transparent; assumption: participant-level disposition records are complete.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

- **[Provided]** Include adults with acute intracerebral bleeding of the specified volume.
- **[Provided]** Baseline CT/MRI must occur within 2 hours before randomization.
- **[Provided]** Participants must have received oral apixaban, rivaroxaban, or edoxaban at protocol-specified doses within 15 hours, subject to the alternative anti-fXa qualification pathway.
- **[Provided]** Symptom onset to baseline scan must be <6 hours.
- **[Provided]** Randomization outside 15 hours after the last dose, or after an unknown last-dose time, requires standard-of-care local anti-fXa activity >100 ng/mL within 2 hours before consent and mandates high-dose andexanet.
- **[Provided]** Consent may be obtained from the participant, a legally authorized representative/proxy, or through emergency consent where lawful.

**[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** Planned hematoma-affecting surgery within 12 hours.
- **[Provided]** GCS <7, subject to the specified non-neurologic sedation exception.
- **[Provided]** NIHSS >35.
- **[Provided]** Hematoma volume ≤0.5 mL or ≥60 mL.
- **[Provided]** Inability to use the same CT/MRI modality.
- **[Provided]** Expected survival <1 month.
- **[Provided]** Recent thrombotic/cardiovascular events.
- **[Provided]** Cardiogenic shock.
- **[Provided]** Severe sepsis/septic shock.
- **[Provided]** Pregnancy or lactation, with required pregnancy-testing and contraception precautions.
- **[Provided]** Restricted anticoagulants or blood products.
- **[Provided]** Prior/planned andexanet.
- **[Provided]** Investigational drug.
- **[Provided]** Tumor-related bleeding.
- **[Provided]** Hypersensitivity.

**[Derived]** The stated volume exclusions imply an eligible hematoma-volume interval strictly greater than 0.5 mL and strictly less than 60 mL.

**[Clarification Required]** The adult age threshold, qualifying FXa-inhibitor doses, sedation exception, surgery-window reference time, thrombotic/cardiovascular event definitions and lookback periods, restricted exposures and washouts, and hypersensitivity scope must be specified to make eligibility reproducible.

**[Clarification Required]** Pregnancy-testing and contraception procedures, investigational-drug restrictions, and the scope of “prior/planned andexanet” relative to assigned study treatment require definition because they affect eligibility and participant protection.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** Recruitment is multicenter, targeting approximately 900 participants, with possible SSR-supported expansion to 1200.

**[Clarification Required]** Participating settings, number of sites, geographic scope, recruitment duration, and enrollment-management procedures are unspecified. These determine feasibility and operational planning.

**[Proposed]** Screen potentially eligible patients consecutively where feasible without delaying urgent clinical care, and document reasons for non-enrollment. Rationale: reduce discretionary selection in a time-critical setting; assumption: emergency workflows permit screening documentation.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Proposed]** Distinguish stopping assigned treatment from withdrawing study participation, withdrawing permission for data collection, and loss to follow-up. Rationale: these have different consequences for safety monitoring and analysis; assumption: consent documents support this distinction.

**[Proposed]** Continue scheduled outcome and safety follow-up after treatment discontinuation when permission remains, and record withdrawal dates and reasons. Rationale: preserve outcome ascertainment after nonadherence; assumption: ongoing contact is lawful and acceptable to the participant or representative.

**[Proposed]** Use permitted contacts and available clinical records to establish vital status and outcomes before declaring loss to follow-up. Rationale: reduce avoidable missingness; assumption: relevant access and permissions exist.

**[Clarification Required]** Rules for retaining previously collected data, collecting information after consent withdrawal, and attempting further contact must be specified because permissible follow-up depends on consent and jurisdiction.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Provided]** Scheduled baseline assessments include brain imaging, anti-fXa activity, thrombin generation, TFPI, NIHSS, GCS, and premorbid mRS. Randomization also requires intended usual care and symptom-onset-to-imaging classification.

**[Proposed]** Summarize demographics, qualifying FXa inhibitor and dose timing, hemorrhage characteristics, neurologic severity, laboratory measures, and randomization strata by assigned group. Rationale: describe clinical comparability and applicability; assumption: the relevant variables are collected.

**[Proposed]** Use counts and percentages for categorical variables and means/standard deviations or medians/interquartile ranges for continuous variables, with missing counts and no baseline significance testing. Rationale: descriptive summaries are more informative than tests of randomized baseline imbalance; assumption: distribution-appropriate summaries can be selected without treatment-outcome review.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is binary effective hemostasis at 12 hours post-randomization, comparing andexanet with usual care.

**[Clarification Required]** The operational definition of effective hemostasis is absent. Required decisions include any imaging, neurologic, rescue-treatment, surgery, survival, and evaluability components and their thresholds. These cannot be inferred from the outcome name and are essential for adjudication and analysis.

**[Proposed]** Use the assigned-treatment strategy in all randomized participants as the primary estimand, contrasting the probability of effective hemostasis with andexanet versus usual care. Rationale: align inference with randomized treatment assignment; assumption: an intention-to-treat treatment-strategy effect is the intended clinical question.

**[Proposed]** For provisional endpoint handling, classify death before the 12-hour assessment as unsuccessful hemostasis and retain participants receiving rescue therapy or deviating from assigned treatment in their randomized groups. Rationale: avoid survivor-only selection and preserve randomization; assumption: this handling is compatible with the finalized hemostasis definition.

**[Clarification Required]** The EAC charter must define adjudication procedures, assessor disagreement resolution, and treatment of rescue therapy, surgery, incomplete assessments, and death. These rules must be reconciled with the provisional estimand before confirmatory use.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The secondary outcome is percent change in anti-fXa activity from baseline to nadir during the first 2 hours post-randomization.

**[Proposed]** Define the sampled nadir as the minimum eligible 1- and 2-hour post-randomization values and calculate \(100(A_{\text{nadir}}-A_0)/A_0\), so negative values indicate reductions. Rationale: provide a reproducible signed outcome using scheduled samples; assumption: baseline is positive and both post-baseline measurements are valid and comparable.

**[Clarification Required]** The anti-fXa analysis assay, unit harmonization across drugs, qualifying sample windows, quantification-limit rules, and handling of zero baseline or incomplete early sampling must be specified because they can materially alter percent change and nadir classification.

**[Provided]** Exploratory efficacy outcomes comprise thrombin generation, neurologic deterioration, mRS, NIHSS/GCS, imaging-only hemostasis, rescue therapy, anti-fXa/hemostasis correlation, EQ-5D-5L, and utility-weighted mRS.

**[Clarification Required]** Definitions and assessment horizons for neurologic deterioration, imaging-only hemostasis, and rescue therapy; thrombin-generation parameters; utility-weighted mRS weights; and the EQ-5D-5L valuation method are unspecified. These choices determine the outcomes being estimated.

**[Proposed]** Describe NIHSS/GCS trajectories, mRS distributions, and EQ-5D-5L results at their supplied assessment times, reporting actual timing for early-termination assessments. Rationale: represent neurologic and functional recovery without silently equating early termination with Day 30; assumption: assessment dates are available.

**[Proposed]** Analyze scheduled TFPI measurements descriptively as an exploratory biomarker. Rationale: measurements are scheduled but no confirmatory TFPI objective is supplied; assumption: an interpretable assay parameter is defined before analysis.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** Use a two-sided CMH test for primary hemostasis proportions, stratified by symptom-onset-to-baseline-imaging time (<180 versus ≥180 minutes), with the finalized sequential threshold. Rationale: match the test used for sample-size planning; assumption: the planning test is adopted as the confirmatory analysis and strata support valid estimation.

**[Proposed]** Report successes and denominators by arm and stratum, arm-specific proportions, and an andexanet-minus-usual-care risk difference standardized to the pooled randomized distribution of the two timing strata. Rationale: provide clinically interpretable absolute effects alongside the CMH test; assumption: both treatments are represented sufficiently within strata.

**[Proposed]** Analyze secondary percent change using linear regression with treatment, baseline anti-fXa activity, and both randomization factors, using heteroscedasticity-robust standard errors. Report adjusted mean differences and distributional summaries. Rationale: compare the stated continuous outcome while improving precision; assumption: percent changes are defined, assays are comparable, and the sample supports large-sample inference.

**[Proposed]** Apply the secondary significance test only through the finalized primary-to-secondary gatekeeping and interim procedure. Rationale: preserve the supplied testing hierarchy; assumption: the joint multiplicity procedure is approved before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** For exploratory continuous trajectories, use baseline-adjusted repeated-measures models; for binary outcomes, report proportions and risk contrasts; for mRS, report the full distribution and an ordinal treatment-effect model where appropriate. Rationale: match analyses to outcome structure; assumption: model assumptions are assessed and departures are addressed transparently.

**[Proposed]** Examine proportional-odds assumptions before interpreting an ordinal common odds ratio, and provide category-specific results if the assumption is unsuitable. Rationale: avoid a misleading single effect summary; assumption: category counts permit meaningful assessment.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Tabulate missingness by treatment, endpoint, assessment time, and reason, distinguishing death, withdrawal, technical failure, and missed visits. Rationale: clarify the mechanisms and consequences of missing information; assumption: reasons can be collected reliably.

**[Proposed]** In the principal binary hemostasis analysis, classify an outcome that remains unassessable after adjudication as unsuccessful. Rationale: retain all randomized participants and require documented success; assumption: failure to document hemostasis is an acceptable endpoint policy. This policy is not assumed to bias the treatment contrast conservatively.

**[Proposed]** Perform primary-outcome sensitivity analyses using multiple imputation under missing at random and arm-specific missing-not-at-random tipping-point scenarios. Rationale: assess dependence on the unsuccessful-outcome rule; assumption: available baseline severity and stratification variables adequately inform the imputation model under the missing-at-random analysis.

**[Proposed]** For secondary anti-fXa analyses, impute missing scheduled laboratory values before calculating nadir and percent change, rather than treating a single observed post-baseline value as a fully observed two-sample nadir. Rationale: preserve the proposed endpoint definition; assumption: a defensible joint model conditional on treatment, baseline activity, drug, and randomization factors can be fitted.

**[Proposed]** Use complete-case and available-sample nadir analyses as secondary-outcome sensitivities, clearly identifying their different missingness requirements. Rationale: expose dependence on imputation and sampling completeness; assumption: these analyses are supportive rather than replacements for the principal method.

**[Proposed]** Do not impute fictitious post-death laboratory or neurologic measurements. Use instrument-defined death categories where applicable and otherwise specify a survivor or composite estimand explicitly. Rationale: death is an intercurrent event, not ordinary intermittent missingness; assumption: instrument scoring and estimand rules are finalized.

**[Clarification Required]** Handling of structurally undefined anti-fXa percent changes, early-termination functional assessments, and death in functional or utility outcomes requires endpoint-specific decisions because ordinary missing-data methods do not resolve these issues.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Conduct supportive primary-outcome analyses adjusting for both randomization factors and prespecified baseline severity variables, together with a per-protocol analysis. Rationale: assess robustness to prognostic imbalance and major deviations; assumption: variables and models are fixed before comparative outcome review.

**[Proposed]** Explore treatment interactions with qualifying FXa inhibitor, intended usual-care category, onset-to-imaging stratum, and baseline hemorrhage or neurologic severity. Rationale: investigate clinically plausible heterogeneity; assumption: subgroup definitions are prespecified and sample sizes are adequate. Interpret interaction estimates as exploratory.

**[Proposed]** Evaluate the association between early anti-fXa change and effective hemostasis using graphical summaries and baseline-adjusted regression. Rationale: address the specified pharmacodynamic/clinical correlation objective; assumption: sufficient paired data exist. Do not interpret the association as a randomized causal mediation effect.

**[Provided]** If the study stops after interim analysis, DCO2 supports efficacy sensitivity analyses including all participants, whereas DCO1 supports confirmatory efficacy.

**[Proposed]** Present DCO2 efficacy results as supportive and distinguish them from DCO1 confirmatory conclusions. Rationale: prevent later data from being treated as an additional unadjusted confirmatory look; assumption: the stopping and data-cut rules remain as supplied.

**[Proposed]** Report early-termination functional outcomes separately by assessment timing and assess sensitivity to restricting summaries to the agreed Day 30 window. Rationale: recovery time can affect functional scores; assumption: sufficient observations fall within that window.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Provided]** Safety objectives include TEs, mortality, invasive procedures, hospital use, AEs, vital signs, and immunogenicity.

**[Clarification Required]** Safety collection windows, TE definitions and adjudication, AE seriousness/severity/relationship rules, procedure categories, hospital-use measures, and immunogenicity sampling and positivity criteria must be specified. These determine safety denominators, exposure periods, and event classification.

**[Proposed]** Provisionally summarize safety from randomization through scheduled Day 30 (+7) follow-up, with approximately Day 120 antibody-positive follow-up reported separately. Rationale: align summaries with supplied follow-up opportunities; assumption: safety collection spans these periods and the final protocol confirms the precise event windows.

**[Proposed]** Report participants with at least one TE or AE, event counts, serious events, severity, relationship assessments, onset timing, and actual treatment exposure, without treating lack of statistical significance as evidence of equivalent safety. Rationale: characterize both incidence and clinical importance; assumption: events are consistently coded and exposure records are available.

**[Proposed]** Summarize mortality with counts and risk differences over the agreed follow-up period and, if informative, time-to-death estimates censoring at last known alive. Rationale: present absolute risk and timing; assumption: follow-up times are recorded and censoring is sufficiently noninformative for survival estimates.

**[Proposed]** Summarize invasive procedures and hospital use using counts, proportions, and distributional summaries appropriate to the finalized measures. Rationale: describe resource use without imposing unsupported endpoint definitions; assumption: collection and discharge/death dates support the selected measures.

**[Proposed]** Summarize vital-sign changes from baseline and immunogenicity testing denominators, positive results, persistence, and available clinical correlates. Rationale: make longitudinal findings and incomplete sampling transparent; assumption: assay definitions and sampling schedules are finalized.

**[Provided]** Participants with positive anti-andexanet antibodies have an additional approximately Day 120 visit.
