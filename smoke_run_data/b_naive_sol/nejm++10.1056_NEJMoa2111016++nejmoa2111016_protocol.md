# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** This phase 3b trial compares edoxaban-based and vitamin K antagonist (VKA)-based regimens in people with atrial fibrillation (AF) requiring chronic oral anticoagulation after transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI).

**[Provided]** The final statistical analysis plan (SAP), version 3.0, governs where it supplements or differs from the final protocol.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary:** Compare edoxaban-based versus VKA-based regimens for net adverse clinical events (NACE), comprising all-cause death, myocardial infarction (MI), ischemic stroke, systemic thromboembolism (SEE), valve thrombosis, or ISTH major bleeding.
- **[Provided]** **P2—Primary:** Compare the regimens for ISTH major bleeding.
- **[Provided]** **S1—Secondary:** Compare alternative NACE definitions using TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO bleeding definitions; MACE; MACCE; cardiovascular mortality; stroke subtypes; SEE; MI; valve thrombosis; and repeat target-lesion revascularization.
- **[Provided]** **S2—Secondary:** Compare ISTH, TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO bleeding endpoints; intracranial, life-threatening, and fatal bleeding; mortality; adverse events (AEs); laboratory parameters; ECG; and vital signs.
- **[Provided]** **S3—Exploratory:** Compare cardiovascular hospital admissions lasting ≥24 hours, hospital length of stay, ICU days, PACT-Q treatment satisfaction, EQ-5D-5L quality of life, and optional biomarkers.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a multinational, multicentre, prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded endpoint evaluation (PROBE), parallel-group phase 3b trial. An independent Clinical Events Committee (CEC) adjudicates events, and an independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) monitors safety.

**[Provided]** Participants are allocated 1:1 to edoxaban-based or VKA-based treatment. Randomized treatment starts within 24 hours of randomization and continues for up to 36 months. Minimum treatment duration is 12 months, except that the last enrolled participants have a minimum duration of 6 months. Treatment is followed by 30 days of observational follow-up.

- **[Provided]** **Edoxaban-based regimen:** Edoxaban 60 mg once daily, reduced to 30 mg once daily when the locally approved label’s dose-reduction criteria are met.
- **[Provided]** **VKA-based regimen:** A locally approved VKA, investigator-adjusted to INR 2.0–3.0. In Japan, the target is INR 1.6–2.6 for participants aged ≥70 years and 2.0–3.0 for those aged <70 years.
- **[Provided]** **Antiplatelet co-therapy:** The intended regimen is declared at randomization. Without another indication, no antiplatelet therapy (APT) or single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) for up to 90 days is permitted. After PCI, SAPT continues indefinitely, with dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) limited to 3 months after each stent. Aspirin 75–100 mg/day or clopidogrel 75 mg/day is preferred.

**[Clarification Required]** The applicable country-specific dose-reduction criteria and procedures for reassessing eligibility for dose reduction are not supplied. These must be operationalized to ensure consistent dosing and deviation classification.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** An interactive response system (IXRS) assigns a unique participant identifier and treatment. An independent biostatistician generates the randomization schedule under IXRS-vendor oversight. Crossover is not permitted.

**[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by TAVI procedure with stenting, yes/no, and need for edoxaban dose adjustment under the local label, yes/no. The schedule contains a randomization block number.

**[Clarification Required]** Block sizes and whether any minimization method is used are not specified. These details are needed to document implementation and assess allocation predictability.

**[Proposed]** Restrict access to the allocation schedule and reveal treatment through IXRS only after eligibility confirmation and entry of stratification factors. Rationale: this protects allocation concealment despite open-label treatment. Assumption: IXRS supports these access controls.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** Approximately 1,400 participants are planned for randomization in an event-driven design requiring at least 320 NACE events across both groups.

**[Provided]** The calculation concerns time to first NACE and its treatment hazard ratio (HR). Assumptions are a VKA annual NACE rate of 14%, an anticipated observed edoxaban-to-VKA HR of 0.95, a noninferiority margin of 1.38, and an anticipated median follow-up of two years.

**[Provided]** The stated planning alpha is 0.05, with 80% power for NACE noninferiority. The specified assessment compares the upper bound of a two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) for the HR with 1.38.

**[Provided]** The stated planning rationale is that approximately 1,400 participants, with the assumed event rate and follow-up, yield at least 320 NACE events for 80% power. The Executive Committee reviewed the acceptability of the noninferiority margin.

**[Provided]** Sample size or follow-up duration may be adjusted for actual versus expected endpoint and dropout rates. No clustering design effect applies because randomization is individual.

**[Clarification Required]** Additional event-time or variability assumptions, numerical dropout/non-adherence inflation, calculation software, and power for the bleeding hypotheses are not supplied. These omissions limit independent reproduction of the calculation and assessment of operating characteristics beyond NACE.

**[Clarification Required]** The procedure, timing, treatment-blinding status, and approval process for sample-size or follow-up adjustments are unspecified. These decisions matter because treatment-informed adaptation could affect confirmatory error control.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Derived]** Define HR as the edoxaban-based hazard divided by the VKA-based hazard; values below 1 favor edoxaban for these adverse outcomes.

1. **[Provided]** **NACE noninferiority:** Success requires the upper bound of the two-sided 95% HR CI to be <1.38.
2. **[Provided]** **ISTH major-bleeding noninferiority:** Test only after Step 1 succeeds; success requires the upper bound to be <1.38.
3. **[Provided]** **ISTH major-bleeding superiority:** Test only after Step 2 succeeds; success requires the upper bound to be <1.00.
4. **[Provided]** **NACE superiority:** Test only after Step 3 succeeds; success requires the upper bound to be <1.00.

**[Provided]** Failure at any step stops the confirmatory sequence.

**[Derived]** For each noninferiority comparison, the null hypothesis is HR ≥1.38 and the alternative is HR <1.38. For each superiority comparison, the null is HR ≥1.00 and the alternative is HR <1.00. An upper confidence limit equal to the applicable boundary does not establish success.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** An independent DSMB monitors safety.

**[Clarification Required]** The existence, timing, and scope of formal interim analyses, DSMB review frequency, stopping boundaries, and any alpha-spending procedure are not specified. Safety monitoring alone does not establish a formal interim efficacy-testing plan.

**[Proposed]** Do not introduce formal interim efficacy claims without a prospectively approved testing and error-control amendment. Rationale: unplanned repeated testing can invalidate the confirmatory thresholds. Assumption: no additional interim-testing provisions have already been authorized.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** The main endpoint analysis uses the intention-to-treat (ITT) population over the overall study period, beginning at randomization. For non-ITT sets, the starting point is first dose. Follow-up ends at Month 36/EOT, last visit, or death, whichever occurs first; incomplete follow-up is censored at last known status.

**[Provided]** Assessments occur at baseline/randomization and Months 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, and EOT/Month 36, with a ±14-day window for scheduled post-baseline visits. Post-treatment follow-up occurs at one month, ±7 days, followed by six-monthly follow-up contacts.

**[Provided]** PACT-Q and EQ-5D-5L are assessed at baseline, Month 3, and Month 12 or EOT, whichever occurs first.

**[Clarification Required]** The data-cutoff date, database-lock process, adjudication-readiness requirements, and exact relationship between the ≥320-event target and final analysis authorization are unspecified. These determine which observations enter the final analysis.

**[Clarification Required]** The endpoint scope and duration of the one-month observational follow-up and subsequent six-monthly contacts, including whether they contribute to primary analyses, are unspecified. Their relationship to EOT and the stated overall-study analysis window must be resolved to avoid inconsistent censoring.

**[Proposed]** Finalize endpoint adjudication, resolve material data queries, reconcile deaths and bleeding reports, and freeze analysis specifications before database lock. Rationale: this supports reproducible endpoint classification. Assumption: the final cutoff allows sufficient time for adjudication and reconciliation.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** Confirmatory decisions use the sequential testing procedure and upper bounds of two-sided 95% HR CIs. The supplied planning alpha is 0.05.

**[Derived]** A hypothesis reached after an earlier failure cannot receive a confirmatory claim under the supplied sequence, even if its individual confidence limit meets its numerical threshold.

**[Proposed]** Present secondary, exploratory, subgroup, and unreached confirmatory comparisons using effect estimates and nominal 95% CIs, with any p-values explicitly identified as nonconfirmatory. Rationale: no additional multiplicity procedure is supplied. Assumption: no separate confirmatory secondary family is intended.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Prospectively classify important deviations involving eligibility, randomization, dosing, prohibited crossover, antiplatelet treatment, treatment initiation, and endpoint ascertainment. Summarize counts and reasons by randomized group. Rationale: these deviations may affect interpretation of noninferiority and safety. Assumption: dated treatment and visit records are available.

**[Clarification Required]** The major-deviation definitions, adherence thresholds, and rules governing exclusion from supportive populations are unspecified. They must be finalized without treatment-outcome-driven selection.

**[Proposed]** Retain participants with deviations in the ITT analysis and document actual treatment separately. Rationale: retaining randomized assignment preserves the randomized comparison. Assumption: outcome collection remains permissible.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Provided]** The main endpoint analysis is ITT; analyses using non-ITT sets begin at first dose rather than randomization.

**[Derived]** The ITT comparison follows randomized assignment rather than subsequent treatment adherence or switching.

**[Proposed]** Operationalize the ITT set as all randomized participants, analyzed as assigned. Rationale: this makes the primary population explicit and preserves randomization. Assumption: no authorized exception to inclusion of randomized participants exists.

**[Proposed]** Define a supportive per-protocol population using prospectively specified important-deviation criteria. Rationale: agreement with ITT is informative in a noninferiority setting where non-adherence may dilute differences. Assumption: defensible adherence and deviation definitions can be established before comparative analyses.

**[Proposed]** Define a safety population containing all participants receiving at least one dose, summarized by treatment actually received. Rationale: exposure-based summaries support attribution of treatment-associated harms. Assumption: actual dosing is reliably recorded.

**[Clarification Required]** Exact per-protocol rules, handling of participants receiving both treatments, and any additional modified-ITT or on-treatment populations are unspecified. These choices determine membership and interpretation of supporting analyses.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Maintain a screening log recording assessment, eligibility, consent, randomization, and principal reasons for non-enrollment. Rationale: this permits transparent participant accounting and assessment of selection. Assumption: collection complies with applicable privacy and consent requirements.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Eligible participants are adults with successful TAVI, defined by correct single-valve position, gradient <20 mmHg, peak velocity <3.0 m/s, regurgitation ≤2, and no overt stroke or uncontrolled bleeding.

**[Provided]** Participants must have pre-existing or new AF documented for >30 seconds on ECG and require chronic oral anticoagulation.

**[Provided]** Exclusions are high bleeding risk; contraindication to either treatment; prolonged DAPT requirement; recent stroke, MI, or major surgery; mechanical valve; severe renal or hepatic disease; instability; active malignancy; specified laboratory abnormalities; pregnancy; investigator-assessed inability to comply; prior randomization; substance dependence; or life expectancy <6 months.

**[Clarification Required]** Operational definitions are missing for high bleeding risk, “recent” events or surgery, prolonged DAPT, severe renal/hepatic disease, instability, laboratory abnormalities, and applicable contraindications. The regurgitation grading convention and permissible interval between TAVI and randomization are also unspecified. These details are necessary for reproducible eligibility assessment.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** Recruitment is multinational and multicentre, with approximately 1,400 randomized participants planned.

**[Clarification Required]** Participating countries and sites, recruitment dates, site targets, and the consent procedure are not supplied. These determine operational feasibility, oversight requirements, and the recruitment timeline.

**[Proposed]** Assess potentially eligible participants systematically at participating sites and document consent before trial-specific procedures. Rationale: systematic screening reduces avoidable selection and consent protects participants. Assumption: locally approved consent and screening procedures are available.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Provided]** Incomplete endpoint follow-up is censored at the participant’s last known status.

**[Proposed]** Distinguish treatment discontinuation, withdrawal from scheduled visits, withdrawal of permission for further data collection, and loss to follow-up. Continue permitted endpoint ascertainment after treatment discontinuation. Rationale: stopping treatment need not end ITT follow-up. Assumption: participant permission and applicable requirements allow continued ascertainment.

**[Proposed]** Record discontinuation dates, reasons, subsequent anticoagulation, and the last date on which each relevant endpoint status was known. Rationale: these support exposure summaries, censoring, and missing-data assessments. Assumption: these data can be obtained reliably.

**[Clarification Required]** Withdrawal procedures, tracing methods, permitted remote or record-based follow-up, and endpoint-specific last-contact rules are unspecified. These affect completeness and the credibility of censoring assumptions.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Proposed]** Summarize available baseline demographics, AF history, relevant cardiovascular and bleeding history, renal and hepatic measures, TAVI characteristics, randomized stratification factors, and predeclared antiplatelet therapy by assigned group. Use descriptive summaries without baseline significance tests. Rationale: these describe clinical comparability without treating chance imbalance as a hypothesis test. Assumption: these variables are collected.

**[Clarification Required]** The complete baseline variable list, measurement windows, and baseline-value selection rules are not supplied. These are needed to standardize summaries and any covariate-adjusted analyses.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** **P1:** Time to first NACE, comprising all-cause death, MI, ischemic stroke, SEE, valve thrombosis, or ISTH major bleeding.

**[Provided]** **P2:** ISTH major bleeding, compared between randomized regimens using an HR and the specified noninferiority/superiority sequence.

**[Derived]** For the time-to-first NACE analysis, only the earliest qualifying component determines the composite event time.

**[Proposed]** Analyze P2 as time to first ISTH major bleeding. Rationale: a first-event survival analysis provides a coherent HR-based comparison alongside P1. Assumption: the intended bleeding estimand is first major bleeding rather than recurrent bleeding.

**[Clarification Required]** Detailed event definitions, applicable ISTH criteria, event dating, handling of simultaneous components, and adjudication procedures are not supplied. These are necessary for consistent classification and reproducible event times.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** Secondary clinical outcomes include alternative NACE definitions based on TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO bleeding; MACE; MACCE; cardiovascular mortality; stroke subtypes; SEE; MI; valve thrombosis; and repeat target-lesion revascularization.

**[Provided]** Secondary safety outcomes include ISTH, TIMI, BARC, and GUSTO bleeding endpoints; intracranial, life-threatening, and fatal bleeding; mortality; AEs; laboratory parameters; ECG; and vital signs.

**[Provided]** Exploratory outcomes include cardiovascular admissions lasting ≥24 hours, hospital length of stay, ICU days, PACT-Q treatment satisfaction, EQ-5D-5L quality of life, and optional biomarkers.

**[Clarification Required]** Exact MACE/MACCE components, bleeding categories, stroke classifications, revascularization definitions, admission-counting conventions, and overlap rules are unspecified. Endpoint-specific definitions must be fixed before analysis to prevent inconsistent comparisons.

**[Clarification Required]** Questionnaire scoring rules, EQ-5D-5L valuation approach, handling of early EOT assessments, and biomarker identities and sampling times are unspecified. These decisions determine the analyzable outcomes and their interpretation.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** Estimate primary HRs and two-sided 95% CIs using Cox proportional-hazards models stratified by the two randomization factors. Rationale: this aligns the analysis with stratified allocation and the supplied HR decision rules. Assumption: event numbers support estimation within the resulting strata and proportional hazards is an adequate summary.

**[Proposed]** Accompany HRs with participant counts, first-event counts, follow-up duration, and descriptive incidence rates. Present Kaplan–Meier estimates for NACE and all-cause mortality. Rationale: absolute summaries complement relative hazards. Assumption: censoring is sufficiently independent for these survival estimates.

**[Proposed]** For nonfatal endpoints, estimate cause-specific HRs and show cumulative incidence accounting for death as a competing event. Rationale: death precludes subsequent nonfatal events and should not be interpreted as ordinary loss to follow-up when estimating absolute risk. Assumption: a cause-specific HR is the intended relative-effect estimand.

**[Clarification Required]** The final primary model, treatment-effect estimand, competing-death convention for major bleeding, tie-handling method, and fallback for sparse or nonestimable models are not supplied. These choices can change HR estimates and confirmatory conclusions.

**[Proposed]** Assess proportional hazards using graphical diagnostics and residual-based checks; describe departures and provide supportive time-varying-effect summaries when warranted. Rationale: a single HR may obscure changing effects. Assumption: event timing is sufficiently informative; diagnostics do not replace the prespecified confirmatory rule.

**[Proposed]** Analyze other time-to-first clinical outcomes using analogous survival methods, with competing-risk summaries where relevant. Rationale: consistent methods aid interpretation across clinical outcomes. Assumption: endpoint definitions and follow-up windows are harmonized.

**[Proposed]** Summarize cardiovascular admission counts and, where estimable, compare rates using a negative-binomial model with observed follow-up as an offset. Describe hospital and ICU days using distributions and totals. Rationale: admissions can recur and resource-use measures may be skewed. Assumption: ascertainment is comparable and event counts support the model.

**[Proposed]** Analyze questionnaire scores using baseline-adjusted longitudinal models after scoring and visit-alignment rules are finalized. Rationale: this uses repeated measurements while accounting for baseline differences. Assumption: the selected model fits the score distribution and missingness is conditionally at random.

**[Proposed]** Summarize optional biomarkers in the participating subset and treat comparisons as exploratory. Rationale: optional availability may limit precision and representativeness. Assumption: assay and sampling procedures are sufficiently comparable.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Provided]** Incomplete endpoint follow-up is censored at last known status.

**[Proposed]** Report missingness and reasons by treatment and outcome, including the proportion lacking follow-up after randomization. Do not equate an unobserved event with an observed event-free outcome. Rationale: transparent accounting distinguishes administrative censoring from potentially informative loss. Assumption: missingness reasons can be recorded.

**[Proposed]** Do not impute event times in the primary analysis; apply the finalized endpoint-specific censoring rules. Rationale: invented event dates would alter the supplied time-to-event framework. Assumption: censoring is noninformative conditional on the analysis structure.

**[Proposed]** If loss to follow-up is material, investigate sensitivity to informative censoring using prespecified weighting or scenario analyses. Rationale: treatment-related dropout could bias comparisons, especially noninferiority. Assumption: relevant predictors or clinically defensible scenarios are available.

**[Proposed]** For longitudinal questionnaire analyses, use the model’s conditional missing-at-random assumption and conduct a missing-not-at-random sensitivity analysis if missingness is substantial. Rationale: missing scores may depend on unobserved health status. Assumption: sufficient observations and defensible sensitivity parameters are available.

**[Clarification Required]** Rules for partial event dates, unresolved adjudications, missing baseline covariates, and the threshold triggering missing-data sensitivity analyses are unspecified. These must be resolved before final analysis to prevent outcome-dependent handling.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Conduct supportive per-protocol and, if defined prospectively, on-treatment analyses of the primary outcomes without replacing the main ITT result. Rationale: consistency across exposure and adherence perspectives strengthens interpretation of noninferiority. Assumption: population and censoring definitions are finalized before comparative results are examined.

**[Clarification Required]** On-treatment risk windows, interruption allowances, post-discontinuation grace periods, and the status of supportive analyses in noninferiority interpretation are not supplied. These choices materially affect included follow-up and potential selection bias.

**[Proposed]** Explore treatment interactions for the randomization factors and, if collected, baseline age, renal function, region, and predeclared antiplatelet strategy. Report interaction estimates and uncertainty rather than separate subgroup significance claims. Rationale: this assesses heterogeneity while limiting overinterpretation. Assumption: subgroup definitions are prespecified and event counts are adequate.

**[Proposed]** Describe individual NACE components and recurrent clinical events separately from the first-event primary analysis. Rationale: component and recurrence patterns may explain the composite result. Assumption: all qualifying events, not only first events, are collected.

### 5.6 Harms

**[Provided]** Safety evaluation includes bleeding, mortality, AEs, laboratory parameters, ECG, and vital signs. An independent DSMB monitors safety, and an independent CEC adjudicates events.

**[Proposed]** In the safety population, summarize participants with AEs, serious AEs, treatment discontinuations due to AEs, and bleeding categories by actual treatment. Report both affected-participant counts and event counts where recurrent events are captured. Rationale: participant incidence and event burden address different safety questions. Assumption: seriousness, discontinuation reasons, and recurrence are recorded.

**[Proposed]** Summarize laboratory, ECG, and vital-sign measurements descriptively, including changes from baseline and clinically important abnormalities under predefined criteria. Rationale: aggregate changes and individual abnormalities provide complementary safety information. Assumption: measurement schedules and clinically meaningful thresholds can be specified.

**[Clarification Required]** AE collection windows, treatment-emergent definitions, coding dictionary, severity and causality conventions, expedited-reporting procedures, laboratory alert thresholds, and DSMB escalation rules are not supplied. These are necessary for consistent surveillance and participant protection.

**[Clarification Required]** Statistical software, versions, packages, and sample-size software are unspecified. These must be documented to make calculations and analyses reproducible.

**[Proposed]** Use version-controlled analysis programs and independently validate primary endpoint derivations, population membership, HRs, confidence limits, and sequential-testing decisions. Rationale: independent checks reduce implementation errors affecting confirmatory conclusions. Assumption: qualified programming and statistical review resources are available.
