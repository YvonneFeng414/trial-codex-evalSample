# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** C3G and IC-MPGN are complement-driven glomerular diseases that may affect native kidneys or recur after kidney transplantation. Pegcetacoplan inhibits C3 and C3b to limit inappropriate complement activation and glomerular C3-deposit injury. Reduction in proteinuria is the expected early renal signal.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary:** Assess twice-weekly subcutaneous pegcetacoplan versus placebo in primary C3G/IC-MPGN on the basis of proteinuria reduction. The primary outcome is proteinuria reduction.
- **[Provided]** **S1—Secondary:** Assess the effect of pegcetacoplan on eGFR. The outcome is eGFR.
- **[Provided]** **S2—Secondary:** Assess the effect of pegcetacoplan on additional C3G/IC-MPGN disease-related parameters. The outcomes are additional disease-related parameters.
- **[Provided]** **S3—Secondary:** Evaluate pegcetacoplan safety over 52 weeks of treatment. The outcome is safety over 52 weeks of treatment.
- **[Provided]** **S4—Exploratory:** Characterize additional clinical, laboratory, and histologic findings in response to pegcetacoplan. The outcomes are these additional findings.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a phase 3, randomized, multicenter, placebo-controlled, double-blind study comprising a 10-week screening period, a 26-week randomized controlled period (RCP), a 26-week open-label period (OLP), and an 8-week follow-up for participants not entering the long-term extension.

**[Provided]** Participants are allocated 1:1 to pegcetacoplan or matching placebo during the RCP. The pegcetacoplan group continues pegcetacoplan during the OLP; the placebo group switches to pegcetacoplan during the OLP. Treatment is administered by subcutaneous infusion twice weekly.

- **[Provided]** Adults and adolescents ≥50 kg receive 1080 mg/20 mL twice weekly.
- **[Provided]** Adolescents weighing 35 to <50 kg receive 648 mg for the first dose, followed by 810 mg/15 mL twice weekly.
- **[Provided]** Adolescents weighing 30 to <35 kg receive 540 mg for the first two doses, followed by 648 mg/12 mL twice weekly.

**[Provided]** The RCP is blinded, and all participants receive open-label pegcetacoplan during the OLP. Participants, sponsor, investigators, and site staff remain blinded to RCP allocation through completion of all week-26 assessments and RCP database lock. Central evaluations, including central pathology, are blinded. Only non-site drug-supply/logistics personnel are unblinded.

**[Derived]** Planned pegcetacoplan exposure is 52 weeks for participants initially assigned pegcetacoplan and 26 weeks for participants initially assigned placebo.

**[Clarification Required]** Rules for dose changes after weight changes, interrupted treatment, missed doses, infusion administration, and any adult weight category not clearly covered by the supplied wording must be finalized. These rules determine consistent treatment delivery and exposure measurement.

**[Clarification Required]** The complete visit schedule, assessment windows, concomitant-treatment restrictions, rescue-treatment rules, and procedures for medically necessary emergency unblinding are not supplied. These decisions are necessary for participant safety and consistent conduct and must preserve the specified blinding arrangements.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** Randomization is performed centrally using a computer-generated schedule with stratified permuted blocks.

**[Provided]** Stratification is first by posttransplant recurrence versus nontransplant status and then by availability versus unavailability of a baseline renal biopsy obtained during screening or historically within 28 weeks.

**[Clarification Required]** Block sizes, any variation in block size, allocation-system implementation, and any use of minimization are unspecified. These details are needed to reproduce allocation and document concealment; confidential block information should not be disclosed to recruiting personnel.

**[Clarification Required]** The reference date for the historical 28-week biopsy window and the rules for determining biopsy availability before randomization require confirmation. These decisions affect stratum assignment and histologic analysis eligibility.

**[Proposed]** Document eligibility and stratification variables before releasing an allocation, and maintain an allocation-system audit trail. Rationale: prevent selective allocation and permit verification. Assumption: the central randomization system can restrict allocation access appropriately.

### 2.3 Sample Size

**[Provided]** Planned enrollment was approximately 80–100 randomized participants, with 40–50 per arm, including at least 78 native-kidney participants and at least 63 participants with native-kidney C3G. Final enrollment after enrollment closure was 124.

**[Provided]** The Amendment 4 calculation used the continuous log-transformed week-26 uPCR ratio to baseline, with the following assumptions:

- **[Provided]** Control: 20% uPCR reduction and mean log ratio −0.22.
- **[Provided]** Pegcetacoplan: 60% uPCR reduction and mean log ratio −0.92.
- **[Provided]** Target contrast: mean log ratios of −0.92 versus −0.22.
- **[Provided]** Common log-scale standard deviation: 0.88.
- **[Provided]** One-sided alpha: 0.025.
- **[Provided]** Power: at least 90% with 70 total participants before attrition inflation.
- **[Provided]** Attrition inflation: 10% for potential missing assessments and COVID-19 impact.
- **[Provided]** Basis: preliminary study data. The stated rationale was that 70 participants provided the targeted power under these assumptions, with inflation supporting enrollment targets.

**[Derived]** The assumed treatment-minus-control difference on the supplied log scale is −0.70.

**[Provided]** The Amendment 4 calculation test and calculation software were not specified. The conditional-power re-estimation plan was not retained.

**[Provided]** The original protocol specified a two-sample t-test and conditional-power re-estimation at approximately 50% completion, without Type I error adjustment and capped at 120 participants. Software for that re-estimation was not specified.

**[Provided]** This is not an event-driven design; no required event count applies. The comparison is superiority versus placebo, with no non-inferiority or equivalence margin. Randomization is individual rather than clustered, so a clustering design effect does not apply.

**[Clarification Required]** Document the amendment chronology, the reason final enrollment reached 124, realized arm counts, and whether the native-kidney enrollment minimums were achieved. These distinguish planned enrollment requirements from actual recruitment.

**[Clarification Required]** Confirm the Amendment 4 calculation method and whether any originally planned conditional-power assessment occurred before its removal. This is necessary to reproduce the calculation and assess whether adaptation affected operating characteristics.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** The primary null hypothesis is no between-group difference in the week-26 log-transformed uPCR ratio to baseline; the alternative is a difference.

**[Derived]** Defining \(\Delta\) as the pegcetacoplan-minus-placebo difference in mean log-transformed uPCR ratio, the stated hypotheses are \(H_0:\Delta=0\) versus \(H_1:\Delta\ne0\). A negative contrast represents a greater reduction with pegcetacoplan.

**[Provided]** Separate comparative hypotheses test no difference versus a difference in composite renal response, ≥50% uPCR response, eGFR change, histologic activity-score change, C3c response, proteinuria <1 g/day, albumin normalization, C3 normalization, FACIT-Fatigue change, and KDQOL change.

**[Provided]** Primary, key secondary, and additional secondary endpoints are tested in a fixed sequence to control trial-wise Type I error.

**[Clarification Required]** The exact testing sequence, classification of endpoints as key versus additional secondary, test sidedness, and rejection thresholds require confirmation. The stated difference alternative and the one-sided 0.025 sample-size assumption do not independently establish the final inferential convention.

**[Proposed]** In the finalized fixed sequence, proceed to the next hypothesis only after rejecting every preceding hypothesis at the prespecified threshold; treat subsequent results as descriptive after the first failure. Rationale: implement fixed-sequence family-wise error control. Assumption: the complete sequence and valid component tests are fixed before unblinding.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** The original conditional-power re-estimation at approximately 50% completion was not retained in Amendment 4.

**[Clarification Required]** Any other statistical interim analyses, monitoring committee arrangements, or stopping boundaries are unspecified. These decisions matter because repeated efficacy testing and access to treatment-specific data can affect Type I error and blinding.

**[Proposed]** Do not introduce an additional efficacy interim analysis without a prospective amendment specifying its error-control implications. Rationale: avoid unplanned repeated testing. Assumption: no other interim efficacy analysis is already authorized.

**[Proposed]** Conduct ongoing blinded safety surveillance without expanding access to RCP allocation beyond the specified unblinded personnel. Rationale: support safety oversight while preserving masking. Assumption: any necessary emergency procedures will be separately approved and documented.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

**[Provided]** The formal statistical analysis plan (SAP) is finalized before database lock. RCP analysis follows completion or discontinuation of all participants, entry, review, cleaning and finalization of corresponding data, lock of the week-26 analysis database, and unblinding.

**[Provided]** Primary and secondary endpoints are assessed at week 26 and evaluated at week 52 as exploratory. Primary uPCR uses an equal-weighted average over weeks 24, 25, and 26. Twenty-four-hour urine collections occur at weeks 24 and 48.

**[Clarification Required]** Define the operational completion milestone for the RCP analysis, the week-52 and follow-up database locks, and associated data cutoffs. This prevents ambiguity about which observations enter each analysis.

**[Proposed]** Preserve a frozen week-26 analysis dataset and finalized analysis programs before release of RCP allocation. Rationale: maintain an auditable separation between confirmatory RCP analyses and subsequent exploratory analyses. Assumption: data-management systems support versioned locks.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** The sample-size calculation uses one-sided alpha 0.025, and confirmatory endpoints are subject to fixed-sequence trial-wise Type I error control.

**[Clarification Required]** The final SAP must reconcile the stated two-direction difference hypotheses with the one-sided planning alpha and specify the beneficial direction for every endpoint. This determines which p-values support confirmatory claims.

**[Proposed]** Report treatment effects with two-sided 95% confidence intervals and clearly identified p-value sidedness. Rationale: communicate effect magnitude and precision. Assumption: these intervals are descriptive unless their relationship to the finalized testing rule is explicitly established.

**[Proposed]** Identify confidence intervals that are not multiplicity-adjusted and label exploratory p-values as nominal. Rationale: avoid implying simultaneous coverage or confirmatory evidence outside the testing sequence. Assumption: no additional multiplicity procedure is subsequently adopted.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Before unblinding, classify deviations by their potential effect on participant safety, treatment exposure, eligibility, endpoint measurement, or interpretation. Rationale: prevent outcome-driven classification. Assumption: deviation records are sufficiently complete for blinded review.

**[Proposed]** Summarize deviations by randomized group and period, including incorrect dosing, missed infusions, prohibited treatment, eligibility violations, and assessments outside finalized windows. Rationale: describe implementation and potential bias. Assumption: these categories will be operationally defined before lock.

**[Proposed]** Retain participants with deviations in the randomized primary analysis and examine important deviations in supportive analyses. Rationale: preserve the randomized comparison. Assumption: any supportive exclusion rules are prespecified and applied without knowledge of comparative outcomes.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Proposed]** Define the primary randomized analysis population as all randomized participants, analyzed according to assigned RCP treatment. Rationale: preserve the benefits of randomization. Assumption: no exceptional post-randomization exclusion is authorized.

**[Proposed]** Define the safety population as all participants receiving at least one dose, classified by treatment actually received within each study period. Rationale: attribute harms to exposure, particularly after crossover. Assumption: actual dosing records are available.

**[Proposed]** Define a supportive per-protocol population using blinded, prespecified major-deviation criteria. Rationale: assess robustness to substantial departures from treatment and assessment requirements. Assumption: exclusions will not replace or redefine the primary randomized analysis.

**[Clarification Required]** The histologic analysis population, acceptable baseline and follow-up biopsy requirements, and handling of unavailable or unevaluable specimens require definition. Biopsy availability is a stratification factor, so histologic results cannot automatically represent all randomized participants.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

**[Proposed]** Record numbers screened, eligible, excluded with reasons, randomized, treated, completing each period, entering extension, and completing follow-up. Rationale: make selection and attrition transparent. Assumption: screening and disposition records permit reliable participant-level reconciliation.

**[Proposed]** Distinguish screen failures from randomized participants later found ineligible, and summarize screening abnormalities descriptively. Rationale: prevent changes to randomized denominators and identify recruitment constraints. Assumption: permitted screening data are retained.

**[Clarification Required]** Screening counts, failure reasons, and rules for repeat screening are not supplied. They are needed to complete participant disposition and determine whether repeated assessments influence eligibility.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** Primary C3G/IC-MPGN.
- **[Provided]** Active renal disease.
- **[Provided]** Proteinuria ≥1 g/day and first-morning urine (FMU) uPCR ≥1000 mg/g.
- **[Provided]** eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m².
- **[Provided]** Stable renal regimen.
- **[Provided]** Required vaccination status.
- **[Provided]** Reproductive safeguards.
- **[Provided]** Capacity to consent or assent and administer treatment, or caregiver support.
- **[Provided]** Adults and, where approved, adolescents aged 12–17 years weighing ≥30 kg.

**[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** Prior pegcetacoplan.
- **[Provided]** Improving disease.
- **[Provided]** Relevant secondary disease, infection, or malignancy.
- **[Provided]** Prohibited complement therapies.
- **[Provided]** Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- **[Provided]** Severe infection.
- **[Provided]** Hereditary fructose intolerance.

**[Clarification Required]** Diagnostic criteria, definitions of active and improving disease, stability duration for the renal regimen, and qualifying secondary diseases require operational specification. These determine reproducible eligibility decisions.

**[Clarification Required]** Required vaccines and timing, reproductive safeguards, prohibited-therapy lists and washout periods, infection and malignancy criteria, and eligibility measurement procedures are unspecified. These details materially affect safety and enrollment consistency.

**[Clarification Required]** The eGFR equations and handling of measurements near eligibility thresholds must be specified for adults and adolescents. Different calculation or repeat-testing rules could change eligibility.

### 4.3 Recruitment

**[Provided]** Recruitment is multicenter, with adolescents eligible only where approved. Enrollment closed with 124 participants.

**[Clarification Required]** Countries, centers, recruitment dates, recruitment methods, and achieved native-kidney and native-kidney C3G counts are not supplied. These are needed to describe recruitment and assess whether enrollment requirements were met.

**[Proposed]** Obtain applicable ethics and regulatory approvals before recruitment, with informed consent and age-appropriate assent or legally required permission. Rationale: protect participants, including adolescents. Assumption: procedures follow the requirements applicable at each participating site.

**[Proposed]** Monitor the supplied native-kidney enrollment minimums without examining comparative outcomes. Rationale: support the intended population composition without compromising blinding. Assumption: disease and transplant categories are available during recruitment.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

**[Provided]** Participants not entering the long-term extension undergo an 8-week follow-up.

**[Proposed]** Distinguish treatment discontinuation from withdrawal of permission for study assessments, and continue scheduled outcome collection after treatment discontinuation when consent permits. Rationale: reduce missing outcomes and support randomized treatment-effect estimation. Assumption: continued assessments are clinically and operationally feasible.

**[Proposed]** Record dates and reasons for treatment discontinuation, study withdrawal, loss to follow-up, and extension entry. Rationale: permit period-specific disposition and missing-data assessment. Assumption: these events can be reliably distinguished.

**[Clarification Required]** Treatment stopping criteria, early-termination assessments, follow-up after early discontinuation, and extension eligibility and transition procedures are unspecified. These decisions determine safety surveillance and completeness of endpoint collection.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Proposed]** Summarize age, sex, disease category, transplant status, biopsy availability, proteinuria/uPCR, eGFR, relevant disease measures, and renal therapies by randomized group. Rationale: describe the population and assess clinically important imbalances. Assumption: these variables are collected consistently.

**[Proposed]** Present continuous characteristics using sample size, mean, standard deviation, median, and range, and categorical characteristics using counts and percentages with missing denominators identified. Do not use baseline significance tests. Rationale: describe randomized groups without treating chance imbalance as evidence of allocation failure. Assumption: summaries are descriptive.

**[Clarification Required]** Baseline definitions, including selection or averaging of repeated urine measurements and the reference assessment for each laboratory, questionnaire, and histologic outcome, require specification. They directly affect change scores and treatment-effect estimates.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is proteinuria reduction with twice-weekly subcutaneous pegcetacoplan versus placebo. The primary analytical endpoint is the week-26 log-transformed uPCR ratio to baseline, using an equal-weighted average over weeks 24, 25, and 26.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify whether the equal-weighted average is formed from uPCR values, ratios to baseline, or log ratios. These constructions are not interchangeable and can produce different treatment estimates.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the primary urine specimen type, replicate handling, collection validity rules, assay procedures, visit windows, and baseline uPCR construction. FMU uPCR is specified for eligibility, but that does not establish the complete primary endpoint measurement procedure.

**[Proposed]** Use natural logarithms for analysis. Rationale: the supplied mean log ratios are consistent with natural-log transformations of the assumed remaining proteinuria fractions. Assumption: natural logarithms were intended in the sample-size calculation.

**[Proposed]** Use a provisional treatment-policy strategy for treatment discontinuation, nonadherence, and changes in background treatment, retaining subsequent measurable outcomes in the randomized comparison. Rationale: estimate the effect of assignment under study conditions. Assumption: post-event outcome collection is permitted and feasible.

**[Clarification Required]** Finalize the estimand and handling of death, dialysis, graft failure, rescue treatment, and other events that may change the meaning or availability of renal measurements. These cannot all be treated as ordinary missing observations without a clinical decision.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** Analyze the primary log-ratio endpoint using analysis of covariance with randomized treatment, baseline log-uPCR, and the two randomization stratification factors. Rationale: adjust for prognostic baseline information while respecting the design. Assumption: the finalized endpoint and imputed or observed data support an approximately appropriate linear-model analysis.

**[Proposed]** Report adjusted group estimates, the pegcetacoplan-minus-placebo log-scale contrast, its confidence interval, and the treatment p-value under the finalized testing convention. Rationale: provide a direct estimate of the randomized contrast. Assumption: the model specification is fixed before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** Exponentiate the adjusted log-scale contrast to report the ratio of geometric mean ratios to baseline. Express \(100[1-\exp(\widehat{\Delta})]\%\) as the relative reduction in the pegcetacoplan-to-placebo ratio, not as an absolute percentage-point reduction from baseline. Rationale: give an interpretable effect while preserving the log-scale estimand. Assumption: natural-log analysis is adopted.

**[Proposed]** Treat the participant, not each weekly urine measurement, as the independent analysis unit. Rationale: weeks 24, 25, and 26 contribute to one participant-level primary endpoint and are correlated. Assumption: the supplied equal-weighted construction is retained.

**[Proposed]** Analyze continuous secondary changes using endpoint-specific covariance models with treatment, the corresponding baseline value, and randomization factors. Rationale: provide consistent baseline-adjusted comparisons. Assumption: each endpoint is suitably modeled as continuous; bounded or markedly non-normal outcomes require prespecified alternatives.

**[Proposed]** Analyze binary endpoints using logistic regression with treatment and randomization factors, reporting odds ratios and model-standardized response probabilities and risk differences. Rationale: accommodate stratification and provide both relative and absolute effects. Assumption: the outcome definitions are finalized and data support model estimation.

**[Proposed]** Prespecify penalized logistic regression for separation and a documented fallback when sparse strata prevent estimation. Rationale: reduce unstable or non-estimable binary comparisons in a small study. Assumption: the fallback and its testing properties are finalized before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** Check continuous-model residuals and influential observations, and use prespecified robust or distribution-appropriate sensitivity analyses when assumptions are materially violated. Rationale: assess robustness without selecting analyses by favorable results. Assumption: diagnostic criteria and alternative methods are defined prospectively.

**[Clarification Required]** Final model details, sparse-stratum handling, questionnaire-specific models, and test statistics must be locked in the SAP. These choices affect reproducibility and the validity of the fixed testing sequence.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Summarize missingness by randomized group, assessment, period, and reason, distinguishing missed visits, invalid specimens, treatment discontinuation, and clinically undefined outcomes. Rationale: identify mechanisms that may bias inference. Assumption: reasons are recorded with sufficient detail.

**[Proposed]** Under a missing-at-random assumption, use multiple imputation for missing primary components, incorporating treatment, randomization factors, baseline renal measures, and available RCP uPCR measurements. Construct the finalized three-week endpoint within each imputed dataset and combine estimates using Rubin’s rules. Rationale: retain randomized participants and reflect imputation uncertainty. Assumption: missingness is explainable by included observed information and the imputation model is compatible with the analysis.

**[Proposed]** Impute missing weekly components rather than silently reweighting the available weeks. Rationale: preserve the specified equal contribution of weeks 24, 25, and 26. Assumption: the final endpoint construction permits component-level imputation.

**[Proposed]** Assess departures from missing at random using prespecified delta-adjustment or tipping-point analyses, including less favorable assumptions for missing pegcetacoplan outcomes. Rationale: evaluate sensitivity to informative missingness. Assumption: clinically interpretable departures and their direction are agreed before unblinding.

**[Proposed]** Avoid automatic last-observation-carried-forward or replacement of missing renal measurements with zero. Rationale: these substitutions can distort change and uncertainty. Assumption: no endpoint-specific rule explicitly requires such coding.

**[Clarification Required]** Specify the number of imputations, auxiliary variables, handling of missing baseline data, nonpositive uPCR values, assay limits, and secondary-endpoint missingness. These affect computational stability and endpoint validity.

**[Clarification Required]** Decide separately how death, dialysis, graft failure, and structurally unavailable biopsies enter each estimand. Imputing such outcomes as ordinary missed measurements may answer an unintended clinical question.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Provided]** Week-52 evaluations of primary and secondary endpoints are exploratory.

**[Proposed]** Summarize week-52 outcomes by original randomized group and describe treatment trajectories across the RCP and OLP without implying a concurrent placebo-controlled comparison at week 52. Rationale: both groups receive pegcetacoplan during the OLP. Assumption: longitudinal outcome collection is adequate.

**[Proposed]** Conduct exploratory subgroup analyses by transplant status, disease category, and baseline biopsy availability, using treatment-by-subgroup interactions where estimable. Rationale: assess consistency across clinically relevant design categories. Assumption: subgroup definitions are fixed before unblinding; the study is not assumed powered for subgroup claims.

**[Proposed]** Provide supportive per-protocol, alternative missing-data, and primary-model robustness analyses without replacing the randomized primary result. Rationale: assess sensitivity to implementation and modeling assumptions. Assumption: these analyses are prespecified and clearly distinguished from confirmatory testing.

**[Proposed]** Summarize biopsy availability and paired-specimen evaluability by group, and qualify histologic findings as pertaining to the defined biopsy population. Rationale: selective specimen availability can limit generalizability. Assumption: specimen disposition is documented.

**[Proposed]** Use exploratory repeated-measures models for available longitudinal renal outcomes, accounting for within-participant correlation. Rationale: describe the timing and persistence of response. Assumption: actual assessment times and adequate repeated data are available.
