# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

**[Provided]** Pediatric type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) has limited glucose-lowering treatment options. This Phase 3 study evaluates once-weekly dulaglutide added to diet and exercise, with or without metformin and/or basal insulin, versus placebo.

**[Provided]** HbA1c was selected as an accepted endpoint for pediatric T2DM glycemic trials.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary efficacy:** Test superiority of pooled dulaglutide 0.75 mg and 1.5 mg administered subcutaneously (SC) once weekly versus placebo for change in central-laboratory HbA1c from baseline to Week 26 in children and adolescents with inadequately controlled T2DM.
- **[Provided]** **S1—Secondary efficacy:** Compare individual dulaglutide doses and, where specified, pooled dulaglutide with placebo for individual-dose HbA1c change, fasting blood glucose (FBG) change, HbA1c <7.0%, and BMI change at Week 26. Assess HbA1c, FBG, and BMI changes and HbA1c <7.0% at Week 52.
- **[Provided]** **S2—Safety and PK/PD:** Assess safety at Week 26 versus placebo and at Week 52 within dulaglutide groups, including self-reported hypoglycemia/rescue, adjudicated pancreatitis/pancreatic enzymes, thyroid treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs)/calcitonin, and ADA, allergic/hypersensitivity reactions, and injection-site reactions. Characterize pharmacokinetics (PK) and exposure relationships with safety and efficacy.
- **[Provided]** **S3—Exploratory:** At Weeks 26 and 52, assess HbA1c ≤6.5%; HbA1c <7.0% and HbA1c ≤6.5% without severe, documented symptomatic (<70 mg/dL), or probable hypoglycemia; weight; BMI standard deviation score (SDS); height and height SDS; waist; morning prolactin, IGF-1, estradiol, testosterone in males, LH, and cortisol; lipids; Tanner stage; EQ-5D-Y visual analogue scale (VAS) and domain levels; and basal-insulin-dose change. Assess HbA1c change at Week 13 and insulin resistance, beta-cell function, and adiponectin at Weeks 13 and 26.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

**[Provided]** This is a Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-arm superiority trial with a 26-week open-label extension and a 30-day safety follow-up.

**[Provided]** Allocation is 1:1:1 at baseline, Visit 2. SC study treatment is administered weekly:

- **[Provided]** Dulaglutide 0.75 mg/week during both the double-blind and open-label periods.
- **[Provided]** Dulaglutide 1.5 mg/week during both the double-blind and open-label periods.
- **[Provided]** Placebo/week during the double-blind period, switching to dulaglutide 0.75 mg/week at Week 26.

**[Provided]** Week-26 reporting groups are **All Dula**—pooled 1.5 mg and 0.75 mg—**Dula 1.5**, **Dula 0.75**, and **Placebo**. Placebo is the reference group.

**[Provided]** Post-Week-26 reporting groups are **All Dula**—pooled 0.75/0.75 and 1.5/1.5 sequences—**Dula 1.5/1.5**, **Dula 0.75/0.75**, and **Placebo/Dula 0.75**. No between-group inference is made after Week 26.

**[Clarification Required]** Injection volumes, formulation/adjuvant content, placebo matching, administration procedures, and management of missed doses are unspecified. These details are needed for reproducible treatment delivery and maintenance of blinding.

**[Clarification Required]** The parties covered by double blinding, emergency-unblinding procedures, and access restrictions after the primary database lock require specification. These decisions affect protection of the remaining follow-up data.

### 2.2 Randomization

**[Provided]** A computer-generated random sequence is implemented through an interactive web response system (IWRS) after eligibility confirmation at Visit 2.

**[Provided]** Randomization is stratified by screening HbA1c <8.0% versus ≥8.0%, metformin use yes/no, and insulin use yes/no.

**[Clarification Required]** Blocking or minimization methods, any block sizes, sequence-generation responsibilities, and allocation-access controls are unspecified. These details are needed to reproduce allocation and assess concealment.

**[Proposed]** Restrict access to the allocation sequence to authorized personnel independent of blinded clinical assessment. Rationale: this protects concealment and outcome assessment. Assumption: the IWRS supports role-based access and audit trails.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** The initial plan was approximately 150 enrolled participants, 50 per arm, to obtain 120 Week-26 completers, assuming 20% dropout.
- **[Provided]** Approximately 750 individuals were expected to be screened, assuming an 80% screen-failure rate.
- **[Provided]** The sample-size endpoint is continuous change in HbA1c from baseline to Week 26, comparing pooled dulaglutide with placebo. Absolute treatment- and control-arm mean changes were not specified.
- **[Provided]** The initial assumed between-arm difference was −0.65 percentage points in HbA1c change, with SD 1.0 percentage point and two-sided alpha 0.05.
- **[Provided]** Initial power was at least 90% for pooled dulaglutide and 80% for each individual dose versus placebo.
- **[Provided]** Under an SSR scenario with SD 1.3 percentage points, enrollment up to 189 participants, 63 per arm, provided at least 80% pooled power and 69% power for each dose.
- **[Provided]** The 2019 SSR used an SAC-estimated HbA1c SD of 1.4 percentage points, an assumed difference of −0.8 percentage points, and 10% Week-26 HbA1c missingness; it confirmed approximately 150 participants.
- **[Provided]** The assumed statistical test was a superiority comparison of HbA1c change. Required event counts and non-inferiority/equivalence margins are not applicable.
- **[Provided]** No clustering or design effect was described; the study is an individually randomized parallel-arm trial.
- **[Provided]** Assumptions reflected limited pediatric GLP-1 receptor agonist experience, broad baseline HbA1c values, and inclusion of metformin and/or basal-insulin users. The rationale was to preserve power while limiting placebo exposure and accommodating uncertainty in pediatric endpoint variability.
- **[Clarification Required]** The original calculation method and derivation of the reported power values require documentation if numerical reproduction is required. The supplied assumptions alone do not establish the exact calculation algorithm or multiplicity treatment used for individual-dose power.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

**[Provided]** Two primary estimands compare pooled dulaglutide with placebo for Week-26 HbA1c:

- **[Provided]** **Efficacy estimand:** intention-to-treat (ITT), with post-rescue data censored.
- **[Provided]** **Treatment-regimen estimand:** ITT regardless of rescue treatment or adherence.

**[Provided]** Each primary estimand has the full two-sided 0.05 significance level.

**[Provided]** After success of H1, pooled-dulaglutide HbA1c, a graphical procedure transfers alpha among H2–H3, individual-dose HbA1c; H4–H6, HbA1c <7.0%; H7–H9, FBG; and H10–H12, BMI. The three-comparison endpoint families cover pooled dulaglutide, 1.5 mg, and 0.75 mg versus placebo.

**[Provided]** The graphical scheme is run separately for treatment-regimen washout-MI, efficacy, and EMA-restricted efficacy populations. It may be revised using blinded Week-26 correlations before database lock.

**[Proposed]** For continuous change endpoints, define treatment effects as dulaglutide minus placebo and test a zero difference against a two-sided alternative. Claim superiority only for a statistically significant effect in the favorable direction. Rationale: this operationalizes the supplied two-sided superiority framework. Assumption: lower HbA1c, FBG, and BMI changes represent the intended favorable directions.

**[Proposed]** For HbA1c target attainment, define favorable effects as higher response probabilities with dulaglutide. Rationale: the endpoint represents achievement of a glycemic target. Assumption: response definitions are identical across randomized groups.

**[Clarification Required]** The graphical procedure’s initial weights, transition weights, recycling rules, exact hypothesis-to-dose mapping, and endpoint test statistics are not supplied. These are necessary to implement multiplicity control.

**[Clarification Required]** Whether overall primary success requires one or both primary estimands to succeed, and how claims are coordinated across the separately tested analysis frameworks, is unspecified. Separate full-alpha procedures do not by themselves define an overall cross-framework error-control or success rule.

**[Clarification Required]** EMA-restricted efficacy eligibility and its relationship to the full ITT population are undefined. These restrictions must be fixed before analysis because they determine the population supporting that procedure.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

**[Provided]** The SAC could recommend a variability-only sample-size re-estimation after approximately 100 participants had enrolled. Enrollment was bounded at 150–189, and Lilly decided whether to implement the recommendation.

**[Provided]** The blinded Week-26 SD was estimated using a restricted maximum likelihood mixed model for repeated measures (REML MMRM) containing baseline HbA1c, insulin/metformin use, treatment, visit, and treatment-by-visit interaction.

**[Provided]** The SSR does not require alpha adjustment.

**[Clarification Required]** The SAC’s remit, data access, available Week-26 information at the SSR, and implementation of a treatment term while maintaining blinding require documentation. These details establish whether the stated blinded, variability-only procedure was maintained.

**[Clarification Required]** The relationship between the variability-only SSR rule and the 2019 use of revised treatment-difference and missingness assumptions requires explanation. It matters whether these were planning scenarios or inputs to an adaptation decision.

**[Clarification Required]** No efficacy or futility interim analysis, statistical stopping boundary, or formal safety-stopping procedure is specified. Any such monitoring must be defined before use because it may affect participant protection and operating characteristics.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **[Provided]** Baseline is Visit 2.
- **[Provided]** The primary database lock occurs after all randomized participants complete Week 26/Visit 9. The sponsor unblinds after that lock to analyze and report primary data.
- **[Provided]** Final database lock occurs after all participants complete the 4-week safety follow-up/Visit 801.
- **[Provided]** Week 13 is fasting, with predose and 1-to-12-hour postdose PK sampling.
- **[Provided]** Week 26 is fasting, with predose PK, central laboratories, ECG, and EQ-5D-Y; the visit window is ±6 days.
- **[Provided]** Week-39 PK sampling is permitted ≤2 days postdose.
- **[Provided]** Week 52 is fasting, with PK, ECG, and EQ-5D-Y; the visit window is ±7 days.
- **[Provided]** Visit 801/Week 56 occurs approximately 30 days later and includes safety follow-up and PK.
- **[Provided]** EQ-5D-Y is assessed at baseline, Weeks 26 and 52, or early termination.

**[Proposed]** Finalize and version-control the statistical analysis plan, analysis derivations, and any blinded correlation-based graphical revisions before primary lock and sponsor unblinding. Rationale: this limits data-driven analytical changes. Assumption: blinded data review is feasible before lock.

**[Clarification Required]** Rules for database lock when participants withdraw or are lost to follow-up, and operational reconciliation of “4-week,” “approximately 30-day,” and Week-56 follow-up descriptions, are needed to establish data completeness and visit assignment.

**[Clarification Required]** Visit windows other than the supplied Week-26 and Week-52 windows, unscheduled-visit assignment, and selection among multiple measurements are unspecified. These decisions can change endpoint values and must be prespecified.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

**[Provided]** Primary testing uses two-sided alpha 0.05 separately for the efficacy and treatment-regimen estimands. Subsequent confirmatory testing follows the graphical procedure.

**[Proposed]** Report treatment-effect estimates with nominal two-sided 95% confidence intervals, explicitly distinguishing nominal intervals from multiplicity-controlled conclusions. Rationale: intervals communicate precision without implying unsupported simultaneous coverage. Assumption: the chosen models provide valid standard errors or suitable alternatives.

**[Clarification Required]** Whether multiplicity-adjusted confidence intervals are required, and how they would correspond to the final graph, is unspecified. This matters for consistent interpretation of confirmatory estimates and tests.

**[Proposed]** Treat analyses outside the finalized confirmatory graph as exploratory, with clearly identified nominal inference where applicable. Rationale: this avoids overstating evidence from numerous endpoints. Assumption: no additional confirmatory family is subsequently prespecified.

**[Provided]** No between-group inference is made after Week 26.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

**[Proposed]** Maintain a blinded deviation listing covering eligibility, assigned treatment, dosing, prohibited medication, rescue, visit timing, and endpoint assessment. Classify important deviations before unblinding. Rationale: consistent classification reduces outcome-driven exclusions. Assumption: source records support reliable identification.

**[Proposed]** Do not exclude randomized participants from ITT analyses solely because of a protocol deviation; apply only the estimand-specific data-handling rules. Rationale: this preserves randomized comparisons. Assumption: participants permit use of their available data.

**[Clarification Required]** Thresholds defining important deviations, acceptable adherence, dosing interruptions, and prohibited concomitant treatment are unspecified. They are needed for consistent reporting and any supportive restricted analysis.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

**[Provided]** Both primary estimands use ITT populations, with different handling of post-rescue data.

**[Proposed]** Operationally define ITT as all randomized participants analyzed according to assigned baseline treatment. Rationale: this implements the supplied ITT principle. Assumption: randomization records and permitted data use are available.

**[Provided]** Efficacy analyses censor post-rescue observations; treatment-regimen analyses retain the treatment assignment regardless of rescue or adherence.

**[Clarification Required]** Efficacy-estimand handling of treatment discontinuation, nonadherence, treatment switching, and intercurrent events other than rescue is unspecified. These decisions are necessary to define the targeted treatment effect fully.

**[Proposed]** Define the safety population as participants receiving at least one study-treatment dose, summarized by treatment actually received and relevant treatment period. Rationale: safety interpretation depends on exposure. Assumption: dosing records reliably identify actual exposure.

**[Proposed]** Define PK and ADA evaluability separately according to availability of interpretable samples and necessary dosing or assay information; report exclusions and reasons. Rationale: specialized analyses require usable measurements. Assumption: evaluability rules are finalized without reference to treatment outcomes.

**[Clarification Required]** The EMA-restricted efficacy population and any per-protocol population require explicit membership rules. Otherwise their analyses cannot be reproduced or interpreted.

Rationale: participant disposition supports assessment of selection and attrition. Assumption: screening and disposition logs are maintained.

**[Clarification Required]** Definitions of enrollment, repeat screening, and counting unique screened individuals are unspecified. They affect recruitment totals and observed screen-failure rates.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

**[Provided]** Inclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** Male or female, age 10 to <18 years, with IDF-ISPAD T2DM.
- **[Provided]** Lifestyle measures and, when used, metformin and basal insulin stable for ≥8 weeks before screening.
- **[Provided]** Metformin dose ≥1000 mg/day or the highest tolerated/local-approved dose.
- **[Provided]** Screening HbA1c >6.5% to ≤11.0%, or ≤9.0% if newly diagnosed/lifestyle only.
- **[Provided]** BMI >85th percentile.
- **[Provided]** Weight ≥50 kg.
- **[Provided]** Assent and parental consent.

**[Provided]** Exclusion criteria are:

- **[Provided]** T1DM/antibodies.
- **[Provided]** Ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar syndrome.
- **[Provided]** Gastrointestinal, pancreatic, thyroid, renal, or cardiovascular risks.
- **[Provided]** Hb variants.
- **[Provided]** Pregnancy, breastfeeding, or ineffective contraception.
- **[Provided]** Unstable psychiatric treatment.
- **[Provided]** Prohibited medicines or participation in other trials.
- **[Provided]** Other antihyperglycemic medicines require a ≥3-month washout.
- **[Provided]** Bolus insulin requires a ≥6-week washout, except ≤14-day acute-condition rescue.

**[Clarification Required]** Operational diagnostic criteria, relevant antibodies and cutoffs, definitions of newly diagnosed disease and treatment stability, the BMI reference population, and interpretation of the newly diagnosed/lifestyle-only HbA1c restriction require specification. These details determine eligibility consistently across sites.

**[Clarification Required]** Detailed risk exclusions, laboratory thresholds, look-back periods, relevant Hb variants, prohibited medicines, contraception requirements, and the definition of unstable psychiatric treatment are unspecified. The broad exclusion categories cannot alone support reproducible screening.

**[Clarification Required]** Rescue eligibility, glucose thresholds, rescue agents, timing, background-treatment adjustments, and treatment-discontinuation rules are unspecified. These decisions affect safety, censoring, and the interpretation of both primary estimands.

**[Clarification Required]** Procedures for loss to follow-up, withdrawal of data-use permission, incident pregnancy, and early-termination safety assessment require specification. They determine permissible follow-up and handling of incomplete records.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

**[Provided]** Baseline is Visit 2; randomization strata use screening HbA1c, metformin use, and insulin use.

**[Proposed]** Summarize baseline demographics, disease duration, glycemic measures, anthropometry, Tanner stage, and background medication and dose information where collected, by randomized arm and pooled dulaglutide. Rationale: these characterize the population and contextualize outcomes. Assumption: the corresponding baseline data are available.

**[Proposed]** Use descriptive summaries—counts and percentages for categorical variables and appropriate location and dispersion measures for continuous variables—without baseline significance tests. Rationale: randomization, rather than baseline testing, establishes the comparison framework. Assumption: any covariate adjustment is prespecified independently of observed imbalance.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is change in central-laboratory HbA1c from baseline to Week 26, comparing pooled dulaglutide 0.75 mg and 1.5 mg with placebo.

**[Derived]** For a participant with both measurements, HbA1c change is Week-26 HbA1c minus baseline HbA1c, expressed in percentage points.

**[Provided]** The primary outcome is evaluated under both the efficacy estimand, ITT with post-rescue censoring, and the treatment-regimen estimand, ITT regardless of rescue/adherence.

**[Derived]** All Dula overlaps the two individual active-dose groups and is not a fourth independent randomized arm.

**[Proposed]** Estimate pooled dulaglutide using an equally weighted contrast of the two active-dose effects from a three-arm model. Rationale: this reflects equal planned allocation to the active doses without allowing incidental group-size differences to redefine the pooled effect. Assumption: the intended pooled estimand gives both doses equal weight.

**[Clarification Required]** Pooled-dose weighting is not explicitly specified. It must be confirmed before lock because equal-dose weighting and participant-weighted pooling can estimate different effects.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

**[Provided]** Week-26 secondary efficacy outcomes are individual-dose HbA1c change, FBG change, HbA1c <7.0%, and BMI change versus placebo, including pooled dulaglutide where specified.

**[Provided]** Week-52 secondary efficacy outcomes are HbA1c, FBG, and BMI changes and HbA1c <7.0%. No between-group inference is made after Week 26.

**[Provided]** Exploratory outcomes at Weeks 26 and 52 are:

- **[Provided]** HbA1c ≤6.5%.
- **[Provided]** HbA1c <7.0% without severe, documented symptomatic (<70 mg/dL), or probable hypoglycemia.
- **[Provided]** HbA1c ≤6.5% without severe, documented symptomatic (<70 mg/dL), or probable hypoglycemia.
- **[Provided]** Weight, BMI SDS, height, height SDS, and waist.
- **[Provided]** Morning prolactin, IGF-1, estradiol, testosterone in males, LH, and cortisol.
- **[Provided]** Lipids and Tanner stage.
- **[Provided]** EQ-5D-Y VAS and domain levels.
- **[Provided]** Basal-insulin-dose change.

**[Provided]** Additional exploratory outcomes are HbA1c change at Week 13 and insulin resistance, beta-cell function, and adiponectin at Weeks 13 and 26.

**[Provided]** Secondary safety and PK/PD outcomes include the safety measures detailed in Section 5.6 and characterization of PK and exposure relationships with efficacy and safety.

**[Clarification Required]** Definitions and calculation methods for insulin resistance, beta-cell function, BMI/height SDS, hormone and lipid summaries, and basal-insulin-dose change are unspecified. Reference standards, units, and eligible analysis subsets affect reproducibility.

**[Clarification Required]** The observation period for “without hypoglycemia,” definitions of severe and probable hypoglycemia, and handling of incompletely observed hypoglycemia histories are unspecified. These decisions directly determine composite responder status.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

**[Proposed]** For the efficacy estimand, analyze repeated HbA1c changes using REML MMRM with categorical treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline HbA1c, and baseline insulin and metformin use. Obtain Week-26 treatment contrasts from the fitted model after post-rescue censoring. Rationale: this uses longitudinal information and aligns structurally with the supplied SSR model. Assumption: the conditional mean model and missing-at-random assumptions are adequate; this efficacy model is proposed, not a supplied primary-analysis specification.

**[Proposed]** Initially use an unstructured within-participant covariance matrix, with a prespecified simpler fallback if estimation fails. Rationale: this avoids imposing an unnecessary correlation pattern while allowing a reproducible convergence solution. Assumption: repeated-measure availability supports estimation.

**[Clarification Required]** The complete efficacy-model specification—including included visits, baseline-by-visit terms, screening HbA1c stratum adjustment, covariance fallback, degrees-of-freedom method, and convergence criteria—requires finalization. These choices affect estimates, standard errors, and tests.

**[Provided]** The treatment-regimen analysis framework includes washout-MI.

**[Proposed]** For treatment-regimen analyses, retain observed outcomes regardless of rescue/adherence and analyze completed Week-26 datasets using baseline-adjusted three-arm models, combining estimates and variances using Rubin’s rules. Rationale: this supports the supplied all-regimen target while accommodating missing outcomes. Assumption: the finalized washout-MI procedure targets that estimand and is compatible with the analysis model.

**[Proposed]** Analyze continuous Week-26 secondary outcomes using analogous baseline-adjusted longitudinal models, with the corresponding baseline outcome and prespecified randomization factors. Rationale: consistent modeling improves interpretability across continuous endpoints. Assumption: outcome distributions and residual behavior support these models.

**[Proposed]** Analyze Week-26 binary glycemic outcomes using treatment models adjusted for baseline HbA1c and insulin/metformin use; report response counts, estimated probabilities, treatment contrasts, and confidence intervals. Rationale: this quantifies both absolute and relative evidence for target attainment. Assumption: event counts support the prespecified model.

**[Clarification Required]** The binary-model link, primary effect scale, graph-compatible test statistic, and sparse-data fallback are unspecified. They must be fixed before testing because alternative choices can yield different p-values.

**[Proposed]** Summarize Week-52 efficacy by the specified treatment sequences using observed values, changes, responder proportions, and within-sequence uncertainty where appropriate, without between-group tests or comparative confidence intervals. Rationale: this respects the supplied restriction on post-Week-26 inference. Assumption: descriptive estimates are not interpreted as randomized extension comparisons.

**[Proposed]** Summarize Tanner stage and EQ-5D-Y domain levels categorically, and analyze VAS and other continuous exploratory measures using methods appropriate to their scales. Rationale: these outcomes have different measurement properties. Assumption: scoring conventions and valid ranges are finalized before analysis.

### 5.4 Missing Data

**[Proposed]** Tabulate missing assessments by treatment, visit, and reason, distinguishing rescue-related censoring from unobserved measurements, treatment discontinuation, and withdrawal. Rationale: different mechanisms have different implications for the estimands. Assumption: missingness reasons are recorded adequately.

**[Proposed]** Interpret the efficacy MMRM as estimating outcomes under the specified post-rescue censoring strategy, conditional on included covariates and observed history. Rationale: censoring does not eliminate the need for assumptions about unobserved outcomes. Assumption: those outcomes are missing at random under the fitted model.

**[Clarification Required]** Rescue-date conventions, treatment of measurements on the rescue date, and handling of rescue that begins and ends between visits are unspecified. These rules determine which observations enter efficacy analyses.

**[Clarification Required]** “Washout-MI” lacks an operational definition: washout duration, eligible donor/reference data, post-discontinuation and post-rescue assumptions, imputation model, and handling of participants without postbaseline data are not supplied. These are essential to implementing the treatment-regimen analysis.

**[Proposed]** Include randomized treatment, baseline characteristics relevant to outcome and missingness, observed longitudinal outcomes, and rescue/discontinuation information in the imputation strategy where compatible with its estimand. Rationale: these data can improve prediction and plausibility of conditional assumptions. Assumption: the sample supports the specified model without excessive complexity.

**[Proposed]** Select the number of imputations using Monte Carlo stability checks, retain reproducible random seeds, and examine imputation diagnostics. Rationale: this limits simulation error and detects implausible imputations. Assumption: computational resources and an implementable imputation model are available.

**[Proposed]** Evaluate departures from missing-at-random assumptions using prespecified pattern-mixture or delta-adjusted sensitivity analyses, including a tipping-point assessment for the primary outcome. Rationale: this assesses robustness to informative missingness. Assumption: clinically interpretable departures can be specified without reference to unblinded results.

**[Proposed]** When binary glycemic outcomes are derived from imputed HbA1c, apply the supplied thresholds within each completed dataset. Do not equate missing hypoglycemia information with absence of hypoglycemia. Rationale: consistent derivation avoids incompatible responder and continuous analyses. Assumption: composite-event ascertainment has a separately finalized missing-data rule.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

**[Proposed]** Examine Week-26 treatment-effect consistency across screening HbA1c strata and baseline metformin and insulin use using interaction terms and descriptive estimates. **[Proposed]** Develop population PK and exposure–response analyses using actual dose histories, sample times, and interpretable concentration measurements. **[Clarification Required]** PK structural models, exposure metrics, covariates, assay limits, handling of below-quantification observations, and exposure–response model forms are unspecified. These decisions determine model identifiability and interpretation.
