# Clinical Study Protocol and Statistical Analysis Framework

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

- **[Provided]** This phase IIa study evaluates BAY 1213790, a fully human IgG1 anti-factor XIa antibody that binds FXIa near its catalytic center, in patients undergoing elective primary unilateral total knee arthroplasty (TKA).
- **[Provided]** TKA carries substantial venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk, while established thromboprophylaxis entails bleeding risk. Selective FXIa inhibition is being assessed for VTE prevention with a potentially favorable bleeding profile.
- **[Provided]** BAY 1213790 dose selection across 0.3–1.8 mg/kg was informed by preclinical work, Phase 1 population pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) analyses, and systems pharmacology.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **[Provided]** **P1—Primary:** Assess safety and efficacy of different BAY 1213790 doses versus enoxaparin in elective primary unilateral TKA. The objective-level outcome is safety and efficacy of these doses versus enoxaparin.
- **[Provided]** **S1—Secondary:** Compare safety and efficacy of BAY 1213790 with apixaban. The objective-level outcome is safety and efficacy compared with apixaban.
- **[Provided]** **S2—Exploratory:** Assess BAY 1213790 dose-response for efficacy and safety. The objective-level outcome is the efficacy and safety dose-response relationship.
- **[Provided]** **S3—Exploratory:** Characterize BAY 1213790 PK/PD and their relationship. The objective-level outcome is BAY 1213790 PK/PD and their relationship.
- **[Provided]** **S4—Other:** Further characterize BAY 1213790 using additional PD markers and investigate thrombosis mechanisms using additional PD markers. The objective-level outcome is additional PD-marker characterization and investigation of thrombosis mechanisms.

## 2. Study Methods

### 2.1 Trial Design

- **[Provided]** This is a randomized, active-comparator-controlled, multicenter phase IIa study. Treatment assignment is open-label; observers are blinded to BAY 1213790 dose, and CIAC adjudication is blinded.
- **[Provided]** Part A administers BAY 1213790 after surgery. Sequential Part B administers BAY 1213790 before surgery. The parts do not recruit in parallel.
- **[Provided]** Allocation is 5:1:1 for pooled BAY 1213790:enoxaparin:apixaban, adaptable after arm closure or opening. BAY recipients are allocated 1:1:1 among Part A doses and 1:1 among Part B doses, subject to adjustment after arm closure or opening.
- **[Provided]** **Part A:** BAY 1213790 0.3, 0.6, or 1.2 mg/kg is administered once intravenously after adequate hemostasis on the day after TKA, preferably in the morning and no later than 24 hours after wound closure. A 1.8-mg/kg arm may open.
- **[Provided]** **Part B:** BAY 1213790 0.3 or 1.2 mg/kg is administered once intravenously in the second half of pre-surgery Day 1. IDMC/SC may select 0.3, 0.6, 1.2, or 1.8 mg/kg or add an arm. Part B begins only after Part A completion and review of at least 75% of Part A primary assessments.
- **[Provided]** **Enoxaparin in both parts:** 40 mg subcutaneously daily, starting the evening before TKA or 6–8 hours after TKA and continuing for at least 10 days to venography.
- **[Provided]** **Apixaban in both parts:** 2.5 mg orally twice daily, starting after TKA and within 24 hours of wound closure, continuing for at least 10 days to venography.
- **[Clarification Required]** Infusion preparation, infusion duration, dose-weight conventions, adequate-hemostasis criteria, treatment interruption rules, and permitted rescue or concomitant antithrombotic treatment require specification because they determine consistent administration and interpretation of treatment exposure.
- **[Clarification Required]** The responsibilities and operating charters of CIAC and IDMC/SC, including adjudicated outcomes and access to unblinded information, require confirmation to operationalize the supplied oversight and blinding arrangements.

### 2.2 Randomization

- **[Provided]** IxRS first randomizes patients to BAY 1213790, enoxaparin, or apixaban, then randomizes BAY recipients to a dose arm.
- **[Provided]** Stratification factors, minimization, and blocking are not specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** The sequence-generation method, concealment procedures, any stratification or blocking, and implementation of allocation changes must be finalized because they affect allocation integrity and statistical adjustment.
- **[Proposed]** Maintain an auditable IxRS record of allocation probabilities, active arms, and effective dates of adaptations. Rationale: changing arm availability must be reconstructable for analysis; assumption: IxRS can retain this information.
- **[Proposed]** Restrict dose-identifying records from dose-blinded observers and treatment-identifying records from blinded adjudicators. Rationale: this operationalizes the stated blinding; assumption: access controls can distinguish these roles.

### 2.3 Sample Size

- **[Provided]** The initial target is 700 patients: 500 BAY 1213790 recipients across five intended arms and 100 recipients each of enoxaparin and apixaban. Maximum enrollment is 900 patients.
- **[Provided]** Arm targets are 100 patients per BAY and comparator arm. Each comparator is to enroll at least 60 patients in Part A and at least 40 in Part B.
- **[Provided]** Calculations use the primary efficacy composite VTE endpoint at Day 12+3 as a binary event proportion, a risk-difference effect measure, a 5% non-inferiority margin, and one-sided 5% significance.
- **[Provided]** Assumed event proportions are 25% for enoxaparin, 10% for apixaban, and 10% or 4% for BAY 1213790 in the respective scenarios.
- **[Provided]** For BAY 1213790 at 10% versus enoxaparin at 25%, calculated power is 81% for superiority and 97% for non-inferiority. For BAY 1213790 at 4% versus enoxaparin at 25%, calculated power is 99% for superiority and 99% for non-inferiority.
- **[Provided]** Apixaban scenarios provide descriptive calculation context: superiority/non-inferiority powers of 0%/29% and 43%/88%.
- **[Provided]** The assumed tests use the lower bound of a two-sided 90% normal-approximation confidence interval (CI) for risk difference for one-sided non-inferiority and superiority testing.
- **[Provided]** Approximately 20% unavailable or non-evaluable venography due to quality or dropout is allowed, yielding 80 evaluable patients per arm.
- **[Provided]** A separate variability assumption and required-event target are not applicable: calculations concern binary proportions rather than a continuous outcome or event-driven design. No clustering design effect applies because patients are individually randomized.
- **[Provided]** The assumptions are based on the event-rate scenarios and 20% non-evaluable venography allowance. The phase II rationale is to characterize dose safety and efficacy, with examples illustrating non-inferiority/superiority precision versus enoxaparin.
- **[Provided]** After approximately 75% of Part A Visit 7 assessments, pooled unavailable venography exceeding 30% may trigger increased enrollment to regain 80% superiority power. Arm or dose enrollment may also change on IDMC/SC advice, within 900 patients.
- **[Clarification Required]** The comparator pooling strategy across parts must be specified. The initial comparator totals are shared between sequential parts, so part-specific versus pooled comparisons have different precision and power.
- **[Clarification Required]** The re-estimation formula, event-rate assumptions, rounding rules, and target allocation following re-estimation require specification. The supplied trigger and enrollment cap do not uniquely determine a revised sample size.

### 2.4 Hypothesis Framework

- **[Provided]** For each BAY dose/time arm versus enoxaparin, non-inferiority is tested first, followed by superiority, each one-sided at global α=5%. The fixed sequence stops after the first unrejected null.
- **[Provided]** The supplied decision rules state that non-inferiority requires the lower 90% CI bound for BAY minus enoxaparin to be below 5%, and superiority requires that lower bound to be below 0.
- **[Provided]** No hypothesis testing is planned versus apixaban; comparisons use descriptive proportions and 90% CIs.
- **[Clarification Required]** The confidence-bound decision rule must be resolved before inferential implementation. For a VTE risk difference defined as BAY minus enoxaparin, a lower bound below 5% or 0 does not establish that BAY excludes an unacceptable increase or has a lower event risk; uncertainty alone can satisfy those conditions.
- **[Proposed]** If the contrast remains \(\Delta=p_{\mathrm{BAY}}-p_{\mathrm{enoxaparin}}\), a candidate formal amendment is to test non-inferiority as \(H_0:\Delta\geq0.05\) versus \(H_1:\Delta<0.05\), using the **upper** 90% CI bound below 0.05, followed by superiority as \(H_0:\Delta\geq0\) versus \(H_1:\Delta<0\), using the upper bound below 0. Rationale: this aligns the bound with lower VTE risk being favorable; assumption: the stated contrast, margin, and intended clinical direction are correct. This is not an authorized replacement for the supplied rule.
- **[Clarification Required]** The complete fixed sequence across dose/time arms, including newly opened arms, must be specified. Within-arm non-inferiority-to-superiority ordering alone does not establish global 5% error control across multiple arms.
- **[Proposed]** Withhold formal non-inferiority and superiority declarations until the contrast/bound issue and complete multiplicity sequence are approved before access to relevant unblinded results. Rationale: unresolved rules prevent valid prespecified decisions; assumption: formal declarations remain intended.

### 2.5 Statistical Interim Analyses

- **[Provided]** The specified interim activities include the approximately 75% Part A Visit 7 review for unavailable venography and possible sample-size increase, IDMC/SC advice on arm or dose enrollment, and the review required before Part B initiation.
- **[Clarification Required]** Whether these reviews include comparative efficacy testing, formal safety stopping boundaries, or futility criteria is not specified. These decisions matter for operating characteristics, patient protection, and multiplicity.
- **[Clarification Required]** The denominator for pooled unavailable venography and its relationship to non-evaluable venography must be defined because these determine whether the greater-than-30% trigger is reached.
- **[Proposed]** Conduct availability-based re-estimation using treatment-masked pooled information, with any unblinded review confined to authorized oversight personnel. Rationale: this reduces operational bias; assumption: the re-estimation can be performed without treatment-specific efficacy estimates.
- **[Proposed]** Record each review’s data cutoff, information reviewed, recommendation, authorization, and allocation changes. Rationale: adaptations require an audit trail; assumption: oversight decisions are documented prospectively.

### 2.6 Timing of Analyses

- **[Provided]** Primary completion occurs when all patients have been assessed at Visit 7. Study end is the last patient’s last visit.
- **[Provided]** Database blinding continues through final database lock after the last patient’s last visit and authorization of release.
- **[Provided]** Screening occurs on Day −13 to Day 1; randomization is on Day 1; TKA is on Day 2.
- **[Provided]** Treatment visits occur on Days 3, 4, 6±1, and 12+3. Bilateral venography and primary outcome assessment occur at Visit 7, Day 12+3.
- **[Provided]** Post-treatment visits occur on Day 30±5, Day 90±7, and Day 150±7.
- **[Clarification Required]** The timing and permitted access for any primary-completion analysis before final database lock must be specified. Primary completion and final study completion are distinct milestones, and database blinding is required through final lock.
- **[Clarification Required]** The scope of database blinding requires definition because treatment assignment is open-label while dose observation and adjudication have separate blinding requirements.
- **[Proposed]** Finalize endpoint derivations, analysis populations, and statistical code specifications before authorized database release. Rationale: this limits data-driven analysis choices; assumption: these specifications can be completed while the analytical database remains blinded.

## 3. Statistical Principles

### 3.1 Significance and Confidence Intervals

- **[Provided]** The supplied inferential framework uses one-sided global α=5% and two-sided 90% CIs for BAY versus enoxaparin, subject to its fixed sequence.
- **[Provided]** Apixaban comparisons are descriptive and use proportions and 90% CIs without hypothesis testing.
- **[Clarification Required]** Formal significance statements remain dependent on resolution of the confidence-bound direction and cross-arm testing sequence described in Section 2.4.
- **[Proposed]** Report event counts, denominators, proportions, risk differences, and 90% CIs for each prespecified comparison. Rationale: these show effect magnitude and precision independently of testing; assumption: endpoint definitions and analysis sets are approved.
- **[Proposed]** Label analyses outside the approved testing sequence as exploratory, with no confirmatory interpretation of nominal intervals or P values. Rationale: this prevents unsupported multiplicity claims; assumption: no additional error-control procedure is subsequently specified.

### 3.2 Protocol Deviations

- **[Proposed]** Classify deviations before unblinding by eligibility, assigned treatment, dose or timing, comparator adherence, prohibited medication, assessment timing, and blinding. Rationale: these domains can affect efficacy, safety, or ascertainment; assumption: the corresponding protocol requirements are finalized.
- **[Proposed]** Review important deviations without knowledge of comparative outcomes and retain a reproducible classification log. Rationale: this reduces outcome-driven exclusions; assumption: blinded review is operationally feasible.
- **[Clarification Required]** Exact thresholds for important deviations and per-protocol exclusions require approval, particularly for infusion timing, minimum comparator treatment duration, and venography windows, because they determine analysis membership.

### 3.3 Analysis Populations

- **[Proposed]** Define an all-randomized population, analyzed by assigned treatment and dose/time arm, retaining patients who do not receive treatment. Rationale: this preserves the randomized comparison; assumption: consent permits use of their available data.
- **[Proposed]** Define an efficacy-evaluable population consisting of randomized patients whose primary endpoint can be classified under the approved ascertainment rules, analyzed as assigned. Rationale: the sample-size calculation anticipates evaluable assessments; assumption: endpoint classification is possible independently of treatment knowledge.
- **[Proposed]** Define a per-protocol population using prospectively approved important-deviation exclusions. Rationale: a complementary adherence-focused analysis is useful for non-inferiority interpretation; assumption: exclusions can be specified without comparative outcome knowledge.
- **[Proposed]** Define a safety population of patients receiving any study treatment, analyzed by actual exposure, and a PK/PD population with usable measurements and documented BAY dosing. Rationale: these analyses concern observed exposure; assumption: dosing and sample records are reliable.
- **[Clarification Required]** Selection of the primary efficacy population, handling of treatment crossover or mixed exposures, and the required relationship between randomized and per-protocol conclusions must be finalized because no analysis populations are supplied.

## 4. Trial Population

### 4.1 Screening Data

- **[Proposed]** Maintain screening and disposition records showing assessed eligibility, exclusion reasons, consent, randomization, treatment receipt, surgery, Visit 7 assessment, and follow-up completion. Rationale: these distinguish recruitment losses from post-randomization missingness; assumption: screening records can be retained under applicable consent arrangements.
- **[Proposed]** Present disposition by part and assigned arm, including reasons for non-treatment, unavailable venography, withdrawal, and death. Rationale: these patterns may explain differences in evaluability; assumption: reasons are collected consistently.

### 4.2 Eligibility Criteria Summary

- **[Provided]** Inclusion requires consent, age at least 18 years, elective primary unilateral TKA, compliance with reproductive/contraception requirements, and ability to follow instructions. Part B additionally requires general anesthesia.
- **[Provided]** Exclusions include clinically significant bleeding risk involving specified hematologic, coagulation, hepatic, hypertension, surgery, or bleeding-disorder conditions; prior DVT; weight greater than 135 kg; CrCl below 60 mL/min by MDRD; and recent myocardial infarction or stroke.
- **[Provided]** Further exclusions are active cancer with specified exceptions, comparator contraindication, full anticoagulation or dual antiplatelet treatment, compression/stimulator use, contrast allergy, prior assignment, recent investigational treatment, and site affiliation.
- **[Provided]** Part A excludes postoperative epidural analgesia. Part B excludes regional anesthesia and epidural analgesia.
- **[Clarification Required]** The detailed bleeding-risk thresholds, cancer exceptions, definitions and windows for “recent” conditions or treatment, reproductive requirements, and scope of site affiliation require specification because the summary is insufficient for reproducible eligibility decisions.
- **[Clarification Required]** The operational renal-function calculation, units, and handling of borderline results require confirmation while retaining the supplied criterion, “CrCl <60 mL/min by MDRD.” These details affect eligibility classification.
- **[Clarification Required]** Permitted exceptions, if any, for compression/stimulator use and comparator contraindications must be defined because these exclusions affect perioperative management and eligibility.

### 4.3 Recruitment

- **[Provided]** Recruitment is multicenter and sequential across Parts A and B, without parallel recruitment.
- **[Provided]** Enrollment follows the initial 700-patient target, arm targets and comparator part minima, permitted adaptations, and 900-patient maximum.
- **[Clarification Required]** Countries, sites, recruitment dates, site targets, and the operational definition of Part A completion require confirmation because they determine recruitment feasibility and the transition to Part B.
- **[Proposed]** Monitor enrollment centrally by part, arm, and comparator minimum, implementing changes only through authorized IxRS updates. Rationale: this supports the supplied allocation and enrollment constraints; assumption: timely centralized enrollment data are available.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and Follow-up

- **[Proposed]** Distinguish stopping study treatment, declining a procedure, withdrawing follow-up consent, and loss to follow-up. Rationale: these have different implications for exposure and outcome availability; assumption: the consent process permits these distinctions.
- **[Proposed]** Continue scheduled outcome and safety follow-up after treatment discontinuation when consent permits, recording reasons and subsequent antithrombotic treatment. Rationale: continued ascertainment reduces informative missingness; assumption: participation is not fully withdrawn.
- **[Clarification Required]** Withdrawal criteria, contact procedures, permitted use of previously collected data, and management of patients unable to undergo venography require specification because they affect participant rights and endpoint completeness.

### 4.5 Baseline Characteristics

- **[Proposed]** Summarize age, sex, weight, renal function, relevant medical history, baseline laboratory results, anesthesia, and available VTE/bleeding risk characteristics by part and assigned arm. Rationale: these characterize comparability and clinical context; assumption: these variables are collected.
- **[Proposed]** Use counts and percentages for categorical variables and suitable measures of location and dispersion for continuous variables; report missing counts without baseline significance tests. Rationale: descriptive assessment is appropriate for randomized-group characterization; assumption: no additional baseline reporting requirement is specified.
- **[Clarification Required]** Define baseline separately for variables measured before BAY dosing and before surgery, since treatment precedes surgery in Part B but follows it in Part A. This distinction matters for change-from-baseline and adjustment analyses.

## 5. Analysis

### 5.1 Primary Outcomes

- **[Provided]** The primary efficacy calculation outcome is the binary composite VTE endpoint at Visit 7, Day 12+3, when bilateral venography and primary outcome assessments are scheduled.
- **[Derived]** The primary efficacy proportion is patient-based rather than an event-count rate.
- **[Clarification Required]** The composite components, diagnostic criteria, anatomical categories, event window, and treatment of symptomatic events, fatal events, or events before venography must be defined. These determine whether each patient is an endpoint case.
- **[Clarification Required]** Rules for complete, unilateral, technically inadequate, or absent venography and for classification using other clinical evidence require specification. Absence of evaluable venography does not by itself establish either VTE or no VTE.
- **[Clarification Required]** A primary safety endpoint, its definitions, observation window, adjudication requirements, and relationship to the primary testing family are not supplied. The primary safety objective cannot be converted into a reproducible primary analysis without these decisions.

### 5.2 Secondary Outcomes

- **[Provided]** Secondary or exploratory objective-level outcomes cover safety and efficacy versus apixaban, BAY efficacy and safety dose-response, BAY PK/PD and their relationship, and additional PD markers for drug characterization and thrombosis mechanisms.
- **[Clarification Required]** Endpoint-level definitions for these objectives—including individual efficacy components, bleeding outcomes, PK parameters, PD assays, and sampling times—require specification because objective statements alone do not determine statistical variables.
- **[Proposed]** Once defined, summarize individual VTE components alongside the composite and report the approved safety outcomes by arm. Rationale: component results help interpret composite effects and benefit-risk patterns; assumption: component ascertainment is sufficiently reliable.
- **[Proposed]** Treat PK/PD, additional-marker, and mechanism analyses as exploratory rather than adding them to the primary testing sequence. Rationale: their supplied objectives are characterization and exploration; assumption: no separate confirmatory claim is intended.

### 5.3 Analysis Methods

- **[Proposed]** Keep each BAY dose/time arm distinct in the main displays, with comparator results shown separately by part and pooled. Rationale: dose and administration timing are explicit design features, and parts are sequential; assumption: part and treatment timing are recorded.
- **[Clarification Required]** Specify whether each formal BAY comparison uses concurrent within-part controls, pooled controls across parts, or an adjusted combination. This affects comparability, estimands, effective sample sizes, and consistency with the power calculations.
- **[Proposed]** For each approved efficacy comparison, estimate \(\widehat{\Delta}=\widehat p_{\mathrm{BAY}}-\widehat p_{\mathrm{enoxaparin}}\). As a candidate method aligned with the supplied calculation assumptions, use the unpooled normal-approximation 90% CI:
  \(\widehat{\Delta}\pm z_{0.95}\sqrt{\widehat p_B(1-\widehat p_B)/n_B+\widehat p_E(1-\widehat p_E)/n_E}\).
  Rationale: this provides an explicit implementation of the stated risk-difference/normal-approximation approach; assumption: independent binary observations and adequate approximation. Formal decisions remain subject to Section 2.4.
- **[Proposed]** Add a score-based risk-difference interval as a sensitivity analysis, retaining normal-approximation results for transparency. Rationale: low event counts can make normal intervals unreliable; assumption: the sensitivity method is selected before unblinding.
- **[Proposed]** For apixaban, report descriptive event proportions, BAY-minus-apixaban risk differences, and 90% CIs without inferential testing. Rationale: this implements the supplied descriptive comparison while showing effect size; assumption: comparable endpoint definitions are used.
- **[Proposed]** Summarize approved safety endpoints as participant counts and proportions by actual exposure, with comparative estimates where clinically interpretable. Rationale: safety is a primary objective and depends on treatment received; assumption: exposure and safety windows are finalized.
- **[Clarification Required]** Define how treatment nonreceipt, delayed surgery, canceled surgery, rescue anticoagulation, treatment switching, and death before assessment enter the efficacy estimand. These events can change both outcome interpretation and the population being compared.
- **[Proposed]** Use any adjusted efficacy model as supportive, with covariates and part effects specified before unblinding. Rationale: adjustment may address prognostic imbalance or sequential-part differences without replacing the randomized comparison; assumption: adequate events and relevant baseline variables are available.

### 5.4 Missing Data

- **[Provided]** The design allows approximately 20% unavailable/non-evaluable venography and permits a sample-size review when pooled unavailable venography exceeds 30%.
- **[Proposed]** Report missingness by arm, part, reason, assessment, and availability of other endpoint evidence. Rationale: missingness may reflect clinical events, withdrawal, or technical quality rather than a single mechanism; assumption: reason codes are collected.
- **[Proposed]** Do not classify missing venography automatically as no VTE. Apply approved clinical endpoint rules first and retain unresolved outcomes as missing. Rationale: automatic non-event assignment could bias treatment comparisons; assumption: endpoint status can sometimes be established without venography.
- **[Clarification Required]** The primary missing-outcome strategy must be approved together with the primary analysis population. Complete-case analysis, imputation, and clinical classification address different assumptions and can materially affect non-inferiority.
- **[Proposed]** Conduct extreme-case sensitivity analyses assigning unresolved BAY outcomes as events and comparator outcomes as non-events, and then reversing those assignments. Rationale: these bound the influence of missing binary outcomes; assumption: unresolved outcomes remain binary under the final definition.
- **[Proposed]** Add a model-based imputation analysis and a departure-from-missing-at-random sensitivity analysis if data support them. Rationale: these examine more plausible and informative missingness scenarios; assumption: relevant predictors and sufficient observations are available.
- **[Proposed]** Do not impute unobserved adverse events as absent; distinguish incomplete safety follow-up from observed absence of events. Rationale: follow-up incompleteness limits safety ascertainment; assumption: observation duration is recorded.

### 5.5 Additional Analyses

- **[Proposed]** Examine BAY efficacy and safety dose-response separately within administration-timing parts, initially using dose-specific estimates and plots. Rationale: this addresses S2 without imposing a shared pre-/postoperative relationship; assumption: sufficient observations exist at each opened dose.
- **[Clarification Required]** Any formal dose-response model, dose scale, monotonicity assumption, or pooling across parts requires prespecification because these choices are not supplied and can materially alter conclusions.
- **[Proposed]** Describe BAY concentration-time and PD-time profiles and explore exposure–PD and exposure–outcome relationships using methods supported by the sampling design. Rationale: this addresses S3 without assuming unprovided sampling density; assumption: dosing times, sample times, and usable assays are available.
- **[Proposed]** Summarize additional PD markers and explore associations with thrombosis outcomes without causal claims. Rationale: this supports S4 while acknowledging exploratory biomarker interpretation; assumption: marker assays and analysis timing are prespecified.
- **[Proposed]** Compare randomized, efficacy-evaluable, and per-protocol findings and examine sensitivity to comparator pooling and venography availability. Rationale: these assess robustness to key design and missing-data choices; assumption: each analysis can be constructed from approved definitions.
- **[Proposed]** Avoid interpreting cross-part differences as a randomized comparison of preoperative versus postoperative administration. Rationale: sequential enrollment does not independently randomize administration timing; assumption: no additional timing randomization exists.

### 5.6 Harms

- **[Clarification Required]** Definitions and ascertainment windows for bleeding, other adverse events, serious adverse events, laboratory abnormalities, and deaths must be finalized, together with severity, relatedness, and adjudication rules. These decisions determine safety endpoint consistency.
- **[Proposed]** Collect and reconcile harms at scheduled contacts through Day 150±7. Rationale: this uses the supplied follow-up schedule to support safety characterization; assumption: safety surveillance is intended through the final scheduled visit.
- **[Proposed]** Present participants with any event, numbers of events, severity, seriousness, discontinuations, and deaths by actual exposure, separately identifying perioperative timing where available. Rationale: participant incidence and recurrent-event burden provide complementary safety information; assumption: these attributes are collected.
- **[Proposed]** Evaluate bleeding using standardized, prospectively approved categories, with major and clinically relevant nonmajor categories considered during endpoint finalization. Rationale: clinically interpretable categories support benefit-risk assessment; assumption: suitable definitions and source data can be adopted. These categories are not supplied primary endpoints.
- **[Proposed]** Record clinically necessary treatment interruption, additional anticoagulation, transfusion, and other event management without delaying care for research procedures. Rationale: management affects both participant safety and outcome interpretation; assumption: site clinical care takes precedence over study assessments.
- **[Clarification Required]** Expedited reporting timelines, emergency unblinding procedures, and quantitative or qualitative safety stopping rules require approval because the supplied design does not specify them.
