## Requirements

- Use the Evaluation Input as the only source of study-specific facts.  
- Preserve all supplied objectives, design features, groups, interventions or index tests, numerical values, timing, eligibility criteria, assumptions, thresholds, and decision rules.  
- General clinical and statistical reasoning may be used, but missing study-specific facts must not be presented as known facts.  
- Do not identify the source study, use external information, consult a reference answer, or include citations or provenance.  
- A required heading does not imply that the corresponding information was supplied.

## Evidence Status and Rationale

Label every substantive paragraph, bullet, and table row with exactly one of the following:

- **\[Provided\]** — explicitly stated in the Evaluation Input.  
- **\[Derived\]** — follows necessarily and unambiguously from the Evaluation Input without an additional judgment or assumption.  
- **\[Proposed\]** — added using clinical, statistical, or operational judgment.  
- **\[Clarification Required\]** — depends on a material study-specific decision that cannot be determined from the Evaluation Input.

Section headings do not require labels.

If a paragraph, bullet, or table row contains content with different evidence statuses, split it into separate statements.

Do not label a judgment-based choice as **\[Derived\]** or unsupported study-specific information as **\[Provided\]**.

For each **\[Proposed\]** item, provide a brief rationale and state any important assumption.

For each **\[Clarification Required\]** item, identify the missing decision and explain why it matters.

Do not provide hidden reasoning or a step-by-step chain-of-thought explanation.
